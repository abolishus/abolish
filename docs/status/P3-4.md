## What was found (part 2a)

Recorded in [[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]], Amendment (sources pinned by commit there):

- **Curve.** The curve is Baby Jubjub's prime-order subgroup with `B8`, as assumed. `@noble/curves`' `babyjubjub` has the same parameters, so the attesters' checks stay on `@noble/*`.
- **Reuse.** zkPassport's salted mode verifies one combined proof against a public key `pk` inside every disclosure circuit. That is option B's shape, so its output side isn't reused. Its `oprf-auth` query circuit binds only `comm_in`, so FaceMatch isn't required in the circuit, only by TACEO's nodes and the SDK. It binds no registration record, though. Our query and output circuits are therefore our own. They compose TACEO's audited gadgets and take `comm_in` from zkPassport's pinned identity chain.
- **`H1`.** `H1` is `hash_to_curve`'s two-map structure under our scoped tag, not TACEO's single-map `encode` (`encode_to_curve`, not a random oracle). It is evaluated only in-circuit, so there is no TypeScript hash-to-curve gap.
- **SOD signature.** The signature is canonical. ECDSA must have low `s` and range-checked `r` and `s` (`noir-ecdsa` v0.5.0), and RSA signatures must be below the modulus. One document therefore gives one `x`.
- **zkPassport's app** can't query our panel. It supports only TACEO's 2-of-3 network, and salted mode needs strict FaceMatch. Real-document Tier 2 through the panel therefore waits for our own app (P5-3, P5-4). Part 3's QR handoff runs against MOCK documents.
- **"Poseidon2"** names both the Poseidon2 permutation (zkPassport, TACEO, Noir) and circomlib's two-input Poseidon ([[membership]]). Part 2b names each without ambiguity.

## Known-weak

- Anyone holding a document's chip data (the issuing state first) can query for it as its holder would, and learns its `N` and whether it registered (passive authentication only). The panel publishes query and duplicate counts so a mass probe is visible.
- A Diffie–Hellman OPRF leaves discrete-log relations on the key: a discrete-log break recovers the OPRF key `sk` from one recorded or self-made registration and, with chip data, maps a stolen attester list.
- `sk` is long-lived and held by people, unlike [[0006-trustees]] rule 7's per-election keys; resharing relies on erasure of old shares, and losing `n − k + 1` shares means re-registering Tier 2.
- Registration needs six of seven seats online; the attesters' nullifier lists aren't rebuilt from chain and IPFS.
- The query and output proofs carry `dg1` in their witness and are only computationally zero-knowledge towards the seats.
- The app that runs the query learns `x` and `N`, so a malicious build learns both (T-19). Since part 2a that app is our own (P5-3, P5-4), because zkPassport's app can't query the panel. Until it exists, real documents can't register at Tier 2 through the panel.
- `H1` and the query and output circuits are our composition of TACEO's audited gadgets, with no published vectors (T-36). Least Authority's findings D and E on the Elligator2 map need part 2b's argument.

## Independent review (part 1)

An in-session subagent reviewed the ADR against the `crypto-review` prompt. Its two blocking findings were fixed: the attesters couldn't check the combined evaluation `Z` (now each recombines it from the seats' signed, proven answers, and `B` and `Z` are public inputs of the output proof), and one corrupt or lagging seat shared by two quorums could attest a duplicate (now `max(k, n − 1)` seats sign each group position, only once every earlier `N` reached them). Its non-blocking findings and nits were all taken: the evaluation proof as a proof of knowledge with `J` from hash-to-group and signed answers, the output proof's binding to the registration record, the online-probe residual for any chip-data holder, the active-authentication wording, harvested pairs and registration KEM keys, the key's lifecycle against rule 7, the owner attribution, SOD signature malleability, who runs the query, zero knowledge towards the seats, scoped hashes with Poseidon2 for `H2`, the threshold one-more Diffie–Hellman assumption, and a new nullifier type with a MOCK test key.
