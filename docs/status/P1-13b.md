# P1-13b: Receipt evidence model

- State: in progress (part 1, [[0021-receipt-evidence]], merged in #PR; part 2, the spec, next; part 3, the code and vectors, after it)
- Branch: `claude/p1-13b`
- Phase: Phase 1: Threat model, spec, crypto core, circuits, verifier, reference election (CLI only)

## Scope

The receipt's later checks ([[receipts]], Later checks): the checks after the segment's deadline against the checkpoints anchored on L1, the check with a copy of the anchored board, and what makes a receipt evidence a third party can check, with [[board]]'s client rule 2. Split out of P1-13 part 2 (#48) at the owner's request in the session of 2026-10-12.

## Requirements

From #48's reviews (its draft of these checks and the review threads are in #48's history):

- **Time.** A receipt carries no time, so a key obtained after an election could forge evidence against it for good. The evidence must be bounded in time (the draft used 30 days of L1 time after the seal), and a voter's evidence of a real drop must survive the bound (the draft used salted L1 commitments carried by any transaction whose input is a list of 32-byte commitments, judged at `min(latest, dispute_end)`, never at the commitment's block).
- **Withheld boards.** An operator can anchor a fork that no checkpoint contradicts from L1 alone, and withhold the board until any bound has passed; clients must keep their evidence (the draft committed every receipt in doubt).
- **Keys.** The receipt key set that judges an election is fixed before voting starts (in the election definition, or recorded per election from the release current when it opens), so a later release can't void receipts, and a key not bound to an election can't forge against it.
- **Presence.** A dropped ballot is shown only by a valid ballot that no admitted entry of the anchored board carries by `(tier, ν, M)`, whatever its selection outcome; a rejected or re-randomised copy proves nothing. Needs P1-12's ballot record and admission.
- **Privacy.** Commitments are salted and posted through relayers; proofs are fetched for every segment, never for the voter's own alone (T-45, T-11).
- **Admission and selection.** The voter's later check shows the ballot's admission verdict and whether it is the selected ballot of its tier and nullifier ([[revoting]], Residuals; T-41, T-42).
- **The voter's own view.** After any bound, the voter's client still shows its own result, marked as no longer evidence (G-7).

## Part 1: the ADR

- [[0021-receipt-evidence]], option A: receipt keys in the election definition (`receipt_keys`, in the next unfrozen definition version, coordinated with P1-15 part 4b's version 2), attributed to the operator only under a key the verifier release also pins; a dispute end `t_E` (the seal's anchoring window plus 30 days), after which a receipt is evidence only with a salted commitment in L1 calldata from before `t_E`, made by the client when the receipt is in doubt; the client's later check from every segment's entry-hash list and a whole verifier report, never a query naming its entry; verdicts `not-evidence`, `board-failed`, `pending`, `forked`, `included`, `unresolved`, and a drop as a `forked` receipt for an admissible ballot that no admitted entry carries by `(tier, ν, M)`. Rejected: keys from the release history with no time bound (B), and every receipt committed at cast on L2 (C).
- Threat files T-26 and T-25 record the change.
- Tested: documentation only; `vp check` formats it.

Known-weak:

- Before `t_E`, whoever holds a receipt key (a leak, a seizure, a compelled hand-over) can forge evidence against the operator (T-69, T-53).
- A client that never checks before `t_E` and didn't commit at cast loses its evidence.
- A withheld board leaves committed evidence `unresolved` and the election unverifiable until P4-3 makes non-publication attributable.
- The whole-board hash list is 32 bytes per entry, heavy on weak connections; admission and selection come from a verifier report the client trusts.
