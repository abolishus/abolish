# T-18: Voting-identity secret theft

- Area: Ballot secrecy, unlinkability and privacy (G-3, G-4, G-5, G-12)
- Adversary: A-3, A-4
- Status: **Partial by design.** A thief can vote as the victim in every open poll and see which polls the victim voted in (by recomputing nullifiers). Revocation and re-registration need a design (open question for P2-6).

## Threat

Malware, a stolen unlocked device, or a weakness in the PRF-encrypted recovery path leaks the Semaphore secret.

## Mitigation

The secret never leaves the device in plaintext; the backup is encrypted with a WebAuthn PRF-derived key (P2-6). Re-voting lets the owner override a thief's ballot until the poll closes (P1-13).

**Constraint on any replacement design** ([[0028-rolling-electorates]], Nullifier scope; [[membership]], Scope): a rolling poll gives one secret one nullifier across every step, so it counts one ballot per registered secret over the poll's whole span. A design that lets a Tier 2 document, or a Tier 1 identity keeping its vouches, register a new commitment MUST keep one counted ballot per document (or identity) in every rolling poll: the old secret may have voted in a poll the new one could join, and nothing on the board links the two. The simplest sound rule: admit a replacement only once every rolling poll open while the old commitment was live has closed. Two cases it must cover: removal is per occurrence, and a registrar of another listed group can re-add a removed member's public commitment (T-06), so a revocation must remove every occurrence, and even then doesn't stop a ballot with a back-dated L1 reference in a poll already open ([[membership]], Root clock and steps); and re-registering a whole tier under a new key or provider ([[0010-tier2-identity-provider]], [[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]]) gives one document two secrets, so no poll, fixed or rolling, may list both the old and the new tier's groups.

Index: [[THREAT_MODEL]].

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P2-6 part 1): [[0017-identity-recovery-backup]] fixes the recovery path: the backup opens only with a UV-gated PRF output from a passkey on the ballot client's dedicated RP ID, which syncs to the voter's other devices, so a thief needs that passkey and its user verification as well as the stored envelope; whoever takes over the voter's passkey-provider account and enrols their own device has both. Revocation and re-registration remain open; P2-6 carries them as a follow-up.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-6 part 2): [[identity-backup]] fixes the identity secret's encoding and its uniform sampling (`1 ≤ s < l`, by rejection), and seals it in a fixed-size envelope under a 256-bit key from the backup passkey's PRF output, so a stored envelope can't be opened or brute-forced without that passkey and its user verification.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-12 part 3b, [[delivery]], Client rules): for a seat whose trustee didn't receipt, the client keeps `ct_i` and `m_i` after cast, until the voter decides whether to post it or the delivery deadline passes, never `e_i` or `w_i`, so nothing kept proves the vote; a device thief in that window could post the seat against the voter's wishes, dropping that share to classical privacy (stated in [[delivery]], Residuals).
- 2026-10-13 (P2-17 part 1, [[membership]], Rolling scope): the constraint on any replacement design is recorded under Mitigation, where the revocation follow-up (P2-6) reads it.
- 2026-10-17 (P3-2 part 5a, [[0024-tier0-registration]], Amendment 1): self-paid groups have no removals, and removed leaves hold no precedence in the first-occurrence rule, so a commitment removed from a signed group can be self-added again and counts. No Tier 0 removal revokes a commitment in a tier that lists a self-paid group; revocation needs a proof by the secret's holder, still this threat's open design.
- 2026-10-18 (P2-8 part 3b-2c-2b-1): the ballot client keeps the secret and its tier groups in its own origin's IndexedDB (`apps/ballot/src/identity.ts`), as Semaphore's own clients do: unencrypted at rest, so malware or a forensic copy of an unlocked device's browser profile reads it. Every read hands out a fresh copy that the page zeroes once the worker has its own; nothing else on the page holds it. A record of another version or shape is refused as unreadable rather than guessed at, and the voter is told not to clear the site's data, since the identity may still be there.
- 2026-10-18 (P2-8 part 3b-2c-2b-2c-2b-2): recovery on a new device is offered only on a device that holds no identity and never replaces one. A recovered identity is marked in its stored record, and the ballot client refuses to seal or cast with it until the recovered client can first show the voter every ballot under their nullifiers ([[0017-identity-recovery-backup]], Consequences), so a recovered device can't void an earlier vote by ignoring the records it lacks. The screens tell the voter to answer each poll from one device.
- 2026-10-18 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2a): a cast now keeps each qualified seat's ML-KEM message `m_i` beside its ballot in this origin's IndexedDB, unencrypted, as [[delivery]] Client rule 4 needs for a posting the voter may choose; `e_i`, `w_i` and the polynomials are still erased at cast. Until a seat receipts, whoever reads the device's browser storage can strip that share's ML-KEM layer, leaving it only Diffie–Hellman privacy. Erasing a receipted seat's `m_i` (Client rule 2) comes with the receipts (part 3c-1b-2b).
- 2026-10-18 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2a): a seat's receipt that passes in the ballot worker now also erases that seat's `m_i` from the worker's seal (`receiptSealV3` in `packages/crypto`: the bytes zeroed and the reference dropped), so a recast of the seal hands the page no `m_i` for a receipted seat and only the receipt is kept for it. The erasure happens only on a receipt that counts for a cast of that seal under the election's own key set, so a forged or misdirected answer never destroys a share a posting may still need (T-25). The page's own copy from an earlier cast, and bytes the browser keeps until it compacts, are not reached by this.
- 2026-10-18 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-1): before the ballot client offers the voter a posting of an unreceipted seat, the worker checks the kept `ct_i` and `m_i` as a posting under that seat's registered KEM key (`castPostingSeatV3` in `packages/crypto`), so a voter never gives up a seat's everlasting privacy for an `m_i` that device storage altered or truncated, which the delivery check would ignore; the worker zeroes its copy once judged. Nothing calls it until delivery exists. It doesn't lessen what a device thief can do with an intact `m_i` before the seat receipts.
- 2026-10-18 (P2-8 part 3b-2c-2b-2b-2d, `apps/ballot` `replaceMovedIdentity`): the client's one path that discards an identity: only on the voter's tap after two L2 endpoints of different operators show its Tier 0 request moved to the same other group past the retry bound, read afresh at the tap, and only, in one transaction, while the device still holds that secret, still waiting on its request and in no group of any tier. Two colluding endpoint operators could still fake a move and make a voter discard an identity that counts; a release should require more.
- 2026-10-22 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2a): an unreceipted seat's `ct_i` and `m_i` no longer stay on the device for good. Once the poll's posting cutoff `c_D = t_D − F` has passed by both the L2's finalized head and the device's clock, the ballot client erases them from every stored cast of that poll and tier (cast record version 5), on the next opening of the poll; receipted seats keep their receipt. A voter who never opens the poll again still keeps them, and the browser may keep replaced bytes on disk until it compacts the database.
- 2026-10-22 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-1): a voter who declines to post a cast's unreceipted seats has their `ct_i` and `m_i` erased at once from the cast record (and from any recast of the same seal), and every `m_i` the ballot worker's seals keep for that ballot zeroed and dropped (`declineSealV3` in `packages/crypto`), so a decline leaves nothing a device thief could post against the voter's wishes. Before any offer or posting, the stored share is checked again in the worker, and every copy of `m` the page handed on is zeroed once used. The browser may still keep replaced bytes on disk until it compacts the database.
- 2026-10-22 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-2b-1, [[posting-relay]]): `m_i` outlives a relay request only in the worker's in-flight entry, until the posting's event is final or can never be in time; a refused pair leaves only an outcome code that expires after 20 seconds, and no log line names a record, `m`, a ballot hash, a nullifier or an address.
- 2026-10-23 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-2b-2b-2b-2a, [[posting-relay]], Pruning): the posting worker's in-flight entries, which hold `m_i`, are deleted against the L2's finalized head: every entry of an election once the head passes its `t_D`, whatever its state, and before then an entry whose posting is in a `SharePosted` event at the finalized head once the posting key's count is past its nonce. Residual: `m_i` outlives its final event while a transaction of it is pending at an unconsumed nonce, never past `t_D`; nothing calls the pruning until the worker's deployment (part 2b-2b-2b-2b).
- 2026-10-23 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-2b-2b-2b-2b-2, [[posting-relay]] runbook): the posting worker runs as a deployment of its own that refuses the other workers' Redis and every other job's variables, so its queue lives only in a store with no snapshot, append-only file, eviction, volume or backup, and its pruning now runs once a minute. Residual: Postgres backups taken while a posting is in flight keep its `m_i` for their lifetime; the runbook keeps them at the shortest retention and on the host only, which nothing enforces.
- 2026-10-26 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-2d-2b-1, [[posting-relay]], Own wallet, A refused send): forcing a posting whose send the wallet's node refused asks the build's own L2 endpoint for an estimate of the call, which carries `m_i`, before the deposit makes it public, and only after the wallet's node already had it; a voter who then declines the deposit has given `m_i` to that endpoint too. Known-weak, and the `refused` note says so.
- 2026-10-26 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-2d-2b-2a, [[posting-relay]], The ballot's own deadline): the client sends a ballot only once its seats are receipted or posted, and no path admits a ballot past `closes_at`, so a posting made from the close to `c_D` gave up `m_i` for a ballot that could no longer count. The client now reads its posting deadlines from the close (`c_B = min(c_D, closes_at − POSTING_BALLOT_MARGIN)`, and the force cutoff `c_L = c_B − MAX_SEQUENCER_DRIFT` for the wallet's deposits, with the halt cutoff `c_W = c_B − F` kept for the warning), offers nothing past `c_E = closes_at − 1,800`, and from `c_B` only a late, unforced run that warns a posting not recorded by `c_S` gives up `m_i` for nothing, and erases its unreceipted shares at the close, at least 35 hours sooner than at `c_D`. Specified here; the client builds it in part 2d-2b-2b.
- 2026-10-27 (P2-8 part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-2d-2b-2b-1, [[posting-relay]], The ballot's own deadline): the client erases an unreceipted seat's `ct_i` and `m_i` once the close has passed by the finalized head and the device's clock, at least 35 hours sooner than at `c_D`, and its relay and wallet runs no longer post past the ballot cutoff `c_B` (a late relay run past `c_E`), so `m_i` is no longer given up for a ballot past the close by the client's own runs.
