# P1-14: Trustee key registration

- State: done (part 1 in #46 and its review follow-ups; part 2, the key ceremony, in #80, merged 2026-10-12)
- PR: #46 (part 1, merged 2026-10-11; `claude-review` and `crypto-review` passed), plus a follow-up for `crypto-review`'s non-blocking findings; #80 (part 2)
- Branch: `claude/p1-14`
- Phase: Phase 1: Threat model, spec, crypto core, circuits, verifier, reference election (CLI only)

## Scope

Trustee key registration per [[0002-everlasting-privacy]]: a per-election hybrid KEM key and a signing key per trustee, with proofs of possession, a public anchored transcript and its verification. No DKG for plurality and approval; a ranked-choice mixnet that needs one chooses a bias-resistant DKG in its own ADR.

## Plan

The item is built in parts, each its own PR on this branch's name:

1. **Identity keys, signatures and the panel identifier** (done, #46). Everything a trustee signs, and the trustee registry checks, needs the signature scheme first; none of it depends on the KEM. [[0012-trustee-identity-signatures]] chooses ECDSA over P-256 with SHA-256; [[trustee-identity]] specifies it.
2. **The ceremony transcript** (#80, [[0018-ceremony-possession]] and [[ceremony]]; `abolish/v1/ceremony-transcript`): each trustee's per-election hybrid KEM key with its proof of possession (`abolish/v1/fs/ceremony/possession`, an encapsulated challenge per [[0002-everlasting-privacy]]), the ceremony context and run identifier ([[domain-separation]], Fiat–Shamir), and the transcript's verification ([[verifier]] 3.1). This waits on the KEM, which P1-12 specifies and which is blocked on the owner's `@noble/post-quantum` question ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Decision). Part 2 also specifies the separate per-election signing key that [[trustee-identity]] (Which key signs what) and [[verifier]] 3.1 require, registered in each trustee's ceremony entry; folding it into the identity key would drop the per-election containment and needs a spec and ADR change. It must also say that a seat approves at most one transcript per definition stub, and how the registry treats two quorums for one stub (ceremony re-runs, [[domain-separation]], Fiat–Shamir), with P1-18.

## What was built (part 2)

- [[0018-ceremony-possession]] (`Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`): option A. Four rounds, each message signed by the seat's pinned identity key over the hash of every earlier round: keys (the KEM key, the per-election signing key, a Schnorr proof of knowledge of the DH key and the signing key's signature); an ML-KEM-768 encapsulation from every seat to every other; a pair-bound hash of each decapsulated shared secret; and the challenges' messages `μ`, revealed only once every answer is fixed. Rejected: one challenger (the creator, the party T-37 is written against) and no ML-KEM possession at all (an unopenable key would surface only at intake, costing every ballot's privacy).
- [[ceremony]], new spec page: the ceremony context (`abolish/v1/ceremony-context`: chain ID, trustee registry, election, stub hash, panel, `run_id`), the round chain (`abolish/v1/ceremony-round`), the signed message (`abolish/v1/sig/ceremony-message`), the proof of knowledge (`abolish/v1/fs/ceremony/possession`) and the signing key's proof of possession (`abolish/v1/sig/ceremony-signing-key`), the transcript record (`0x0005`, version 1) and its hash, approval and re-runs (one approved transcript per stub; the registry refuses a second, P1-18), and the transcript check: transcript-level codes `ceremony-order`, `ceremony-shape`, `ceremony-signature` and `ceremony-quorum`, and per-seat reasons `absent`, `possession`, `duplicate-key`, `no-challenges`, `no-answers`, `no-reveals`, `bad-challenge` and `wrong-answer`, each attributed to the seat whose signed messages (or missing message) show it. Output: the registered key set of the qualified seats.
- [[domain-separation]]: the reserved possession and transcript tags specified; four tags added. [[versioning]]: `0x0005` version 1. [[verifier]] 3.2 names the codes. [[trustee-identity]] and [[share-encryption]] point at the new page.
- `packages/crypto/src/ceremony.ts`: `ceremonyKeygen`, `keysMessage`, `challengesMessage`, `answersMessage`, `revealsMessage` (a node refuses to sign over earlier rounds that fail the check, or for a seat that isn't its own), the transcript codec and hash, `ceremonyContext` and `checkCeremonyTranscript`. Internal `keysMessageWith` and `challengesMessageWith` take explicit randomness; the entry point doesn't export them (tested).

## Tested (part 2)

- `docs/spec/vectors/ceremony.json` from an independent Python implementation (`packages/crypto/scripts/ceremony-vectors.py`, standard library only, with share-encryption-vectors.py's ML-KEM-768, checked against Go's accumulated FIPS 203 vectors first, and trustee-vectors.py's P-256 ECDSA, which reproduces RFC 6979 A.2.5 first), with its own transcript check: 26 transcripts. Four honest runs (3-of-4, 2-of-2, 11-of-16 at `MAX_TRUSTEES` with 240 challenges, and 3-of-4 with one seat absent) reproduced byte for byte from their seats' listed secrets; one vector per disqualification reason (a bad proof of knowledge, a signing-key signature by another key, a shared DH key, a shared signing key, the identity key as signing key, a copied entry, a copied `ek` with the seat's own DH key, missing challenges, answers or reveals, a bad challenge, a wrong answer); the relay found in review, by one seat (`relay-2`), by two corrupt seats of a 2-of-4 panel (`relay-sacrificed`), and through a round 2 shown only to the key's holder (`relay-view-split`), each copier disqualified; a quorum failure; transcript-level failures (seats out of order, reveals without challenges, a short challenge list, an equivocated round-1 message under a later round's chain, a byte changed after signing, another run identifier); 10 decode rejections.
- fast-check (passing at `FC_NUM_RUNS=1000`): an honest ceremony of 2 to 4 seats qualifies every seat and registers the keys each sent (at `FC_NUM_RUNS/50` runs, at least 10, because ML-KEM is slow in JavaScript); any changed byte of a transcript fails the check; random bytes decode only if they re-encode to themselves.
- The check fails under another chain ID, registry, election, stub or threshold. A node refuses another seat's identity key, a seat outside the panel, a round 1 with a forged signature or out of order, answering for a KEM key it didn't register, a wrong number of `μ`s, `μ`s that don't re-encapsulate to the challenges the transcript holds for it, and a proof nonce of 0.

## Known-weak (part 2)

- **Stub checks are specified, not built.** [[ceremony]] (Stub checks) has trustee nodes decode the stub and run verifier 1.3, 2.3 and 2.4 on it before taking part and before approving (T-17), as P1-18 part 2 handed to this item. No trustee node exists yet, and the `membership_vk_hash` and root checks need P1-17b; until then nodes refuse every stub.
- **Reveal at most once per run.** `revealsMessage` keeps no state; the node must record its one round-4 message (spec, Soundness).
- **The quorum floor is `k`.** [[0006-trustees]] (rule 7, proposed, owner-only) would require `n − 1` seats; until the owner decides, a definition can bind a ceremony in which `n − k` seats were absent or disqualified, which leaves the tally no failure margin (T-54). A release can pin a stricter rule per panel.
- **A coordinator can withhold a seat's messages** and so exclude it, down to `k` seats that will approve; it can't add or change a key. Visible only to the seat withheld, which refuses to approve.
- **How a ballot leaves out an unqualified seat** is the ballot record's (P1-12 part 3).
- **No contract consumes the transcript yet.** The trustee registry and its one-transcript-per-stub rule are P1-18's.
- **Not constant time; secrets as `bigint`.** `x` and the proof nonce can't be zeroised; `dk` is overwritten after the answers are made.
- **No published vectors for the composition.** The independent implementation and the review carry the weight (T-36).

## Independent review (part 2)

In-session `crypto-review` subagent, on the first complete diff:

- **Blocking, fixed: the relay.** The challenge's ML-KEM message was `μ` itself, bound to nothing. A seat registering a copy of an honest seat's `ek` could, rushing within a round, send the honest seat's challenge to it back to that seat as its own challenge, copy the honest seat's answer, and reveal the honest seat's `μ`: every check passed and the copier qualified with a key it couldn't open (shown with a working proof of concept at `n = 2`). Fix: the message is `m = H(DS("abolish/v1/ceremony-challenge", ceremony_context ‖ u8(j) ‖ u8(i) ‖ μ))`, so a relayed ciphertext fails its reveal and each relay disqualifies the relayer; qualifying a seat that can't open its key then takes one disqualified corrupt seat per honest qualified seat, which fails the quorum when fewer than `k` are corrupt ([[ceremony]], Soundness). The ADR and the spec's withholding note now cover rushing within a round. Two vectors (`relay-2`, `relay-sacrificed`).
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** `revealsMessage` now refuses `μ`s that don't re-encapsulate to the seat's challenges in the transcript (a restarted node would otherwise get itself disqualified).
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** a vector for two passing seats sharing a signing key (`duplicate-signing-key`); a duplicate with a seat failing possession was already `copied-entry`.
- **Nit, fixed:** `keysMessage` redraws a zero proof nonce instead of throwing.

`crypto-review` on #80, first ready run:

- **Blocking, fixed: the view-split relay.** Answers were the raw shared secrets. A corrupt seat could sign two round-2 messages, the coordinator showing the holder of a copied `ek` the one that relays another seat's challenge to it; the holder's round-3 message is then left out of the transcript (`no-answers`), but its raw decapsulation was already published and the copier used it, qualifying while only the honest holder was disqualified. Fix: every answer is `H(DS("abolish/v1/ceremony-answer", ceremony_context ‖ u8(j) ‖ u8(i) ‖ ss))`, so a seat publishes only values bound to itself as target and nothing it publishes answers a challenge to another seat, whatever view it was shown; the Soundness argument is now direct (no counting, no consistent-view assumption), and nodes SHOULD keep what they signed over to expose a seat that signs two messages in a round. Vector `relay-view-split`.
- **From `claude-review` (non-blocking, applied):** T-54's dated entry moved under History.

Re-review of the fixes (same subagent, its proof of concept adapted): **nothing blocking.** The relay now fails `ceremony-quorum` with the relayer `bad-challenge`; the soundness bound held for relays through seats outside `S_2`, one relayer serving several victims, a withheld or disqualified holder, withheld honest reveals and relayers already disqualified. Its three wording nits were applied: the per-holder scope of the relay count, `μ` (not `m`) in the ADR, and why a shared `ek` among corrupt seats has no rule while a shared `dh` or signing key does.

## What was built (part 1)

- [[0012-trustee-identity-signatures]]: ECDSA P-256/SHA-256 with the low-s rule, chosen over Ed25519 (no audited on-chain verifier), secp256k1 (narrower hardware custody) and ML-DSA (owner's audit question, no on-chain verifier). `Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`.
- [[trustee-identity]]:
  - `p256-key`: 33-byte SEC 1 compressed points, strictly decoded (prefix, `x < p`, on the curve);
  - `p256-sig`: `r ‖ s`, `1 ≤ r < n`, `1 ≤ s ≤ (n − 1) / 2`;
  - a signature's message is `DS(tag, m)` under a tag registered with the new primitive Signature ([[domain-separation]]);
  - `panel_id = H(DS("abolish/v1/panel", u8(k) ‖ u8(n) ‖ Q_1 ‖ … ‖ Q_n))`, with `2 ≤ k ≤ n ≤ MAX_TRUSTEES` and distinct keys;
  - the ceremony registration statement `abolish/v1/sig/ceremony-registration` (125 bytes: chain ID, registry, panel, seat, stub hash, transcript hash), which P1-18's trustee registry checks;
  - the on-chain rule: `P256VERIFY` through OpenZeppelin's `P256`, with the range and low-s checks in the contract.
- `packages/crypto`: `p256Key` and `p256Sig` codecs, `identityKey`, `signStatement` and `verifyStatement`, `panelId`, `ceremonyRegistrationMessage`, `signCeremonyRegistration` and `verifyCeremonyRegistration`. Signing uses RFC 6979 nonces. Registration verification takes the pinned panel and computes `panel_id` and the seat's key itself.

## Tested

- Published vectors, vendored with source and sha256: RFC 6979 Appendix A.2.5 (P-256/SHA-256 key pair and both signatures; one has a high `s`, so normalisation is exercised) and the `[P-256,SHA-256]` section of NIST CAVP's ECDSA SigVer file (15 cases; passing cases accepted exactly when low-s, and the low-s twin of a high-s passing case accepted).
- `docs/spec/vectors/trustee.json`, from an independent Python implementation (`packages/crypto/scripts/trustee-vectors.py`, standard library only, which first reproduces RFC 6979 A.2.5 nonce included). Every signature in it was also checked by hand with OpenSSL's `dgst -verify` (not re-run in CI), last after #47: OpenSSL accepts the 7 valid signatures, the 2 high-s twins (ECDSA allows high `s`; our rule doesn't) and the 2 key-encoding vectors (OpenSSL accepts uncompressed and hybrid SEC 1 keys; our rule doesn't), and rejects the rest that it can parse, including the restored `sig-key-uncompressed` (G's point with key-1's signature). It has 24 keys (13 rejected encodings, and the two `x = 0` points as valid keys with no known secret), 24 signatures (17 rejected, including the signing key's own uncompressed and hybrid forms), 14 panels (8 rejected, each with its error code) and 4 registrations. The valid signatures include a digest at or above `n` (found by a search over about 2³² messages) and an `R` with `x(R) ≥ n` under a key constructed for it, the two cases where ECDSA verifiers have disagreed. TypeScript reproduces every RFC 6979 signature byte for byte.
- fast-check properties (passing at `FC_NUM_RUNS=1000`): honest signatures verify, are low-s and re-encode; any flipped bit of signature, message or key fails; every high-s twin is rejected although plain ECDSA accepts it; random compressed encodings are rejected or re-encode to themselves; `panel_id` changes with the threshold, a seat swap or a dropped seat; a registration fails with any field changed.
- Mutation checks by hand: dropping the low-s rule, allowing `k = 1`, allowing repeated keys, dropping `k` from the panel hash, or signing without normalisation each fail tests. Dropping our own prefix, `x < p` or `r`-range checks doesn't: `@noble/curves` rejects the same inputs, so those checks are defence in depth.
- The tag registry test covers the two new tags; `circuit.json` regenerated for them (and for `abolish/v1/membership-message`, missing since P1-13).

## Independent review

An in-session subagent reviewed the diff against `.github/review/crypto-review.md` and found nothing blocking. Its nine non-blocking findings and nits were all addressed:

1. Quorum must count distinct seats (ECDSA allows many signatures per signer): now normative in [[trustee-identity]], Ceremony registration.
2. Which key signs what conflicted with [[0002-everlasting-privacy]], T-37 and [[verifier]] 7.2: the identity key signs the registration and the ceremony entry that registers per-election keys; later statements are signed by the registered per-election key.
3. Edge cases where ECDSA verifiers split (`e ≥ n`, `x(R) ≥ n`) had no vectors, and Wycheproof was cited but not vendored: both vectors added and the reductions made explicit; Wycheproof claims removed (a vendored JSON file would be rewritten by the formatter).
4. Key and digest discipline: one key per use, and the signer builds `m` and the digest itself.
5. OpenZeppelin's `P256.verify` already applies low-s: the contract's own check is now described as defence in depth.
6. Audit wording for `p256` corrected.
7. Panel error codes are now asserted (`PanelError.code`).
8. The vector file no longer claims an OpenSSL check it doesn't record (this file records the check by hand).
9. EIP-155 chain IDs are 256-bit: a registry on a chain whose ID exceeds `u64` refuses registrations.

A second pass over the fixes found nothing blocking; its four follow-ups were applied: [[verifier]] 3.1 now matches the key roles, every ceremony-phase message (possession answers and complaints included) is signed by the identity key, the stolen-key note distinguishes identity from per-election keys, and the order of the panel checks (and so the error code) is specified. After `claude-review` noted that `verifyCeremonyRegistration` threw on an impossible seat index, it now returns false for a seat outside the panel or a field with no encoding (registrations come from untrusted data), reads each field once, and still throws for a bad pinned panel; a third subagent pass found nothing blocking.

## Follow-up to `crypto-review` on #46

`crypto-review` passed #46 with four non-blocking findings, fixed in the follow-up PR:

1. ADR 0012 claimed P-256 was the only option checkable on chain; secp256k1 is too (`ecrecover`, OpenZeppelin `ECDSA`). The ADR now weighs custody (B) against secp256k1's rigid parameters (P-256's come from an unexplained seed) and keeps B, and the spec states the residual.
2. The generator's comment said `x = 0` is off the curve; it is on it. The two `x = 0` points are now accepted key vectors (`@noble/curves` 2.4.0 accepts them), and the aliasing vector uses `p + x` for the smallest on-curve `x ≥ 1`.
3. `sig-key-uncompressed` used another key's signature, so it didn't isolate the encoding rule; it and a new hybrid-form vector now use the signing key's own point.
4. T-37's history gets a corrective dated line stating the key roles as the spec does (the original entry is kept: the history is append-only).

Also from that review: a test now asserts that `ds()` refuses a Signature tag. The vector that tests uncompressed keys got a new id, `sig-key-uncompressed-own`, because its key changed; `sig-key-uncompressed` and `key-x-above-p` were removed there, against [[versioning]]; the second follow-up restored them unchanged (superseded by `sig-key-uncompressed-own`, `sig-key-hybrid` and `key-x-p-plus-on-curve-x`). For P1-18: the registry's deployment check must accept or reject every `trustee.json` key vector exactly as `p256Key` does (including the two `x = 0` points, which `P256VERIFY` and OpenZeppelin's `P256.isValidPublicKey` must be shown to accept), and its tests consume them.

## Follow-up to `crypto-review` on #47

`crypto-review` passed #47 with three non-blocking findings, fixed in a second follow-up:

1. #47 removed two vectors published in #46, against [[versioning]] (a published vector is never edited or removed). Both are restored byte for byte and appended last, the [[vectors/README|vectors README]] explains their misleading names, and a test pins every published vector (`packages/crypto/tests/vectors/trustee-published.json`: the sha256 of each one's JSON, by section and id), so a regeneration that drops or edits one fails CI.
2. Nothing in CI checked that the key-encoding vectors fail only because of the encoding: a test now asserts that each carries key-1's point (with the hybrid prefix matching `y`'s parity) and verifies under that point, and the generator asserts the same. The OpenSSL note above is corrected (OpenSSL accepts those two vectors by design).
3. ADR 0012 now lists requiring both a P-256 and a secp256k1 signature on ceremony-phase messages as an option the owner can choose.

## Known-weak (part 1)

- **Identity signatures are not post-quantum.** A hybrid signature suite needs an audited ML-DSA and an on-chain verifier ([[0012-trustee-identity-signatures]], option D).
- **A stolen identity key speaks for its seat** in every election until a verifier release unpins it (T-37). Custody is the trustees' ([[0006-trustees]], rule 6).
- **No contract checks these signatures yet.** The registry, and its gas cost through `P256VERIFY` and OpenZeppelin's fallback, are P1-18 part 3; no Solidity consumer of `trustee.json` exists yet.
- **The registration's quorum** (how many distinct seats must sign) is P1-18's.
- **No Wycheproof vectors.** Its P-256 sets aren't vendored, because the formatter would rewrite a vendored JSON file; adding them needs a formatter ignore entry, which is owner-reviewed (`vite.config.ts`).
