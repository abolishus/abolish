## Scope

The trustee node [[0006-trustees]] (Consequences) calls for, added after the owner decided the panel's structure on 2026-10-12: the deployable each trustee runs on infrastructure it controls (an app, or a mode of `apps/node`), since open polls close too often for trustees who act by hand. It runs only what the spec already defines, calling `packages/crypto` and `packages/verifier` and never reimplementing them:

- **Ceremony** ([[ceremony]]): every node duty the page sets, among them the stub checks; fresh keys and challenge secrets per run, refusing a `(stub_hash, run_id)` already seen; at most one round-4 message per run, recorded before it is sent; reveals only over round 3 as the coordinator closed it; and approval of at most one transcript per `stub_hash`, only one with at least `max(k, n − 1)` qualified seats (rule 7) that holds every message the seat sent and whose round chain it signed, with the ceremony registration signature ([[trustee-identity]]). The list here is a summary; [[ceremony]] decides.
- **Intake** ([[share-encryption]], Intake and receipts; [[delivery]]): [[verifier]] 2.5 on the election before receipting any share; receipts during voting, complaints within the complaint window, and the contract-path receipt obligation, bounded as [[P1-12c]] specifies.
- **Tally** ([[tally]]): [[verifier]] 2.8 before releasing any share; summed shares only for cells of the registered partition over the anchored counted set, checked against the L1 anchor first; the summed-share message; openings.
- **Destruction** (P1-15 part 3): the KEM secret after the tally, the signing key after its last statement.
- **Partitions** ([[0006-trustees]], Consequences): never a share for a partition the spec doesn't require; every tier is decrypted, and small tiers carry T-16's warning; below-floor regions are folded into their tier's remainder cell, never released on their own ([[0006-trustees]], Small cells).

## Part 2

The transport (`apps/trustee-node` README, Transport): `TrusteeNode` polls a `Coordinator` for what it asks of the seat and posts what `CeremonySeat` answers; `HttpCoordinator` speaks it over HTTPS (HTTP only to loopback), follows no redirect and bounds every body; nothing listens on the node. The bodies are envelopes in [[notation]]'s encoding around the spec's own records (the stub, and a ceremony transcript holding the rounds closed so far), and the node recomputes `stub_hash` rather than taking it. The configuration pins the panel against its `panelId` and reads the identity key from an owner-only file (`keygen` writes one); `run` runs one seat (refusing every stub until part 3's electorate check), and `local-ceremony` runs a whole ceremony between local nodes and `LocalCoordinator` over loopback HTTP. The trustee's runbook is [[trustee-node]] (`docs/runbooks/trustee-node.md`). Tested with whole ceremonies through lost polls, posts and responses (a fast-check property: every seat still qualifies and approves, and no seat is ever refused), a restart between rounds, a seat withheld from round 2, malformed and foreign requests, retried electorate checks, the HTTP client's URL rule, body bound and error bodies, the configuration's and key file's refusals, and every command, `run` stopping at once when aborted mid-poll (T-14, T-37, T-54).

## Part 4a

`IntakeSeat` (`apps/trustee-node` README, Intake): before taking any ballot of an election, the seat checks that it approved the stub and that the transcript is the one it approved, whose check still returns the key set its approval signed (3.2); the stub checks again (2.5's pinned panel, 2.3, 1.3 and 2.4 through the `ElectorateCheck`); and finalized L2 showing the definition registered under that key set, through a `RegistrationCheck` the deployment supplies (part 3) and that refuses until it can decide. Only a pass is kept. Each delivery then goes through crypto's `intakeBallot` (verifier 5.1 to 5.4 with the membership proof, then decryption); on a pass the ciphertext, shares, tier and nullifier are written once under the ballot hash, and each copy's L1 reference beside it, before the share receipt record (`0x000b`) is returned; the cutoff is checked again once the proofs are done. From `c_I` (`intakeCutoff` at the release's windows) the seat refuses intake. `CeremonySeat.intakeSecrets` gives intake the approval's transcript and key-set hashes and the approved run's KEM and signing secrets. Tested with ballots carrying real Groth16 membership proofs from `@abolishus/membership-prover`: every seat's receipt verifies under its registered signing key, the same receipt after a restart over the same store, `share-hash`, `delivery-malformed` and `membership-proof` refusals storing nothing, each election refusal (an unapproved stub, another transcript, the electorate and registration checks until they decide, other pins), the same core cast again under a newer L1 reference (one receipt, both references kept), and the cutoff at its boundary, including a delivery that finishes at it (T-52, T-54, T-37, T-14, T-41).

Known-weak: no endpoint and no `RegistrationCheck` in a deployment, so a deployed node takes no ballot; the obligation isn't decided yet (part 4b), though what it reads is stored; storage per member isn't bounded (every distinct ballot is kept); the node's own clock closes intake; definition version 1 only; shares sit unencrypted in owner-only files until the destruction duty (part 5).

## Part 4b-1

The intake endpoint (`apps/trustee-node` README, Intake endpoint): `serveIntake` serves an `IntakeSeat` at `POST /v1/intake`, the route a client reaches through an anonymiser when the operator's fails ([[delivery]], Client rules 4) and the operator's relay delivers through. The body is a transport envelope (`{stub, transcript, record, ciphertext}`, each `bytes<2^20>`); 200 returns the share receipt record, 422 a refusal's code, 503 a full queue with `Retry-After`. It allows any origin, since a delivery carries no credential and the receipt is public. Its limits: a body bound checked on the declared length before reading and while streaming; a queue bounding the deliveries held at once, checked one at a time (`intakeBallot` is synchronous); header and request timeouts checked each second; and a bound on open connections per queue place. A per-member cap on stored ballots was built and dropped in review: intake doesn't check the L1 reference, so a coercer holding a member's device once could make ballots ahead and spend the allowance as it opens, leaving the member's later re-vote with no receipt (T-45); it waits on part 4b-2's L1 reads. It serves HTTPS with TLS 1.3 and `X25519MLKEM768` only ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Consequences), or plain HTTP on loopback behind a proxy. `run` serves it with an `intake` section, refusing every delivery until part 3. Tested with the envelope's round trip and truncations (fast-check), a real seat's receipt over HTTP equal to its in-process one, every status, the body bound declared and streamed, the queue and one-at-a-time checking, an abandoned upload, TLS refusing classical-only and TLS 1.2 clients, the configuration and TLS key file, and `run` serving and closing the endpoint (T-39, T-52, T-11, T-54).

Known-weak: no deployment takes a ballot until part 3 supplies the electorate and registration checks; definition version 1 only, while the website creates version 5; storage per member isn't bounded (every distinct ballot is kept) and each repeated delivery runs every check, decapsulation included; a slow sender holds a queue place for up to about 31 seconds; no per-address limit (deliveries come through anonymisers and the relay), so non-members can fill the queue and delay members (liveness only); clients without `X25519MLKEM768` can't reach the endpoint directly; nothing pins the endpoint's certificate beyond the Web PKI.

## Part 4b-2a

The decision at the intake cutoff (`apps/trustee-node` README, Obligation): `decideObligation` decides once, from `c_I` by the node's clock, what the seat owes ([[delivery]], What a node owes). The view is the copies it receipted (from its intake records, `M` recomputed from the ballot hash, tier and each copy's L1 reference), less the ballots it shows absent, with the board and contract copies an `ObligationView` supplies admission-checked and with their entries, over finalized L1. `owedReceipts` gives the receipts it sends by `c_D` and `complaintScope` its complaint scope; the decision waits while finalized L1 doesn't pass `closes_at`, throws above `2M` (`ApprovedElection` now carries `members` and `opensAt`) or when a board copy is of a ballot shown absent, and is written once under `obligations/<stub_hash>`, every later call returning it whatever the view says by then. Intake reads its clock again after its records are durable and gives no receipt past `c_I`, so the decision covers every receipt the seat gave. Tested with ballots carrying real membership proofs: nothing decided before `c_I`, waiting on L1, the receipted ballot owed, a newer board ballot superseding it (owed nothing, the newer one in the complaint scope), a ballot shown absent dropped, a copy boarded after `s_A` displacing nothing, a reorganised reference owed once over two casts of one core, the first decision returned over a changed view, both refusals writing nothing, and a delivery whose records become durable at `c_I` getting no receipt (T-52, T-54, T-25, T-38).

Known-weak: nothing sends the decision yet (part 4b-2b), and no deployment supplies the view or calls it; the view's source is trusted to have checked each copy and gathered it before `c_I`, which the decision can't tell, and `2M` catches only non-members' copies; a node down at `c_I` decides over what it holds when it comes back; the clock is the node's own, and one stepped backwards across `c_I` mid-decision could let a receipt leave uncovered; the per-member storage bound from part 4b-1's Known-weak still waits (it needs the node's view of the L1 head at intake, not only at `c_I`); definition version 1 only.

## Part 4b-2b-1

The receipt duty (`apps/trustee-node` README, Receipt duty): `ReceiptDuty` signs the receipts the decision at `c_I` owes as share-receipt batches (`batchShareReceipts`, each pair's `share_hash` from the intake record written before that receipt was given), writes them once under `deliveries/<stub_hash>/receipts` before sending any, and resends each stored batch that the seat's L2 node doesn't show recorded in finalized history, through the board's `submitReceiptBatch` by `c_D = t_D − F`, until every one is; from `t_D` it stops and reports `unrecorded`. `boardSender` makes the availability call and the receipt-batch call (`RECEIPT_CALL_GAS` as both the call's and the deposit's gas limit) from one account and one queue, and `l2RecordedReceiptBatches` reads the board's `ShareReceiptBatchSubmitted` logs for the definition and seat through the event check. Tested with ballots carrying real membership proofs: nothing before `c_I`, the owed receipt signed as one batch that verifies under the seat's registered signing key with intake's share hash and sent by `c_D`, resent byte for byte until recorded, the same bytes after a restart and over a later view, another batch of the seat's not counted, nothing sent when nothing is owed, sending stopped past `t_D`, and a failed send leaving the batch to resend; the call's canonical calldata, gas and shared nonces, and its deposit, on Anvil; the read over honest and dishonest L2 nodes (T-25, T-52, T-53, T-54).

Known-weak: no deployment supplies the decision's view or steps the duty yet (part 4b-2b-2); every owed receipt is sent, including one whose passing receipt is anchored, which the spec lets a seat skip (gas only); the seat's own L2 node is trusted for which batches are recorded (hiding one makes the seat resend, a no-op; showing one the board never recorded stops the resend); the signed transactions are held in memory, so a restart signs again at new nonces; elections that commit roots are still refused at the stub; definition version 1 only.

## Part 4b-2b-2a

Logs are read under the definition's pinned contract-event version. Tested with ballots carrying real membership proofs over an Anvil L1 whose blocks they name and a scripted L2 node: the run's bounds and blocks as Anvil holds them, a cut run refused, no copies and nothing absent without an attested extent (the newest receipted ballot owed), board copies with their segments and an inadmissible payload dropped, a newer receipted ballot never boarded shown absent so the older counted one is owed, a newer ballot recorded by the contract owed and a relayed one in the complaint scope, a forged log keeping its named ballot from being absent, a forged event archived on the board not doing so, a lagging L2 node refused while anything could be shown absent, the board's from-block and the L2 finalized block bounding the read, a definition other than the approved one, both chains, an L2 block that can't be read; `heldExtent` before and after attesting, with another `C_A` shown and with a pinned run gone; the verifier's ballot hash against `M` (T-25, T-52, T-53, T-54).

## Part 4b-2b-2b

The decision and the receipt duty in `run` (`apps/trustee-node` README, Receipt duty): `ReceiptDuties` steps each approved election's `ReceiptDuty` on the availability duty's schedule (now one generic schedule in `src/duties.ts` over two kinds) until `recorded` or `unrecorded`, logging `receipts` and `receipts-error` once per change; a node's failure is retried, a refusal no retry changes (`ViewError`, `L1AnchorError`) is reported and gives the election up, anything else ends the loop. `run` builds each duty over `obligationView` with the seat's own nodes, the deployment's registry and board and the configured panel, and the receipt-batch call of the one `boardSender` the availability duty also sends through, so the two never take one nonce. The view's extent is read through an `AvailabilitySeat` set up as the availability duty's (same store, reader and blocks), and only once the seat stored its attestation (`attested`), so an unattested seat decides over its intake whatever its L1 reader says. An attested extent the pinned blocks no longer hold is now the typed refusal `ViewError` `extent-lost`, given up and reported, where it used to throw a bare error that would have ended the node. Tested: the schedule over scripted statuses (each change logged once, settling at `recorded` and `unrecorded`, retried failures, `ViewError` and `L1AnchorError` giving up, a bare error ending the loop, abandonment once `t_D` passes before setup); with ballots carrying real membership proofs over an Anvil L1 and a scripted L2 node, nothing decided before `c_I`, then the older counted ballot's receipt signed as one batch over the deployment's view (the newer one never boarded), resent until recorded, a node on another chain given up with nothing sent, and an L1 node that can't answer retried until it can; `dutyWiring`'s view and receipt-duty options from the configuration, and no extent, with no node asked, for a seat that didn't attest (T-25, T-52, T-54).

Known-weak: nothing is owed in a deployment until part 3's electorate and registration checks let intake take a ballot; nothing pages the operator on `unrecorded` or a given-up election (the log only); a given-up election is stepped again after a restart, as the availability duty's; elections are stepped side by side but each poll waits for its slowest step, so a slow view delays the other elections' next step; every owed receipt is sent, including one whose passing receipt is anchored (gas only); definition version 1 only; complaints are part 4b-3.

## Part 4b-3a

The complaint duty (`apps/trustee-node` README, Complaint duty): `ComplaintDuty` takes the complaint scope `decideObligation` fixed at `c_I` and, from `t_D + W_C` by the node's clock with the seat's own L2 node's finalized history past it, reads the board contract's postings for its seat through `ComplaintPath.postings`. It keeps those submitted by `t_D` that name the definition, the seat and a scope ballot the seat didn't receipt (its shares passed the seat's own intake, so a passing posting of it opens again), takes each such ballot's committed vote from the deployment's ballot source by the ballot hash recomputed from the record (T-38), runs the public check and, for the first passing posting of a ballot, opens its shares, and owes a complaint exactly when they fail decryption or the share check: `proveComplaint`'s proof, signed as share-complaint batches (`batchShareComplaints`) and written once under `deliveries/<stub_hash>/complaints` before any is sent. It waits (`waiting-ballots`) rather than decide without a ballot the source doesn't give, until `c_K − R`, when it decides without the ones still withheld, sends every stored batch not among `ComplaintPath.recorded` by `c_K = t_K − F`, returns `recorded` once all are, and stops at `t_K` (`unrecorded`). `ApprovedElection` now carries the seat's KEM key pair, its public key the one its run registered. Tested over `share-encryption.json`'s ballots, panel and ciphertexts from an independent implementation: a complaint about each selected dishonest ballot (shares failing the share check at seat 2, decryption at seat 2 and at seat 1) whose proof passes `checkComplaint` and whose batch verifies under the seat's signing key; waiting on the clock and on L2 history at `t_D + W_C`; the stored bytes resent after a restart and over later postings, another batch not counted, a failed send resent; nothing sent past `t_K`; nothing owed for shares that open, a posting failing the public check, one submitted after `t_D`, for another seat or definition, a superseded ballot outside the scope, a ballot the seat doesn't hold, or a record that doesn't decode; a source's other ballots never taken for the scope's; a withheld ballot given up at `c_K − R` while the rest are complained about; a receipted ballot neither waited on nor complained about; and a fast-check property over postings, restarts, send failures and recordings that the seat signs one set, complaining about exactly the failing posting in time (T-29, T-52, T-54, T-38).

A seat that didn't attest the counted extent doesn't sum, though `k` others can. `run` doesn't yet build the source or step the tally duty (part 5b-2b-3).

## Part 5b-2b-3a

The tally duty in `run` (`apps/trustee-node` README, Tally duty; [[trustee-node]] runbook, Tally duty): with `availability.tally` in the configuration (the channel's address and TLS files, each other seat's channel URL by seat index, a CA file and a send timeout), `run` serves the summed-share channel and steps `TallyDuties`, one `TallyDuty` per approved election, built over the definition registered on the seat's own L2 node (`tallyDefinition`, checked as the view's is), the transcript the seat's approval signed for (`CeremonySeat.approvedTranscript`: the seat now writes its own copy at approval, before it signs, so the tally never takes the transcript from the board the operator serves; T-37), `summingRule` over the counted extent's L1 reader and `l2AvailabilityRecords`, `countedSource` over the counted extent the seat attested at point 2 (none for a seat with no point-2 attestation, without asking its nodes), and `httpPeers`. Each duty is stepped until every cell is opened, the election is unavailable, the counted set is refused, or `t_D` (`tallyDeadline`: the later of `t_S + TALLY_WINDOW` and the anchoring deadline of segment `s_T − 1`) is behind the node (no step begins past it, nor is a duty set up, so a node restarted then signs and sends nothing); failures are retried or give the election up as for the other duties, and `tally`/`tally-error` events report each change once. The channel hands each message to every built, unsettled duty in turn; crypto's checks refuse another election's at its context, so nothing parses a message to route it (T-39, T-15, T-54, T-14). Tested: `TallyDuties`' settling, retries, no step or setup past `t_D` (a restart included) and message hand-off to every unsettled duty (`tests/duties.test.ts`), `t_D` on both sides of the anchoring deadline from hand-computed values, the configuration's tally section and its refusals (`tests/config.test.ts`), `run` serving the channel and acknowledging a message, closing it when stopped, and refusing bad peer URLs and a missing CA file (`tests/cli.test.ts`), the counted source's extent only from a point-2 attestation, `tallyDefinition` with the seats' tally duties opening the counted set over it (`tests/counted-source.test.ts`), and the transcript kept across a restart (`tests/availability.test.ts`).

Known-weak: openings are signed and stored but not yet sent to the board, nor the board's openings read back (part 5b-2b-3b), so no deployed node publishes a result yet; no seat sums in practice until the electorate check (part 3); a seat that didn't attest the counted extent never sums; each message costs a context check per election being tallied at once (T-52); an approval recorded before this part has no transcript copy, so its election's tally is given up (`ViewError` `transcript`; no deployed seat holds one); `t_D` is computed in the node, outside the `crypto-review` gate with no vector, and on its own clock rather than finalized L1's, which part 5c (destruction) fixes by moving it into `packages/crypto` with vectors; a refusal settles the election until a restart. An independent in-session review found one blocking issue, fixed: a step could still begin past `t_D` (and a restarted node built and stepped each election once), so a seat could combine and send sums after it; now none begins. Its other findings are addressed (the channel and gateway closed when intake fails to start, an approval without its transcript giving the election up rather than stopping the node, stronger hand-off and counted-extent tests) or recorded above (`t_D`'s home and clock, refusals final).

## Part 5b-2b-3b-1

The board's openings read back (`apps/trustee-node` README, Tally duty; [[trustee-node]] runbook, Tally duty): once the counted set is fixed, each `TallyDuty` step reads the board's tally openings (`TallyDutyOptions.board`) and judges them itself with crypto's `checkTally` against its own aggregates of the set it fixed; a cell with a passing opening, attributed or not, is `published` with the segment of the first in board order, kept once seen, and the seat sends and signs nothing more for it ([[tally]], Destruction, When). `boardOpenings` (`src/openings.ts`) reads the board through the latest counted checkpoint the seat's own finalized L1 node shows, by the CIDs the checkpoints name, from the seat's directory then the gateways, checks it against the anchors (`verifyBoard`), and gives every tally-opening payload with its segment; `unread` when a run can't be read or nothing is anchored, the failing code when the board fails a check no later read settles; it reads the board again only when the counted checkpoints change, and a read that fails is `unread`, never holding up the sums. `l1BoardAnchors` takes `tally`, reading through `s_T` (`tallySegment`, which `tallyDeadline` now shares). `TallyDuties` settles an election once every cell is published (not once the seat opened it), or at `t_D`. Tested: the reader over boards of ballots and openings (every opening through the latest anchor with its segment and nothing else, a later anchor adding what it covers, unread with nothing anchored, a withheld run or a source's wrong blocks, a board failing a check reported, one read per checkpoint and none kept while unread, the budget refused); the duty with real openings from real seats (published by another seat's opening and the seat sending nothing more, an unattributed passing opening counting, openings not opening an option, over another set, with another number of entries, stray or undecodable publishing nothing, the first passing one in board order giving the segment, kept through later unread, failed and empty reads, a failed board publishing nothing, nothing read before the set is fixed, under version 5 each cell on its own); the schedule settling only on every cell published; a failing read leaving the seat sending; an anchor shown out of order, a late re-anchor of an earlier segment read again, a seal past the reader's last segment; the reader through `s_T` on Anvil; the wiring refusing a node on another chain (T-53, T-24, T-49, T-54).

Known-weak: nothing sends the openings to the operator yet (part 5b-2b-3b-2), so a deployed node still publishes no result; each changed checkpoint reads the whole board through it again, ballots included, within `maxBoardBytes`, and a board past it stays `unread` until `t_D`; published cells are kept in memory, so a restart reads the board again; the seat's L1 node is trusted for which block is finalized, as every read here is (T-53); the reader proves every segment from `f` to `s_T`; nothing yet acts on `published` (destruction, part 5c). An independent in-session review found nothing blocking; its findings are addressed above (a failing read no longer stops the step's sums, an unanchored segment in the copy is a board failure rather than passed over, the read keyed on every counted checkpoint, the reader set up at the first read so it never gives the tally duty up).

## Part 5b-2b-3b-2a

The Boarding rule's judge ([[tally]], Opening record, Boarding): crypto's `tallyOpeningBoarding` takes one tally-opening payload under version 1 or 2, the election's definition hash, rules and registered key set (`TallyElection`), each cell's aggregate over the anchored counted set (`aggregateCell`, summed once by a caller judging many) and the tally-opening payloads already on the board in board order, and boards it only if it decodes under the pin, names the definition and a cell of the partition (`tally-stray`), has `m` entries (`tally-missing`), is over the cell's aggregate (`tally-aggregate`), opens every option (`tally-opening`, with the first that it doesn't), is attributed by a registered seat's signature (`tally-unattributed`), and no earlier opening of the cell is attributed to the same seat (`tally-second` over another signed message, `tally-repeat` over the same one, naming the earlier one). An earlier opening blocks a seat only when its signature attributes it there, so an unattributed one naming the seat never keeps the seat's own off the board (T-29, T-54); undecodable earlier entries are passed over. It judges the opening alone, never the cell, so a passing opening of a cell that fails `tally-range` is still boarded. What it boards is fixed by its cell's aggregate, so the board carries no chosen bytes beyond a registered seat's signature nonce, one per seat and cell, and no summed shares dressed as a failing opening (T-15, T-17). The codes and their order are the verifier's 7.4 breaks (`packages/verifier/src/tallies.ts`), so what the operator refuses is what the verifier would report against it. Tested in `packages/crypto/tests/tally-boarding.test.ts`: every opening of every election of `tally-opening.json` (versions 1 and 2) judged after the ones before it, against the verdict read off the independent implementation's own report (decoding, sorting, per-opening code, attribution); a repeat, a second after the seat's failing opening, an unattributed opening naming the seat not blocking it, another seat's opening of the cell and the seat's of another cell not blocking, undecodable entries passed over, the pin enforced, and the argument checks; a fast-check model against `checkTally` and `sortTallyOpenings` over random boards of each election's openings, and that every single-byte change of a boardable opening is refused. Mutation-checked: dropping any of the conditions, or telling a repeat from a second wrongly, fails a test.

Known-weak: nothing calls it yet; the operator's boarding (part 5b-2b-3b-2b) needs the anchored counted set over the operator's own board, its L1 run and its authenticated contract events. Version 3's partial openings (an author panel, with `tally-early`) and running openings (`0x0018`) have their own boarding rules, not judged here. The verifier's 7.4 keeps its own reading of the same breaks over the whole board rather than calling this, and no test yet compares the two on one board.

## Notes

- These rules bind honest nodes only: a trustee can change its own node, and `k` trustees together can decrypt anything (T-14).
- Rule 6 of [[0006-trustees]] applies in full, since whoever controls the node software reaches every share at once (T-61): among its points, no auto-update; each trustee rebuilds the signed, reproducible release (P0-3) and publishes which one it runs; releases roll out one trustee at a time with a delay between them, so no release reaches `k` nodes before trustees and the public have had time to inspect it; a share sits in an HSM only where decryption runs inside it (a TPM-sealed key protects against disk theft, not the node software); and a second implementation should exist before Phase 5.
- Phase 1's reference election (P1-20) and simulations (P1-21) simulate seven trustees, 4-of-7, with two offline; this node replaces the simulation in Phase 2's reference election against staging (P2-12).
