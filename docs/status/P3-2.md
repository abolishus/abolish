## Scope

Semaphore groups per tier on L2, and a test that no on-chain data links a member to any poll.

## Plan

1. **The group registry and Tier 0** (this part): [[contracts]], Group registry. Groups whose tier is fixed at creation; Tier 0 additions and removals approved by a threshold of registrars pinned at deployment (EIP-712, [[0004-l2-choice]]), each signed batch bound to its group, tier and position; forbidden commitments refused ([[membership]], Commitments and removals); every record logged whole through the event accumulator so verifier 2.4 rebuilds tier trees from the chain. `packages/crypto` decodes the records and checks the approvals, for the verifier and the signing service.
2. **Tier 1 additions**, with [[0014-tier1-vouching]] (P3-3 parts 2 and 3), once Semaphore proofs verify (P1-17b).
3. **Tier 2 additions**, with document proofs (P3-4, [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]]).
4. **Tier 0 registration and sponsorship**, under [[0024-tier0-registration]]:
   - 4a (#151): the ADR. Registration is account-free, on the ballot path, each paid for by its own Turnstile solve on a separate challenge page, and batched on a fixed period with a shuffled order;
   - 4b: the registration spec and the challenge page's handoff to the ballot client;
   - 4c: our ERC-4337 registration paymaster ([[0004-l2-choice]]);
   - 4d: the signing service, in parts: 4d-1 (#290) the route and its queue; 4d-2a (#298) the batch job's core in `packages/core`, with the chain behind an interface; 4d-2b the chain adapter, the worker's jobs and submission. Together: route, `tier0-register` Turnstile action, queue and batch job, refusing forbidden and duplicate commitments, and whether it still signs a commitment whose self-paid copy appeared after it accepted the request, [[0024-tier0-registration]]) with its T-10 tests.
5. **A self-paid Tier 0 addition** (option D of [[0024-tier0-registration]]): the brief's direct-submit path at Tier 0, in the group registry and verifier 2.4, with [[membership]]'s first-occurrence rule changed so a self-paid copy never displaces an earlier registration, before any registry is deployed.

No group registry may be deployed before parts 2 and 3 exist: a deployed registry's rules can't change.

## `crypto-review` on #104

- **Blocking, fixed:** the verifier numbered leaves by log order and never rechecked a record's signed `first_index` or a removal's signed `size`, so the replay and reorder protection rested on L2 state, which T-71 treats as rewritable (governance, a false output root): a replayed, reordered or omitted batch would have inflated a group, dropped members or misapplied removals undetected. The verifier now replays each group with its own count and every registry check, and a record that breaks the signed sequence fails every definition naming the group (`group-history`). `applyGroupRecords` in `packages/crypto` does exactly that replay; `sequences` vectors (honest, replayed, reordered, omitted, stale, unsigned, forbidden) are checked by the Python generator, the TypeScript replay and the contract, which refuses exactly the histories the verifier rejects. The residual is a dropped tail, stated in T-71 and Known-weak.
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** the library refused records with forbidden commitments while the spec kept them with leaf 0; the replay rule now fails the group on any such record, so both agree, with a vector.
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** T-71's Mitigation still named the Safe's owners for Tier 0, and a NatSpec comment placed the registrar keys in hardware wallets; both corrected. ADR 0004 itself is unchanged under the touch-only rule; its departures are under Decisions to review.
- `claude-review`'s non-blocking finding (T-69 had no entry for the online registrar quorum): added.
- Untested: the `c + count ≤ 2³²` part of the replay's position check, which needs a history of 2³² leaves; the contract tests the same bound by setting its count directly.
- In-session re-review of the fix: one blocking finding, fixed (verifier 2.4 and [[membership]] still said forbidden commitments and stray removals never fail a definition; both now defer to the group registry's strict replay, and keep the lenient rules for group contracts without a signed sequence). Non-blocking, stated rather than closed: a rewrite can drop any removal, not only the tail, since removals carry no sequence number (`omitted-removal` vector; Verification, T-71, T-06, T-13). Nits fixed: the verifier's report order per record is specified, with a `two-faults` vector; records are selected by their events' topics, which must match the record; the Python replay checks the group too, with an `other-group` vector. The 2³² bound stays untested in the replay.
- Left for P1-19 (verifier part 4, which replays the registry's events): checking each record against its event's topics in the library, as `applyGroupRecords` takes records already selected by topic; the spec states the rule.

## Part 4a: [[0024-tier0-registration]]

Built: the ADR (option B with option D as the direct path, accepted by the agent, owner may veto), the Decisions to review entry in STATUS.md, and history lines in T-01, T-10, T-11, T-13, T-16, T-25 and T-52. Docs only.

Why B: an account costs one passkey ceremony and one Turnstile solve, so registering one commitment per account (option A) charges a Sybil exactly what a solve per registration does, while recording account → commitment for every registrant; account-gated blind tokens (option C, RFC 9497 and 9578) avoid the record at the price of a new protocol and issuer key, and buy nothing over B while accounts are free.

- Until part 5, Tier 0 registration can be refused by the registrars or Cloudflare, with no direct-submit path (T-25).
- Anyone can queue registrations at the price of a solve (T-52); 4b fixes the excess rule. A period's batch may hold a single registration at low volume (T-13).
- If the owner reads the brief's "Tier 0: passkey" or Phase 2's "at Tier 0 with accounts" as requiring an account to vote, option C replaces B before 4d builds the route.

Independent review (in-session subagent): four blocking findings, all fixed. Cloudflare sees the commitment, since it terminates TLS for the registration request (the ADR had said it didn't); the edge keeps rate-limit matches with addresses (the ADR had said it kept none); the brief's direct-submit rule went unmet at Tier 0 without saying so (now option D, part 5); and Phase 2's "with accounts" wasn't weighed (now stated as a reading the owner may veto). Non-blocking, fixed: the `turnstile.ts` rationale and host check (4d), [[contracts]]' stale Known-weak (4b), B's remaining join by address, the batch's anonymity set, no arrival time in the queue and BullMQ's events stream, T-11 and T-52 history lines, a citation, T-18 and T-46 in the threat list, the token handoff's form.

Re-review of the fixes: one blocking finding, fixed: as first written, option D refused a commitment already live in the tier on chain, which would let anyone kill a signed batch by self-adding one of its commitments first; D must never make a signed batch revert, and part 5 decides how duplicates across the two paths are handled. `claude-review` then found the second way, also fixed: a self-paid addition to a signed group would move the position every in-flight batch binds, so self-paid additions go only into self-paid groups, never signed against (and T-52 records both hazards). Its next pass found that D, with the contract leaving duplicates to the verifier, would let anyone move a voter between regional cells through the first-occurrence rule; fixed by requiring part 5 to make signed groups win over self-paid ones and, among self-paid groups, the earliest addition in chain order (T-16 records it). Non-blocking, fixed: D's Sybil cost is below a solve, not equal to one; T-01's line names D; the literal reading of "Tier 0: passkey" (a passkey on the ballot RP ID at registration) is stated with why it's left out; the backup isn't called optional; a typo in T-13 and two overstatements.

Non-blocking, fixed: T-16 added to the list of history lines.

## Part 4b: [[registration]]

Built: [[registration]], the Tier 0 registration spec (docs only; `packages/crypto` gains nothing, since the batch is part 1's addition record).

- **Challenge page and handoff.** Turnstile runs on a static page at a pinned challenge origin whose host is neither the ballot RP ID nor under it nor under abolish.us (a page may use a registrable suffix of its host as RP ID, so Cloudflare's script could otherwise ask for the backup passkey's PRF output), receives no cookie of ours and sets none. The ballot client frames it (`frame-src` gains only that origin, a `ci:` change P2-8 makes) and receives the token by `postMessage`, checked by origin, source and shape; the page posts only to an origin `frame-ancestors` lists. Navigation with a return URL and a pop-up were rejected.
- **Request and answers.** `POST /registrations/tier0` with `commitment`, `region` and `turnstile` in the body. Malformed, forbidden-commitment, unknown-region, busy and unavailable answers come before Turnstile, so no token is spent on them; queued, already-live and newly accepted commitments all answer 200, so the route can't time a registration or say whether a commitment is waiting.
- **Queue and batch.** One Tier 0 map from each queued commitment to its one group, with no arrival time and surviving restarts; groups batched one after another so no commitment is signed into two; one job per 10-minute boundary; a recheck against the latest L2 state (self-paid groups included) before signing; a short queue held up to six boundaries for eight commitments; at most 256 per batch, chosen uniformly at random when more wait, the rest carried; a queue bound of 1,024 per group. Batches are sorted by commitment value instead of the ADR's shuffle, so their order is checkable from outside.
- **Outcome.** The client reads each Tier 0 group's records in full and never asks about its commitment; a commitment counted in another group is an alarm and a new identity.
- [[contracts]]' Known-weak no longer calls Tier 0 registration a passkey account behind Turnstile, and names the paymaster's part (4c). History lines in T-10, T-11, T-13, T-16, T-25, T-41 and T-52.

Tested: documentation only; `vp check` formats it.

Known-weak:

- Cloudflare sees each request whole, and the solve on the challenge page, which it can join by address and time (T-11).
- No path without our service and Cloudflare until part 5 (T-25).
- A Sybil can fill a batch or a queue with its own commitments (T-01, T-13, T-52).
- Queued and dropped commitments answer alike but cost the service different work, so their response times may differ slightly; 4d weighs a padded path.
- `apps/node`'s and the native app's origins aren't in `frame-ancestors` until those entry points exist.
- About 256 solves per group per batch keep a queue full and turn new registrations away while the flood lasts, at our paymaster's cost (T-52, T-25).
- The busy answer lets someone who knows a commitment learn whether it is newly queued, for about a thousand solves.

Independent review (in-session subagent, `crypto-review` checklist): two blocking findings, both fixed. Queues per group let a party that saw a request resend the commitment for another region and have the service sign it into two groups (moving the registrant by the first-occurrence rule): now one Tier 0 map with each commitment queued for one group, batches formed group by group against it and every in-flight batch (T-16). Re-signing a position after a revert broke the contracts' one-batch-per-position rule while the old record stayed submittable: now the same record is resubmitted, and a position is re-signed only when the old record can never land. Non-blocking, fixed: the claim that no answer reveals queue state (the busy probe is now stated); the flood's refusal of new requests and its paymaster cost (Known-weak, T-52, T-25); the 70-minute bound (now the hold's share, with finality and floods on top); the pre-Turnstile chain read (now a Tier 0 index the service keeps current, never a per-request RPC); the frame gets no WebAuthn permission and the challenge origin is never a related origin of the RP ID; Tor support is ours only as far as our page and route go; the queue must survive restarts. Left as is: the departures from [[0024-tier0-registration]] (sorted batches, an explicit forbidden-commitment refusal) are stated in the spec rather than in the ADR.

CI's `claude-review`: one blocking finding, fixed: the queue bound was checked only before Turnstile's round trip, so concurrent requests could overfill it; step 8 now checks it again atomically with the insert, before anything about the commitment, and an abandoned record's returning commitments are bounded by one batch (the per-batch chance is then at least 1/5).

## Part 4c: the registration paymaster

Built: `packages/contracts/src/RegistrationPaymaster.sol` ([[contracts]], Registration paymaster), our own ERC-4337 paymaster for EntryPoint v0.8, as [[0004-l2-choice]] and [[0024-tier0-registration]] require. It sponsors an operation only if its sender is the signing service's account, it deploys no account, `paymasterAndData` carries no data, its call is exactly `execute(registry, 0, addMembers(…))` in the standard ABI encoding, the inner call included, and its `maxCost` is at most the cap; it returns no context and reads no storage, so bundlers accept it unstaked (ERC-7562). The EntryPoint, registry, service account, admin and cap are immutable; the admin (the timelock behind our Safe; deployment refuses one without code or with only an EIP-7702 delegation) can only withdraw the deposit and manage the stake (T-62). It emits nothing and holds nothing a verifier reads. Vendored: three interface files of eth-infinitism's account-abstraction `v0.8.0` (`IPaymaster`, `PackedUserOperation`, `IStakeManager`), byte-identical to the tag and the npm package, with their sums and tree hash in the package README. Bindings regenerated. History lines in T-13, T-52, T-62 and T-69; [[registration]] points at the new section.

Tested (`test/RegistrationPaymaster.t.sol`, against a stand-in EntryPoint and account and a real group registry): a sponsored operation lands a batch signed by the vectors' registrars and costs the deposit; each rule refuses its case, with fuzzing over every other sender, every cost above the cap and every single-byte change to the call outside the record; a changed record is sponsored and refused by the registry, costing at most the cap; deployment refusals; `postOp` always reverts; only the admin moves funds; the paymaster logs nothing. Checked by mutation: without the padding check, the `initCode` check or the inner call's length rule, tests fail. The length rule came from the fuzz test: changing the length word within the same padded size (292 to 302) had let `addMembers` carry ten zero bytes after the record, which the registry refuses at the deposit's cost.

Known-weak:

- A seized service account key can drain the deposit to itself as its own bundler, `maxCost` per operation (T-69, T-52); a per-period limit needs storage writes, which only a staked paymaster may make. The deposit is kept to a few days' batches.
- Anyone who sees a sponsored operation can land its record first, so the sponsored one reverts and is still charged; our own or a private bundler avoids it.
- The paymaster assumes the service account's `execute(address,uint256,bytes)` means what ERC-4337's reference account's does; the deployment record names the account, which must not be upgradeable.
- No invariant suite: the contract keeps no state of its own, so stateless fuzzing of each rule stands in for one.
- Tested against a stand-in EntryPoint, not EntryPoint v0.8 itself, whose code isn't vendored; a run against the real EntryPoint on Anvil comes with 4d's batch job.
- Nothing deploys it yet (testnet deploys run from Actions, with the registry).

## Part 4d-2a: the batch job's core

Built ([[registration]], Queue and The batch):

- `packages/crypto`: `signGroupApproval`, a registrar's approval of a group-registry digest (RFC 6979, low-s, `v` = 27 + recovery id), recovered before it is returned.
- `packages/core/src/tier0-batch.ts`: `Tier0Batches.refreshIndex` (the Tier 0 index rebuilt in a side key and swapped in whole, with its time, for the route's step 6) and `runBoundary` (the six steps per published group). One batch per position: a record is kept before it is first sent, resent unchanged until it lands, waited on while unfinal or while the group's latest count differs from its finalized count, and abandoned only once finalized history holds another record at its position, its commitments returned even past the bound. Every write is a Lua script fenced by the job's lock token. The chain is behind `Tier0Chain`.
- [[registration]]: the decision option D of [[0024-tier0-registration]] left to 4d: a commitment whose self-paid copy appeared while it waited is dropped, never signed anyway (Why drop a later self-paid copy). Step 1 also waits on an unfinalized addition we didn't send. History lines in T-06, T-13 and T-69.

Tested: see the PR (#298): vectors reproduced by the signer; every step, the lock and a lapsed lock, selection, and a property over interleavings of requests, boundaries, finality and failed submissions keeping the queue's counts exact; mutation checks for each rule.

Known-weak:

- Nothing runs it until 4d-2b (the chain adapter over viem, reading at one pinned block, the worker's index and boundary jobs, submission through the paymaster's bundler, a startup check of the registrars against `RegistrarsPinned`, and an Anvil run against EntryPoint v0.8).
- The index is rebuilt whole each refresh, linear in Tier 0's size.
- The registrar keys are online in the job (T-69); the paymaster's budget across groups isn't applied (one group is served).
- CI runs the scripts on Redis, not Dragonfly.

Independent review (in-session subagent, `crypto-review` checklist, with a proof-of-concept against live Redis): one blocking finding, fixed.

- **Blocking, fixed:** step 1 combined `group()` and `additionAt()`, which may come from L2 nodes at different blocks, so a lagging count could lead the job to sign a second record for a position, and a lagging `additionAt` could have our own landed batch abandoned and its commitments signed again. Now a record is abandoned only when another is final at its position and the finalized count has passed it, everything else unsettled waits, and before signing the job refuses a position below the end of any batch of ours it saw final (`positions`, set in the same script as the finish) or one where any addition already sits. Tests use a registry whose two reads lag each other, and each guard is the only one standing in one of them.
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** a Redis error carried the script's arguments (records and commitments not yet sent); every script failure is now a fresh error quoting nothing, tested. Nothing tied a run to a boundary, so a retry could shorten the hold; each period's boundary now runs once (`boundary`), tested, and a lock held by an index refresh is the caller's to retry. `group()` must take the tier from a finalized `GroupCreated` (the interface says so). The spec now says a kept record that doesn't decode stops the job instead of being abandoned. T-06 says the one-record rule rests on Redis keeping the in-flight record; T-13 lists every key.
- **Left for 4d-2b:** pinning both reads to one block in the adapter; checking the configured registrars and threshold against `RegistrarsPinned` at startup, and the `first_index + count ≤ 2³²` bound; renewing the lock around a slow index read (the fencing already keeps a lapsed runner from writing).
- **Nit, kept:** the signer keys stay in the job's memory for its life; `signGroupApproval` zeroes only its own copy, as stated (keys online, T-69).

Next: 4d-2b.
