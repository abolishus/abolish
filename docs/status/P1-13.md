## Scope

Benaloh challenge/spoil, ballot receipts, and re-voting semantics (last ballot counts).

## Plan

Built in parts, each its own PR on this branch's name:

1. **Re-vote rule** (merged in #45): [[0011-revote-rule]], [[revoting]], the membership message `M`, and selection in `packages/crypto`.
2. **Receipts** (`0x0004`, #48): [[0013-ballot-receipts]] and [[receipts]]: the record, the operator's signed statement and the check at cast. The later checks (after the deadline, with the board, and what a receipt is evidence of) are [[P1-13b]], split out at the owner's request (session, 2026-10-12) after #48's reviews kept finding new problems in that evidence model.
3. **Challenge and spoil** (`0x0003`): Benaloh openings, how challenged ballots are built and placed so they can't be tied to the cast ballot ([[verifier]] 6.2, T-42, T-47).
   - 3a (merged in #143): [[0021-challenged-ballots]], where the challenged core is committed and whether it is boarded.
   - 3b (#152): the core hash, the opening record `0x0003` version 1 and its checks, the rewritten [[verifier]] 6.2 and client rules, `packages/crypto`'s `coreHash`, codec, `openChallenge` and `checkChallengeOpening`, and vectors from an independent Python implementation, as the ADR's Consequences list.

## What was built (part 3b: openings)

- [[ballot-validity]] gains the core hash (`abolish/v1/ballot-core`, registered in [[domain-separation]], and so a new `circuit.json` entry); Casting step 1 is the core; a challenged ballot is never a `0x0002` record. [[versioning]] specifies `0x0003` version 1; [[parameters]] adds `OPENING_WINDOW` (7 days, a constant of opening version 1), which starts at `t_S` and ends with the tally window, so it never moves the seal; [[tally]]'s `W_S` gains nothing from openings, which can't change a counted set. [[verifier]] 6.2 is rewritten and 2.3 says the profile pins the opening's version; [[board]] and [[revoting]] close the challenged-ballot timing residual.
- `packages/crypto/src/challenge.ts`: `coreHash`, `ballotCoreHash`, `openingCoreHash`, `encodeOpening` and `decodeOpening`, `openChallenge` (only shares `encryptShares` made, for this election, to its registered keys; seats outside the registered key set get a zero hash and reveal nothing) and `checkChallengeOpening`. `proveValidity` refuses a sharing that was opened and `openChallenge` one that was proved. `encryptSeat` is shared by encryption and the check, so an opening rebuilds exactly the ciphertext a client sent.

## Independent review (part 3b)

A fresh in-session subagent reviewed the diff against `.github/review/crypto-review.md`, regenerated the vectors (equal to the committed file) and confirmed the generator imports nothing from `packages/crypto`. Findings:

- Blocking, fixed: `opening-cast` and the checker's warning compared tracking codes, so a malicious client (or anyone holding an opening) could cast an opened core's commitments under fresh share hashes or another tier, after which the voter's published opening would reveal a counted vote and pass `opening-cast`. The first fix compared whole `vss` lists; a second round found that still bypassable, since only `C_j = E_{j,0}` fixes a vote and the higher coefficients can be re-blinded or the `C_j` moved to another option. Both now look for any ballot commitment the opening opens ([[challenge]], Opened commitments; `opensBallotCommitment`), the checker records the opened `C_j` and `r_j`, and vectors cover fresh share hashes, another tier, re-blinding, a moved option and fresh `r_j`.
- CI review, non-blocking, fixed: an opening also opens any commitment to the other vote under an opened `r_j` (`g^{1−v_j} · h^{r_j}`), so `opening-cast` now also matches `h^{r_j}` and `g · h^{r_j}`; a commitment derived from an opened one by a chosen relation (`C_j · h`, say) is stated as a residual; the `OPENING_WINDOW` comment in `parameters.ts` counts from `t_S`, as the spec does.
- Non-blocking, fixed: openings were allowed from `closes_at + l1_inclusion_bound`, but a contract-path ballot, or one carrying the next segment under board client rule 1, can land later, so `opening-cast` wasn't final. Openings are now boarded only after the counted extent (`s_S`), up to `t_S + OPENING_WINDOW`, which ends with the tally window and so doesn't move the seal; `opening-cast` is then always the operator's.
- Non-blocking, fixed: one place builds the core (`ballotCore`, `trackingCode`, used by `openChallenge`), so the code shown, the core opened and the core cast agree; the docs say a client drops the whole `EncryptedShares` after a cast, since it holds every seat's randomness; three more order vectors (tier before shape, shape before unqualified, unqualified before vss); `checkChallengeOpening` documents that it takes `decodeOpening`'s output.
- Second round, non-blocking, fixed: an opened `C_j` cast in another election is stated as a residual (T-47); `OPENING_WINDOW` ending with the tally window holds only while it is at most `TALLY_WINDOW`, now stated; [[election-definition]]'s seal names the openings' term from `t_S`.
- Non-blocking, left to P1-19: the verifier's board-level opening reports (Known-weak below).

- Follow-up after #152 (its CI review, non-blocking; an in-session review of the first draft found the same gap for `f_j` and `e_i`, `w_i`): an opening reveals every coefficient of every `f_j` and `g_j`, every seat's shares and each seat's `e_i` and `w_i`, not only `r_j`; `opening-cast` and `opensBallotCommitment` now also match `h^s` and `g · h^s` for each of them, with four vectors added after the frozen ones, verifier 6.2 and T-47 say so; the CI review found that the first version also matched each vote `v_j` (0 or 1), so one ballot carrying `g` or `h` as a commitment would have blocked every opening, and the votes are now left out, with a vector, and randomness derived from revealed scalars by a chosen relation is named in the residual.

- Second follow-up after #160 (its CI reviews, non-blocking): 0 and 1 are left out of what an opening opens, rather than each vote, so an out-of-rules vote reused as randomness is matched (a new `vote-random` opening and a vector); `openedCommitments(opening)` builds the set once, so a verifier's `opening-cast` does no group operation per ballot (T-52); the generator's comments and the spec's test-vector list are current.

## What was built (part 3a: challenged ballots)

- [[0021-challenged-ballots]] (`Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`): option B. A challenged ballot is never boarded on its own: the casting device shows the core's tracking code (the full core hash, as a QR code) before the cast-or-challenge choice, a second device the voter chooses records it, checks a challenge's opening against it and later finds the cast ballot by it, and the opening record carries its own core, published only at the voter's request, after close. Rejected: boarding the challenged core at challenge time (a timing link to the cast ballot, which [[revoting]]'s residual asked P1-13 to remove, and unauthenticated traffic during voting), and a nullifier-bound challenged ballot (breaks [[revoting]] Client rule 6).
- Threat files T-42, T-44 and T-47 record the decision; STATUS lists it under Decisions to review.

## Independent review

A fresh in-session subagent reviewed the diff against `.github/review/crypto-review.md`. It regenerated the vectors (equal values; the committed file is the generator's output after `vp check --fix`), confirmed the Python generator is independent, the 105-byte layout unambiguous, the code faithful to the spec, and that no one without the voter's secret can cause a tie. Findings:

- Blocking, fixed: a direct-submitted ballot naming a block that never comes (`2⁶⁴ − 1`) stayed pending for good, so the election could never be verified. Once L1 finalizes a block after close, any reference above the head is now rejected (`l1-ref-after-close`), using Ethereum's strictly increasing timestamps; `checkL1Reference` implements it, with vectors.
- Blocking, fixed: a Python bytecode cache (`scripts/__pycache__/…pyc`) had been committed in the gated path, which `importlib` would run instead of the source when its header matched. Removed, `__pycache__/` ignored, generators run with `python3 -I -B`.
- Non-blocking, fixed: the membership proof is made only after the voter chooses to cast (Client rule 6), so a challenged ballot can't be posted as cast to tie the real one; the block record is persisted before a ballot leaves the device, retries resend identical bytes, and a self-tie alarms (rule 3); the after-finality check isn't a targeted block query (rule 4, T-11); casts per tier are independent (rule 7, T-13); residuals for proposer-coercers and timing; R6 marked conditional on P1-18's close rule; `l1_chain_id`, `closes_at` and `l1_inclusion_bound` attributed to P1-10b; everything deciding later whether a ballot counts must key on content, not the entry (P1-12, P1-15, P1-18, receipts); `selectBallots` documents that messages are recomputed, never decoded.
- Second round, blocking, fixed: with no lower bound, a direct-submitted ballot naming an ancient block needed L1 evidence nobody archives, so verifiers with different L1 sources would disagree or report the election unverifiable. A ballot naming a block before `l1_ref_min` (the first block at or after `opens_at − 3600`) is now rejected by number alone (`l1-ref-before-open`), keeping every lookup in the L1 range around the poll.
- Second round, non-blocking, fixed: the time is checked before the hash, so verifiers with different heads past close report the same code; rule 3 excepts the explicit tie escape and alarms on any ballot under the voter's nullifier that isn't in its records; the voter's receipt check must show admission and selection, since the ranking fields escape Benaloh's audit (T-41, T-42); the published cast time constrains how challenged entries are placed (T-42, T-47); `l1-ref-after-close` is no longer claimed to stop ballots made after close, which P1-18's close rule must keep out; the vector-regeneration claim is stated exactly.
- Third round: nothing blocking. Non-blocking, fixed: a verifier of a closed election uses the first block after close as its head, so lookups really are bounded by the poll's L1 range; `l1RefMin` and its vectors pin how `l1_ref_min` is derived, with `opens_at` and `closes_at` in Unix seconds; verifier 6.1 states R6 as conditional on P1-18; T-35, T-36, T-45 and T-53 record the change; a vector covers a correctly hashed block after close at the head.
- Review of the third round's fix: nothing blocking. Non-blocking, fixed: R6 is stated as conditional on P1-18 throughout; the evidence range names block `l1_ref_min − 1` and the search's probes, and applies to 5.8 only; every reference is pending before the window is known; a vector pins the rejection of a `low` inside the window. `low = 0` and a window starting at time 0 are covered by a property test only, since the vector chain starts at block 100.

## Known-weak

- The L1 reference is checked only given finalized L1 blocks: reading them is the verifier's (P1-19). The ballot record carrying the fields is P1-12's, blocked on owner questions.
- The last blocks before close stay coercible (every last-ballot-counts scheme has this window; the rule widens it by about 36 seconds), re-votes are public to anyone who knows the nullifier, a proposer-coercer can stretch the freeze window by the slots it controls, R6 depends on P1-18's close rule, and a closed election verifies only once L1 has finalized every named block ([[revoting]], Residuals).
