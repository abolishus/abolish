## Scope

What makes a poll feel alive: results pages, live bars, the voter's own state, following and reactions ([[PROJECT_BRIEF]], Priority). From `everyday-poll-page.tsx`, `everyday-poll-closed-page.tsx`, `poll-results-page.tsx` and `receipt-page.tsx`.

- **Results**: sentences first, then bars with plain percentages and counts, the tier in words; live polls ease to each newly published batch and say when, never interpolating ([[language]], Motion; [[P2-15]]); sealed polls say when results appear.
- **The voter's own state**: "You answered" and "Change your answer" live in the ballot client on the device, never on the server.
- **Following**: an account may follow a poll and be told when results land or a live batch is published. Following is not answering and says nothing about it.
- **Reactions**: emoji reactions on the poll itself, one per account per emoji, counts public, never on an answer (owner, 2026-10-21). Comments come later.

Rules for every part, as [[P2-19]]'s: everyday register by default ([[language]]); no hard-coded strings (`messages.ts`); CSP-safe (no inline `style`); server-rendered where the page is the website's; a fixed analytics template per route, never a path or poll ID (T-67); each component a story with a play function passing axe at desktop width and 360px; SSR and serve tests per route; the mockup's story deleted in the PR whose page matches it. Privacy holds everywhere: nothing an account does on the website (following, reacting, sharing, notifications) is ever stored, logged or keyed with a ballot, nullifier, receipt or the fact of having answered, and the T-10 no-join tests cover every new table, queue and log line ([[PROJECT_BRIEF]], Identity).

## Part 4a: follows in `packages/core`

For part 4b (T-10, T-11), as part 5b's: the control is offered on the website's poll page whether or not the account answered, never in the ballot client, never gated on or prompted by answering, so a follow says nothing about participation and isn't timed to a ballot.

- **Who:** an account verified by e-mail, the address its notices will go to ([[0038-verified-accounts]]), as for reactions.
- **Tables** (migration 0050, account class, T-10): `account_follows` (account, `election_id`; one row each, with no time column, T-11; indexed by poll for part 4c's sender) and `account_follow_hours` (account, UTC hour, changes; no poll). The app role may follow and stop, never change a follow in place. No foreign key reaches the definitions; core checks the poll exists. The no-join test allows `account_follows.election_id`, which names the poll followed, never an answer.
- **Rules** (`src/follows.ts`): `setFollow` follows or stops and says whether anything changed; when following, refused for an unverified account (`account`), an unknown election (`unknown-election`) or a hidden poll (`hidden`, judged inside the write's transaction); stopping is always allowed, so an account whose address is unlinked, or whose poll is hidden, can still stop; then past 60 changes in the UTC hour (`too-many-this-hour`, T-52), counted in the statement that checks it and rolled back with the follow. A change that changes nothing isn't counted. A delisted poll can still be followed, since its results still land. `isFollowing` answers the account's own page, hidden polls included. `pruneFollowHours` drops hours before the last one and runs on the website's account prune timer. There is no public count: following never orders or ranks a poll (T-68).
- **Export:** the account's export carries `follows`, its polls by election ID (layout version 3; the website's export client moves with it).
- **Tested** against the database (`tests/follows.test.ts`): the Drizzle tables match the migration; columns (no time but the hour), grants, check constraints and the cascade on account deletion; a fast-check property of any sequence of follows and stops by several accounts over two polls against a model; concurrent follows by one account and by two; every refusal; the hour's limit, its concurrency, the next hour and pruning; and that the module touches no board table but the definitions. Also `accounts.test.ts` (grants), `no-join.test.ts`, `account-export.test.ts` (a property that one account's export holds its own follows and never another's), and in `apps/web` `prune.test.ts` and `export-client.test.ts` (version 3; version 2 refused).
- **Known-weak:** as part 5a's, no column keeps a time but the database still orders and dates each follow (`xmin`, the WAL, backups), so an insider or a dump can line up a follow with a ballot boarded on a quiet poll moments before (T-11). Follows are kept until the account stops or is deleted, and the poll index makes "the verified accounts following poll P" one query for whoever holds the database, which is a list worth compelling though it says nothing of answers; part 4c should drop a poll's follows once its final results notice is sent. A moderator hiding a poll while a follow's transaction commits can still let that one land. How many notices a poll's followers cost when its results land, and what the e-mail and push providers learn from them (T-73), is part 4c's to decide.

## Part 4c-2a: the landing timer and the push notice's words

- **The timer** (`apps/web/src/node/landing.ts`, started by `src/node/main.ts` beside the prune timer, every 10 minutes on every instance): each run pages through `followedPolls` from where the last stopped, at most `LAND_PAGES` (3) pages of 1000, reads each poll's results through the API's `GET /elections/results`, 4 at a time, and calls `landResults` for each whose board openings pass (`state: "read"`, `passed`), so every followed poll is read once a pass and a follow set after a landing is told on the next. `RESULTS_NOT_COUNTED`, `RESULTS_NOT_READY`, `unopened` and openings that don't yet pass are "not yet"; any other answer or failure is a failed read, logged once a run as `ResultsReadFailed`, a failed landing as `ResultsLandFailed` and a run that throws as `ResultsLandingFailed`, never with a poll (T-10). The API is asked by the poll's ID alone, from the server, as the poll page asks (T-10). A notice is due only after the next UTC midnight, so a pass spread over a few runs tells no one later.
- **The push notice** (`messages.ts`, `messages.es-US.ts`): the service worker's fixed words now cover either kind, "Something new on Abolish: a moderation decision, or results for a poll you follow. Open your account page to see which.", and the account page's push help says only that there is something to see (T-76). They ship with the timer, so nothing lands results under the old words.
- **Tested:** `tests/landing.test.ts` against Postgres as the app role: a poll whose results are in has its followers' follows turned into their list and their notices queued after the next midnight, while another poll's follow stays; a follow set after a landing is told on the next pass; a failed read keeps its poll followed and is logged once by kind, while the run lands the rest; a run that throws is logged by kind and retried; a pass over more than one run's pages reads every poll once, in byte order, carrying on in the next run and starting over after; `resultsInFrom` reads only `read` with `passed` as in, the two defined not-yet codes as not yet, and anything else (an undefined error of the same code included) as failed, asking by the poll's ID alone. The rest of `apps/web`'s suite passes with the new words (`offline.test.ts`, `ssr.test.ts`'s worker config, `messages.test.ts`).
- **Known-weak:** until part 4c-2b the account page doesn't list the polls with results, though the e-mail says it does; the export carries them (`followResults`). Every instance runs the timer, so N instances read each followed poll N times a pass, and each lands it once (a second landing tells no one). The API sees which polls the website asks about, so that some account follows each (T-10). A poll whose openings never all pass keeps its follows and is read every pass. "In" is the board's openings passing, not the verifier's verdict, as the results page says.

## Part 4c-1: results notices in `packages/core`

- **When results land** (`landResults(client, electionId, { now })`, `src/follows.ts`): the caller has judged the poll's results in (core holds no results; part 4c-2's timer judges them from the API's read). In one statement, each of the poll's follows is deleted and becomes a row of `account_follow_results` (migration 0051, account class: account and `election_id`, no time, T-10, T-11), and each follower's notice is queued with its `results` flag, due after the UTC midnight that follows `now`, or joins the one waiting with its tries reset, as `queueNotice` renews one for a decision. A follow set at the same moment is either told or still following. The follows are gone after, since a poll's results land once: part 4a's known-weak list of "the accounts following poll P" no longer holds a counted poll's followers, and a second call tells no one. Following again after starts a new follow.
- **The list** (`followResults`, `dropFollowResult`): the polls the account followed whose results are in, for its own page only, kept until it takes one off or is deleted. In the export as `followResults` (layout version 4; the website's export client moves with it).
- **`followedPolls(client, { after, limit })`**: the polls some account follows, by election ID in byte order, at most `MAX_FOLLOWED_POLLS` (1000) a page, for part 4c-2's timer; never who follows or how many (T-68).
- **The notice** (`src/account-notices.ts`, `src/account-emails.ts`): `account_notices` gains `moderation` (default true, so a waiting notice keeps its meaning) and `results`, at least one set (a check). An account still gets one notice a day at most; its words (`about`: `moderation`, `results` or `both`, English and Spanish) say results are in for a poll it follows, alone or beside a moderation decision, and name no poll, link or time, so the provider learns only that the account followed some poll whose results came in that day; which one is on the account page (T-73). The app role may update only the tries, due time and flags.
- **Tested** against the database: `tests/follows.test.ts` (the new table matches its migration, has no time, its grants and check, the cascade on account deletion; each follow listed and noticed with the due time, another poll's follower untouched, a second landing telling no one; results joining a moderation notice already waiting and tried, keeping its later due time, and a decision joining results; a follow set while results land either told or still following; taking a poll off one's own list only, and following again after; `followedPolls` paging every followed poll once in byte order; malformed arguments). `tests/account-notices.test.ts` (the columns and update grants, the flags' check; followers told once in their language after the next midnight, not before; an author moderated the day a followed poll's results land getting one notice of both; results landing while a moderation notice is in the provider's hands sent after its lease rather than lost; each kind's words distinct, naming no poll, in both languages). `account-emails.test.ts` (every kind plain text, no link), `account-export.test.ts` (version 4 with `followResults`), `no-join.test.ts`, `accounts.test.ts` (grants), and in `apps/web` `export-client.test.ts` (version 4; 2 and 3 refused) and `prune.test.ts` (the message's `about`).
- **Known-weak:** A poll whose results never complete keeps its follows. On a day when only one followed poll's results land, the notice's day narrows it to that poll for the provider (T-73). The list of polls with results grows until the account clears it, with no cap but the polls it could follow. A results landing that renews a notice while its moderation-only send is in the provider's hands leaves both flags set, so the next notice repeats the moderation news beside the results (as a decision renewing one mid-send already re-sent it). Two landings at once with followers in common take the notices' locks in account order; a deadlock still possible under another plan aborts one landing whole, to be landed again on the timer's next run (part 4c-2). An account may follow a counted poll again, and is then told again on a later landing.

## Part 5a: reactions in `packages/core`

For part 5b (T-10, T-11): the control is offered on the website's poll page whether or not the account answered, never in the ballot client, never gated on or prompted by answering, so a reaction says nothing about participation and isn't timed to a ballot.

Taken first: parts 1, 2 and 4 need published tallies, which nothing produces yet ([[P2-9]] part 4, [[P2-13]]), and part 3 is the ballot client's.

- **The set:** `REACTIONS` is fixed: `heart`, `laugh`, `wow`, `thinking`, `sad` and `clap`, stored by name; the website draws each. It holds no agree or disagree, thumbs or the like, so no count reads as a second, unverified tally beside the poll's own ([[PROJECT_BRIEF]]: on the poll, never on an answer). A fixed set carries no text to moderate (T-17, T-68).
- **Who:** an account verified by e-mail, the rule token issuance uses today ([[0038-verified-accounts]]); the phone joins both when [[P2-31]] adds it.
- **Tables** (migration 0032, account class, T-10): `account_reactions` (account, `election_id`, reaction; one row each, with no time column, T-11) and `account_reaction_hours` (account, UTC hour, changes; no poll). The app role may set and take back a reaction, never change one in place. No foreign key reaches the definitions; core checks the poll exists. The no-join test allows `account_reactions.election_id`, which names the poll reacted to, never an answer.
- **Rules** (`src/reactions.ts`): `setReaction` sets or takes back one reaction and says whether anything changed; refused, in order, outside the set (`invalid`), and when setting, for an unverified account (`account`), an unknown election (`unknown-election`) or a hidden poll (`hidden`, judged inside the write's transaction); taking one back is always allowed, so an account whose address is unlinked can still withdraw it; then past 60 changes in the UTC hour (`too-many-this-hour`, T-52), counted in the statement that checks it, so concurrent changes can't both take the last one, and rolled back with the reaction. A change that changes nothing isn't counted. `readReactionCounts` gives every reaction's count (zero included) and refuses a hidden poll; a delisted one keeps its reactions. `readOwnReactions` gives the account's own. `pruneReactionHours` drops hours before the last one (a margin, so an instance whose clock runs ahead never wipes the current hour's limits) and runs on the website's account prune timer.
- **Tested** against the database: the Drizzle tables match the migration and the set matches the table's check; columns (no time but the hour), grants, check constraints and the cascade on account deletion; a property test of any sequence of changes by several accounts against a model of the table; concurrent setting by two accounts and by one; per-poll counts; every refusal; the hour's limit, its concurrency, the next hour and pruning; and that the module touches no board table but the definitions.
- **Known-weak:** no column keeps a time, but the database still orders and dates each reaction (`xmin`, the WAL, backups, statement logs), so an insider or a dump can line up "account A reacted to poll P" with a ballot boarded on P moments before, which links A to having answered when few answer P at that moment (T-11; [[P2-11]] keeps statement logs and `track_commit_timestamp` off). A moderator hiding a poll while a reaction's transaction commits can still let that one land; the counts are refused from then on. A change that changes nothing is never counted, so its cost is bounded only by the proxy (T-52). The counts are as Sybil-resistant as e-mail verification, which is to say barely (T-01), and are never used to order or rank polls (T-68). A count is public the moment it changes, so whoever watches a poll's counts sees reactions arrive, though never whose.

## Part 3: "You've already answered" in the ballot client

- **What it does:** on opening a poll, once no tier has a recorded cast left to finish (`resume`, which comes first), a device whose newest cast in one of the poll's tiers carries the board's checked answer and names this poll's definition (`answeredCast`, `apps/ballot/src/casts.ts`) opens on "You've already answered this poll" (`Answered`, `src/sealed.tsx`) instead of the questions: that cast's tracking code with its fingerprint, that a new ballot from this device replaces the earlier one until the poll closes, that changing starts every question from nothing chosen because the device keeps nowhere how the voter answered, and that a pressured voter can change their answers later, somewhere private ([[revoting]]). "Change my answers" starts the questions afresh, as "Answer again" does after sending; keeping the earlier answers needs no tap.
- **Only the device's own record** (T-10, T-11): nothing is asked of any server to decide it, and the website's poll page says nothing about whether its visitor answered. Only the newest cast is read: under a newer one that is unsent, the poll opens on that cast's offer to send it, as before; under one the voter declined or whose shares were erased, on the questions. The board's answer is unsigned, so the screen says the board took the ballot, never that it counted, and that this is the board's word until checked from another device (T-26).
- **Tested:** `tests/casts.test.ts` for `answeredCast`: none for no casts, an unboarded newest (unsent, declined or erased) whatever is boarded before it, a newest of another poll or that isn't a version-3 record; the tracking code equal to the published core-hash vectors' (`docs/spec/vectors`); and a fast-check property that it is said exactly when the newest cast is boarded. `src/open.stories.tsx`: a boarded cast opens on the screen with its tracking code and "Change my answers" leads to the first question with nothing chosen and nothing sent; a boarded cast naming another definition opens on the questions; a later unsent cast, in the same tier or in a later one of the poll's tiers, is offered before anything is said to be answered. `src/sealed.stories.tsx`: the screen light and dark, its focus, the board's word, one button. Every story with axe at desktop width and 360px; both catalogs by `tests/messages.test.ts`.
- **Known-weak:** the device knows only its own casts: a voter who answered from another device, or whose records were lost, sees the questions as if new (T-01's Tier 0 residual, [[first-answer]] question 5). The answered cast is the first of the poll's tiers to have one, while a new cast goes in the first tier of the identity's memberships the poll admits; for a voter in several tiers they can differ, and then the new ballot is a second one in another tier rather than a replacement, as the sent screen's "Answer again" already risks (everyday polls are Tier 0 only until [[P3-3]] and [[P3-4]]). A tier whose casts can't be read is passed over here, as `resume` already does, so another tier's answered cast can be shown though the unreadable one may hold a newer cast; the cast itself still stops on the unreadable record. A voter whose newest cast was declined, with an earlier one on the board, sees the questions rather than being told that the earlier ballot stands. Whoever picks up an unlocked device learns from this screen that it answered the poll, as the stored casts already tell them (T-18). The mockup's "Keep my earlier answers" link back to the poll's page is left out, since the ballot client has no address of the poll's page, so `everyday-change.stories.tsx` stays as its reference.

## Part 1a: results from the counted tally, in the gate

Parts 1, 2 and 4 waited on published tallies. They are published now: the trustee node's tally duty opens each cell ([[P2-13]] part 4c-2b) and the board operator boards the openings its Boarding judge passes over the anchored counted set (`OpeningBoardings` in `packages/core`). What was missing is the read: a poll's results from the openings on its board. Turning openings into counts is 7.2's computation, so it lives in the gate and `core` only calls it (AGENTS.md, The verifier's trust base; T-29). Part 1a builds that computation; 1b is the operator's read in `packages/core` and the API route, and 1c the website's results page and section ([[P2-9]] part 4's pages, built here).

- **`checkCountedTally`** (`packages/crypto`, `tally-opening.ts`): `checkTally`'s opening checks over each cell's aggregate and its number of counted ballots, rather than its ballots. Those two values are all the checks read of a cell. `checkTally` now sums each cell and calls the same code, so the two can't drift. It refuses an aggregate of another shape, a count that isn't a whole number, and malformed rules as the caller's error.
- **`countedTally`** (`packages/verifier`) also gives each cell's count and tier. **`countedResults(tally, payloads)`** gives 7.2's verdict with each cell's and each tier's report, built from the same helpers `countedChecks` uses: a tier has counts only where every one of its cells passes, each question's slice is reported separately, and a cell nothing has opened yet is `tally-missing`. Nothing joins two tiers or two questions (T-16; ADR 0026).
- **Spec:** [[verifier]], Results from the counted tally.

Tested: `packages/crypto/tests/tally-opening.test.ts` (every `tally-opening.json` election gives the vector's verdict through `checkCountedTally`; a fast-check property that it equals `checkTally` over the same ballots summed, under pins 1 and 2; an undercounted cell failing `tally-range`; every refusal). `packages/verifier/tests/counted-results.test.ts` (cell and tier counts summed in the test from the votes cast; a cell not yet opened, with its tier left without counts; questions' slices and methods; an undercounted cell; a fast-check property that every cell's verdict and counts are crypto's `checkTally`'s over the ballots, and a tier has counts exactly when all its cells opened; the length refusals). `tests/counted.test.ts` checks that `countedResults` gives exactly `countedChecks`' 7.2 cells and tiers on its honest election, on an election with an unopened cell, and on a version-3 partition. `tests/counted-set.test.ts` checks the new counts and tiers on the vectors' boards.

Known-weak: the results are 7.2's figures over the counted set the operator fixed. They are not the verifier's verdict, which also needs every other row over the sealed board, so whoever shows them must say they are the board's results and link to checking them (T-34). Author-panel definitions (tally-opening version 3) have no results read yet, because `countedTally` refuses them; everyday polls don't use one until [[P2-16]]'s author-panel parts.

## Part 1b-1: the operator's results read in `packages/core`

- **`OpeningBoardings.readResults(client, electionId)`** (`src/tally-boarding.ts`): a poll's results from the openings boarded so far, as packages/verifier's `countedResults` gives them over the counted tally the board path already judges openings against, so `core` computes nothing of 7.2 itself (AGENTS.md, The verifier's trust base; T-29). It answers `unopened` while no opening is boarded, else `read` with 7.2's verdict, each cell's and tier's report and the number of openings read. It throws `OpeningError` as `submitOpening` does: `unknown-election`, `not-open` where this operator boards no opening of the election (no target, or the counted tally refused), `not-ready` while the tally can't be had yet.
- **Cost (T-52):** before any opening is boarded a read costs the target (read and checked once per election) and one indexed read, and starts no computation. After, it uses the counted tally the board path keeps; one it doesn't keep (after a restart, or evicted) a read computes, but no more often than once per `readEveryMs` (60 s) whichever election is asked for, and never beside another computation, so readers cycling through elections can't keep the worker busy or hold off the seats' openings for long; a seat's opening still starts one as before. The results are kept per election until another opening is boarded (openings are only appended), and reads of the same openings sent at once check them once.
- **`CountedTally`'s `fixed`** now carries each cell's counted ballots and tier, as `countedTally` gives them, which `countedResults` needs.

Tested: `tests/tally-boarding.test.ts`. Every `tally-opening.json` election, versions 1 and 2, submitted through the board path and then read: a cell has counts only where a registered seat's opening of it was boarded, and then the counts the vector's independent implementation opened (or its code, `tally-range`), its signers the vector's attributed seats, and `tally-missing` where no seat's opening passed; a fast-check property over any run of boarded openings of three cells in two tiers, read after each, that a cell's counts and signers, each tier's counts and the verdict are exactly what the boarded openings open, and that a kept read is never one opening behind; `unopened` before any opening without asking for the tally; a tier with counts only once all its cells are opened, and one computation for repeated reads; a tier of two cells summed from the vector's own counts; a read-started computation at most once per `readEveryMs` across elections, with an evicted election waiting for the budget too and a seat's opening still starting one; reads sent at once checking the openings once; every refusal.

Known-weak: as part 1a's, these are the board's results over the counted set the operator fixed, not the verifier's verdict, so whoever shows them must say so and link to checking them (T-34). A worker restart forgets the kept tally, so the first read after it recomputes the counted set; reads of other elections meanwhile answer `not-ready` (one computation at a time). The read sees only openings boarded through `appendEntry`'s `opening` key, as the Boarding judge does; a tally opening appended without it, which no caller does, would be on the board but missing here. An election with no target (one this operator boards no opening of) re-reads its definition and transcript on every read, as `submitOpening` already does. The worker's queue and the API route came in part 1b-2.

## Part 1b-2: the worker's queue and `GET /elections/results`

- **`{results-read}`** (`apps/worker`, board side): a job carries the election alone (T-10, T-11). `ResultsReads` (`src/ballots.ts`) calls `readResults` on the very `OpeningBoardings` that `{opening-submit}` judges openings with, so a read reuses its counted tally and kept results; the queue runs only beside the openings' boarding, four reads at once (`RESULTS_CONCURRENCY`), and refuses a job older than `BALLOT_MAX_AGE_MS`. Core's `unknown-election`, `not-open` and `not-ready` are outcomes; anything else fails the job by kind.
- **`ResultsQueue`** (`src/ballot-queue.ts`): the API's side, reading the outcome through `RESULTS_OUTCOME`, strict and bounded by `MAX_OPTIONS`, `MAX_QUESTIONS`, `MAX_TRUSTEES` and 256 cells. It waits 10 s by default (`RESULTS_TIMEOUT_MS`), below the API's idle timeout since nothing crosses the socket meanwhile, and its events stream keeps 256 events (`RESULTS_EVENTS_MAX_LEN`), as an outcome can carry every cell's counts (T-52).
- **`GET /elections/results`** (`packages/api-contract`, `apps/api`): `{state: "unopened"}`, or `{state: "read", openings, passed, failure?, cells, tiers, questions?}` with counts as decimal strings; a failing cell carries its code, and which option or question it names is left to the verifier's report, so no field is named for an option or a selection (the contract's T-10 field test). Each question gives `size`, `atLeast`, `atMost` and its method. `ELECTION_NOT_FOUND` for an election the server doesn't hold (never queued), `RESULTS_NOT_COUNTED` (409) where the operator boards no opening of it or the server has no queue, `RESULTS_NOT_READY` and `RESULTS_UNAVAILABLE` (503). Moderation never withholds a result (docs/moderation-policy.md). The route's description says these are the board's results, not the verifier's verdict (T-34).

Tested: `apps/worker/tests/results.test.ts` (live Redis and Postgres: `unopened`, then one cell, then every cell and its tier as openings are boarded; no election left in Redis nor in the log; each core refusal as its code, a `rejected` or other error failing the job by kind; no worker, a stale job; the outcome's strictness). `apps/api/tests/results.test.ts` (the same over HTTP through a worker, and the log line; each worker code as its contract error; an unknown election never queued; no queue; a timeout; a fast-check property that every outcome the queue's schema takes is served whole by the contract, and the largest, 256 cells of 64 u64 counts; the queue's bounds equal the contract's). `packages/api-contract/tests/contract.test.ts` (the operation listed; its bounds tied to [[parameters]] and the definition's cell limit).

Known-weak: as part 1b-1's, the board's results over the counted set the operator fixed, not the verifier's verdict (T-34). A read the worker answers after the API's 10 s is answered unavailable, though its results are then kept for the next. The API caches nothing, so every read is a Redis round trip; per-address limits are the proxy's (P2-11).
