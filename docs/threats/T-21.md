# T-21: Tier 2 proof front-running and trust roots

- Area: Ballot secrecy, unlinkability and privacy (G-3, G-4, G-5, G-12)
- Adversary: A-9, A-1, A-6
- Status: **Planned** ([[0010-tier2-identity-provider]]). No third-chain dependency: zkPassport verifies off-chain or on Base. Residuals: the US issuer itself; the release author's choice of pinned anchors (public and reviewed); an operator who omits genuine certificates (which only excludes people); and **nullifier burning by cloned chip data**: anyone holding a passport's chip data (a hotel or border reader, a leaked dump, a malicious proof-app build, the issuing state) can register it first under their own commitment, and since a removal never frees a nullifier, the holder's only recourse is a new document until P3-2 and P3-4 design a dispute path.

## Threat

A Tier 2 proof relayed through the QR handoff is front-run to register an attacker's identity commitment and burn the victim's document nullifier; or the proof app's trust roots (issuer certificate registry, any third-chain dependency) are compromised.

## Mitigation

The Tier 2 proof binds the identity commitment being registered; trust roots are public, versioned and checkable; the ADR confirms no third-chain dependency (P3-1, P3-4).

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P3-1): [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]] chooses zkPassport. The proof binds the identity commitment, tier group, chain ID and registry; our verifier checks proofs locally with the pinned bb.js against vendored verification keys, never zkPassport's hosted verifier or upgradeable contracts, pins the inner-circuit set itself, accepts only documents issued by the USA whose signer chains to US CSCA certificates pinned in the release, and checks each addition against the certificates and archived revocation lists valid at its own L2 block. zkPassport's own roots are never required. Self was rejected because its verification depends on Celo state.
- 2026-10-12 (P3-4 part 1, [[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]]): anyone holding a document's chip data can run the keyed nullifier's query for it as its holder would, learning its nullifier and whether it registered (and burning it if they register first). The panel publishes the number of query evaluations and refused duplicates per period, so a mass probe is visible though it can't be stopped. Each answer, the output proof and the attestation bind the registration record, so a relayed query or output can't be reused for another commitment.
- 2026-10-13 (P3-4 part 2b-3a, [[keyed-nullifier-hashes]]): `H1` and `H2` both take the Tier 2 scope `σ` (chain ID and group registry, from the release's pins) as their first input after the tag, so `N` is bound to one registry on one chain; `σ` doesn't change when the key is reshared.
- 2026-10-17 (P3-4 part 2b-3b-2a, [[nullifier-query]]): a query names the key, the scope (chain ID and registry), the Tier 2 group, the identity commitment and a fresh query identifier; the query proof takes them as one public field `ctx`, and `query_hash`, which every answer signs and the output proof takes as a public field, adds `comm_in` and `B`. So a relayed query, answer or output can't be moved to another commitment, group, key, scope or query. A seat checks the scope and key against its own pins and the group against the registry before the proof.
- 2026-10-17 (P3-4 part 2b-3b-2b-1): each compiled circuit's ABI has exactly the spec's public inputs in its order (`σ`, `ctx`, `comm_in`, then `B`; `σ`, `q`, `B`, `Z`, then `N`), tested, so the proof binds the statement the checks pass. `ctx` and `q` enter no constraint; that a proof fails under another of either is part 2b-3b-2b-2's round-trip test.
- 2026-10-17 (P3-4 part 2b-3b-2b-2a): a proof of either circuit fails under another `ctx` or `q`, and under any other single public input, tested through bb on every vector, so the proof binds the values that enter no constraint. bb's reference string, which proofs' soundness rests on, is vendored in `packages/circuits/crs/` from Ignition's first transcript instead of fetched at first use, pinned by hash and checked to be successive powers of the `x` of `[x]₂`; that no one knows `x` remains Ignition's claim (176 participants, one honest).
