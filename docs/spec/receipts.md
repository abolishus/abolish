# Receipts

What a voter keeps after casting through our API, and the check the client runs when it gets one: that the board the operator showed holds the voter's entry (T-25, T-26). It builds on [[board]]'s chain, tree and inclusion proofs, and on [[trustee-identity]]'s signature suite. The choice of design is [[0013-ballot-receipts]]. Threats: T-25 (censorship), T-26 (unprovable drops), T-69 (seized keys), T-11 and T-45 (checks that name no entry), T-23 (tampering), T-24 (equivocation), T-31 (one encoding), T-30 (cross-protocol signatures), T-52 (bounded inputs). Notation is from [[notation]]; `H` and `DS` are from [[group]] and [[domain-separation]].

This page specifies the record, its signed statement, the checks at cast, and what a receipt is evidence of later, to the voter and to third parties (Later checks, [[0023-receipt-evidence]]).

## Model

- **The operator signs the board it shows.** Right after appending entry `i`, the operator signs the state of its board of `i + 1` entries: the entry's hash, index and segment, the chain head `link_i` and the tree root `root_{i+1}` ([[board]]). The voter's entry is the last one in that state, so the receipt commits to everything the voter was shown before it, in order.
- **Inclusion is checked at cast.** The receipt carries the audit path of entry `i` in the tree of `i + 1` entries, so the client checks inclusion before telling the voter the ballot was received, with no one else's help.
- **What a receipt is not.** It says nothing about whether the ballot inside the entry is admitted or counts; that is decided by the ballot's content ([[revoting]], Residuals). It isn't on the board, the chain or the archive: it stays with the voter.

## Receipt (record type `0x0004`, version 1)

| Field          | Type                  | Meaning                                                                                                      |
| -------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `election_id`  | `bytes[32]`           | The board's election.                                                                                        |
| `index`        | `u64`                 | `i`: the entry's position on the board.                                                                      |
| `segment`      | `u32`                 | The entry's segment.                                                                                         |
| `entry_hash`   | `bytes[32]`           | `entry_hash_i`.                                                                                              |
| `link`         | `bytes[32]`           | `link_i`, the chain head of the board of `i + 1` entries.                                                    |
| `root`         | `bytes[32]`           | `root_{i+1}`, the tree root of the board of `i + 1` entries.                                                 |
| `operator_key` | `bytes[33]`           | The receipt key that signed, as a `p256-key` ([[trustee-identity]], Identity key).                           |
| `signature`    | `bytes[64]`           | A `p256-sig` ([[trustee-identity]], Signatures) on the statement below.                                      |
| `inclusion`    | `list<bytes[32], 64>` | `PATH(i, D[i+1])`: the audit path of entry `i` in the tree of `i + 1` entries ([[board]], Inclusion proofs). |

A decoder rejects an `operator_key` or `signature` that isn't a canonical `p256-key` or `p256-sig` (`non-canonical`), and an `inclusion` of more than 64 hashes (`length-over-max`), before reading any of it (T-52). An audit path in a tree of at most 2⁶⁴ − 1 entries has at most 64 hashes. A receipt is decoded under the version the election's profile pins for record type `0x0004` ([[versioning]]), which also fixes its dispute window (Later checks); it is never part of an election's data.

The **signed statement** is the first seven fields, encoded in order with no record header:

```
m = election_id ‖ u64(index) ‖ u32(segment) ‖ entry_hash ‖ link ‖ root ‖ operator_key
```

173 bytes, signed as `Sign(operator_key, "abolish/v1/sig/receipt", m)` ([[trustee-identity]], Signatures). The tag makes the signature valid for a receipt only (T-30); the statement binds the election and the position, so it can't be moved to another board or entry; the key is inside the statement, so a signature can't be re-attributed to another receipt key. The audit path isn't signed: it is checkable on its own, and a signer could otherwise make two receipts with different bytes for one statement.

**Receipt keys.** The operator signs receipts with a P-256 key that the ballot client's release pins, as a set (more than one, so a key can be rotated without a release on the day). A receipt signed by any other key is no receipt. Standby keys are held with the same custody as the active key, and a retired key's private part is destroyed. Under [[0023-receipt-evidence]] the keys that judge an election are listed in its definition (`receipt_keys`) and each registered beforehand by the anchoring authority in an append-only L1 registry with a retirement record, so evidence under them is the anchoring authority's alone and no later release can change them; that field and the registry are P1-13b part 2b's, with the definition version that carries the field. Until then the keys that judge a receipt are the set the client's release pinned when it accepted the receipt at cast, which the client stores with the receipt, so a later release that drops a key can't turn a voter's alarm into `not-evidence`; the later checks then give the voter its own verdict, but nothing a third party can attribute, since the operator's key and the anchoring authority may be different parties.

**Signing.** The operator appends entries one at a time ([[board]], Model), so after each append it knows `link_i`, `root_{i+1}` and the audit path. It signs only once the entry is durably stored, so a crash after signing can't lose an entry it acknowledged, and answers the submission with the receipt. It signs no other statement under this tag, and never a statement about an index it hasn't appended. It never appends an entry, or signs a receipt, whose segment is below the election's first segment `f` or above its seal `lastSegment` ([[board]], Model and Timing): such an entry is never part of the board, and a receipt for it is `not-evidence` (Verdicts). As for trustee keys ([[trustee-identity]], Security notes), a receipt key is used for receipt statements only, never for another tag or protocol, and the board server builds the 173-byte `m` from the entry it appended and computes `H(DS(tag, m))` itself: a key service that signs digests handed to it would let any caller that can reach it forge receipts. A receipt is issued for every ballot appended through our API at a voter's request; whether and when spoiled-ballot openings, which are held and appended sorted at the period's end ([[board]]), get receipts is P1-13's challenge-and-spoil part.

## Checks at cast

The client runs these as soon as it gets the receipt, before telling the voter that the ballot was received. Its inputs are the receipt's bytes, the election identifier it cast in, the payload it submitted (the complete record, header included), the board-entry version the election's profile pins, the pinned receipt keys, and the segments it accepts under [[board]]'s client rule 1, from the election's first segment `f` to its seal (`lastSegment`): rule 1's one-period tolerance never reaches outside them, since no anchor for a segment outside them counts. In order, the first failure being the result:

1. `decode`: the receipt doesn't decode strictly under the receipt version the election's profile pins (with the [[notation]] code).
2. `receipt-key`: `operator_key` isn't one of the pinned receipt keys.
3. `receipt-signature`: `signature` doesn't verify on `m` under `operator_key` and the tag.
4. `receipt-election`: `election_id` isn't the election cast in.
5. `receipt-segment`: `segment` isn't one the client accepts under client rule 1.
6. `receipt-entry`: `entry_hash` isn't `H(DS("abolish/v1/board-entry", encode(entry)))` for the board entry (record `0x0007`, under the board-entry version the election's profile pins) with this receipt's `election_id`, `index` and `segment`, and the payload the client submitted.
7. `receipt-inclusion`: `inclusion` doesn't verify, by [[board]]'s inclusion-proof procedure, `entry_hash` as entry `index` of the tree of `index + 1` entries with root `root`. An `index` of 2⁶⁴ − 1 always fails here, since the size doesn't fit a `u64`.

A receipt that fails any of these isn't a receipt: the client tells the voter the ballot isn't confirmed, keeps the ballot, and resubmits the identical bytes ([[revoting]], Client rules) or submits it directly (P4-2). These failures aren't alarms: nothing in them is evidence a third party can check, since before step 3 the operator hasn't signed anything, and after it the payload and the accepted segments are known only to the client.

A receipt that passes is kept, with the payload, for the later checks (below), and with any commitment's opening until the voter discards it.

## Later checks

What a receipt shows after its segment's deadline, to its voter and to anyone it is shown to, against the checkpoints anchored on L1 and a copy of the anchored board ([[0023-receipt-evidence]]). Five requirements shape it: evidence bounded in time, so a key obtained later forges nothing; evidence that survives a withheld board; keys fixed before voting starts (Receipt keys, above); presence judged by content, `(tier, ν, M)`; and checks that name no entry to anyone (T-45, T-11).

### Dispute end

Each election has a **dispute end**

```
t_E = epoch + (lastSegment + 1)·period + deadline + DISPUTE_WINDOW
```

the end of the seal's anchoring window ([[board]], Timing) plus `DISPUTE_WINDOW` ([[parameters]]), a constant of the receipt version the definition's profile pins for record type `0x0004`. A receipt is decoded and judged under that version only, so no later receipt version can lengthen or shorten an old election's window, and a receipt of another version is `not-evidence` for the election.

A judge's `now` is the timestamp of the latest finalized L1 block it has read, as for the board checks ([[board]], Board checks). While `now < t_E`, a receipt is evidence on its own. From `t_E` on, it is evidence only together with the opening of a **commitment** to it that was on L1 in a finalized block whose timestamp is at most `t_E` (below). A key obtained after `t_E` forges nothing, since no new commitment can predate `t_E`; a commitment made before `t_E` keeps the receipt evidence for good, however long the board is withheld.

### Commitment

```
C = H(DS("abolish/v1/receipt-commitment", salt ‖ encode(receipt)))
```

where `salt` is 32 bytes fresh from a CSPRNG for each commitment and `encode(receipt)` is the receipt's full encoding, record header included. The salt makes `C` reveal nothing about the receipt, so anyone may carry it.

`C` is on L1 when it is a 32-byte word, at an offset that is a multiple of 32, of the calldata of a transaction included in an L1 block whose calldata length is a multiple of 32: to any address or none, reverted or not. The calldata is the transaction's `data` field: element 5 of a legacy transaction's RLP list, element 6 of a type `0x01` ([EIP-2930](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2930)) one, and element 7 of types `0x02` ([EIP-1559](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1559)), `0x03` ([EIP-4844](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4844)) and `0x04` ([EIP-7702](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702)). So a relayer batches any number of voters' commitments into one transaction, as a bare concatenation sent to an address with no code. No contract is involved, so nothing on L1 can refuse a commitment but the block builders.

### Opening

The **opening** proves that `C` was on L1 in a finalized block by its timestamp, from a recent beacon state the judge trusts, with no old L1 block, receipt or log from anyone, so it survives history expiry ([EIP-4444](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4444)). It is a structure, not a record: it has no header, its layout is fixed by the pinned receipt version (this one is version 1's), and it is kept by the voter beside the receipt, never posted.

| Field            | Type                      | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `salt`           | `bytes[32]`               | The commitment's salt.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `tx_index`       | `u32`                     | The transaction's position in its block.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `tx_proof`       | `list<bytes<132096>, 16>` | The nodes of the block's transaction trie, root first, from `transactionsRoot` to the leaf at key `RLP(tx_index)`, whose value is the transaction's [EIP-2718](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2718) encoding (a legacy transaction's RLP list, or the type byte and its RLP payload). |
| `header`         | `bytes<1024>`             | The execution block header, RLP encoded.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `beacon_slot`    | `u64`                     | The slot of the beacon block whose execution payload is that block.                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `proposer_index` | `u64`                     | That beacon block header's `proposer_index`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `parent_root`    | `bytes[32]`               | Its `parent_root`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `state_root`     | `bytes[32]`               | Its `state_root`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `body_root`      | `bytes[32]`               | Its `body_root`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `payload_proof`  | `list<bytes[32], 9>`      | The SSZ Merkle branch of `body.execution_payload.block_hash` under `body_root`, leaf first: exactly as many hashes as the fork's depth (step 5).                                                                                                                                          |
| `summary_proof`  | `list<bytes[32], 13>`     | Either the 13-hash SSZ Merkle branch of the beacon block's root, at leaf `beacon_slot mod 8192`, under the `block_summary_root` of its 8192-slot period, leaf first, or empty if the opening was made before that period completed; any other length fails step 6.                        |

The bounds: 128 KiB is the largest transaction the execution clients' pools relay, and a relayer's or client's own transaction is far smaller; a node of the transaction trie is at most a branch (17 references) or a leaf holding one transaction; a transaction trie is at most a few nibbles deep for any block's transaction count.

A judge checks an opening with `t_E`, a finalized beacon state it trusts on the chain the definition's `l1_chain_id` names (its own beacon node's, or fields of it proven by SSZ branches from a finalized state root its light client trusts, by the consensus specs' `BeaconState` layout; the opening carries no such branch, since the state changes after the opening is made), and the L1's fork schedule pinned by its release for that chain. In order, the first failure being the result:

1. `opening-decode`: the opening doesn't decode strictly.
2. `opening-transaction`: the transaction trie proof doesn't verify, by the Merkle Patricia trie rules of the [Ethereum yellow paper](https://ethereum.github.io/yellowpaper/paper.pdf) (Appendix D), the leaf at key `RLP(tx_index)` under the header's `transactionsRoot` (element 4 of its RLP list), with nothing left over; or the leaf's value isn't a transaction of a type listed above, or its calldata length isn't a multiple of 32.
3. `opening-commitment`: no 32-byte word of the calldata at an offset that is a multiple of 32 equals `C`, recomputed from `salt` and the receipt.
4. `opening-late`: the header's `timestamp` (element 11) is after `t_E`.
5. `opening-payload`: with `beacon_slot`'s fork from the pinned schedule, `keccak256(header)` isn't the leaf that `payload_proof` proves at the fork's generalized index under `body_root`, by `is_valid_merkle_branch` of the [consensus specs](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs) (SHA-256), with exactly the fork's depth of hashes: 412 (depth 8) for Capella, 812 (depth 9) for Deneb, Electra and Fulu. A slot before Capella fails here. If the trusted state's slot, or `beacon_slot`, is in a fork after Fulu, or the trusted state's `fork.current_version` isn't one the release knows, the result is `opening-fork-unknown`: such a fork may move the field or change the containers (EIP-7732 moves the execution payload out of the block body), so the opening is unverifiable by this judge until this table and a verifier release list the fork, never a failure of the opening.
6. `opening-canonical`: let `R` be `hash_tree_root` of the beacon block header (`beacon_slot`, `proposer_index`, `parent_root`, `state_root`, `body_root`; SSZ, so the two integers are little-endian). If `summary_proof` has 13 hashes, `R` at leaf `beacon_slot mod 8192` must verify under the `block_summary_root` of `historical_summaries[beacon_slot div 8192 − capella_slot div 8192]` in the judge's trusted state, where `capella_slot` is the first slot of Capella in the pinned schedule; if it is empty, `R` must equal `block_roots[beacon_slot mod 8192]` in that state, and the state's slot must be after `beacon_slot` and at most `beacon_slot + 8192`. A `summary_proof` of any other length, or a summary or root the state doesn't hold, fails here.

A block whose root is in a finalized state's `historical_summaries` or `block_roots` at its own slot is a canonical, finalized ancestor of that state, so the timestamp in step 4 is the time of a block of the finalized chain. An empty `summary_proof` is good only for about 27 hours (8,192 slots of 12 seconds); the client completes it once the period has completed, building the branch from the period's 8,192 block roots, which beacon nodes keep for about five months (`MIN_EPOCHS_FOR_BLOCK_REQUESTS`) and era archives keep for good; fetching them names the period but not the transaction.

### Entry-hash lists

The client checks its receipt from every segment's **entry-hash list**: `entry_hash_a ‖ … ‖ entry_hash_b` for the entries `a` to `b` of the segment, in index order, 32 bytes per entry ([[board]], Segments and checkpoints). It is derived from the segment object, published beside it under its own content address ([[content-addressing]]) and found the same way (P4-3), and fetched from any source, or from a community node the voter runs (P4-5). Nothing authenticates a list but the anchored roots: the client concatenates the lists of the election's segments in order and compares the root of every prefix that ends at a counted checkpoint's `size` with that checkpoint's `root`; a list that disagrees is a bad copy from that source, never a verdict. Roots only: a checkpoint's `head` needs the entries, so a wrong head with an honest root is the verifiers' to catch ([[board]], Board checks, step 3).

### Verdicts

A judge (the voter's own client, or anyone the voter shows its evidence to) takes the receipt and the payload it covers; the election definition with its registration, and the election's first segment `f` and seal `lastSegment` ([[contracts]], Board anchor, Verification); every anchor counted for the election at `now`, read from L1 state as a verifier reads them ([[board]], Board checks, step 1); from `t_E` on, an opening; and, for some verdicts, entry-hash lists or a consistency proof. Let `s` be the receipt's `segment`. In order, the first that applies being the verdict. A verdict is attributable to the anchoring authority, and evidence to a third party, only once the keys that judge the election are its own (Receipt keys, above: P1-13b part 2b); until then every verdict below is the voter's own, and "attributable" reads as "the voter's alarm".

1. `not-evidence`, with a reason:
   - the checks at cast fail, with their code: `decode` under the pinned receipt version, `receipt-key` against the keys that judge the election (Receipt keys, above), `receipt-signature`, `receipt-election`, `receipt-entry` or `receipt-inclusion`;
   - `receipt-segment`: `s < f` or `s > lastSegment`. No client accepts such a receipt at cast, and the operator never signs one (Signing), so it can't frame an honest operator;
   - `expired`: `now ≥ t_E` and no opening passes all six checks, with the first failing opening's code (or `opening-fork-unknown`, which leaves the receipt unjudged by this judge rather than `not-evidence`).
2. `board-failed`: the board checks that need no copy already fail the election: [[board]], Board checks, steps 1 and 2, or `unsealed`. The failure is attributed already; the receipt adds nothing to it.
3. `pending`: segment `s`'s window (end of period `s` plus `deadline`) hasn't closed by `now`.
4. `unanchored`, attributable to the anchoring authority from L1 alone: no counted checkpoint is for segment `s`. A segment with entries must have one ([[board]], Timing), and a valid receipt shows the operator boarded an entry in `s`.
5. `forked`, attributable to the anchoring authority from L1 alone: a counted checkpoint for a segment before `s` has a `size` above `index` (the voter's entry, of segment `s`, can't be in it), or a counted checkpoint for `s` or later has a `size` at most `index`.
6. Let `K_s` be the counted checkpoint for `s`, and `r′` the root of the first `index + 1` hashes of a source's entry-hash lists:
   - `forked`: for some counted checkpoint `K` for `s` or a later segment, of size `m` and root `root_m`, that a source's lists match, `r′` differs from the receipt's `root` and the consistency proof from `(index + 1, r′)` to `(m, root_m)` ([[board]], Consistency proofs) verifies. Two different roots of one size can't both extend `root_m` without a collision of the tagged tree hashes, so the operator signed a board that isn't a prefix of the anchored one.
   - `included`: one source's lists match every counted checkpoint for `s` and later segments, `r′` equals `root`, and so the consistency proof from `(index + 1, root)` to each of those checkpoints verifies: entry `index` of every anchored board from `K_s` on is the voter's entry.
7. `unresolved`: no source's lists match every counted checkpoint for `s` and later segments (a copy that matches `K_s` but not a later checkpoint included), so neither proof can be made for all of them. The election is then unverifiable or failed ([[board]], Residuals: withheld tails; Board checks, step 3); the receipt stays evidence, committed, until a copy is found.

`included` and both kinds of `forked` are shown to a third party with proofs of logarithmic size (the consistency proofs and `r′`) and the checkpoints they end at, whose hashes are the anchored words; `unanchored` and the L1-only `forked` need only the anchors. A verdict reached before the seal's window has closed can change as later checkpoints are anchored: to `board-failed` (steps 1 and 2, or `unsealed`, which need no copy), `forked` or `unresolved`. The client's last check after the seal's window (Client rules, 1) is the one that settles it.

### Drops

An `unanchored` or `forked` receipt is also a **drop** if its payload is a ballot record that passes every admission check that depends only on the record, the definition and finalized L1 ([[verifier]] 5.1 to 5.5, and the L1-reference checks of [[revoting]]), and no admitted entry of the anchored board carries its `(tier, ν, M)`, whatever that entry's selection outcome. A copy with the same `(tier, ν, M)` (a re-randomised proof, a direct-submitted copy, the client's resubmission) means the ballot wasn't lost, so the verdict stands and the drop doesn't; an entry carrying it that is rejected doesn't count as present. A ballot the close rule (5.5) rejects could never have counted, so its loss isn't a drop. Judging a drop needs the whole anchored board, which a verifier holds anyway.

### Client rules

1. **Following the board.** The client never asks for anything about its own entry. From when the voter opens the election, never from the cast, it fetches each segment's entry-hash list once that segment's checkpoint is anchored or its window closes, the same lists for every voter, and reads every anchor for the election from L1 itself. After the seal's window, it fetches once more, at a time drawn uniformly from the end of the seal's anchoring window to 14 days before `t_E`, to judge every receipt it holds again. A release MUST let a receipt reach a verdict within a day of its segment's window closing while the voter's client runs, on a schedule independent of the segments of every receipt it holds (a fixed daily fetch of every newly anchored list, say), never a wake timed to its own segment's deadline (T-11, T-45).
2. **Admission and selection.** For an `included` receipt it reads a whole verifier report ([[verifier]], 8) by the same route as the lists, never a query for its own nullifier, and finds the ballot there by its tracking code ([[0021-challenged-ballots]]). It shows the ballot's admission verdict and whether it is the selected ballot of its tier and nullifier ([[revoting]]); a ballot that isn't admitted, or a tier and nullifier whose selected ballot isn't the latest the voter cast, is an alarm, except a tie the voter made on purpose ([[revoting]], Client rules). A report that marks the election failed or unverifiable is an alarm too.
3. **Alarms.** `board-failed`, `unanchored`, `forked` and `unresolved` are alarms, shown with the verdict and, for a drop, as one. `pending` and `included` aren't.
4. **Committing.** The client commits a receipt once it is **in doubt**: `unanchored`, `forked` or `unresolved`, or still without a verdict (it couldn't read L1 or fetch the lists) within 7 days of `t_E`. `pending` alone isn't doubt, so a client checking during the election never commits near its cast time (T-11), and neither is `board-failed`, attributed without the receipt. It may commit every receipt at cast if the voter chooses, then only through a batching relayer, or after a random delay of at least a day from its own address, since a commitment sent at cast from an address linked to the voter shows when it cast. An `unanchored` or `forked` verdict arrives right after one segment's window, so it doesn't commit then: it commits at a time drawn uniformly from the moment the receipt fell in doubt to 7 days before `t_E` (at once if that is already past), so the commitment's time doesn't name the segment; the voter may ask to commit at once, accepting that. It sends `C` through a relayer that batches many voters' words (community nodes, P4-5; any relayer, since the word reveals nothing), or from its own funded address, and sends it through another if the word isn't in a finalized block within an hour. Once it is, the client builds its opening and runs opening checks 1 to 4 (5 and 6 as soon as it can), and commits again through another relayer if any fails: a relayer could carry the word unaligned, or in a transaction no opening can prove. It keeps the opening, and completes an empty `summary_proof` once the period has completed. An honest receipt never needs a commitment.
5. **After `t_E`.** A client holding an uncommitted receipt still shows its own verdict, marked as no longer evidence (G-7).

## Residuals

- **A receipt names the voter's entry,** and so its nullifier: anyone the voter shows a receipt to learns which ballots are the voter's, and sees every re-vote under that nullifier (T-45, [[revoting]], Residuals). A receipt reveals nothing about the vote.
- **Withholding without a receipt.** An operator that never appends a submission gives no receipt; the client sees no receipt and submits directly (P4-2). That leaves no evidence of the refusal itself (T-25).
- **The check at cast shows only the operator's signed claim.** Nothing ties the signed root to a real or anchored board, so the operator, or anyone holding a receipt key or able to make the board server's key service sign, can sign a receipt over a made-up tree for a ballot that is on no board, and the check at cast passes (T-25). The later checks catch it once the segment's window has closed (`unanchored` or `forked`); until then the voter can't detect it.
- **The operator's key is online.** It signs every append, so it sits in the board server's KMS, not in cold storage.
- **Before `t_E`, a leaked or compelled receipt key forges evidence** against the anchoring authority, indistinguishably from its own signature, for every election the key judges, each until its `t_E` (T-69, T-53). The dispute window, and the retirement of a key on L1 (Receipt keys), are what bound it.
- **Evidence a client never checks or commits is lost at `t_E`**, unless it committed at cast.
- **A withheld board leaves committed evidence `unresolved`** and the election unverifiable, never failed, until P4-3 makes non-publication attributable ([[board]], Residuals).
- **The lists cost 32 bytes per entry** (32 MB for a million ballots), heavy for a phone on a weak connection; a community node the voter runs, or a later private-retrieval scheme, is the alternative. Lists are fetched from when the voter opens the election, which a source sees as a fetch, not a cast.
- **Admission and selection come from a report** the client didn't compute; it trusts the verifier it chose, and can compare two.
- **Committing costs L1 gas,** paid by a relayer or the voter; a relayer can refuse, and the voter's own funded address can link it to having a receipt in doubt, though never to which (and, for a commitment at cast, to roughly when it cast, which the delay rule blurs).
- **The summary branch needs the period's block roots.** A client that commits and isn't run again for about five months can complete its opening only from an era archive.
- **The opening rests on the judge's beacon state and the pinned fork schedule.** A judge that trusts a wrong state can be shown a wrong history, as for any L1 fact; a fork that changes the beacon containers needs a release before openings from it can be checked.
