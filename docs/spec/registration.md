# Tier 0 registration

How a voter's commitment joins a Tier 0 group through our signing service, as [[0024-tier0-registration]] (option B) decides: the challenge page and how its Turnstile token reaches the ballot client, the request and its answers, the queue, the period, the batch and its order, and how the client learns the outcome. Notation is from [[notation]]; groups, records and approvals are [[contracts]], Group registry's; the commitment is [[membership]]'s. Threats: T-01, T-06, T-10, T-11, T-13, T-16, T-18, T-25, T-41, T-43, T-46, T-52, T-67, T-69.

Registration is operational, never part of an election: no verifier reads the request, the queue or the service's state. What a verifier checks is the group registry's records ([[verifier]] 2.4), which the registrars' approvals authorise however the service chose them. So this page binds the service, its route and the ballot client, not the verifier. It adds no record type, hash or encoding, and `packages/crypto` gains nothing: the batch is [[contracts]]' addition record and its approvals.

What this page doesn't cover:

- **The direct path** (option D of [[0024-tier0-registration]]): self-paid Tier 0 additions in self-paid groups, and [[membership]]'s first-occurrence rule changed to rank them, are P3-2 part 5's. Until it exists, a registrant refused by our service or by Cloudflare has no way in (T-25). This page's checks against self-paid groups apply once they exist.
- **The paymaster** ([[contracts]], Registration paymaster), which pays for the service's batches and nothing else.
- **The build:** the route, the queue and the batch job (P3-2 part 4d), and the challenge page and the client's side (P2-8).

## Roles

- **The ballot client** holds the voting identity ([[identity-backup]], Identity secret) and computes its commitment. It sends the request, and checks the outcome on chain.
- **The challenge page**, a static page on an origin of its own, runs Cloudflare's Turnstile widget and hands its token to the ballot client. It never sees the commitment.
- **The signing service** (the registration route of `apps/api`, a queue and a scheduled batch job in `apps/worker`) checks the request, queues the commitment, and once a period signs one batch per group with the registrars' keys ([[contracts]], Group registry, Deployment) and submits it.

## Values

| Name                     | Value            | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------ | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `REGISTRATION_PERIOD`    | 600              | Seconds in a registration period. Period `k` is `[600 · k, 600 · (k + 1))` in UNIX time; batches are formed only at period boundaries.                                                       |
| `MIN_REGISTRATION_BATCH` | 8                | The fewest commitments the service signs into one batch, unless the group has been held `MAX_REGISTRATION_HOLD` boundaries (The batch).                                                      |
| `MAX_REGISTRATION_HOLD`  | 6                | Boundaries in a row at which a group's short queue may be held before it is signed anyway, so holding adds at most an hour to a commitment's wait (finality and floods add more, The batch). |
| `REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX` | 1,024            | The most commitments queued for one group; four full batches (`MAX_BATCH` = 256, [[contracts]], Additions).                                                                                  |
| `TURNSTILE_ACTION_TIER0` | `tier0-register` | The Turnstile action a registration's token must be solved for.                                                                                                                              |

They are values of this page, not of any record: changing one changes the service and its clients, never an election. Why these values is below (Why these values).

## Groups

The service adds members only to **published groups**: for each region code it serves, exactly one Tier 0 group of the group registry, created by its registrars' operator and listed in the deployment record (`packages/contracts/deployments/<chain-id>.json`) and the signed release, so the ballot client and P1-19's report know the one group per tier and region the registrars add to ([[0019-regional-cells]], Consequences). A region code is a `u32` from the published list (P5-1), 0 meaning no stated region. Until the group registry records a region per group ([[0019-regional-cells]] requires it before any registry is deployed), the service serves region 0 only, with one published group.

A member's region is the one they asked for, unverified: results say so (P3-6; T-16).

## Challenge page

Turnstile's widget is third-party code, which the ballot client never runs (AGENTS.md; P2-8's bundle tests), so the widget runs on a page of its own and only its token crosses to the client.

**Origin.** The challenge page is served at one HTTPS origin, the **challenge origin**, pinned in the signed release next to the entry points (P4-4 part 2). Its host:

- MUST NOT be the ballot RP ID, a subdomain of it, or under any registrable domain that is the RP ID of a passkey we ask voters or account holders to make (the second domain, abolish.us). A page may use as its RP ID its own host or any registrable domain suffix of it (W3C Web Authentication Level 3, the definition of RP ID), so Cloudflare's script on such a host, compromised or compelled, could request an assertion with the PRF extension under the backup's RP ID and open the voter's recovery backup ([[identity-backup]]; T-41, T-20), or use an account passkey. [[0020-entry-points]] already keeps every other host off the second domain. Nor is the challenge origin ever listed among the RP ID's related origins (`/.well-known/webauthn`, [[0017-identity-recovery-backup]], A.7).
- MUST NOT receive any cookie of ours: it is on a registrable domain where nothing of ours sets cookies, so an account cookie never reaches it (T-10).
- serves the challenge page and its static assets and nothing else, with no analytics (T-67).

A host on a domain in the Public Suffix List's private section (a hosting provider's per-project subdomain) is its own registrable domain and meets all three without a purchase; the deployment record names the host (P2-11, P4-4).

**The page.** It is static, built reproducibly from this repository with the ballot client (P2-8), and:

- loads Turnstile's script from `https://challenges.cloudflare.com` and renders one widget for the deployment's site key with `action` = `tier0-register`, and nothing else from any other origin. Its policy is sent as headers, since `frame-ancestors` can't be set by a `<meta>` tag: `default-src 'none'`, `script-src 'self' https://challenges.cloudflare.com`, `frame-src https://challenges.cloudflare.com`, `style-src 'self'`, `base-uri 'none'`, `form-action 'none'`, and `frame-ancestors` listing exactly the ballot client's entry points from the signed release; `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`; no `Set-Cookie`;
- reads nothing from the voter, makes no request of its own, and reads only its own URL's fragment;
- works over Tor as far as it is ours to: nothing on the page or the route refuses or degrades a Tor exit ([[0024-tier0-registration]], option B, Cons; T-11). Whether Turnstile lets a Tor user solve is Cloudflare's, and it often doesn't; such a voter's only other path is part 5's direct one (T-25).

**The handoff.** The ballot client shows the page in a frame and receives the token by `postMessage`:

1. The client adds a frame `src` = `<challenge origin>/#origin=<the client's serialized origin>`, with `sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin"`, `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` and a title, and no `allow` attribute, so the frame gets no `publickey-credentials-get` or `publickey-credentials-create` permission and can't call WebAuthn even if a host or RP ID were misconfigured; its own policy lists the challenge origin in `frame-src` and nowhere else (a change to the ballot client's pinned policy, made with P2-8 through a `ci:` PR to its check). The fragment never reaches a server.
2. When the widget calls back with a token, the page calls `parent.postMessage({ v: 1, token }, origin)`, only if `origin` is one of the entry points `frame-ancestors` lists; otherwise it posts nothing. The browser enforces `frame-ancestors` and delivers the message only if the parent's origin is `origin`, so a page that isn't a listed entry point can neither frame the page nor receive a token from it.
3. The client accepts a message only if `event.origin` is the pinned challenge origin, `event.source` is its own frame's window, and `event.data` is an object with exactly the keys `v` (the number 1) and `token` (a string of 1 to 2,048 characters from `0x21` to `0x7e`); it ignores anything else. It then sends the request (Request) within Turnstile's 300 seconds and removes the frame.

The frame keeps the client's state in memory: nothing is stored or carried across a navigation, and the client sends the commitment only to the registration route, never to the page. A top-level navigation to the page and back, with the token in a return URL's fragment, was rejected: it needs a list of allowed return URLs (an open redirect otherwise), puts the token in the browser's history, and loses the client's state unless it is stored first. A pop-up with `window.opener` was rejected for pop-up blockers and for the `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy` the client may set.

## Request

`POST /registrations/tier0` on the API, on the ballot path: like every route it carries no account cookie or `Authorization` header and is refused with one (T-10), and the handler sees the body alone, never a header, cookie or address. The body is a strict JSON object of exactly:

| Field        | Value                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `commitment` | The identity commitment as a `field<bn254>`: 32 bytes, big-endian, as 64 lowercase hex digits with no `0x` ([[notation]]; the API's `hexBytes` form). |
| `region`     | The region code as a canonical decimal string: no sign, no leading zero, at most 2³² − 1.                                                             |
| `turnstile`  | The challenge page's token, 1 to 2,048 characters from `0x21` to `0x7e`.                                                                              |

The token travels in the body, like every other input of the route, so no procedure reads a header (T-10).

## Answers

The service checks, in this order, and answers the first failure:

| Step | Check                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Answer                                                                  |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | The transport: no account credential, a body within the route's bound, strict JSON with no repeated key (as every route).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | 400 (as every route)                                                    |
| 2    | The body has exactly the fields above in their forms (the contract's input schema, so 400 as every route's input); then `commitment` is below `r`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | 400 for a form; 422 `REGISTRATION_MALFORMED`, `non-canonical`           |
| 3    | The commitment isn't forbidden: `0`, `1` or `Poseidon2(0, 1)` ([[membership]], Commitments and removals).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | 422 `REGISTRATION_MALFORMED`, `forbidden-commitment`                    |
| 4    | The region has a published group (Groups).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | 422 `REGION_UNKNOWN`                                                    |
| 5    | The group's queue holds fewer than `REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX` commitments.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | 503 `REGISTRATION_BUSY`                                                 |
| 6    | The service's Tier 0 index is current: the commitments of every Tier 0 group, signed and self-paid, kept up to the L2 node's latest block (unfinalized included), never read per request. If it lags by more than a minute, it stops here.                                                                                                                                                        | 503 `REGISTRATION_UNAVAILABLE`                                          |
| 7    | Turnstile: the token passes siteverify once, for `tier0-register` and the challenge origin's host, without `remoteip` (T-11).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | 403 `CHALLENGE_REFUSED`, or 503 `CHALLENGE_UNAVAILABLE`                 |
| 8    | In one atomic step (one Redis script, say): if the group's queue now holds `REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX` commitments, answer busy, whatever the commitment; otherwise queue the commitment for the group, unless the index has it in any Tier 0 group, any group's in-flight batch holds it, or it is already queued for any group (it then stays queued once, for the group it was first queued for). | 503 `REGISTRATION_BUSY` (the token is spent), else 200, an empty object |

**No answer depends on the commitment's own state.** Steps 2 to 4 depend only on the request and the published list, steps 5 and 8's busy answer on the size of the group's queue (step 8 checks it before anything about the commitment, and again atomically with the insert, since step 7's round trip lets concurrent requests pass step 5 together), and steps 6 and 7 on the index's and Cloudflare's availability. A commitment already queued, already live in a group (signed or self-paid), in flight or newly queued gets the same answer, so the route doesn't say whether a commitment is waiting, which would time a registration more finely than its batch does (T-13), and a repeat costs its sender a solve and changes nothing. One indirect probe remains: someone who knows a commitment can fill a group's queue to one below `REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX`, send the commitment, then a fresh one, which is refused as busy only if the first was newly queued; that costs about a thousand solves per probe (Known-weak). The client learns the outcome from the chain alone (Outcome).

The checks that cost nothing come first, so a malformed or unserviceable request never spends the voter's token (steps 2 to 6 before 7), and a flood of them costs a parse, a set size and an index lookup, never a request to an L2 node. [[0024-tier0-registration]] asked that the refusals of a forbidden, live or queued commitment answer alike; this page refuses a forbidden one explicitly instead (step 3), since forbidden values are public constants and saying so reveals nothing, and answers the live and queued ones as accepted.

## Queue

The queue is one **unordered map** for Tier 0, from each queued commitment to the one group it is queued for (a Redis hash, say), so a commitment is queued for at most one group at a time, whichever region later requests ask for: otherwise a party that sees a request (T-11, T-69) could resend the commitment for another region and have our service sign it into two groups, moving the registrant by the first-occurrence rule and overstating a region (T-16). It keeps no arrival time, order, request identifier, address, token or count per commitment, and no job, key or log line derived from a commitment (T-10, T-11, T-13). BullMQ's events stream keeps job IDs and times, so the batch is one scheduled job per boundary, never a job per request ([[0024-tier0-registration]], Consequences). The route's log is the API's: method, route, status and duration (T-10).

The queue MUST survive a restart of the service (Redis persistence, say); a queue lost anyway is recovered only by clients sending their requests again (Outcome). Its store MUST NOT evict keys (Redis's `maxmemory-policy noeviction`; Dragonfly without cache mode): evicting the per-group counts apart from the map would lift the queue's bound, and evicting the map would lose accepted commitments. Its persistence MUST NOT sync each write to disk before answering (snapshots, or an append-only file synced once a second), or a newly queued commitment would answer measurably later than one already known (Known-weak, Timing). The service keeps, besides the map and the Tier 0 index (public chain data), per group only:

- `held`, the number of boundaries in a row at which the group's queue was held short (The batch);
- the group's **in-flight batch**: the signed addition record it has submitted and that isn't yet final on L2, kept durably so that a restart resubmits the same record rather than signing its position again ([[contracts]], Additions: at most one batch per position).

A commitment leaves the map when it is signed into a batch, or dropped (The batch); a batch's record is deleted once it is final. Nothing about a registration remains then that the chain doesn't show.

## The batch

At each period boundary, once per boundary (a retry or a second scheduler in the same period does nothing, so neither `held` nor a batch's time moves off the boundaries), the service takes the published groups **one at a time**, in ascending `group_id`, each against the map, the index and every group's in-flight batch as the steps before left them, so no commitment is signed into two batches:

1. **Wait for the last batch.** If the group has an in-flight batch that isn't final, nothing else happens for the group at this boundary, and `held` doesn't change. The service signs a position only once the previous batch is final ([[contracts]], Additions), so the next position is the group's member count in finalized L2 history. If the group's latest count differs from its finalized count with no in-flight batch of ours (a record of ours whose store was lost, say), if any addition already sits at the finalized count, or if the count is below the end of a batch of ours already seen final (an L2 node behind the one that showed it), the service waits too, rather than sign a second record for a position. A record is judged from reads that may come from nodes at different blocks, so it is abandoned only when another record is final at its position and the finalized count has passed it, and waited on in every other unsettled case.
2. **Recheck.** Bring the index up to the L2 node's latest block, unfinalized included, and drop every commitment queued for the group that is now in any Tier 0 group, signed or self-paid, or in any group's in-flight batch, or is forbidden. So a self-paid copy made while the commitment waited keeps it out of our batch, and a Turnstile solve never buys a signed copy of a commitment someone else registered ([[0024-tier0-registration]], option D). A commitment whose self-paid copy appeared after its request was accepted is dropped too, never signed anyway (P3-2 part 4d-2's decision, which that ADR's option D left open; Why drop a later self-paid copy), and its client sees that (Outcome). If the index can't be brought up to date, nothing else happens for the group at this boundary.
3. **Hold a short queue.** If fewer than `MIN_REGISTRATION_BATCH` commitments remain and `held < MAX_REGISTRATION_HOLD`, add one to `held` and stop. An empty queue isn't held: `held` becomes 0.
4. **Select.** If more than `MAX_BATCH` remain, choose `MAX_BATCH` of them uniformly at random with a CSPRNG (a Fisher–Yates shuffle drawing its indices from `crypto.getRandomValues` by rejection sampling, keeping the first `MAX_BATCH`); the rest stay queued for the next boundary. Otherwise take them all.
5. **Order.** Sort the batch by commitment value, ascending. So the order is a public function of the batch's contents, independent of arrival and of any randomness, and anyone can check it: a batch on chain that isn't sorted shows the service leaked an order (T-13). [[0024-tier0-registration]] asked for a CSPRNG shuffle, which hides arrival as well but can't be checked from outside.
6. **Sign and submit.** Sign the `AddMembers` digest at the group's next position with the registrars' keys, keep the record as the in-flight batch, remove its commitments from the map, set `held` to 0, and submit the record through the paymaster ([[contracts]], Registration paymaster). A record that fails to land (out of gas, refused by the paymaster) is resubmitted **unchanged** until it lands: it stays valid while the group's count is its `first_index`, its calldata is public once sent, and a second batch signed for the same position would race it, breaking [[contracts]]' rule of at most one batch per position. Only a record that can never land, because finalized history holds another record at its position, is abandoned (a kept record that doesn't decode means a corrupted store, and stops the service for its operator rather than have it guess which commitments the record held): its commitments go back into the map, even past `REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX` (they were accepted, and are never dropped silently), and the next batch takes the position. So a group's queue holds at most `REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX + MAX_BATCH` commitments, and that only after an abandoned record, which an honest chain and correct code never produce.

**Excess.** A group adds at most `MAX_BATCH` commitments per boundary, and no more often than its batches become final, so its throughput is one batch per period or per L2 finality, whichever is longer; the rest wait, never dropped silently. With a full queue (`REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX`) every queued commitment is selected at each batch with probability at least `MAX_BATCH / REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX` = 1/4 (1/5 just after an abandoned record's commitments return), whoever else is queued, so a flood delays a commitment already queued by about four batches in expectation. New requests meanwhile get `REGISTRATION_BUSY`, before their token is spent unless the queue filled during their Turnstile check, rather than growing the queue; a flooder who keeps the queue full (about 256 solves per group per batch) turns away new registrations for as long as it pays, and its commitments join the group at our paymaster's expense (T-52, Known-weak). The deployment's paymaster budget may bound batches across groups too (P3-2 part 4c); a group it skips at a boundary is treated as in step 1.

What a batch shows: on L2, each commitment joined its group in one batch at one position, at about one boundary. Within a batch the order says nothing. Across batches, the time shows that the request was accepted during the periods since the group's previous batch: one period with a busy group, up to `MAX_REGISTRATION_HOLD + 1` of them with a quiet one, among at least `MIN_REGISTRATION_BATCH` registrations unless the group was held as long as allowed (T-13).

## Outcome

The client never asks the service about its commitment (T-11; [[identity-backup]] does the same for recovery). It learns the outcome from the chain, reading each Tier 0 group's records in full from an L2 node or an indexer copy it downloads whole ([[membership]], Owned elsewhere), and:

- **Registered** once its commitment is in a final addition of the published group of the region it asked for, and that leaf is its counted occurrence in Tier 0 ([[membership]], Cell tree, the first-occurrence rule; part 5 changes the rule for self-paid groups).
- **Moved** if its commitment's counted occurrence is in any other group: a copy someone else added (registrars breaking the no-repeat rule, T-06; or, with part 5, a self-paid copy made while the request waited, by a party that saw it: Cloudflare, our hosting, or a malicious or compelled deploy, T-11, T-69). The client alarms, as before casting ([[0019-regional-cells]], Consequences), and registers again only with a new identity secret, since the commitment's place can't be taken back (T-16).
- **Not yet** otherwise. The wait has no fixed bound (finality, floods and the paymaster add to the hold), so the client retries on a heuristic: after about `(MAX_REGISTRATION_HOLD + 2) · REGISTRATION_PERIOD` seconds plus L2 finality with no addition of its commitment, it may solve a new challenge and send the same request again, as long as the commitment is in no group. A repeat is harmless (Answers).

The client keeps the identity's secret whatever the outcome, and the backup is made as [[identity-backup]] says, before or after registration: neither depends on the other.

## Why these values

- **`REGISTRATION_PERIOD`.** Ten minutes is the most [[0024-tier0-registration]] allows. Shorter periods time a registration more finely, and on a quiet group give smaller batches; longer ones delay a voter who registers before a poll's root block. Aligned to UNIX time, the boundaries are public and fixed, so a batch's time carries no choice of ours.
- **`MIN_REGISTRATION_BATCH` and `MAX_REGISTRATION_HOLD`.** A batch of one shows its single registrant's period; holding a short queue for up to an hour gathers about eight at a quiet group, so a registration's time is shared with that many others before anything on chain shows it. Eight is small next to a cell's floor (`MIN_CELL_ELECTORATE`, [[parameters]]) because what it hides is a time, not a vote, and a longer hold costs every quiet group's voters an hour more; at a busy group the floor is met each period and nobody waits. Neither value can stop a Sybil from filling a batch with its own commitments, so at a group with one honest registrant in an hour the time still points at them among the Sybil's (T-13, T-01).
- **`REGISTRATION_QUEUE_MAX`.** Four batches keep a queued commitment's chance per batch at least a quarter under any flood, and bound what a flood can make the service hold to 32 KiB of commitments per group (40 KiB after an abandoned record).

- **Why drop a later self-paid copy.** Once part 5 exists, a queued commitment can gain a self-paid copy while it waits, from one of two parties. The registrant may have gone direct after waiting: signing the commitment too would give it a second occurrence, and, signed copies winning, override the region the registrant chose for it. Or a party that saw the request (T-11, T-69) may have copied it to move the registrant (T-16): signing anyway would undo that move, but those parties can already refuse to sign or log the request, and the client detects the move and starts again with a new identity (Outcome). Dropping keeps one rule without exception, which anyone can check on chain: no signed batch carries a commitment live in any Tier 0 group when the batch was signed (as of the latest block the service read just before signing; T-06). Until part 5 there are no self-paid groups, so the rule changes nothing yet.

## Known-weak

- **Our proxy and hosting see each request whole** (T-11, [[0024-tier0-registration]], option B, Cons): Cloudflare terminates TLS in front of the route and sees the address, the token and the commitment together, and Railway may log the address. Batching hides nothing from them, and a malicious or compelled deploy can log them too (T-69). What this page guarantees is only that nothing of ours keeps them by design, which part 4d's tests check; Tor is the voter's mitigation.
- **Cloudflare also sees the challenge.** Its script runs on the challenge page, so it sees the solve's browser signals and address, and the time, which it can join to the request's by address and timing. That is the same party as above; it never sees the commitment on the page itself.
- **No path without us until part 5** (T-25): a voter refused by Cloudflare, or by a service that won't sign, can't register at Tier 0.
- **A Tier 0 identity costs one solve** (T-01, Not mitigated): anyone with solves can register as many commitments as they like and pad a region past the floor (T-16).
- **A flood turns registration away** (T-52, T-25): about 256 solves per group per batch keep a group's queue full, so new requests are refused as busy for as long as the flood is paid for, and the flood's commitments are added at our paymaster's cost. Edge rate limits per address (P2-11) raise the price; until part 5 a voter turned away has no other path.
- **A queue probe.** The busy answer depends on the queue's size, so someone who already knows a commitment (mostly the parties of T-11) can learn whether it is newly queued for about a thousand solves (Answers).
- **Timing.** The answer to a commitment that is queued and one that is dropped is the same, but the service does different work for them (a set insert or none), so their response times can differ by a little next to Turnstile's round trip. Not padded (part 4d): the difference is one hash write and one counter increment inside the single Redis script of step 8, which runs whichever case it is, so it is microseconds against a request that spends milliseconds to seconds on the network and in Turnstile's round trip, as long as the store doesn't sync each write before answering (Queue); and the parties best placed to time it (T-11) see the commitment anyway.
- **The region is the voter's word**, at Tier 0 and Tier 1 (T-16; [[0019-regional-cells]]).
- **`apps/node` and the native app.** `frame-ancestors` lists the release's entry points; `apps/node`'s local origin (P4-5) and the native shell's (P5-2) are listed when those entry points exist, and until then a voter there can't get a token through the frame.
