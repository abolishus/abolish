## Scope

Benaloh challenge/spoil, ballot receipts, and re-voting semantics (last ballot counts).

## Plan

Built in parts, each its own PR on this branch's name:

1. **Re-vote rule** (merged in #45): [[0011-revote-rule]], [[revoting]], the membership message `M`, and selection in `packages/crypto`.
2. **Receipts** (`0x0004`, #48): [[0013-ballot-receipts]] and [[receipts]]: the record, the operator's signed statement and the check at cast. 3. **Challenge and spoil** (`0x0003`): Benaloh openings, how challenged ballots are built and placed so they can't be tied to the cast ballot ([[verifier]] 6.2, T-42, T-47). Waits for P1-12's ballot format (owner questions).

## What was built (part 2: receipts)

- [[0013-ballot-receipts]] (`Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`): option A. Right after appending entry `i`, the operator signs its board of `i + 1` entries (election, index, segment, entry hash, `link_i`, `root_{i+1}`, its key) under `abolish/v1/sig/receipt`, with [[0012-trustee-identity-signatures]]'s suite; the receipt adds the entry's audit path. Rejected: a CT-style promise, an Ethereum-account signature, direct submit only. Its Consequences list what the later checks must solve ([[P1-13b]]).
- [[receipts]], new spec page: the record (`0x0004`, version 1), the 173-byte statement, receipt keys and signing rules, the checks at cast (`decode`, `receipt-key`, `receipt-signature`, `receipt-election`, `receipt-segment`, `receipt-entry`, `receipt-inclusion`), and the requirements the later checks inherit.
- `abolish/v1/receipt` (reserved plain hash) replaced by `abolish/v1/sig/receipt` (Signature); [[versioning]]'s `0x0004` specified; [[board]]'s client rule 1 refuses a wrong segment without alarming (nothing in a refusal is checkable by anyone else).
- `packages/crypto/src/receipt.ts`: `encodeReceipt`, `decodeReceipt`, `receiptStatement`, `signReceipt`, `checkReceiptAtCast`.

- The receipt check doesn't show the ballot's admission verdict or whether it is selected ([[revoting]], Residuals): that needs P1-12's ballot record.

## What was built (part 1)

- [[0011-revote-rule]] (`Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`): option C. Each ballot's membership proof binds a recent L1 block by number and hash; per tier and nullifier the admitted ballot naming the highest block counts; two distinct ballots naming that block tie and count nothing. Rejected: board order (fails R3), client sequence numbers (fail R2 or R3), and C with a sequence-number tie-break (reopens the dead zone).
- [[revoting]], new spec page: `M = H(DS("abolish/v1/membership-message", definition_hash ‖ u8(tier) ‖ ballot_hash ‖ u64(l1_ref_number) ‖ l1_ref_hash))`; admission checks `l1-ref-before-open`, `l1-ref-after-close` and `l1-ref-unknown`, in that order, with a block beyond the finalized head pending only until L1 finalizes a block after close; selection (repeats by (tier, ν, `M`), grouping, ranking, ties, no fallback, no spoiled ballots); client rules; how R1–R6 are met; residuals.
- [[verifier]] 5.3, 5.7, 6.1 updated and 5.8 added; [[membership]] Message points at `M`; `abolish/v1/membership-message` specified in [[domain-separation]]; [[versioning]]'s ballot row names the new fields.
- `packages/crypto/src/revoting.ts`: `membershipMessage`, `membershipMessageField`, `l1RefMin`, `checkL1Reference`, `selectBallots`.
- Threat files T-11, T-13, T-33, T-35, T-36, T-38, T-41, T-42, T-45, T-47, T-53.

## Tested

- `docs/spec/vectors/revoting.json`, from an independent Python implementation (`packages/crypto/scripts/revoting-vectors.py`, hashlib plus membership-vectors.py's self-checked Keccak; selection by sorting rather than scanning): 9 messages and 2 rejections; 8 `l1_ref_min` searches (exactly at the window's start, a second either side, inside a missed slot, at and beyond the head, and a `low` inside the window rejected); 15 L1-reference verdicts over a vector chain (before open, an ancient block with no lookup, wrong hash, after close, at close, the same verdict for two heads past close, pending, and a far-future number pending and then rejected); 10 selection cases (re-vote posted before the coerced ballot, withheld coerced ballot, tie voiding the group with no fallback, a tie below the top, repeats, replay after re-vote, a repeat of a tied ballot, tiers kept apart, a mixed board) and 4 rejected inputs.
- fast-check properties (passing at `FC_NUM_RUNS=1000`): `M` changes with every input; once a block after close is finalized no reference is pending, and only a block at or before close is admitted; verifiers with different heads past close reach the same verdict; a reference before `l1_ref_min` is rejected with no lookup; `l1RefMin`'s binary search agrees with a linear scan; `F(M) < 2²⁴⁸`; malformed inputs rejected; selection partitions its input with at most one selected ballot per group; what counts is unchanged when ballots move to other positions and arrive in another order (R3); a ballot naming a newer block than its group always counts (R2); a second distinct ballot on the top block voids the group with no fallback; replaying any ballot never changes what counts (R1).

## Independent review

A fresh in-session subagent reviewed the diff against `.github/review/crypto-review.md`. It regenerated the vectors (equal values; the committed file is the generator's output after `vp check --fix`), confirmed the Python generator is independent, the 105-byte layout unambiguous, the code faithful to the spec, and that no one without the voter's secret can cause a tie. Findings:

- Blocking, fixed: a direct-submitted ballot naming a block that never comes (`2⁶⁴ − 1`) stayed pending for good, so the election could never be verified. Once L1 finalizes a block after close, any reference above the head is now rejected (`l1-ref-after-close`), using Ethereum's strictly increasing timestamps; `checkL1Reference` implements it, with vectors.
- Blocking, fixed: a Python bytecode cache (`scripts/__pycache__/…pyc`) had been committed in the gated path, which `importlib` would run instead of the source when its header matched. Removed, `__pycache__/` ignored, generators run with `python3 -I -B`.
- Non-blocking, fixed: the membership proof is made only after the voter chooses to cast (Client rule 6), so a challenged ballot can't be posted as cast to tie the real one; the block record is persisted before a ballot leaves the device, retries resend identical bytes, and a self-tie alarms (rule 3); the after-finality check isn't a targeted block query (rule 4, T-11); casts per tier are independent (rule 7, T-13); residuals for proposer-coercers and timing; R6 marked conditional on P1-18's close rule; `l1_chain_id`, `closes_at` and `l1_inclusion_bound` attributed to P1-10b; everything deciding later whether a ballot counts must key on content, not the entry (P1-12, P1-15, P1-18, receipts); `selectBallots` documents that messages are recomputed, never decoded.
- Second round, blocking, fixed: with no lower bound, a direct-submitted ballot naming an ancient block needed L1 evidence nobody archives, so verifiers with different L1 sources would disagree or report the election unverifiable. A ballot naming a block before `l1_ref_min` (the first block at or after `opens_at − 3600`) is now rejected by number alone (`l1-ref-before-open`), keeping every lookup in the L1 range around the poll.
- Second round, non-blocking, fixed: the time is checked before the hash, so verifiers with different heads past close report the same code; rule 3 excepts the explicit tie escape and alarms on any ballot under the voter's nullifier that isn't in its records; the voter's receipt check must show admission and selection, since the ranking fields escape Benaloh's audit (T-41, T-42); the published cast time constrains how challenged entries are placed (T-42, T-47); `l1-ref-after-close` is no longer claimed to stop ballots made after close, which P1-18's close rule must keep out; the vector-regeneration claim is stated exactly.
- Third round: nothing blocking. Non-blocking, fixed: a verifier of a closed election uses the first block after close as its head, so lookups really are bounded by the poll's L1 range; `l1RefMin` and its vectors pin how `l1_ref_min` is derived, with `opens_at` and `closes_at` in Unix seconds; verifier 6.1 states R6 as conditional on P1-18; T-35, T-36, T-45 and T-53 record the change; a vector covers a correctly hashed block after close at the head.
- Review of the third round's fix: nothing blocking. Non-blocking, fixed: R6 is stated as conditional on P1-18 throughout; the evidence range names block `l1_ref_min − 1` and the search's probes, and applies to 5.8 only; every reference is pending before the window is known; a vector pins the rejection of a `low` inside the window. `low = 0` and a window starting at time 0 are covered by a property test only, since the vector chain starts at block 100.

## Known-weak

- The L1 reference is checked only given finalized L1 blocks: reading them is the verifier's (P1-19). The ballot record carrying the fields is P1-12's, blocked on owner questions.
- The last blocks before close stay coercible (every last-ballot-counts scheme has this window; the rule widens it by about 36 seconds), re-votes are public to anyone who knows the nullifier, a proposer-coercer can stretch the freeze window by the slots it controls, R6 depends on P1-18's close rule, and a closed election verifies only once L1 has finalized every named block ([[revoting]], Residuals).
