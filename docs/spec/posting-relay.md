# Posting relay

How a voter's share posting reaches the board's contract path through our API, which pays its gas, as [[0042-posting-relay]] decides (option A, its rules 1 to 5): the request and its answers, the checks before the relay pays, its caps, the queue and the worker that sends from the posting key, the forced path through L1, and what the client may rely on. Notation is from [[notation]]; the posting record, its public check, the windows and the client rules are [[delivery]]'s and [[share-encryption]]'s; the board's `submitPosting` and its `SharePosted` event are [[contracts]]' (Board, Delivery records, Events). Threats: T-11, T-13, T-15, T-17, T-18, T-25, T-50, T-52, T-54, T-69.

The relay is operational, never part of an election: no verifier reads the request, the queue or the relay's state. What a verifier checks is the board's `SharePosted` events, read by [[delivery]]'s delivery check whoever sent them. So this page binds the relay, its route and the ballot client, not the verifier. It adds no record type, hash or encoding: the request carries the share posting (`0x000d`, version 1 or 2) and the ballot record it delivers unchanged, as `submitPosting(bytes posting, bytes ballot)` takes them. `packages/crypto` has every check the relay runs on the bytes (the records' decoders, the ballot hash, `checkPosting` and its version-2 counterpart).

What this page doesn't cover:

- **The voter's choice to post** ([[delivery]], Client rules, rule 4): the offer, its wording and the decline are the ballot client's ([[P2-8]] part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-1, #578).
- **The voter's own wallet** ([[0042-posting-relay]], rule 6, option B): [[P2-8]] part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-2d.
- **The build:** the route, the queue, the worker and the posting key's runbook ([[P2-8]] part 3c-1b-2b-2b-2d-2b-2b, after this page), and the client's `post` through it (part 2c).

## Roles

- **The voter's ballot client** holds `ct_i` and `m_i` for each seat it may post ([[delivery]], Client rules, rule 4) and the ballot record the posting delivers. It sends the pair and judges the outcome itself, from the board's events (Outcome).
- **The route** (`apps/api`) checks the request's bytes and the election, queues the pair, and answers what the worker did with it.
- **The posting worker** (`apps/worker`, in a deployment of its own, the only one holding the posting key) re-checks what can change, simulates, applies the caps, sends `submitPosting` from the **posting key**, keeps each sent posting until it is final, and forces it through L1 if the sequencer doesn't include it.

The posting key is an externally owned account used for nothing else. It has no role in any contract: a posting's authorisation is its `m_i`, which only a party that knows it can make pass ([[share-encryption]], Public posting), and the ballot it carries, which only a member can make ([[contracts]], Board, Delivery records). The key only pays, on L2 for its calls and on L1 for its deposits.

And three settings of the worker's deployment, which the operator chooses and which change no answer's meaning: the **election budget**, the most wei the relay spends on one election's postings, L2 execution, L2's L1 data fee and L1 deposits together; the **fee cap**, the most wei per L2 gas any relayed call is signed to pay (as the Tier 1 relay's, [[tier1-relay]]); and the **L1 fee cap**, the most wei per L1 gas any deposit is signed to pay; a deposit's cancellation may pay up to twice it.

They are values of this page, not of any record: changing one changes the relay and its clients, never an election. Why these values is below.

## Request

`POST /postings` on the API, on the ballot path: like every route it carries no account cookie or `Authorization` header and is refused with one (T-10), and CORS allows the ballot client's origins with no credentials, as `POST /ballots` does. The handler sees the body alone, never a header, cookie or address. The body is a strict JSON object of exactly:

| Field     | Value                                                                                              |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `posting` | The share posting record (`0x000d`) with its header, as lowercase hex with no `0x`.                |
| `ballot`  | The ballot record (`0x0002`) the posting delivers, with its header, as lowercase hex with no `0x`. |

There is no election field: the posting's `definition_hash` names it. The route's body bound is 256 KiB, above twice `POSTING_MAX_DATA` in hex.

## Answers

The route checks, in this order, and answers the first failure:

| Step | Check                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Answer                                                                                 |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | The transport: no account credential, a body within the route's bound, strict JSON with no repeated key (as every route), and the API's per-address rate limit.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | 400, or 429 `RATE_LIMITED` (as every route)                                            |
| 2    | The body has exactly the fields above in their forms, and `submitPosting`'s calldata for the pair (selector, two offsets, and each record's length and bytes padded to whole words: `132 + ⌈p/32⌉·32 + ⌈b/32⌉·32` bytes for records of `p` and `b` bytes) is at most `POSTING_MAX_DATA`.                                                                                           | 400                                                                                    |
| 3    | The posting decodes strictly as a share posting and the ballot as a ballot record (`packages/crypto`), the posting's version is the one the ballot's version fixes ([[delivery]], Versions; Version 2), and the posting's `ballot_hash` is the ballot hash recomputed from the ballot record ([[ballot-validity]]), its `definition_hash` the ballot's.                            | 422 `POSTING_REFUSED`, code `malformed`, the failing check's code as `check`           |
| 4    | The election is registered in the deployment's election registry, by the API's own index of it, never read per request, and the index's latest L2 block has a timestamp at most `c_D − POSTING_SEND_MARGIN` (Sending). An index that lags the L2 node by more than a minute stops here.                                                                                            | 422 `POSTING_REFUSED`, code `unknown-election` or `too-late`; 503 `RELAY_UNAVAILABLE`  |
| 5    | The posting passes the public check against the election's registered key set ([[share-encryption]], Public posting, and Public posting (ballot version 4): `posting-hash`, `posting-proof`, `posting-encapsulation`), the posting's seat in the key set (`posting-seat`) and its cell one of the definition's (`posting-cell`), so the relay pays only for postings that deliver. | 422 `POSTING_REFUSED`, with the check's code                                           |
| 6    | In one atomic step (one Redis script): if `keccak256(posting)` already has an outcome (Caps, Queue), answer it; else if the queue holds `POSTING_QUEUE_MAX` pairs, answer busy; else queue the pair, unless the same posting is already queued.                                                                                                                                    | the outcome; 503 `RELAY_BUSY`                                                          |
| 7    | The route waits up to `POSTING_WAIT` for the worker's outcome for `keccak256(posting)`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | 200 `sent`; 422 `POSTING_REFUSED` with the worker's code; 503 `RELAY_BUSY` if none yet |

A `sent` answer is `{ "transaction": <hash>, "layer": "l2" | "l1" }`: the hash of the last transaction that carried the posting, an L2 call or, once forced, the L1 deposit. `RELAY_BUSY` means only "not sent yet": the client sends the same bytes again later, and the relay never pays twice for them (Caps). Every answer is a hint ([[0042-posting-relay]], rule 2): the client stands down only on the board's event (Outcome).

**Why the route runs the public check.** It is the relay's most expensive check (an ML-KEM re-encapsulation and a few ristretto255 operations, about a millisecond), but a pair that fails it never takes a queue place or reaches the key's process, and only the maker of the ballot record, who knows its `m_i`, can make postings of it that pass. That is the voter for a member's ballot; a made-up ballot record passes too, since its membership proof is the worker's to check (Known-weak). The board can't run it ([[contracts]], Board, Delivery records), so without it the relay would pay for junk postings anyone who saw `ct_i` can make (T-52).

**What a refusal says.** Steps 2, 3 and 5 depend only on the pair and the registered key set; step 4 on the index; step 6's busy answer on the queue's size. None depends on whether the ballot was cast, receipted or posted before, except a repeat of the same bytes, which its sender already holds. The worker's refusals (Sending) depend on the chain, the caps and the budget, so a `ballot-cap` refusal says that this (`tier`, `ν`) already had postings paid for two other ballot hashes: only a member holding that nullifier's secret, or a party that saw those postings on L2, can make the pair that learns it, and those postings are public once sent (T-13).

## Queue

The queue is one Redis hash from `keccak256(posting)` to the pair, with no arrival time, order, request identifier, address or count per pair, and no BullMQ job, key or log line derived from a request: BullMQ's events stream keeps job IDs and times, so the worker drains the hash on a tick (every 2 seconds), never a job per request (T-11, T-13). It writes the route's index (step 4) at the end of each tick it completes, from the latest block read at its start, so a worker that can't judge the queue lets the index age out and the route stops queueing pairs nothing answers. An outcome is one Redis key per `keccak256(posting)` that the worker writes and that expires after `POSTING_WAIT`; the durable record of a sent posting is the worker's (Caps). The queue's store MUST NOT evict keys, and MUST NOT persist, snapshot or back up the queue or the outcomes, which hold `m_i` for pairs that may be refused (T-18); it need not survive a restart, since a pair lost before it was sent was never answered `sent`, and the client sends it again.

A pair leaves the queue when the worker sends or refuses it. Nothing about a request remains then but what Caps keeps, which the chain shows once the posting is sent; a refused pair leaves nothing but its expiring outcome (T-18).

The route's log is the API's: method, route, status and duration (T-10); the worker logs per pair only the election's definition hash and an outcome code, never a record, `m`, a ballot hash, a nullifier, a transaction or an address ([[0042-posting-relay]], rule 1; T-11, T-18).

## Sending

The worker takes the queued pairs one at a time, in no order the route sets (Redis's hash order), and for each, refusing on the first failure:

1. **Decodes again** and recomputes `keccak256(posting)`, the ballot hash and the ballot's (`tier`, `ν`): its `tier`, or under ballot versions 2 to 5 its cell's tier, and its nullifier. A pair the route queued always decodes; the worker doesn't take the route's word for the fields it counts by (`malformed`).
2. **Repeat.** A posting whose bytes the relay already sent is answered `sent` with its transaction, and nothing is sent ([[0042-posting-relay]], rule 4).
3. **Time.** The L2 node's latest block has a timestamp at most `c_D − POSTING_SEND_MARGIN` (`too-late`). This reads the latest block, not the finalized one rule 3.2 of [[0042-posting-relay]] names: the latest is never earlier, so this refuses at least as much. The block is read once per tick and must be at most `POSTING_HEAD_AGE` behind the worker's clock, else it is no head: the tick judges nothing and writes no index, which ages out (Queue), so a node behind the chain can't pass the cutoff on stale time. A pair the tick reaches once its head is older than that is left queued for the next tick, unanswered, before step 5. An election the worker can't judge under its own pins and index, though the route queued its pair, has no `c_D` to read and is refused (`unavailable`).
4. **Admission, then simulation.** First the ballot passes the verifier's admission ([[verifier]] 5.1 to 5.4, its membership proof included), the ballots of each election's pairs in a tick verified together ([[ballot-validity]], Batched admission), so a pair whose ballot no member made leaves the queue before any node call; a ballot it refuses is refused as the board's own ballot check would refuse it (`simulation`). Then `submitPosting(posting, ballot)` from the posting key, called against the latest block with gas `POSTING_GAS_LIMIT_MAX`, succeeds, so the board's own checks (the ballot check with its membership proof, `posting-length`, `posting-ballot`, `posting-hash`) pass before any gas is spent (`simulation`). The call's gas limit `g` is the larger of the gas it used times `POSTING_GAS_HEADROOM`, rounded up, and the portal's minimum gas limit for a deposit of its calldata, `21,000 + 40 · d` for `d` bytes, so the same `g` serves the L2 call and a deposit; a `g` above `POSTING_GAS_LIMIT_MAX` is refused (`too-costly`). A revert refuses at once; a simulation that fails without one leaves the pair queued for the next tick, and is refused (`unavailable`) once it has so failed on five ticks over at least a minute, so pairs a node errors on can't hold queue places for good (T-52). The same holds for a step 2, 5 or 6 that fails without an answer (the durable record unreadable, say).
5. **Caps.** The election and (`tier`, `ν`) have postings paid for fewer than `POSTING_BALLOT_CAP` other ballot hashes (`ballot-cap`), and the election's spend plus the call's most cost (`g` at the fee cap, with the L1 data fee the L2's fee oracle gives for its calldata, and a deposit's reserve if it may be forced, Depositing below) is within the election budget (`budget`).
6. **Sends.** Signs the call with the next nonce of the posting key, gas limit `g` and fees at most the fee cap, broadcasts it, and before answering records durably: `keccak256(posting)` with the transaction's hash, the ballot hash under the election and (`tier`, `ν`), the most cost added to the election's spend, and the in-flight entry (below). Then it answers `sent`.

A pending transaction is rebroadcast, identical, every `POSTING_RESEND`, and once it has been pending `2 · POSTING_RESEND` since it was signed it is replaced at its nonce with both fees raised by an eighth (rounded up), carrying the same calldata, while the raised fees are within the fee cap; past that it is only rebroadcast. A replacement's most cost is unchanged, since the most cost is counted at the fee cap. Once transactions that carried the posting are in L2 blocks at the latest head, the election's spend is reduced to what their receipts show (each one's gas used at its effective gas price, and its L1 data fee: a reverted one is paid for too, and nothing more is sent for it), beside the reserve for a deposit counted at the first send, which stays counted until the deposit is settled; the record names the newest of them. A posting's transactions share a nonce except for a signing again at the next nonce (below), so two of them landing is the extra call that rule allows, and both are counted. If their blocks leave the latest head, the posting is counted at its most cost again and tended as pending. The worker reads the key's nonce at the latest block before each signing. A transaction whose nonce another transaction of the key consumed (a deposit, below), with none of the posting's transactions in a block, is judged by the posting's event at the latest head: with it, nothing more is sent and none of its cost on L2 is counted, its deposit's reserve staying counted; without it, and while the latest block is by the send cutoff and at most `POSTING_HEAD_AGE` behind the worker's clock (Time), the posting is signed again at the next nonce, its L2 most cost (`g` at the fee cap and its L1 data fee) added to the spend within the budget, else it is left to its deposit, if any. The key's nonce is read before the receipts and again after the event, and a count that moved between the two leaves the posting for the next tend, so a nonce one of the posting's own transactions consumed shows that transaction's receipt unless the node's view of the chain changed between the reads without the count moving (Known-weak). A posting is so paid for at most once more than it lands, and the extra call is a no-op repeat ([[contracts]], Board, Repeats).

**In flight.** Each sent posting is kept, with its calldata, election, `c_I`, `c_D` and `t_D`, the time it was first sent and every transaction that carried it, when its latest transaction was signed and last broadcast, its most cost and its deposit's reserve, each deposit and cancellation of it on L1 (Depositing), which outlive the rest of the entry until their nonce settles (Pruning), and which of its transactions are in blocks at the latest head with what their receipts show, until its exact bytes are in a `SharePosted` event of the board at the L2's finalized head, whoever sent them, or until the finalized head passes `t_D` without them, after which it can never be in time ([[delivery]], Windows). Nothing else of it is kept. This is the only place `m_i` outlives the request, and only until the chain holds it or never can (T-18). The time it was first sent is the request's time to within a tick, which [[0042-posting-relay]], rule 5, says isn't kept; it is what `POSTING_FORCE_AFTER` is measured from, it is public once the call is included, and it goes with the entry, so it outlives the chain's own record only for a posting the sequencer withholds (T-11).

**Pruning.** The worker reads the L2's finalized head and prunes against it, never against one whose timestamp is ahead of its own clock, which only a faulty or lying node answers and which would delete live elections' Caps, so their postings could be paid for again (T-25, T-52). Once the head's timestamp passes an election's `t_D`, every entry of the election is deleted, whatever its state, with what Caps keeps for it. Before then, an entry whose exact bytes are in a `SharePosted` event of the board at the finalized head is deleted once the key's count at the latest block is past its nonce: a transaction of it still pending holds its nonce, which the next send would otherwise sign at, so it stays and is tended until the nonce is consumed, and its `m_i` outlives the event by as long as that transaction is pending, never past `t_D`. Its Caps rows stay until `t_D`, so its bytes are never paid for again and its ballot hash still counts. An entry signed again at another nonce while its event was read stays for the next prune. An entry with a deposit or cancellation still pending on L1 stays too, before or after `t_D`, so its L1 nonce is tended until a transaction of it is in an L1 block at the L1's finalized head; from `t_D`, or once its bytes are final, only what tends that nonce is kept (the pending transactions' nonce, hashes, fees and times, and the signed cancellation), and the posting, its ballot and the signed deposit are deleted, so `m_i` never outlives `t_D` (T-18) and no L1 nonce is left pending unseen. A deposit pending then is cancelled at once if it hasn't been (Depositing).

**Forced through L1** ([[0042-posting-relay]], rule 5; [[0004-l2-choice]], Forced inclusion; T-50). A posting whose transactions are in no L2 block `POSTING_FORCE_AFTER` after it was first sent is deposited on L1: `depositTransaction` on the portal the release pins, from the posting key on L1 (the worker reads the portal at startup from the deployment's L1 relay, which the board's election registry names, and refuses to start unless the L1 node is on the chain the registry names, that relay names the board, the portal holds code and the key's L1 account holds none, since a contract's deposit is aliased on L2), to the board, value 0, gas limit `g`, the same calldata, which `POSTING_MAX_DATA` keeps within the deposit's cap. On L2 the deposit runs as the posting key's own call ([[0004-l2-choice]], an externally owned sender keeps its address), so the board sees the same call. The relay deposits only while two things hold:

- **By the deposit cutoff** `c_F = min(c_I, c_P − F − POSTING_FINALITY)`: the deposit's L1 block can have a timestamp at most `c_F`, so it is signed only on an L1 head whose timestamp `T` has `T + POSTING_DEPOSIT_SPAN ≤ c_F`, and cancelled if it isn't in an L1 block by `T + POSTING_DEPOSIT_PATIENCE` (Depositing). Then it reaches L2, and the L2's finalized head, by `c_P`, so the client knows by its own deadline whether it landed and still has `POSTING_CLIENT_MARGIN` for the voter's own wallet (Outcome), and it is never sent inside this election's interval from `c_I` to `c_D` that every seat's forced receipts take ([[delivery]], Capacity). With `F` of 13 hours and `R` at most 9, `c_F` is `c_P − F − POSTING_FINALITY`.
- **Outside every interval Capacity counts**: no registered election's receipt interval (`c_I` to `c_D`) or complaint interval (`t_D + W_C` to `c_K`), each half-open as Capacity takes it, meets the deposit's **landing span**, from `T` to `T + POSTING_DEPOSIT_SPAN`, half-open as they are, the L1 times it is meant to land at. Capacity sums those intervals over every election a seat serves, so a deposit inside any of them takes gas the trustees' check counted on (T-54). A posting due to be forced inside one waits for its end, if that is still before `c_F`. The registered elections are those of the index the route judges by (Answers, step 4), each with its `closes_at` and `l1_inclusion_bound` read from its definition as the worker's admission reads them, under the release's `F` and `R` and the registry's `W_C` (`CONTRACT_EVENT_WINDOW`, [[parameters]]); if any registered election that isn't known to be past its `c_K` can't be read, nothing is deposited on that tend, since its intervals are unknown (T-54). An election is known to be past its `c_K` only from its definition, so one registration whose definition stays unreadable (one under a profile the release doesn't pin, say) stops every deposit until the worker can read it (Known-weak). The index's progress is checked against the registry itself: the worker reads the election registry's `DefinitionRegistered` events in L2 blocks at the finalized head, from the index's latest registration's block on, and if one whose block is `POSTING_INDEX_LAG` or more behind its clock isn't in the index, the index has stopped and nothing is deposited on that tend (T-54). Nor is anything deposited while that finalized head's timestamp is more than `F` behind the worker's clock or ahead of it: the OP Stack finalizes an L2 block within its sequencing window and L1's finality, which `F` bounds, so only a stalled or lying node answers an older head, and over one that stopped the check would pass however long the index had stopped too (T-25). A younger one is harmless: a definition registers only before it opens (the registry's `AlreadyOpen`), so its first interval starts at least `DELIVERY_WINDOW − F − R` (34 hours) after its block, whose time is at most `MAX_SEQUENCER_DRIFT` ahead of L1's, which leaves `POSTING_INDEX_LAG` and a landing span inside it; so is a registration above a finalized head at most `F` old, since `F` plus a landing span and the drift is within the same 34 hours.

**Depositing.** On each tend, after its L2 transactions are judged (above), a posting is deposited if none of its transactions is in an L2 block at the L2's **safe head** (the head derived from L1 data, which a sequencer can't withhold from L1 and still claim), `POSTING_FORCE_AFTER` has passed since it was first sent, it has no deposit pending or landed, its exact bytes are in no `SharePosted` event of the board at the safe head (a posting someone else sent needs no deposit), and both conditions above hold on the L1 node's latest block, whose timestamp `T` must be at most `POSTING_HEAD_AGE` behind the worker's clock and never ahead of it (a head from the future would move the landing span off the times the deposit can land at; T-25, T-54). The safe head, not the latest: a censoring sequencer can put the call in blocks it shows but never batches, and drop them once `c_F` has passed, so a soft confirmation never stands in for inclusion here (T-50); the latest head still decides the spend, as above. A call in a block at the latest head but not yet at the safe head after `POSTING_FORCE_AFTER` is deposited anyway, at most a no-op repeat. Then the worker:

1. **Prices it.** The L1 transaction calls `depositTransaction(board, 0, g, false, calldata)` on the portal from the posting key with value 0. Its gas limit is the L1 node's gas estimate for that call at its latest block times `POSTING_GAS_HEADROOM`, rounded up, since the portal burns L1 gas in proportion to `g` and the deposit market's price, which can rise before the deposit is mined; an estimate that reverts, or a limit above EIP-7825's `2²⁴`, sends no deposit on this tend. Its **reserve** is that gas limit at the L1 fee cap plus 21,000 gas, for a cancellation, at twice the L1 fee cap: the most its nonce can cost, whichever of them is mined.
2. **Counts it.** A first send counts a deposit's reserve, priced as in step 1 when it is sent, if `POSTING_FORCE_AFTER + POSTING_DEPOSIT_SPAN` from it is still by `c_F` (Caps, Spend); an L1 node that fails then fails the send as any node call does (step 4), and an estimate that reverts or passes the cap counts none. The posting's **counted reserve** is what was counted so far for its deposits. A deposit is sent only if its election's spend, with the counted reserve raised to this reserve if it is lower (a first deposit) or by this reserve whole (any later deposit of the same posting), is within the budget; the raise is then added to the spend, else nothing is sent and the tend counts it as held. So a posting whose first send didn't count a reserve (it wasn't forceable then) can still be deposited if the budget allows, and a counted reserve higher than the deposit's stays counted until it settles.
3. **Signs and records it.** It signs the call with the posting key's next nonce on L1 (its count at the L1 node's latest block), only while no other deposit or cancellation of the key is pending, else the posting waits for a later tend: a deposit signed behind a stuck nonce couldn't be mined, or cancelled, until that nonce settled, past the span it was checked for (T-54), so the key has at most one L1 transaction pending. It signs with fees whose rise by an eighth is still within the L1 fee cap, so it can always be replaced and cancelled, records the deposit (its signed bytes, hash, nonce, fees, `T`, and its reserve) with the posting's entry and names it in the record as the posting's last transaction, layer `l1`, and only then broadcasts it, as a send's (step 6).

A pending deposit is rebroadcast, identical, every `POSTING_RESEND`, and replaced at its nonce with both fees raised by an eighth (rounded up) once pending `2 · POSTING_RESEND` since it was signed, while its raised fees raised by an eighth once more are within the L1 fee cap. A pending deposit is tended to its end even once an L2 transaction of the posting is in a block: the deposit then runs as a no-op repeat ([[contracts]], Board, Repeats), and cancelling it on a sighting at the latest head would give up a censored posting's forcing for a soft confirmation (T-50). Once the later of the L1 head's timestamp and the worker's clock passes `T + POSTING_DEPOSIT_PATIENCE` without it in an L1 block, it is **cancelled**: replaced at its nonce by a transfer of 0 wei from the posting key to itself with gas limit 21,000 and both fees raised by an eighth over the deposit's, so the race between them is run inside the span the worker checked, with half of it left for the cancellation to be mined (T-54). The clock as well as the head, so a stalled L1 node doesn't hold the cancellation back. A pending cancellation is rebroadcast and replaced as a deposit is, up to twice the L1 fee cap.

Whichever of them is in an L1 block at the latest L1 head settles the nonce: a deposit whose receipt shows success has **landed**; a reverted deposit or a cancellation hasn't, and the posting may be deposited again, within the budget, while the conditions above hold. Either way that nonce's share of the counted reserve (each L1 nonce keeps the reserve counted for it, a first deposit's including what its first send counted), once the settling transaction's block is at the L1's finalized head, is replaced by its receipt's gas used at its effective price, and a later deposit's reserve stays counted beside it, as an L2 receipt replaces a most cost; if its block leaves the latest L1 head first, the nonce is tended as pending again. A landed deposit reaches L2 within `F` of its L1 block ([[0004-l2-choice]], Forced inclusion), where it runs from the posting key and consumes one of its L2 nonces, which the tending of a consumed nonce (Sending) then judges by the posting's event. A deposit that landed is never sent again, even if its call fails when it runs on L2 (Known-weak). A nonce the key's count at the latest L1 head is past, with no receipt of any of its transactions seen there, stays pending: a receipt index that lags or an endpoint behind a balancer can show the count before the receipt, and one of them may have landed. It holds every other deposit back, keeps its reserve counted and is neither replaced nor cancelled; once the key's count at the L1's finalized head is past it too, it is settled as landed, so the posting is never deposited again, with its reserve kept as what it cost (Known-weak). Nothing is made final on an L1 finalized head numbered past the latest head, or whose timestamp is ahead of the worker's clock (T-25, T-52).

The record names, as the posting's last transaction (Answers), a deposit that is pending or landed, layer `l1`; otherwise its newest L2 transaction, layer `l2`, as after a cancellation, a reverted deposit or a deposit's block leaving the L1 head.

A posting's counted reserve not yet replaced by a receipt is released from its election's spend once no deposit or cancellation of it is pending and none can be sent any more: its exact bytes are in a `SharePosted` event at the finalized head, or the L1 head passes `c_F`, read only from an L1 head that passes the checks above (at most `POSTING_HEAD_AGE` behind the worker's clock and never ahead of it), so a node answering a future head can't release reserves early (T-25, T-52). What its landed deposits, reverted ones and cancellations cost stays counted, as an L2 receipt's does.

This is narrower than [[0042-posting-relay]]'s "by `c_D`", which didn't weigh Capacity or the client's fallback. A posting that can't be deposited under both stays on L2 alone: the relay keeps rebroadcasting it, and the client, which sees neither its event nor a deposit, falls back to the voter's own wallet (Outcome).

**What a posting shows.** Every posting the relay sends has the same payer, so the chain links the posting key only to postings, which are public anyway, and to their ballots, which a posting names in any case (T-13). The relay sends at once, with no batch: a posting's block says when its request came, to within a tick, which the voter's network address beside it already tells the operator ([[0042-posting-relay]], Known-weak).

## Caps

[[0042-posting-relay]], rule 4, so the relay's spend is bounded by the electorate, never by the requests (T-52):

- **Bytes.** A posting is paid for at most once: `keccak256(posting)` with its transaction is kept until the election's `t_D` passes on the L2's finalized head.
- **Ballots.** At most `POSTING_BALLOT_CAP` ballot hashes per election and (`tier`, `ν`). A member holds one nullifier per tier ([[membership]], Scope; Rolling scope), so the relay pays for at most `2 · s · M` postings in an election of `s` seats and `M` members, whatever anyone makes up: a member can make up ballots that pass the board's check and post against them without casting them ([[contracts]], Board, Known-weak), and this caps how many of them the relay pays for. It is the bound a node's receipts have ([[delivery]], What a node owes): a voter's honest postings need at most two ballot hashes per nullifier, the one the selection picks and the newest whose L1 reference isn't admitted (a ballot cast again with the same core after a reorganisation keeps its ballot hash).
- **Spend.** The election budget bounds the relay's spend on one election, whatever its size, L1 data fees and deposits included, and the posting key's floats on L2 and L1, topped up by hand ([[0042-posting-relay]], Consequences), bound it across elections. The budget is the operator's to size: `2 · s · M` postings is the most the caps let it pay for, and a budget below what honest voters need lets a few members spend it first, sending later voters to their own wallets (Known-weak).

Past a cap the relay answers `refused` with the cap's code, and the client may fall back to the voter's own wallet ([[0042-posting-relay]], Consequences, Re-votes beyond the cap go direct). What Caps keeps is public on the chain once sent (the postings, their ballots' hashes and nullifiers, the gas), except which of them the relay paid for, which the payer's address shows anyway; it is deleted once the election's `t_D` is final.

## Outcome

The client never stands down on the relay's answer ([[0042-posting-relay]], rule 2). A posting is **recorded** once the client finds its exact bytes as the `record` of a `SharePosted` event of the board, read with the posting's `definition_hash` and `ballot_hash` as topics at the L2's finalized head, through the L2 endpoint the build already uses. Only then is the ballot sent ([[delivery]], Client rules, rule 4). The client:

- sends the same pair again when the relay answers busy or can't be reached, until `POSTING_PATIENCE` has passed with no other answer or until `c_D − POSTING_SEND_MARGIN` (the relay's send cutoff), and then falls back;
- falls back (part 2d) at once when the relay answers `refused`;
- after a `sent` answer, waits until `POSTING_FORCE_AFTER + POSTING_SEEN` has passed, then falls back unless the event is in an L2 block at the latest head (a sighting that only delays the fallback, below) or it finds a **qualifying deposit** on L1 itself, through its own L1 endpoint: a `TransactionDeposited` event of the portal the release pins, from any sender, to the board, whose data is the pair's calldata and whose gas limit is at least the gas the client's own simulation of the call through its L2 endpoint used. While a qualifying deposit's L1 block time plus `F` hasn't passed it keeps waiting (until `c_P` at most), since the deposit reaches L2 by then, and a voter's own posting beside it would put their address on L2 next to the ballot for nothing (T-13); once it has passed without the event, the deposit failed and it falls back. A deposit with a lower gas limit doesn't qualify, so a relay can't hold the client with a deposit made to fail (T-25). The relay's `l1` answer names the deposit, but the client looks for it on L1 whatever the relay answered;
- stops relying on the relay at its deadline `c_P` whatever it answered or the latest head showed: if the posting isn't **recorded** by then, its exact bytes at the finalized head, it falls back. A sighting at the latest head is the sequencer's soft confirmation, served by an endpoint the client doesn't trust, so it only ever delays the fallback to `c_P`, never cancels it ([[delivery]], Direct submit; [[0004-l2-choice]], Finality; T-25, T-50). The relay's cutoffs (Sending) make sure a posting it sent, or its deposit, can be finalized by `c_P`, so the fallback never waits on the relay past `c_P`, and the voter's own wallet still has `POSTING_CLIENT_MARGIN` to send by `c_D` ([[0042-posting-relay]], rule 6);
- treats the relay as having read `m_i` whenever it answered anything but a transport error, and says so when it offers the fallback ([[0042-posting-relay]], Consequences, The operator learns `m_i`).

## Why these values

- **`POSTING_MAX_DATA`.** The portal's cap on a deposit's data, so a posting the relay sends can always be forced. The largest pair this spec defines is far within it: a version-2 posting at `MAX_OPTIONS` is 5,405 bytes, and the largest ballot record about 82 KB (version 3's 48,380 and version 4's author half at most 33,544 bytes, version 5's `step` four more; [[contracts]], L1 relay, Size), about 87.5 KB of calldata. [[0042-posting-relay]]'s "about 108 KB for their 54 KB" counted only version 3's ballots.
- **`POSTING_BALLOT_CAP`.** [[0042-posting-relay]], rule 4: the number of ballots per (`tier`, `ν`) delivery can select from or a node owes for ([[delivery]], What a node owes).
- **`POSTING_QUEUE_MAX`.** Sixty-four pairs are at most about 5.6 MB in Redis, and the worker clears a full queue in well under a minute; only pairs that pass the public check take a place, though one made with a made-up ballot record passes it until the worker checks the ballot (Known-weak).
- **`POSTING_WAIT`.** As the ballot and receipt routes' wait: long enough for a simulation and a broadcast, short enough to keep a request from holding a connection.
- **`POSTING_MAX_CONCURRENT`.** Each request may hold one of the API server's slots for `POSTING_WAIT`, so a server takes at most eight at once and answers the rest busy, leaving its other slots to ballots and the board (T-52). Busy costs the client only a resend of the same bytes.
- **`POSTING_CLIENT_MARGIN`.** An hour for the voter to choose their own wallet and send from it by `c_D`, forced through L1 if need be.
- **`POSTING_SEND_MARGIN`, `POSTING_SEEN` and `POSTING_PATIENCE`.** A posting the relay sends at its cutoff has `POSTING_FORCE_AFTER` to land, `POSTING_SEEN` for the client to see it and `POSTING_FINALITY` to be finalized before `c_P`, so the relay never sends what the client can't judge recorded by its own deadline. Half an hour covers Base's usual time from an L2 block to the finalized head (a batch posted within minutes, then two L1 epochs, about 13 minutes); a posting that takes longer is one the client falls back on, at worst posted twice. Half an hour of busy answers is far beyond a full queue's minute, so a relay that keeps answering busy only delays the fallback (T-25).
- **`POSTING_HEAD_AGE`.** As the route's index lag: a minute is thirty L2 blocks on Base, far beyond a live node's lag, and small against `POSTING_SEND_MARGIN`'s two hours, so a send on a head that old lands well within the margin the cutoff leaves.
- **`POSTING_FORCE_AFTER`.** Ten minutes is far beyond a live sequencer's inclusion time and short against `F` (13 hours), so a censored posting is forced with most of its window left.
- **`POSTING_DEPOSIT_SPAN` and `POSTING_DEPOSIT_PATIENCE`.** Five minutes is twenty-five L1 blocks and two replacements (`2 · POSTING_RESEND` each) for the deposit, and the next five for its cancellation, which at 21,000 gas can pay up to twice the L1 fee cap per gas. So in the ordinary case every landing time is inside a window the worker checked against `c_F` and every Capacity interval before signing, and a deposit mined late doesn't take gas a trustee's capacity check counted on (Known-weak says when it can). A posting first sent at its send cutoff could only be deposited from `c_D − POSTING_SEND_MARGIN + POSTING_FORCE_AFTER + POSTING_DEPOSIT_SPAN`, about `F` past `c_F`, so only a posting sent well before `c_F` is forced, as Known-weak says.
- **`POSTING_INDEX_LAG`.** Six hours is far beyond the registry sync's ordinary lag behind the output roots it proves the registry's logs under (an hour or so on Base), so a working index never stops the forced path, and well within the 34 hours from a registration to its election's first interval less a landing span and the sequencer's drift, about 33 hours: a registration the index hasn't caught up with can't hide an interval a deposit signed now meets.
- **`POSTING_GAS_HEADROOM` and `POSTING_GAS_LIMIT_MAX`.** A deposit's gas limit is fixed when it is sent on L1, against cold state on L2 ([[delivery]], Capacity), so the call's limit sits a quarter above what the simulation used, and never below the portal's minimum for its data, below which a deposit reverts on L1. Calldata alone costs up to 40 gas a byte under EIP-7623's floor: about 3.5 million for the largest pair this spec defines (87,492 bytes) and 4.8 million for `POSTING_MAX_DATA`. The largest version-3 ballot alone costs about 2.9 million as a transaction ([[contracts]], Board, Gas), and a posting with its ballot check about 434,000 to 544,000 at ordinary sizes. The board's contract test measures the largest pair, a version-2 posting at `MAX_OPTIONS` with version 4's largest record, cold as a deposit runs it: at most about 5.7 million gas as a transaction, its membership proof's pairing included (about 5.4 million in the board's own call; the test's caller adds its own copying), so its gas limit is at most about 7.1 million (`packages/contracts`, `test_TheLargestPairFitsThePostingGasLimit`). Version 5's record, four bytes longer, isn't on the board yet; its calldata pads to the same length, and its test re-measures the pair once it is. Ten million covers it with room, and stays within the 20 million a deposit may use in one L1 block ([[delivery]], Capacity).

## Known-weak

- **The relay reads `m_i` before the chain** (T-15): a relay that checks a posting and never sends it has read that seat's share, which the voter agreed to make public but which stays the operator's alone if the voter then declines. The client says so when it offers the fallback (Outcome); fewer than `k` seats read open nothing ([[tally]], Residuals).
- **Our proxy and hosting see each request whole** (T-11, T-69): the voter's network address beside the ballot, as the ballot route sees it when the same client sends that ballot. Nothing of ours keeps it by design.
- **The relay can refuse or delay** (T-25): it is indistinguishable from one whose checks failed. The voter's own wallet is the answer (part 2d); until it is built, a refused voter can only try again or leave the ballot.
- **No forced path after `c_F`, or inside any election's Capacity interval**: a posting that can't be deposited then rides the sequencer alone through the relay; a censoring sequencer leaves it to the voter's own wallet, whose deposit Capacity doesn't count (T-50, T-54).
- **A deposit can land late** (T-54, T-50): a deposit's lateness is bounded only by its cancellation being mined. A signed deposit is public once broadcast, and pays far more in total than its cancellation, so a builder or proposer that kept it, profit-seeking or a state's, can include it rather than the cancellation, past the landing span and inside an interval that starts after it or past `c_F`. While the worker or its L1 node is down no cancellation is sent, and while L1's base fee stays above the cancellation's fees neither can be mined until it falls. The span makes this the exception, never the rule; the client's own rules still hold, since a late deposit only delays its fallback to `c_P` (Outcome).
- **One unreadable registration stops every deposit** (T-54): an election whose definition the worker can't read might have its intervals anywhere, so nothing is deposited until it can, and postings then ride the sequencer alone. The worker reports it, so the operator can pin what it needs.
- **The index's progress is read from the worker's L2 node** (T-54, T-25): a node that answers a fresh finalized head but leaves a `DefinitionRegistered` event out of its logs lets a stalled index pass the check, and an election registered since isn't weighed, so a deposit can land inside its interval. The same node answers the safe head the deposits judge inclusion by, so a lying L2 node can already keep the relay from forcing; this lets it, together with an index that stopped, misplace a deposit too. A node whose finalized head stops, honest or not, is caught by the head's age.
- **Every first send reads the L1 node** (T-15, T-52): a deposit's reserve is priced from the L1 node's estimate at the first send, so an L1 node that fails fails every send, the ordinary path's included, and a lying one can answer an estimate up to `2²⁴` gas, whose reserve can hold the election's sends `budget`. The estimate carries the posting's calldata, `m_i` included, so the L1 provider reads it before the chain holds it, beside the L2 provider and the relay itself.
- **A nonce whose receipt is never seen counts at its reserve** (T-52): a nonce the count passed at the finalized head with no receipt of its transactions seen is taken as landed and counted at its reserve, the most it can have cost, so its posting isn't forced again even if it was the cancellation that was mined; the posting then rides the sequencer and the client's own rules (Outcome). The key is used for nothing else, so only a node that lost the receipt leads here.
- **A deposit that lands but fails on L2 is not sent again**: its call can fail when it runs, up to `F` after its L1 block (the board's checks against later state, say); the client sees no event by that L1 time plus `F` and falls back to the voter's own wallet (Outcome).
- **Deposits can crowd the portal before an interval** (T-54): the landing span bars overlap only. Under full censorship every posting in flight is deposited, each up to `POSTING_GAS_LIMIT_MAX`, five times the portal's per-block target; a burst ending just before an interval leaves the deposit market's price raised as the seats' forced records begin, decaying by at most an eighth a block. The budget bounds how much the relay can deposit, and one pending at a time paces it to at most one deposit an L1 block, still up to five times the target. Pacing the relay's deposits to the target, or a quiet margin before each interval, is P2-8's to build after part 3b.
- **The deposit market can price a posting out** (T-50): the portal burns L1 gas in proportion to `g` and the deposit market's price, which anyone can raise by filling the market, so a deposit whose L1 transaction would pass EIP-7825's cap, or the budget, isn't sent, leaving the voter's own wallet; a price that rises after the estimate reverts the deposit on L1, which is counted and may be sent again.
- **A budget can be spent by a few** (T-13, T-52): members who make up ballots can spend an election's budget on postings within the caps, sending later voters to their own wallets, whose address is then on L2 beside their ballot.
- **`POSTING_GAS_LIMIT_MAX` is measured against stub registries**: the contract test reads the election's parameters and both key sets from the board tests' stubs, cold, not from deployed registries, whose reads of the same values cost about the same; the at least 2.9 million between the measured limit and the cap covers any difference. A pair over it is refused (`too-costly`), leaving the voter's own wallet.
- **Postings archive chosen bytes at our expense** (T-17): neither the board nor the public check opens `sealed`, so a voter can put about `64·m + 36` chosen bytes per seat in a posting ([[delivery]], Residuals), and the relay pays for it, within the caps.
- **A posting can be front-run**: anyone who sees the pending call can land the same posting first; the relay's call then repeats it as a no-op ([[contracts]], Board, Repeats) and pays for that, once per posting.
- **Anyone can fill the queue** (T-13, T-52): step 5 checks the posting against the ballot record sent with it, never the ballot's membership proof, so a pair passes with any ballot record whose share hashes its maker chose. The worker's simulation refuses such pairs before any gas is spent, but until then they can hold the queue's `POSTING_QUEUE_MAX` places and each server's `POSTING_MAX_CONCURRENT`, so honest voters get busy answers until `POSTING_PATIENCE` and fall back to their own wallets. The worker's admission (Sending, step 4) refuses such a pair on its next tick, before any node call, so each holds a place for one tick, about two seconds; a filler who keeps sending them keeps places taken only at the rate the route's concurrency and the API's rate limit let them in.
- **Timing at the relay.** The relay sends at once, so a posting's block says when its request came, to within a tick (Sending, What a posting shows).
- **The relay judges its transactions at the latest head** (T-25, T-52): inclusion, a consumed nonce and the posting's event are read from the L2 node's latest head, never the finalized one, and the receipts and the event are separate reads that a reorganisation or an endpoint behind a balancer can answer from different views. A view that loses a landed call can have the relay sign it again at the next nonce, paying for it once more (a no-op repeat), and an event that is later reorganised away leaves a posting with nothing more sent until In flight's finality judges it again. Both are bounded by the rule's "at most once more than it lands" and by the budget; the client's own reading of the board at the finalized head decides what the voter relies on (Outcome).
- **The head's age is judged by the worker's own clock** (T-25): a worker whose clock runs behind takes a stale head as fresh by as much as the clock is off. Its host keeps time by NTP, and a clock that far off also misjudges `POSTING_RESEND` and `POSTING_FORCE_AFTER`.
- **Expiry drops a pending nonce** (T-52): an entry deleted at `t_D` while a transaction of it is still pending frees its nonce, so the next send can sign at it or leave a gap behind it, which the tending of a consumed nonce recovers; that transaction's gas, if it lands, is counted against no budget. Only a posting pending since before its send cutoff, about 15 hours earlier, can be.
- **A pruned entry's L2 spend stays as counted**: an entry deleted on its final event keeps its election's spend at whatever its L2 transactions were counted at, its most cost if none was seen in a block, until `t_D`, which can refuse later postings `budget` early but never pays twice. Its deposit's counted reserve is released once no deposit or cancellation of it is pending (Depositing).
- **Retention needs a moving finalized head** (T-18): while the L2's finalized head stalls or can't be read, entries outlive `t_D`, `m_i` with them.
- **The spend isn't a hard bound**: the L1 data fee is an estimate at the send, and a receipt's L1 fee can be higher, so an election's recorded spend can pass its budget by that difference.
