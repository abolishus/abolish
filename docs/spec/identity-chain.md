# Identity chain

What a client proves about its document before a seat answers its Tier 2 query ([[0010-tier2-identity-provider]], Consequences; its Amendment of part 2b-4d): the five sub-proofs of zkPassport's circuits that end in the query's `comm_in`, the anchor sets their root comes from, the pinned circuit set, the proof date and the two predicates, the identity-chain record (record type `0x0024`, version 1) that carries them, and the identity-chain check a seat makes of it. The query and its check are [[nullifier-query]]'s; this page fills the step that page leaves to part 2b-4, so that the `x` a query blinds is the private nullifier of a document an anchor's issuer signed. Notation and decoding rules are from [[notation]]. Threats: T-03, T-05, T-06, T-13, T-15, T-17, T-21.

What this page doesn't specify yet (P3-4 part 2b-4's later steps): how the record travels to the seats with its query ([[nullifier-transport]] seals only the query so far); the concrete circuit set and the issuers' certificates a release pins, with the circuits' sources and keys vendored and the proofs checked as code; the attestation's check of the proof date against the addition's L2 timestamp; and [[verifier]] 2.4. Real documents use none of it before our own app (P5-3, P5-4) and the conditions in [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]]'s Amendment (part 2b-4d) are met.

## The chain

zkPassport splits proving a document into sub-circuits joined by salted commitments: each outputs a commitment that the next takes as its public input and opens again. Five proofs make the chain, all UltraHonk proofs in the zero-knowledge flavour of circuits from the pinned set (Circuit set). Public inputs are listed in the circuit's order, its outputs last:

| Proof         | Circuit                                                       | Public inputs, then outputs                                                                                                           | What it proves                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `dsc`         | Sub-circuit A, `bin/sig-check/dsc/<tbs size>/<algorithm>`     | `certificate_registry_root`; out `comm_dsc`                                                                                           | A CSCA leaf (type 1, country `c`, public key, expiry, fingerprint) is in the certificate tree under `certificate_registry_root`, and that key signed a TBS of at most the bucket's size (Residuals: what that says of the document signer is an open question); `comm_dsc` commits, under a salt, to `c` and that TBS |
| `id`          | Sub-circuit B, `bin/sig-check/id-data/<tbs size>/<algorithm>` | `comm_dsc`; out `comm_id`                                                                                                             | A public key the circuit reads from that TBS signed the SOD's signed attributes; the issuing state in `dg1` is `c`; `comm_id` commits, under a fresh salt, to `c`, the signed attributes, `dg1`, `eContent` and the private nullifier `x = Poseidon2(dg1, eContent, signature)`, which the circuit computes itself    |
| `integrity`   | Sub-circuit C, `bin/data-check/integrity/sa_<hash>/dg_<hash>` | `comm_id`; out `comm_in`                                                                                                              | `dg1`'s hash is in `eContent`, and `eContent`'s hash is in the signed attributes; `comm_in` commits to the five salted hashes ([[nullifier-query]], The query), the fifth in the clear-value form, so it opens to `x`                                                                                                 |
| `age`         | `bin/compare/age/standard`                                    | `comm_in`, `current_date`, `service_scope`, `service_subscope`; out `param_commitment`, `nullifier_type`, `nullifier`, `oprf_pk_hash` | The document isn't expired on `current_date`, and its holder is at least `min_age` years old that day (with `max_age = 0`, no upper bound); `param_commitment` fixes the ages                                                                                                                                         |
| `nationality` | `bin/inclusion-check/nationality/standard`                    | as `age`                                                                                                                              | The document isn't expired on `current_date`, and `dg1`'s nationality, which the circuit refuses when empty, is in the committed country list (padded with empty codes)                                                                                                                                               |

**The fifth slot.** The integrity circuit opens the private nullifier in the clear (`commit_to_disclosure` compares the commitment of B with its value), so `get_hash` takes its clear-value branch and the slot is `Sponge(s_x, x)[0]` with `x` the one B computed. The two disclosure proofs MUST open that slot in the **hash-only form** (`SaltedValue::from_hash`, value 0): `nullify` then outputs `nullifier = 0`, forces `nullifier_secret = 0` and so `oprf_pk_hash = 0`, and reports `NONE_NULLIFIER` (4), or `NON_SALTED_MOCK_NULLIFIER` (2) for a ZKR document. In the clear-value form the age and nationality proofs would output zkPassport's non-salted nullifier `Poseidon2(x, service_scope, service_subscope)`, which anyone holding the chip data recomputes and which the owner ruled PII ([[0010-tier2-identity-provider]], Owner decision): a seat would see it beside the query's identity commitment. The query circuit opens the same slot in the clear-value form, and only it sees `x`.

**`dg1` is opened in the clear.** Both disclosure circuits read the clear `dg1` (its nationality, birth and expiry dates). A hash-only `dg1` carries the value as zeros, and an all-zero `dg1` fails them: the nationality circuit refuses an empty nationality, and the date parser (`noir-date`) requires ASCII digits. So the predicates are about the `dg1` that the chain's commitment holds.

**Requirements on the disclosure proofs.** `service_scope = service_subscope = 0`, so no client-chosen value reaches a seat; `current_date` is the record's `proof_date` in both; `param_commitment` is, for `age`, `P_age = Sponge(1, 2, 18, 0)[0]` (`PROOF_TYPE_AGE`, two parameters, `min_age = 18`, `max_age = 0`), and for `nationality`, `P_nat(c) = Sponge(4, 200, code(c), 0, …, 0)[0]` over 202 inputs (`PROOF_TYPE_NATIONALITY_INCLUSION`, a list of 200 codes, the deployment's issuer `c` first and the empty code after it), with `code(c)` the issuer's three ASCII letters read big-endian (`0x555341` for `USA`, `0x5a4b52` for `ZKR`).

**What the issuer is.** The country in B is `dg1`'s issuing state, and it must equal the country of the CSCA leaf in A, since both enter the same commitment. A deployment's anchor sets hold only leaves of its issuer (Anchor sets), so the chain proves the issuing state as a predicate without disclosing it; the nationality proof adds the holder's nationality, which isn't the same fact (a state can issue passports to nationals of another, and a US passport also goes to US non-citizen nationals, Residuals).

**Salts.** `comm_dsc`, `comm_id` and `comm_in` hide the document only if their salts do. The client MUST draw every salt of a chain (`salt` of A, `salt_out` of B, and C's slot salts, the `dg2` hash and its type sharing one, and its expiry salt) uniformly from its CSPRNG, fresh for each chain, as [[nullifier-query]] already requires of `s_x`. A chain is never reused across queries: the seats would link them by `comm_in`.

## Anchor sets

An anchor set is a list of an issuer's CSCA certificates and a validity interval `[valid_from, valid_until)`, both UTC midnights (multiples of 86,400). Its root is the value sub-circuit A takes as `certificate_registry_root`: zkPassport's version-1 certificate registry root, computed over our leaves only, never read from zkPassport's registry or any operator's list (T-21, T-06).

- **Leaf** (`calculate_certificate_registry_leaf_v1`): `Sponge(0, 0, 0, pack(1 ‖ c ‖ u32(not_after)), fingerprint, pack_31(public_key))[0]`, with the three tag limbs 0, certificate type 1 (CSCA), the issuer's code `c` (3 ASCII bytes), `not_after` the certificate's `validity.notAfter` in Unix seconds (below `2³²`), `pack` reading those 8 bytes as one big-endian integer, and `pack_31` splitting the key bytes, read as one big-endian integer, into 31-byte limbs, the least significant first (`pack_be_bytes_into_fields`).
- **Public key bytes** (`publicKeyToBytes`): for RSA, the modulus big-endian in exactly the key's length in bytes; for ECDSA, `x ‖ y`, each big-endian, padded to the curve's field length. These are the bytes sub-circuit A's key input takes, whose width the circuit fixes.
- **Fingerprint:** `int(H(DS("abolish/v1/identity-chain/csca", der))) >> 8`, over the certificate's DER encoding, so `fingerprint < p`. zkPassport's circuits bind the fingerprint into the leaf but never compare it with anything, so its definition is ours: it names which certificate a leaf stands for, so that a third party recomputes the root from the certificates alone.
- **Certificate tree:** the leaves sorted ascending as integers, in a binary Poseidon2 tree of height 16 (`2¹⁶` leaves at most) whose empty slots hold 0 and whose nodes are `Sponge(left, right)[0]` (`buildMerkleTreeFromCerts`, `compute_merkle_root`). Two certificates with the same leaf are refused when a set is built.
- **Root** (`calculate_certificate_root_v1`): `Sponge(pack(u16(1) ‖ u32(valid_from)), Sponge(cert_root, rev_root, ml_root)[0])[0]` with schema version 1, `valid_from` as the timestamp, and `rev_root` and `ml_root` the roots of empty trees of heights 14 and 8 (no revocation or master-list entries: sub-circuit A binds both roots but checks neither, Residuals).

Each certificate of a set is valid over the whole interval: `not_before ≤ valid_from` and `valid_until − 1 ≤ not_after`. A CSCA that expires, or is withdrawn, leaves the next set; an issuer's sets never overlap (each `valid_from` at or after the previous set's `valid_until`), so a proof date names one set, an expired or withdrawn CSCA isn't reachable through an older set still covering the date, and the set a record names says nothing beyond its date. A set's certificates are an issuer's CSCAs, self-signed and link certificates alike, and only that issuer's: a deployment of issuer `USA` pins only sets of US CSCAs ([[0010-tier2-identity-provider]], Trust anchors), and a MOCK deployment only sets of zkPassport's ZKR test CSCAs, which no `USA` set holds. The release pins each set by its root and interval and archives its certificates' DER encodings with the board ([[0005-permanent-archive]]), so anyone recomputes the root; which sets a release pins, and from which publication of the US CSCAs, is a later part's.

The client builds the leaf's path from the set's certificates, which are public; the path, the leaf and the CSCA are private inputs of A, so a seat learns which set, and which CSCA only through `dsc_key`, whose circuit names the CSCA's signature algorithm, key size and hash: where a set's CSCAs differ in those, the key singles one out (What it reveals to a seat).

## Circuit set

zkPassport generates one A and one B binary per signature algorithm, key size and TBS size bucket (566 in all at `c7da418`) and one C per pair of hash algorithms (25). The release pins, for each of the roles `dsc`, `id` and `integrity`, a list of verification keys, and one key for each of `age` and `nationality`, each named by the SHA-256 of its bytes as the query and output keys are ([[nullifier-query]], Keys and proofs). A circuit outside its role's list is refused, whatever its source; a changed list is a new protocol version, never a parameter, and neither a definition's author nor zkPassport's circuit registry chooses it (T-05, T-06).

- **Built by us.** Each key is `bb write_vk` of the circuit compiled from zkPassport's source at the pinned commit by the toolchain this repository pins (Noir and bb, `AGENTS.md`), with that source and every Nargo dependency it reaches (`noir_rsa`, `noir-bignum`, `noir-ecdsa`, `noir_bigcurve`, `noir-date`, the SHA-2, Poseidon and OPRF libraries among them, which zkPassport pins only by tag) vendored by commit and tree hash and listed, with bb's `noir-recursive` target in the zero-knowledge flavour, as the query and output circuits are. zkPassport's own published keys, its circuit registry root and its outer aggregation circuit aren't used: the seats verify each sub-proof directly, so no recursive verifier and no registry root enters soundness, and zkPassport's own bb version doesn't matter.
- **The algorithm floor.** A role's list holds only circuits whose signature has at least 112-bit security and doesn't hash with SHA-1: RSA (PKCS#1 v1.5 or PSS) of 2048 bits or more, ECDSA over curves of 256 bits or more (stricter than 112 bits asks, leaving out P-224 and brainpoolP224r1), signature hashes from SHA-256 up; and integrity circuits whose two hashes are from SHA-224 up. The pinned set is the subset of those that the pinned issuer's CSCAs and document signers use; a document outside it can't register, and widening the floor is a spec change.
- **One proof format.** Every proof is an UltraHonk proof under its key, with its public inputs supplied by the checker in the order above, each a `field<bn254>`, never by the prover; the proof length and element rules of [[nullifier-query]] (Keys and proofs) apply to each, and a later part confirms them for each pinned circuit with a proof round trip.

## Proof date

`proof_date` is the `current_date` both disclosure proofs were made for, in Unix seconds, and a UTC midnight. The disclosure circuits read it as a day (`Date::from_timestamp`), so a time of day would add nothing to what they prove and would let a seat tell clients apart. A seat accepts it only within a day of its own UTC day: `today − 86,400 ≤ proof_date ≤ today + 86,400`, with `today` its clock's last midnight. That bounds how stale a chain can be when a seat answers, so a chain made while a document was valid can't be kept for later, and allows the clocks' disagreement around midnight. The window has two edges a seat accepts knowingly: a holder who turns 18 tomorrow can register today with a proof dated tomorrow, and a document whose last valid day was yesterday still proves with a proof dated yesterday. Both are a day's slack on an age and an expiry the issuer set, not a second identity (T-03).

At `proof_date` the document is unexpired, its holder is 18 or older and the anchor set covers it; the attesters check `proof_date` again against the addition's L2 timestamp ([[0010-tier2-identity-provider]], Proof date and Validity over time; a later part).

## Record

Record type `0x0024`, version 1. Never on the board, the chain or the archive, and never pinned by a profile: the Tier 2 key fixes its version (versions 1 and 2 of the key transcript, [[nullifier-key]], and version 1 of the reshare transcript, [[nullifier-reshare]], fix version 1), as it fixes the query's.

| Field               | Type           | Meaning                                                          |
| ------------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `proof_date`        | `u64`          | The disclosure proofs' `current_date` (Proof date)               |
| `anchor_root`       | `field<bn254>` | The root of the anchor set the `dsc` proof used                  |
| `dsc_key`           | `bytes[32]`    | SHA-256 of the `dsc` proof's verification key                    |
| `comm_dsc`          | `field<bn254>` | The `dsc` proof's output                                         |
| `dsc_proof`         | `bytes<65536>` | The `dsc` proof                                                  |
| `id_key`            | `bytes[32]`    | SHA-256 of the `id` proof's verification key                     |
| `comm_id`           | `field<bn254>` | The `id` proof's output                                          |
| `id_proof`          | `bytes<65536>` | The `id` proof                                                   |
| `integrity_key`     | `bytes[32]`    | SHA-256 of the `integrity` proof's verification key              |
| `integrity_proof`   | `bytes<65536>` | The `integrity` proof, whose output is the query's `comm_in`     |
| `age_proof`         | `bytes<65536>` | The `age` proof, under the release's one age key                 |
| `nationality_proof` | `bytes<65536>` | The `nationality` proof, under the release's one nationality key |

The record doesn't repeat `comm_in`: it belongs to the query it accompanies, and the check takes it from there. With five proofs of 14,656 bytes a record is 73,503 bytes.

## Identity-chain check

A seat checks the record that accompanies a query after the query check has passed, proof included ([[nullifier-query]], Query check), and answers only if both pass. It checks against its own clock, the release's pins and the query, never against values the record supplies for them. In this order, the first failure is the result:

| Code                | Check                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| decode codes        | The record decodes strictly as version 1 ([[notation]], Strict decoding).                                                              |
| `chain-date`        | `proof_date` is a multiple of 86,400 and within a day of the seat's UTC day (Proof date).                                              |
| `chain-anchor`      | `anchor_root` is the root of an anchor set the release pins for the deployment's issuer, with `valid_from ≤ proof_date < valid_until`. |
| `chain-circuit`     | `dsc_key`, `id_key` and `integrity_key` are each in the pinned list of its own role.                                                   |
| `chain-dsc`         | `dsc_proof` verifies under the key named `dsc_key` with public inputs `anchor_root`, `comm_dsc`.                                       |
| `chain-id`          | `id_proof` verifies under the key named `id_key` with public inputs `comm_dsc`, `comm_id`.                                             |
| `chain-integrity`   | `integrity_proof` verifies under the key named `integrity_key` with public inputs `comm_id` and the query's `comm_in`.                 |
| `chain-age`         | `age_proof` verifies under the pinned age key with public inputs `comm_in`, `proof_date`, 0, 0, `P_age`, `t`, 0, 0.                    |
| `chain-nationality` | `nationality_proof` verifies under the pinned nationality key with public inputs `comm_in`, `proof_date`, 0, 0, `P_nat(c)`, `t`, 0, 0. |

`c` is the deployment's issuer and `t` its nullifier type in zkPassport's numbering: 4 (`NONE_NULLIFIER`) for issuer `USA`, 2 (`NON_SALTED_MOCK_NULLIFIER`) for a MOCK deployment, whose issuer is `ZKR`. The Tier 2 nullifier type is fixed per deployment ([[vouching]], MOCK Tier 2 members; [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]], Nullifier uniqueness and Mock documents), so a seat never accepts both. Each proof check applies [[nullifier-query]]'s length and element rules before verifying. The proofs come last because they are the costly checks, and the chain's three links before the disclosure proofs, since a disclosure proof over a `comm_in` that no valid chain produced shows nothing.

**What it proves.** If every check passes, then, under the soundness of the pinned circuits and of UltraHonk: a CSCA of a pinned set, so of the deployment's issuer and valid on `proof_date`, signed a TBS from which the circuit read a public key that signed this document's SOD (how much that says about the document signer is the open question under Residuals); the document's issuing state is the issuer; on `proof_date` the document was unexpired, its holder 18 or older and of the issuer's nationality; and the query's `comm_in` opens to that document's private nullifier `x`, which the query circuit then blinds. So one document gives one `x`, and the panel one `N` for it ([[nullifier-query]], What it proves), given what [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]]'s Amendment (part 2b-4d) and [[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]]'s Amendment leave open (T-03, T-21).

**What it reveals to a seat.** The anchor set (shared by every document it covers), `proof_date` (a day), the three salted commitments (uniform while their salts are), and the circuits: the CSCA's signature algorithm, key size and hash (from the `dsc` key, which names the CSCA where a set's CSCAs differ in them), the document signer's signature algorithm, key size and TBS size bucket, and the SOD's two hash algorithms. Those partition the issuer's documents by the signer generation that issued them, a coarse fact of the issuing period that a seat sees next to the query's identity commitment (T-17, T-13). The proofs are zero-knowledge only computationally, and their witnesses hold `dg1`, the SOD and the document signer's certificate; a seat that kept them and later broke the assumption would learn the document. Seats delete the record with its query once they have answered or refused, as [[nullifier-query]] requires of queries (T-15).

## Residuals

- **Document signers aren't checked for validity or revocation.** Sub-circuit A proves a CSCA signed some certificate, and B that its key signed the SOD; neither reads the signer certificate's validity period, nor any revocation list (zkPassport's root binds a revocation tree that A never opens). So a document signed by a revoked or expired document signer still proves. [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]]'s condition that validity come from the archived revocation lists is met for CSCAs, through the anchor sets, and not for document signers; its Amendment (part 2b-4d) keeps real documents out until our own app proves it (T-03, T-21).
- **What A and B establish about the document signer is an open question.** It is recorded with the soundness question on zkPassport's sub-circuits that [[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]]'s Amendment holds for the owner, with details withheld until the owner decides on disclosure. Until it is settled the chain doesn't establish, for real documents, that a document signer the issuer certified signed the document, and [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]]'s option B (Amendment, part 2b-4d) must establish it (T-03, T-05).
- **Nationality isn't citizenship.** A US passport records `USA` as the nationality of US non-citizen nationals too, and `dg1` carries no field that tells them apart, so a deployment's Tier 2 admits US nationals; a citizenship-only electorate needs a predicate no document carries (T-03).
- **Ages of 100 and over.** `dg1` holds a two-digit birth year, which the age circuit reads against `proof_date`, so a holder aged 100 or more reads as a child and can't register (coverage, not soundness).
- **Passive authentication only, and the sub-circuits' soundness.** As [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]] and [[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]]'s Amendment record: cloned chip data proves, and zkPassport's circuits are unaudited, with a question on them held for the owner (T-05, T-03).

## Test vectors

`docs/spec/vectors/identity-chain.json` ([[vectors/README|vectors]]): zkPassport's own fixtures for the leaf, the zero values, the tree and the root, reproduced first; the empty revocation and master-list roots; three anchor sets (two consecutive `USA` sets, one holding a CSCA that expires at the first's end, and a `ZKR` set) over stand-in certificates, with each certificate's fingerprint and leaf, each set's tree root and root; `P_age`, `P_nat(USA)` and `P_nat(ZKR)`; records for a `USA` and a MOCK deployment, accepted with proof dates of today, yesterday and tomorrow and on a set's last day, and one with a proof of exactly 65,536 bytes, with every proof's public inputs as the check supplies them; and records failing each code that comes before the proofs: undecodable ones (a trailing byte, a truncated record, version 2, another record type, `anchor_root`, `comm_dsc` and `comm_id` at or above `p`, a proof over its bound with its bytes present), `chain-date` (not a midnight, two days old, two days ahead), `chain-anchor` (an unknown root, the next set's root before its interval, a set's root on its `valid_until`, a ZKR set on a `USA` deployment and the reverse) and `chain-circuit` (an unpinned `dsc`, `id` and `integrity` key, and a key of another role in each of the three). The certificates' DER is labelled test bytes and their keys zkPassport's test keys, the circuit keys are hashes of labels, and the proofs are 96-byte stand-ins: checking real proofs is a later part's. They are printed by `packages/crypto/scripts/identity-chain-vectors.py`, which reuses `keyed-nullifier-hashes-vectors.py`'s Poseidon2 sponge and shares nothing with `packages/crypto`.
