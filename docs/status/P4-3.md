- State: in progress (part 1, content addressing, merged in #179; part 2a, the board data availability ADR, merged in #195; part 2b-1, checkpoint version 2 and the anchor, merged in #202; part 2b-2a, reading segment objects within a bound and the board from L1's CIDs in `packages/crypto`, merged in #315; part 2b-2b, the verifier's sources over the network and its bundle, next)

## Scope

IPFS mirroring of board + ballot client, plus the permanent archive per ADR.

## Plan

1. **Content addressing** (#179): [[content-addressing]]'s CIDs and CARs in `packages/crypto`, with vectors from two independent IPFS implementations, so the parameters stop being provisional.
2. **Board data availability** ([[0029-board-data-availability]], option D): 2a, the ADR (#195); 2b-1, checkpoint version 2 with the segment's CIDs and the new anchor, with P4-1 (#202); 2b-2, the verifier's fetch by CID and the reference election's rebuild from L1 CIDs, in three: 2b-2a, `packages/crypto` reading a segment object within its checkpoints' bound and a board copy from L1's CIDs, over sources that may wait on the network (#315); 2b-2b, the verifier's sources (a local block store and HTTP trustless gateways) and a bundle that takes no `board.bin`; 2b-2c, the reference election's rebuild from L1's CIDs; 2c, availability attestations, `t_A` and the verifier's `unavailable` check (the L2 call with P1-18); 2d, the reference election's withholding run. Originally: make a withheld anchored extent attributable ([[board]], Residuals, withheld tails): a content address per anchored segment bound into what is anchored, and a publication deadline, so a node or verifier can tell non-publication from absence. An ADR first (what is anchored changes, and with it P4-1's contract). It also closes [[P1-12c]]'s residual on a newest ballot that never reached the board, and makes receipts' `unresolved` evidence attributable ([[receipts]], Residuals).
3. **Archive manifests and their Arweave layout** ([[0005-permanent-archive]], Consequences), with an Arweave test harness for the reference election. The first real upload spends money and waits for the owner.
4. **Mirroring**: the worker pins every segment object, entry-hash list and display-text record on IPFS, and the ballot client's releases; community nodes pin by default (P4-5).

## What was built (part 1)

- `packages/crypto`: `contentDag` (fixed 1 MiB raw leaves, a balanced tree of dag-pb UnixFS `File` nodes of at most 1,024 links, every block once in depth-first pre-order), `objectCid`, `cidText` (base32, `b` prefix) and `objectCar` (CARv1). dag-pb, UnixFS and the dag-cbor CAR header are written by hand, so the package keeps to `@noble/*`.
- [[content-addressing]]: the parameters are no longer provisional; how to set Kubo and ipfs-unixfs-importer to them (Kubo's default is 174 links per node, not 1,024); the encodings byte for byte; Test vectors.
- `docs/spec/vectors/content-addressing.json` from `packages/crypto/scripts/content-addressing-vectors.mjs`, run outside the workspace: each CID from Kubo 0.36.0 (its release tarball's sha512 recorded and checked) and from ipfs-unixfs-importer 17.1.1, and each CAR from Kubo's `dag export`, matched by @ipld/car 5.4.7 over the JavaScript blocks in pre-order; refused unless they agree byte for byte.
- T-51 history.

## Tested (part 1)

- 15 vectors: one byte, 100 bytes, exactly one chunk, one chunk and a byte, three chunks, two identical chunks of zeros (one leaf, carried once), and under a small layout profile (256-byte chunks, 4 links) trees of 1, 4, 5, 16, 17, 21, 64 and 65 leaves, with partial groups at every level, and 17 identical chunks. Each CID and CAR is reproduced byte for byte (small CARs whole, larger ones by length and sha256).
- Properties (`FC_NUM_RUNS`): an object of at most one chunk is one raw block whose CID hashes it; every block is listed once with the root first and each CID hashing its block; any changed byte changes the CID. Refusals: an empty object, limits outside the layout.

## Decided (part 2a)

[[0029-board-data-availability]]: option D, `Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`.

- **Checkpoint version 2** names the segment object's and entry-hash list's CIDs on L1, with CID rules the authority fails on. The verifier can then fetch from chain + IPFS alone.
- **The trustee panels are the availability committee, before any result.** Each panel's threshold of seats attests, through the L2 contract path only, that it held the ballot extent (the board through `period(closes_at) + 1`) by `c_I`, and the counted extent before any summed share. Otherwise the election fails `unavailable`, listing who attested, complained or did nothing.
- **Rejected or deferred.** A reachability-judged deadline is rejected (verdicts would depend on the verifier's network, T-53). Archive listing alone is rejected (the authority can list and withhold). EIP-4844 blobs are kept as a later upgrade for entry-hash lists (capacity and cost rule out payloads).

Known-weak (part 2a):

- Nothing is built yet.
- Under the transitional panel, the attestation is the operator's own, so [[P1-12c]]'s residual stays until [[P1-7]] names independent trustees, or the owner accepts it for those polls.
- The tail after the counted extent (openings, tally, destruction statements) stays unverifiable, never failed, when withheld: a quorum there would be a veto over the result (T-54).
- Attest-then-withhold leaves the election unverifiable, with the attesters named.
- A colluding panel threshold can attest to withheld data, as it can open ballots.
- Trustees fetch on a deadline: up to about 20 hours for the ballot extent under the deployed constants. Part 2c adds the allowance to `deliveryWindowFits`; a longer `DELIVERY_WINDOW` would move `COMPLAINT_WINDOW` and the seal later.
- The verdict depends on the L2's history staying retrievable (as every contract event does), and the tally window shrinks by the counted extent's fetch allowance plus `F + R`.
- Under a live poll, a panel threshold that dislikes the running trend can refuse to attest, as it can refuse the tally.
- A board-path ballot first boarded after the ballot extent is never counted: a voter whose newest ballot the operator holds back keeps the older one counted and must direct-submit to change it.

## Independent review (part 2a)

An in-session subagent reviewed the first draft against `.github/review/crypto-review.md` and found four blocking issues, all fixed by redesigning when the attestations happen:

- **Timing.** The draft had seats attest the seal by `t_A ≤ c_I`, but the seal falls about two weeks after close, past the tally and destruction windows. Now the seats attest the ballot extent and the counted extent.
- **Signing key.** The per-election signing key is destroyed before the seal. Both points now come while seats hold it.
- **Veto.** A quorum after the tally would hand trustees a veto over the result, and the draft blamed the authority for it. Both points now come before any summed share, and `unavailable` lists who attested, complained or did nothing, never naming one party.
- **Evidence.** Attestations after the seal had no anchored evidence path. Both points now come before the seal, with a forced-inclusion margin.

A second pass on the revision found two more blocking issues, both fixed:

- **The ballot extent's timing.** Direct-submitted ballots reach the board only after `W_C` plus anchoring, about 26 hours after close, past `c_I − F`. The extent is now the board through `s_A = period(closes_at) + 1`, due about 14 hours after close; nodes read direct-submitted ballots from their own L2 node. Seats attest to holding it by `c_I`, recorded by `c_I + F`, leaving up to about 20 hours to fetch.
- **The verdict without the board.** Attestations carried on the board would be withheld along with it. Attestations and complaints now count only through the L2 contract path, the verifier decides `unavailable` from L1 and the L2's events alone, and `unavailable` takes precedence over a missing copy's unverifiable verdicts.

A third, focused pass found one more blocking gap, fixed: nothing marked a board-path ballot first boarded after `s_A` as late (5.6 covers only direct-submitted ballots, `tally-late` only entries after `s_S`). An operator could board a voter's newest ballot after `c_I` but by `s_S`, where it would be selected, undelivered. Such a ballot is now never selected and is attributed to the operator. Also applied from that pass:

- qualified seats are read from the L2's registration events;
- `unavailable` is returned only over an accumulator-proven replay, otherwise the result is unverifiable;
- the L2 retention wording;
- the spec amendments part 2c carries.

Applied from the second pass's non-blocking findings:

- the counted extent's deadline `t_S + A' + F`;
- seats read the quorum from their own L2 node and finalized L1;
- attesting seats serve from the moment they attest;
- the live-poll caveat;
- late boarding after `s_A` left to 5.6 and `tally-late`;
- the Known-weak additions.

Non-blocking, applied:

- the CID rules (canonical CIDs, agreement with the root and with each other, fresh or carried over with `size`);
- seats bound to publish what they attested;
- refusal of repeats and unregistered seats;
- the gas and blob figures;
- the fetch allowance in the window bound;
- T-54 history;
- the attest-then-withhold residual.

## What was built (part 2b-1)

- [[board]]: checkpoint version 2, `election_id ‖ u32(segment) ‖ u64(size) ‖ head ‖ root ‖ object_cid ‖ hashes_cid` (180 bytes), hashed under `abolish/v1/board-checkpoint-2`. Version 1 was never anchored, so it is gone from the code and its tag retired ([[domain-separation]]). The CIDs are those of the segment object and entry-hash list of `t`, the segment of entry `size − 1`: fresh when the period added entries, carried over otherwise. Board checks: step 2 also fails two checkpoints of one size that differ in a CID (`checkpoint-inconsistent`, from L1 alone), and step 5 adds `checkpoint-cid` after `checkpoint-boundary`, an election failure attributed to the anchoring authority, since it runs only on the authenticated prefix.

- `packages/verifier`: format-1 bundles' anchors carry `objectCid` and `hashesCid`, given checkpoints are 180 bytes, and `checkpoint-cid` maps to 4.1 pass, 4.2 fail ([[verifier]]).

## Tested (part 2b-1)

- `board-vectors.py`, independent of `packages/crypto`, computes every CID from the raw entries (single raw blocks; the multi-block layout is [[content-addressing]]'s vectors') and every expected result; the TypeScript board checks, `copyCheckpoints` and the Solidity hash reproduce them.

## Known-weak (part 2b-1)

- The CIDs name what was withheld, but nothing fails the election for withholding until part 2c's attestations; until then [[board]]'s withheld-tail residual stands.
- Nothing fetches by CID yet: the verifier still reads `board.bin` (part 2b-2), and nothing pins (part 4).
- The anchor checks only the CIDs' framing; whether a digest names the right bytes is known only to a verifier holding the board (`checkpoint-cid`). An authority can anchor a well-framed CID of other bytes and fail the election, as it already can with a wrong head or root (T-53).

## What was built (part 2b-2a)

- [[content-addressing]], Reading within a bound: an object whose length only a bound fixes takes its length from its root (a raw root's own length up to 1 MiB, a dag-pb root's `filesize` over 1 MiB), refused over the bound before any other block is asked for, then read as an object of that length, the root not asked for again.
- [[board]], A copy from L1's CIDs: the counted checkpoints' distinct sizes cut the board into runs; each run's `object_cid` is read within `(n_i − n_{i−1}) · (51 + MAX_BOARD_PAYLOAD)` bytes, once per size, split, and required to be exactly that many entries; the copy is the runs up to the first that fails, and the board checks run on it as on any copy. A Residual: a copy by CID can't show what the anchored CIDs hide.
- `packages/crypto`: the read is one generator walk driven synchronously (`readObject`, `readObjectWithin`) or over a source that waits (`readObjectAsync`, `readObjectWithinAsync`); `readBoardCopy` over an async source. `readObject`'s behaviour is unchanged (the existing tests and part 3a's delivery tests pass as they were).
- T-24, T-49, T-51 and T-53 history.

## Tested (part 2b-2a)

- `content-addressing.json`: every vector read back by its CID alone under its own profile, refused under a bound one byte short after asking only for the root, and under the protocol's limits read only where its DAG is the canonical one.
- Properties (`FC_NUM_RUNS`): within any bound at least its length a read gives the exact read's bytes and asks for exactly its blocks, the root once; below its length it is inconsistent after the root alone; a withheld or altered block is unavailable; a root rehashed with another `filesize`, a dag-pb root over one chunk or less, an empty raw block and a CID of another form are inconsistent; the async readers give the sync readers' results in the same request order, reject on bad arguments and pass a source's error through; a lone hand-built root claiming 2^50 bytes under the small layout or 7 · 2^50 under the protocol's, under a bound of `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`, is checked and found unavailable in milliseconds after two requests (the code before the review's fix runs out of time or memory on it).
- `readBoardCopy` (properties over valid boards): an honest board is read back whole and verifies; each segment object is fetched once however many checkpoints carry its CIDs; a withheld or altered block leaves the copy a whole-run prefix that fails `checkpoint-missing`, never an election failure; a segment left unanchored stops the copy (`not-run`) where the whole board fails `unanchored`; objects that aren't the run (one entry of two, truncated, doubled) are `not-run`, an object one byte over the run's bound is `inconsistent` after its root alone; in a running election, an anchor landing before an earlier period's stops the copy (`not-run`, then `checkpoint-missing`) where the whole board passes; an L1-only failure is thrown before anything is fetched.
- `board.json`: each of the 56 boards read by CID from a mirror holding its own entries' segment objects gives the vector's own verdict, except 20 listed vectors (the mirror holds an altered copy, or the anchored CIDs don't name the runs), which are `checkpoint-missing`, never another verdict.

## Known-weak (part 2b-2a)

- Nothing calls it yet: the verifier still reads `board.bin` (part 2b-2b) and the reference election doesn't rebuild from L1's CIDs (part 2b-2c).
- Where the anchored CIDs don't name the runs their checkpoints bound (a segment left unanchored, a checkpoint inside a segment, a CID of other bytes), the copy by CID stops short and the election is unverifiable from the CIDs, where a whole copy would fail it ([[board]], Residuals); 20 of the 56 board vectors are such boards. Part 2c's attestations make withholding attributable.
- The bound is what a run's entries can encode at `MAX_BOARD_PAYLOAD` each, about 1 MiB per entry, so an anchoring authority can make a reader fetch and hold up to that per run; a mirror can't, since only the anchored CID chooses the length.
- The entry-hash lists aren't read: a client that rebuilds roots from the lists alone ([[receipts]], Entry-hash lists) still needs a reader of its own, with the length fixed at `32 · (n_i − n_{i−1})`.

## Independent review (part 2b-2a)

An in-session subagent reviewed the diff against `.github/review/crypto-review.md` and ran hostile roots against it. One blocking finding, fixed:

- **A root's claim cost what it claimed.** The walk memoised node shapes by position, so checking a lone root claiming a huge `filesize` built a shape per node of the claimed tree, and the first leaf allocated the whole length. An anchoring authority could exhaust any reader rebuilding the board from L1 (2 TiB claimed: 7 s and 1.2 GB; 64 TiB: the process died) with one block. Shapes are now keyed by height, chunks covered and whether the last chunk is among them (at most three per level), and leaves are kept as they hash and joined at the end, so work and memory follow the blocks served. The spec says so, and a test with lone roots claiming `2^50` and `7 · 2^50` bytes fails on the old code.

Non-blocking, applied:

- [[board]]: a CID of other bytes that split into the run's count gives a full copy failing `copy-mismatch`, not a short one, and the same bytes from every source; and an anchored entry over `MAX_BOARD_PAYLOAD` is among the residual's cases.
- [[content-addressing]]: a raw root over 1 MiB is unavailable, never hashed.
- Each answer is copied before it is hashed, so what is checked is what is kept.
- A test of a running election whose later anchor lands before an earlier period's.

Not applied: a bound tighter than `MAX_BOARD_PAYLOAD` per entry (Known-weak above).
