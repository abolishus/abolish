# P2-30: Account-gated Tier 0 registration

- State: in progress (part 1, [[0039-tier0-token-issuance]] and the issuance spec [[tier0-token]] with vectors, merged in #527; part 1b, the review follow-ups (re-issuance after a key's natural expiry, recording a redemption before sending it, the stall guard as a precondition), merged in #530; part 2, the token steps in `packages/crypto`, merged in #539; part 3a, the issuer's record and issuance in `packages/core`, merged in #546; part 3b, the issuance route and its scheduled dead mark, next)
- Branch: `claude/p2-30`
- Phase: Phase 2: Open polls end to end at Tier 0

## Scope

The owner's decision of 2026-10-21 ([[0024-tier0-registration]], Owner decision): voting needs an account, and an account registers at most one Tier 0 voting identity per group, through blind-issued tokens, so the server never learns which identity belongs to which account.

## Rules

- Inside the `crypto-review` gate where it touches the token protocol, `packages/crypto`, `packages/core`'s checks or the ballot client.
- The ballot client blinds and unblinds; `apps/web` only relays with the account's session. Nothing stores or logs an account next to a token's unblinded value, a commitment or its timing; redemptions are delayed and batched so timing can't join them (T-10, T-11).
- The issuer key is rotated by period and its compromise is a new threat entry (it mints Tier 0 identities).

## Plan

1. Spec and vectors: VOPRF issuance and redemption (RFC 9497, as Privacy Pass, RFC 9578) bound to the group and period, with independent vectors.
2. `packages/crypto`: blind, evaluate, unblind, verify.
3. The issuer in `packages/core` and `apps/api`: one token per account (for the group of its region, [[0039-tier0-token-issuance]]), only to an account verified by email and phone ([[P2-31]]); key, rotation, rate limits.
4. `apps/web`'s relay and the ballot client's blinding, the cross-origin handoff, and redemption on the registration request in place of the Turnstile solve.
5. The signing service checks a redeemed token, with delay and batching; the T-10 and T-11 tests.
6. Migration of the registration screens ([[P2-8]]) from Turnstile-only to account-gated.

- **Part 2** (#539): `packages/crypto/src/tier0-token.ts` composes RFC 9497's POPRF steps (Blind, BlindEvaluate with its DLEQ proof, Finalize, Evaluate, batch size 1) from `group.ts`'s wrappers under the RFC's DSTs, as `tests/vectors/rfc.test.ts` does, so the group test's ban on `@noble/curves`' OPRF bundle stands; plus the key identifier and periods, the group `info`, the token input (refusing a forbidden `C` as `forbidden-commitment`, a code the spec and the vectors now list), the redemption's constant-time comparison (`tier0TokenMatches`) and re-issuance's item 3 check (`tier0TokenBlindedFrom`). `Finalize` computes `T` from the published `pkS` and `info` rather than taking it. Tested against `tier0-token.json` (now with the package's own functions in place of `@noble/curves`' bundle), RFC 9497 Appendix A.1.3's vectors 1 and 2, and fast-check properties (completeness, any flipped answer bit fails, other key or group fails, forbidden and non-canonical inputs, key periods, re-issuance). Inverses are Fermat powers (`scalarInverse`), whose control flow depends only on the public exponent, so the issuer's tweaked key, inverted at every redemption, doesn't leak through an extended Euclid's loop count. Known-weak: scalars are bigints, so the issuer's `skS` and the client's `blind` can't be wiped and bigint arithmetic isn't strictly constant time, as elsewhere in the package.
- **Part 3a** (#546): `packages/core/src/tier0-tokens.ts` and migration 0029. `parseTier0IssuerKeys` reads the period keys (`period:hex,…`, each `skS`'s encoding, refused unless a non-zero scalar below `l`, errors naming the entry's position only); `tier0Issuer` takes the deployment's `chain_id`, `group_registry` and region 0's `group_id` and computes `info`. `issueTier0Token` runs Issuance, steps 2 to 4, in the spec's order: `account` (verified by e-mail; the phone joins the check with [[P2-31]] part 3), `key-not-issuing` (a held key of the current period, or the last one's when it is the recorded key, for a retry only), `region-unknown`, `blinded-element`, then, in one transaction with the evaluation, an insert-if-absent on (account, `chain_id`, `group_registry`) into `account_tokens` (account class: region, `key_id`, its public period and `B`; no time) with the count per key and `info` in `tier0_token_issuances` (operational, no account), or a retry of exactly the recorded region, key and `B`; anything else is `token-taken`, and an `inverse` evaluation rolls back the record and the count. The app role may update only a record's key, period and `B`, and can't delete a record (it goes only with its account, by the cascade); `skS` is a non-enumerable field, so no log or `inspect` prints it. `markDeadTier0Tokens` clears the key and period of records whose period ended `q + 1`, keyed on the stored period and the clock, never on which keys the instance holds. Tested against the database: finalised answers equal `Evaluate` at redemption, every refusal, `account` first and a redeeming key's refusal before the region's, retries in `q` and `q + 1` and not after, concurrent first requests with different `B` (one recorded), a redeployed registry, the inverse rollback (by a mocked evaluation), the counts, the dead mark, column privileges, no time column, and the no-join classification.
- **Part 3b must:** serve issuance on `apps/web`'s origin behind the account session, as the e-mail routes are (the account system lives there, never on the API: T-10, [[0017-identity-recovery-backup]]), so the issuer keys sit in `apps/web`'s environment apart from every other secret; run `markDeadTier0Tokens` from the scheduled job; wait for [[P2-31]] part 3 so the route never answers an account without a verified phone, and move the account check into issuance's transaction when the phone joins it; refuse at load a key whose period ended its redemption window, and check each held key against the deployment record's (part 3c); and answer a database error (an account deleted mid-request) generically.
- **Part 3 must** (3c onwards): class the signing service's refusal list (abandoned commitments, no account, time or order) in the no-join tests and build re-issuance's cross-side step as a board-side check-and-mark keyed on the commitment alone (T-10); build re-issuance for a token whose key no longer redeems (retired early or past `q + 1`), against the dead token and its unregistered commitment, shown only by a client that never sent its redemption (and, in parts 4 and 5, that finds `C` in no Tier 0 group addition on chain first, under a cross-tab lock around the sent-record), refusing a commitment ever added to any Tier 0 group (removed leaves included, with a test), with the signing service's list check and queueing serialised with it, and batches counted in flight until final on L2 (with a reorg test) (tests at the natural-expiry boundary too), bound to the account by its stored `B` and `blind`, and replacing the account's record atomically with the queue, with tests for a retirement crossing a redemption in flight, another account's dead token refused and one dead token shown twice refused ([[tier0-token]], Keys); add the issuer keys to the deployment record (a new version of [[deployment]]), decide how many periods ahead a release lists, keep each period's key the same across releases with the verifier's report checking it against the L1 release registry, define moving region, and count redemptions per key and group beside 3a's issuances (T-74).
- **Part 4 must:** in the ballot client, re-issue only a margin past `q + 1` after a natural expiry (at once after a listed early retirement), write the sent-record durably before sending and atomically with `C` and `blind`, hold one lock across tabs around it and re-issuance, read the chain for `C` by light client or several independent RPC endpoints (never Abolish's API), reading every group's addition history in full and never querying one commitment, rebuild a retried re-issuance under the current key after a period change, durably record that a `C` was shown before sending its re-issuance (so it is never redeemed afterwards, whatever storage survives), and, once a re-issuance may have been accepted but its answer was lost, retry with the new request's `key_id'`, `C'` and `blind'` rather than the old dead token's, which no longer match the record ([[tier0-token]], Keys and Redemption; review notes on #530).
- **Part 5 must:** specify and test the client's guard against the issuer's active stall of a quiet region, with a way out of the deadlock a plain join floor causes, bounded inside the key's redemption window ([[tier0-token]], Security notes; a precondition in [[0039-tier0-token-issuance]]); seal the redemption's body to a signing-service key with a hybrid KEM (as [[nullifier-transport]]), so the proxy and platform never see a commitment before its batch and can't shut a one-token account out with a self-paid copy, leaving [[registration]]'s drop rule as it is ([[tier0-token]], Redemption); and test that no record keeps the time of issuance (Postgres's `xmin` included: the row's inserting transaction orders it against timestamped rows, so the scheduled job should rewrite records, as the dead mark does, or the residual stays), and that redemptions are spread over `TOKEN_REDEEM_DELAY`.
- **Known-weak:** the issuer's record is deleted with its account, so account deletion ([[P2-27]]) must keep a deleted account's phone from taking a second token through a new account (T-74). Also: a crash after a redemption is recorded as sent but before it is sent, followed by an absence past `q + 1`, strands the token for good (it counts as sent, so it can't be re-issued): the price of the safe default. Also: one identity per account for good (an identity lost after redemption isn't replaced; a token lost before redemption is replaced only by re-issuance once its key stops redeeming, and only if the client kept `blind` and `C`); tokens can be sold; a new commitment's anonymity set is the region's accounts that took a token in the day before it joined; POPRF rests on 3HashSDHI; the network-level timing of issuance and redemption stays T-11's.
