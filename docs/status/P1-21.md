## Part 2a (#170): the definition and the ceremony

- `packages/sdk` (`simulation/election.ts`): `simulateElection` builds a version-1 election definition from the scenario and its plan (the election type, options and selection range, one electorate entry per tier the plan has, sized by its planned voters, the panel, the pinned membership key), runs the four signed rounds of [[ceremony]] with every seat through `@abolishus/crypto`'s message functions, checks the transcript with `checkCeremonyTranscript` under a context rebuilt from the stub's fields, and returns the definition naming the transcript's hash. A definition that breaks a rule, a failing check or a seat left unregistered throws: each is a bug, never a scenario's doing.
- `runSimulation` now binds every ballot, receipt and opening to that definition's hash, encrypts shares only to the keys the check registered (T-37), and checks every share receipt under the seat's registered signing key with `checkShareReceipt`, as a voter's client does at cast. The report adds `definition.hash` and `ceremony` (the transcript hash and the registered seats).
- Offline seats now take part in the ceremony and are down from the opening: under [[0006-trustees]] rule 7 an election opens only with `max(k, n − 1)` seats in the ceremony, so the brief's "7 trustees, two offline" can only mean offline after it.
- `SIMULATION_STUBS` loses the random definition hash and the keys without rounds, and names what is still a placeholder.

Tested (part 2a): a property over random scenarios (panels of 2 to 7, 2 to 64 options, both election types, any tier weights; a twentieth of `FC_NUM_RUNS`, at least 10) that the definition is well formed and follows the scenario, its hashes and panel ID are the transcript's and the panel's, and the transcript check, rebuilt from the definition alone, registers every seat; the registered keys are each seat's own; the transcript fails the check under another election's identifier, another stub field or another panel; two runs never share an election identifier or a definition hash; a run reports both hashes, with an offline seat registered.

Known-weak (part 2a): the definition's contracts, chain IDs (local ones), election counter, clock and tier roots are placeholders and nothing registers it (P1-18, P3-2); the panel's identity keys are fresh per run, never pinned (P1-7); the participation floor of rule 7 isn't checked here, since every seat takes part (the verifier's 3.2 checks it); a seat offline during the ceremony isn't simulated.

## Part 2b (#173): Benaloh challenges

- `parseScenario` reads an optional `challenges`, the share of voters (`[0, 1]`, default 0) who challenge one attempt before casting a fresh one. `planElection` marks each such voter `challenged`, drawn from a stream of its own seed, so a scenario's voters, votes, times and paths don't change with the share.
- `packages/sdk` (`simulation/challenge.ts`): `checkChallenge`, the voter's checker under [[challenge]]'s client rules: it decodes the opening the device handed over as bytes, rejects one for another tier than the voter's, runs `checkChallengeOpening`, compares the core hash with the tracking code recorded before the choice (all 32 bytes), refuses `opening-vote-outside`, and compares the opened vote with the voter's choice. Each failure is a typed code, evidence that the casting device misbehaved; every check is `@abolishus/crypto`'s (T-42, T-44).
- `runSimulation`: for a challenged voter, the casting device makes an attempt, the checker records its `trackingCode` before the choice, the device opens it with `openChallenge`, and `checkChallenge` must pass. Every cast attempt is a fresh sharing (crypto refuses to prove an opened one) proved over `ballotCore`'s share hashes, so the core cast is the one whose code was shown, and checker rule 4 holds: no challenged attempt opens a commitment of the cast ballot (`opensBallotCommitment`). An honest attempt failing any of these throws: it is a bug, never a scenario's doing. The report adds `ballots.challenged`; `simulation plan` in `apps/admin-cli` prints the planned count.

Tested (part 2b): the scenario's `challenges` through the JSON round-trip property, its default, its refusals (out of range, a string, `null`) and an inherited one ignored; a property that the challenge share changes nothing else in the plan, none at share 0 and all at share 1, and about half of 4,000 voters at 0.5; `checkChallenge` passing an honest opening and reporting each failure with its code (a truncated opening, an opening for another election (reported before its tier), another tier the election has, a tampered coefficient (`opening-vss`), an opening of another core than the recorded one, a device that committed to another vote, and one that committed to a vote outside the rules); a run where every voter challenges first and every cell still opens to the plan; the admin-cli's plan and run agreeing on the challenged count.

Known-weak (part 2b): the checker uses the election the run built, never one it fetched itself from chain and IPFS (checker rule 2); the device is always honest, so the run never shows a cheating device caught (only `checkChallenge`'s tests do); a voter challenges at most once; a challenge costs about one more encryption and its rebuild in the check per voter, so `challenges: 1` roughly doubles a run's time (memory is unchanged, so `MAX_SIMULATED_WORK` doesn't count it); the checker's ordering of its checks lives in `packages/sdk`, outside the `crypto-review` gate, so the real checker (P2-8, P5-4) needs it as a `packages/crypto` API rather than importing this; no opening was published to the board after close until part 2f, and [[verifier]] 6.2 still isn't run (P1-13 part 3b's board side, P1-20).

## Part 2c (#174): re-voting

- `parseScenario` reads an optional `revotes`, the share of voters (`[0, 1]`, default 0) who cast an earlier ballot and then re-vote. Any positive share counts two ballots per voter against `MAX_SIMULATED_WORK`, since the plan, not the parser, draws who re-votes. `planElection` gives each such voter an `earlier` ballot (its own votes, a time drawn in `[0, at]` and the path the outage gives it), drawn from a stream of its own seed with the same number of draws per voter, so the share changes no voter's tier, counted ballot or challenge, and one voter's re-vote changes no other's earlier ballot. The plan's expected counts are each voter's last ballot.
- `packages/sdk` (`simulation/l1.ts`): a simulated L1, a block every 12 seconds from the epoch, all finalized, each with a random 32-byte hash drawn when first read, so a reference's hash is unknowable before its block as on Ethereum (T-45).
- `runSimulation` casts every ballot in time order. After the cast choice (client rule 6) each names the newest block 2 deep at its time (client rule 2), strictly newer than every block its voter named before (client rule 3): with none yet, the client waits for the next block to settle and names it. `checkL1Reference` must admit it under the window `l1RefMin` finds from the definition's `opens_at`, with the first block after `closes_at` as head (verifier 5.8). Its `M` is `membershipMessage` over its `ballotHash`. Trustees keep each ballot's shares until `selectBallots` (verifier 5.7, 6.1) has chosen one per tier and nullifier, and sum only those, so a superseded ballot never reaches a cell (T-33). A repeat, a tie, or anything but each voter's last ballot selected throws: honest voters never cause one. The report adds `ballots.superseded`, and `cast`, `server` and `direct` count every ballot; `simulation plan` prints the planned counts.

Tested (part 2c): the scenario's `revotes` through the JSON round-trip property, its default, its refusals (out of range, a string, `null`) and an inherited one ignored, and the doubled work bound; properties that the re-vote share changes nothing else in the plan and that each earlier ballot is no later than the last, allowed by the election, and direct exactly during the outage; none at share 0, all at share 1 and about half of 4,000 at 0.5; a run where every voter re-votes and half challenge, with ballots whose earlier votes differ from their last, that opens every cell to the last ballots' counts with half the ballots superseded; the admin-cli's plan and run agreeing on every ballot count.

Known-weak (part 2c): the run never shows the rule rejecting anything: no replays (`repeat`), self-ties, references before opening, after close or to a reorganised block, so only `packages/crypto`'s and the verifier's own tests cover those; L1 is simulated, every block final at once, so a reference's finality wait and a reorganisation aren't modelled; the membership message is computed but no proof binds it (P1-17b, P3-2); trustees keep every ballot's shares until selection, `options` per online seat beside its `options × threshold` commitments, about doubling a run's memory over part 2b at any re-vote share, about a gigabyte at the work bound (estimated, not measured); a voter re-votes at most once; a re-vote's wait for a newer block is never checked against close, since the close rule isn't simulated (P1-18).

## Part 2d (#175): the board and receipts at cast

- `packages/sdk` (`simulation/board.ts`): `SimulatedBoard`, the board operator. It appends each entry under the board-entry version the definition pins (4), keeps the chain head and every perfect subtree of the board tree as it grows (`appendSubtrees`), so each root and audit path comes from O(log n) stored hashes, and for a submission through our API signs a receipt right after the append (`signReceipt`, [[receipts]], Signing) with one P-256 receipt key, fresh per run. Every encoding, hash, tree and signature is `@abolishus/crypto`'s. Segments are hourly anchoring periods of L1 time (`segmentAt`), the first segment is the period of `opens_at`, and the seal 24 periods after the one holding `closes_at` (`SIMULATED_SEAL_PERIODS`): all placeholders.
- `runSimulation`: every ballot is now a ballot record (version 1) on the board, carrying its L1 reference and a placeholder membership proof (the verification key's own points, which decode but prove nothing). A server ballot is boarded as it arrives and its client runs `checkReceiptAtCast` on the receipt before the voter is told it was received, accepting the cast's segment within one period, from the first segment to the seal (board client rule 1; T-25, T-26). A direct ballot is boarded when the servers return, with no receipt, in the period they return. After voting, `checkBoard` (verifier 4.1) checks the board as written and its chain must end at the operator's head (T-23); then each ballot's L1 admission (5.8), its `M` (5.7), the re-vote selection (6.1) and the cells' VSS commitments are all read from the boarded records, never from the cast's own state. Trustees keep only their shares by board position. The report adds `board` (entry count, chain head and the receipt key).

Tested (part 2d): a property over random sequences of submissions and direct appends, in non-decreasing segments, that every receipt passes the checks at cast, and that its index, link, root and audit path are the ones `checkBoard`, `merkleRoot` and `inclusionProof` compute from scratch at its size, with the head after the last entry; a receipt refused for another payload (`receipt-entry`), key (`receipt-key`), election (`receipt-election`) and segment (`receipt-segment`); a fresh receipt key per board; the hourly segments; runs that board every ballot (9 with an outage, 18 with re-votes) and still open every cell to the plan.

Known-weak (part 2d): one receipt key that no definition lists and no registry holds (definition version 1 has none; [[0023-receipt-evidence]], P1-13b), so the receipts are checked at cast only, never later against anchored checkpoints; nothing is anchored (P4-1), so 4.2 isn't run; direct ballots are boarded by the operator, not delivered as contract events (P4-2), and get no receipt; the run never shows a receipt refused or a board failing its check (only the board tests and crypto's own do); openings aren't published to the board after close; the membership proof is a placeholder and `checkBallot` (5.1 to 5.4) isn't run, since it would refuse it; the operator's encoded entries and the board check's decoded copy hold every ballot record twice until the tally, on top of part 2c's memory (not measured; `MAX_SIMULATED_WORK` doesn't count it).

## Part 2e (#178): tally openings and destruction on the board

- `packages/sdk` (`simulation/board.ts`): `postClose` fixes the run's post-close timing from `closes_at`, per [[tally]] (Tally window, Destruction): `s_S` at its floor `period(closes_at) + 1`, since the other terms of `W_S` (delivery, complaints, the contract-event window) and `l1_inclusion_bound` are taken as zero; `t_S` as the end of `s_S` plus the anchor's deadline (a half-hour placeholder, `SIMULATED_ANCHOR_DEADLINE`); `s_T`, the period holding `t_S + TALLY_WINDOW`; `t_D`, the later of the tally window's end and `s_T − 1`'s anchoring deadline; and the seal, the period holding `t_D + DESTRUCTION_WINDOW`, the latest post-close window the run has. It replaces part 2d's 24-period placeholder seal. `afterAnchored` gives the segment in which a node that has seen a segment's checkpoint acts.
- `runSimulation`: the trustees read the counted extent (every entry a ballot boarded by `s_S`). From `t_S`, each opened cell's `k` lowest passing seats combine, and the lowest signs the cell's tally opening (`signTallyOpening`, over `aggregateHash` of the cell's counted ballots), which the operator boards. The verifier's `checkTally` (7.2) then runs over every opening on the board, and a cell's `verified` is now its verdict there (the opened counts, one passing opening, signed by the combiner) rather than a direct `checkOpening`. Each online seat, faulty ones included, signs its destruction statement and the operator boards it: in the period after the latest first passing opening's checkpoint is due when every cell opened, otherwise at `t_D` (in `s_T`). `checkDestruction` (8.5) must report every online seat `destroyed`, every offline one `destruction-missing`, and nothing duplicate, unattributed, stray or rejected. Finally `checkBoard` (4.1) runs over the whole board, whose chain must end at the operator's head with no entry after the seal. The report adds `board.counted`, `board.openings` and `destruction` (the destruction segment and each seat's line).

Tested (part 2e): `postClose` against hand-computed values, and a property over any `closes_at` that `s_S` is the floor, `t_S` its checkpoint's deadline, `s_T` holds the tally window's end, `t_D` is no earlier than either bound and falls in `s_T`, and the seal holds the destruction window's end; runs that board one opening per opened cell and one statement per online seat, with offline seats `destruction-missing` and a faulty seat `destroyed`; a run where every cell fails `tally-quorum`, boards no opening and destroys at `t_D`, more than 100 periods after a run that opened.

Known-weak (part 2e): summed shares still reach the combiner in memory, not as summed-share messages ([[tally]], Summed-share message); the operator's boarding rules for openings and statements aren't modelled, since honest records always pass them; nothing is anchored, so nodes act on segments the run computes, never on checkpoints they saw on finalized L1 (P4-1); the run never shows a failing, stray or duplicate opening or statement, or `destruction-early` (only crypto's tests do); nothing destroys the run's key material, which lives until the report is returned; `W_S` and `l1_inclusion_bound` are zero, so `s_S` and the seal are earlier than a real election's (P1-18, [[delivery]]); `postClose` is the only code computing `s_S`, `t_S`, `s_T`, `t_D` and the seal, so the run's destruction check takes `s_T` from the same function it checks, and only its own tests check the formulas (when the verifier needs them, they belong in `packages/crypto`, never copied from here).

## Part 2f (#182): spoiled-ballot openings published after close

- `parseScenario` reads an optional `publications`, the share of challenging voters (`[0, 1]`, default 0) who ask after close for their challenged attempt's opening to be published ([[challenge]], Client rule 4: publishing is the voter's choice). `planElection` marks each such voter `published`, drawn for every voter from a stream of its own seed, so the share changes no voter's tier, ballots or challenge, and only a challenger publishes. Since the operator compares every opening with every counted ballot, a scenario that can publish (both shares positive) keeps `voters × ballots × options` within `MAX_SIMULATED_OPENING_LOOKUPS` (10,000,000, about a quarter of an hour of point encodings), counting two ballots per voter with any re-vote share.
- `packages/sdk` (`simulation/publication.ts`): `admitOpening`, the operator's boarding rules of [[challenge]] (Publication) in its order: strict decoding, `checkChallengeOpening`, then `opening-early` (at or before `s_S`), `opening-late` (after the period holding `t_S + OPENING_WINDOW`), `opening-cast` (`opensBallotCommitment` against every ballot admitted in the counted extent, superseded ones included, never by tracking code) and `opening-repeat` (a core already held or boarded). Every check is `@abolishus/crypto`'s. `postClose` adds `openingsUntil`; `SimulatedBoard.boardOpenings` appends a period's openings when it ends, last in the segment and in strictly increasing byte order ([[board]], Spoiled-ballot openings; T-42, T-47).
- `runSimulation`: each publishing voter's checker keeps the opening bytes the device handed over and the code it recorded before the choice. In the first period after `s_S` each voter asks once; the operator, holding the counted extent, admits each, and an honest opening refused, or admitted as another core or vote than recorded, throws. They are boarded after that period's tally openings, before destruction. After the seal, the board's openings (read through `checkBoard`, which checks their order) must be exactly the recorded codes. The report adds `ballots.published` and `board.spoiled`; `simulation plan` in `apps/admin-cli` prints the planned count.

Tested (part 2f): `publications` through the JSON round-trip property, its default, its refusals (out of range, a string, `null`), an inherited one ignored, and the opening bound (exactly at it, one voter over, doubled by re-votes, and no bound without publishing or challenges); properties that the publication share changes nothing else in the plan, that only challengers publish, none at share 0 and every challenger at 1, about a quarter of 4,000 at challenges and publications of 0.5; `admitOpening` admitting an honest opening (with its core hash and vote, in the window's last segment too) and refusing a truncated one (`decode`), one for another election before any window (`opening-stray`), a tampered polynomial (`opening-vss`), one at or before `s_S` and one after the window, one whose commitment a counted ballot carries under other share hashes and option order (`opening-cast`, not under another definition hash, and a refused one isn't held), and a repeat; `boardOpenings` putting any set of openings after another entry of the segment in byte order that `checkBoard` accepts, returning each one's index, and refusing one opening held twice; `postClose`'s `openingsUntil` by hand and as a property (after `s_S`, no later than the seal); runs where every voter re-votes, challenges and publishes (9 openings checked against 18 counted ballots) and where only some publish, both opening every cell to the plan; the admin-cli's plan and run agreeing on the published count. The plan tests' timeout is raised to 60 s: one of them already took over 5 s at `FC_NUM_RUNS=1000` on main.

Known-weak (part 2f): the operator's boarding rules are the sdk's composition of crypto's checks, as an operator's must be, but the verifier's 6.2 isn't run over the board, since it needs the whole verifier run over anchored output (part 2g on); when `apps/api` boards openings (P2-3) the rules belong in a `packages/crypto` API both share, never copied from here; every voter asks in the first period after `s_S`, so the window's later periods and a refused opening (`opening-early`, `-late`, `-cast`, `-repeat`) appear only in `admitOpening`'s tests, never in a run; the checker's opening reaches the operator in memory, not through the API, and whether an opening gets a receipt is still open ([[receipts]], Signing); the `opening-cast` comparison costs one point encoding per counted commitment per opening, which bounds publishing scenarios at about 1,000 voters at 10 options.

## Part 2g (#185): summed-share messages between seats

- `runSimulation`: from `t_S`, each online seat seals its summed shares for each cell to each other online seat with `sealSummedShares` ([[tally]], Summed-share message), under the recipient's KEM key from the key set the ceremony check registered and signed with its own per-election key (T-37, T-39). Each recipient opens every message sent to it with `openSummedShares` against its own aggregate of the cell (T-29), and each message must pass and name the seat that sealed it and the cell; each honest seat combines its own sums and the senders' with `openCell`. Every honest seat must hold the same passing seats and reach the same combination (seats, counts and randomness), or the run throws; the lowest combined seat still signs the cell's opening. A faulty seat's corrupted sums must fail crypto's `checkSummedShares`, and `sealSummedShares` must then refuse them, or the run throws: the lie never leaves the seat. A cell's `excluded` is now every online seat whose passing sums the honest seats don't hold, rather than `openCell`'s own list. The report adds `trustees.summedShareMessages`.

Tested (part 2g): the runs count their messages (each online seat to each other online seat per cell; only honest seats send, and none when no seat is honest), with every message opened by its recipient, faulty ones included, and passing; a faulty seat excluded from every cell with the cell still opened from `k` honest seats; tally-quorum with the faulty seat excluded; a new run where every online seat is faulty, with no message, no opening and every online seat excluded.

Known-weak (part 2g): no summed-share message is ever refused in a run, since a faulty seat that uses `@abolishus/crypto` can't seal failing sums, so the recipient's codes (`summed-malformed`, `-context`, `-signature`, `-decrypt`, `-check`) appear only in crypto's own tests, and a cell's excluded seats are exactly those that sent nothing: `openCell` never sees a failing sum in a run, so its exclusion is covered only by crypto's tests; a seat that bypasses the library, or a tampered or misdirected message, isn't simulated; messages travel in memory, never over the hybrid post-quantum TLS channel the spec requires, and are never resent; each honest seat combines, but only the lowest boards an opening, so several passing openings of one cell (which the tally check accepts) aren't shown; the sealing costs about one ML-KEM encapsulation, decapsulation and two P-256 operations per message, `n_online × (n_online − 1)` messages per cell, plus a refused seal per recipient for each faulty seat, none of which `MAX_SIMULATED_WORK` counts.

## Part 2h (#186): share delivery on the board

- `runSimulation`: each online seat's node boards its share receipt (`0x000c`, crypto's `shareReceiptRecord`) right after the ballot it receipted, direct ballots' receipts when the servers return ([[delivery]], Boarding rule 2). A new scenario share, `postings` (0 when absent), is the share of ballots whose voter, finding an offline seat's intake unreachable, chooses to post that seat's ciphertext and ML-KEM message (`0x000d`) through the contract path (Client rules 4), every offline seat of that ballot. After the re-vote rule selects, crypto's `checkDelivery` (verifier 5.9) over the counted extent's receipts and the postings decides the counted set, under the real delivery segments and deadlines for the definition's close and `l1_inclusion_bound`; only counted ballots are summed. A ballot whose voter declined is undelivered and counts nothing, with no fallback to an earlier ballot. The run throws if the check reports any record or rejects any, or if a ballot's verdict or missing seats differ from the plan's. The report adds `ballots.postings`, `ballots.undelivered` and `board.shareReceipts`.
- `planElection`: each ballot draws `posts` from its own stream (two draws per voter, so the share changes nothing else), and a voter is `delivered` with no offline seat or when its last ballot is posted; the expected counts are the delivered voters' only. `apps/admin-cli`'s `simulation plan` prints the postings and undelivered ballots the run will report.

What this shows: under the delivery rule the owner accepted (2026-10-12), a seat that is down for the whole voting period makes every ballot undelivered unless its voter posts that seat, lowering its long-term privacy (T-15). The brief's "7 trustees, two offline" scenario counts only the voters who post both offline seats.

Tested (part 2h): runs with every voter posting (every ballot delivered, one posting per offline seat per ballot, re-voters posting both ballots), none posting (every ballot undelivered, every cell opened to zero), half posting (exactly the plan's undelivered voters left out, the rest opened to the plan), re-voters whose earlier ballot was posted and last wasn't (they count nothing: no fallback) and no offline seat (no posting needed); the board's receipt counts; a plan property that the posting share changes no other draw, that every voter is delivered with no offline seat, and that the expected counts are the delivered voters'; parse refusals of a malformed or inherited `postings`; the CLI's plan agreeing with its run.

Known-weak (part 2h): no contract path, so a posting is handed to the delivery check as a contract-path record submitted at its cast time, never recorded by a contract, boarded or archived; the operator boards receipts unchecked (the delivery check is handed every non-ballot entry of the counted extent, so a misrouted record would be crypto's to reject; since part 2m, every share receipt, posting and complaint, the transcript being the extent's first entry) rather than under the delivery boarding rules; offline seats never return, so no seat catches up on postings or complains and no complaint, failing receipt or failing posting is ever simulated (their codes appear only in crypto's tests); a voter's choice is drawn per ballot at cast rather than asked once its delivery has failed, and a decliner never re-votes because of it; the counted extent still ends one period after close (`W_S` taken as zero) while the delivery check applies the real deadline, harmless here since every record is boarded or sent by the close's period; each posting costs a public check (an ML-KEM re-encapsulation and ristretto255 arithmetic), which `MAX_SIMULATED_WORK` doesn't count.

## Part 2i (#203): seats that go offline partway

- `parseScenario`: an optional `trustees.departures`, a list of `{ seat, at }` ascending by seat, `at` a share of the voting period in `(0, 1]`, each seat neither offline nor faulty (a seat down from the start is offline, so `at = 0` is refused).
- `planElection` and the new `downAt`: the seats down for a ballot cast at `at` are the offline ones and those departed by then; a voter's last ballot is delivered when none is down at its cast or when its voter posts. Departures change no draw.
- `runSimulation`: a departing seat takes intake and receipts every ballot cast before its time, a direct one's receipt boarded with it when the servers return, and none after; a posting voter posts every seat down at its ballot's cast, and the delivery check's expected missing seats are each ballot's own. A departed seat sums, sends, combines and destroys nothing, so verifier 8.5 reports it `destruction-missing`; `expectCells` leaves it out of the honest seats. The report adds `trustees.departed`. `apps/admin-cli`'s `simulation plan` counts postings per ballot with `downAt`.

What this shows: a seat lost partway costs only the ballots cast after it, which need their voters' postings (T-15), and its shares of earlier ballots are lost with it, so the tally rests on the seats that stayed (T-29).

Tested (part 2i): a plan property that departures change no draw, that `downAt` is the offline seats plus those departed by each ballot's time, earlier ballots included, and that delivery and the expected counts follow; parse refusals of departures outside the panel, repeated or out of order, at 0 or past 1, malformed, with an unknown or inherited field, sparse, or of an offline or faulty seat; runs with a seat departing during the outage (taking the direct ballots before, missing the rest, only posted ones counted, with the receipt counts, combined seats and destruction codes), with a departure at close (every ballot receipted, no posting, missing the tally and destruction), with departures leaving fewer than `k` seats (`tally-quorum`), and with re-voters crossing a departure (each ballot's receipts, postings and delivery judged by the seats down at its own cast); the CLI's plan agreeing with its run when a seat departs halfway.

Known-weak (part 2i): a departed seat never returns, so it never catches up on postings or complains; a departing seat's receipts of direct ballots are boarded when the servers return even if it is down by then, and nothing models its node or disk afterwards; a voter's choice to post is still drawn per ballot at cast.

## Part 2j (#212): L1 reorganisations

- `parseScenario`: an optional `reorgs`, the share of voters (`[0, 1]`, default 0) whose last ballot names an L1 block reorganised away after the client named it 2 deep. A recast counts as one more ballot per voter in the published-openings bound (each opening is compared with it), but not in `MAX_SIMULATED_WORK`, since it carries the first cast's committed vote.
- `planElection`: a draw from a stream of its own seed, so the share changes no other draw. For a reorganised voter, the plan names the same block the run does (the voter's earlier block and Client rule 3 included, from `l1.ts`'s `nameBlock`) and has the client check it once it is finalized, two epochs (768 s) after its timestamp ([[revoting]], Client rule 4). Before close, the voter casts again then (`reorg.recast`, with its path from the outage); after it, the ballot is lost and `selected` is the earlier ballot, or none ([[revoting]], Residuals). `expected` counts each voter's selected ballot when delivered. The voting period's clock moves to `plan.ts` (`secondsAt`, `SIMULATED_OPENS_AT`, `SIMULATED_CLOSES_AT`) so the plan and the run share it.
- `runSimulation`: the reorganised ballot carries a hash of block `n` that the finalized chain doesn't have (`SimulatedL1.orphan`), and the recast is the same committed vote under a new reference. Its ballot hash is the first cast's, so the trustees' share receipts and the voter's postings already cover it ([[share-encryption]], One public part) and nothing goes to the trustees again; it is boarded by its own path, with the operator's receipt checked at cast. Admission must reject every reorganised ballot `l1-ref-unknown` and admit every other; the re-vote rule must select each voter's planned ballot; the delivery check judges each by its own first cast's seats and postings, and an earlier ballot selected in a lost one's place by its own. The report adds `ballots.reorged` and `ballots.recast`; `superseded` counts only earlier ballots not selected. `apps/admin-cli`'s `simulation plan` prints the same fields.

What this shows: a reorganisation deeper than Client rule 2's margin costs a voter nothing when it is seen before close, since the receipts and postings follow the ballot hash (T-25), and costs a ballot cast in about the last 12 minutes (its block is 24 to 35 seconds old and final 768 s after it), when the earlier ballot, if any, counts instead (T-45's re-vote binding to L1 is why: the rejected ballot never displaces another).

Tested (part 2j): parse refusals of `reorgs` (out of range, a string, `null`), its default and an inherited one ignored, and the openings bound with recasts; plan properties that the share changes no other draw, none at 0 and all at 1 (about half at 0.5), that the recast time, in whole seconds, is the named block's finality by a second reading of the clock (block named 2 deep and after the earlier one, finality 768 s after it), after the first cast and before close or never, with the selection following, and that the expected counts are each voter's selected ballot when delivered, with and without an offline seat; a plan over 4,000 voters losing exactly the ballots whose block is final at or after close; runs with every ballot reorganised and recast (by both paths, receipts and postings only from the first casts), with a re-voter's last ballot lost near close (the earlier one counted), with a single ballot lost (nothing counted for that voter), and with undelivered first casts whose recasts stay undelivered; `simulation plan` agreeing with `simulation run` with reorganisations, and when one loses a re-voter's last ballot.

Known-weak (part 2j): only last ballots are reorganised; the client sees the reorganisation at the finality of the block it named and casts again at once, with no light client, header range or retry modelled; every reorganisation happens before the block is finalized, as on a healthy chain; whether a voter casts again is not drawn (every client offers it and every voter accepts).

Independent review (part 2j): an in-session subagent with `.github/review/crypto-review.md` found nothing blocking. Applied: a recast's time was a share of the period turned back into seconds with a floor, so about one in sixteen was cast a second before its block was final (the plan now carries the recast's exact seconds, and the run orders casts by seconds); the lost window's wording (about 12 minutes); a run test that could pass vacuously under another seed now requires an undelivered recast; the recast cast carries only its votes, time and path, so nothing posts or challenges twice; a recast at close itself is taken as too late, with a comment saying why.

## Part 2k (#215): intake in worker threads

- `runSimulation` returns a promise and takes `workers` (0 to `MAX_INTAKE_WORKERS`, 64; default 0, the calling thread; anything else is a `RangeError` before the run starts). Each cast now runs in two steps: the client's (challenge, sharing, encryption, proof, the code shown, Checker rule 4), then, with each up seat's intake back, the receipts' checks under the registered keys, the ballot record, postings and boarding. Both steps run in cast order; the second waits for each ballot's intake in turn while up to `2 × workers` later ballots' intakes run ahead, so every board index, receipt, posting and selection is what the single-threaded run makes. A recast does all its work in the second step, after its first cast's.
- `packages/sdk` (`simulation/intake.ts`, `simulation/intake-worker.ts`): the worker holds every seat's intake keys, given once at start under a marker (so it never serves another worker that imports the sdk), and runs `@abolishus/crypto`'s `intakeShares` for each seat a ballot lists, unchanged; the committed vote crosses as its canonical encoding, since group elements don't survive a structured clone. A ballot goes to the worker with the fewest waiting. A worker's error or exit fails every waiting and later ballot, and the run rejects; the pool is terminated when the casts end, either way. The worker imports only `@abolishus/crypto` and Node, so Node runs it as TypeScript from the source tree (the tests) and as `dist/intake-worker.mjs`, a second `vp pack` entry (`packages/sdk/vite.config.ts`).
- `apps/admin-cli`: `simulation run --workers <n>`.

Measured (4 cores, 40 voters, 7 seats, 3 options, through `apps/admin-cli` on the packed sdk): 35 s in one thread, 17 s with 3 workers. The calling thread still makes every ballot (proofs included) and checks every receipt, so more cores help less; the brief's 10,000 voters take about half the single-threaded time on 4 cores.

Tested (part 2k): each seat's result from the workers equals intake's in the calling thread, shares and receipts alike, over several ballots in flight at once; a seat's refusal (`share-hash`) comes back as intake reports it without failing the pool; a ballot for a seat the workers hold no keys for rejects and the pool keeps serving; a closed pool rejects; worker counts outside 1 to 64 (0, negative, fractional, NaN, 65) are refused; a run in 3 workers with challenges, publications, re-votes, reorganisations, an offline, a faulty and a departing seat, an outage and partial postings reports exactly what the same scenario reports in one thread (every count, cell, seat and destruction line; only fresh keys and hashes differ), with one progress line per cast in order; `runSimulation` refuses bad `workers`; `apps/admin-cli`'s `--workers 2` against `--workers 0`, and its usage errors.

Known-weak (part 2k): the seats' intake keys are copied into every worker thread of the process for the run and never zeroised (as the in-thread run never destroys them, SIMULATION_STUBS); nothing models a seat's node as its own process or host; the client side and the receipt checks stay on the calling thread.

Independent review (part 2k): an in-session subagent with `.github/review/crypto-review.md` found nothing blocking, and confirmed the packed `dist/index.mjs` runs with workers. Applied: a worker that fails to start now stops those already started (they would have held the process); a test that the worker module serves no worker started without its marker; the README says `workers` needs the sdk loaded from its own `dist`. Not applied: comparing board order directly between the two runs, since the report doesn't carry entries (the order is the in-thread loop's by construction: every board write happens in a cast's second step, in cast order).

## Part 2l (#217): anchoring the board's checkpoints

- `packages/sdk` (`simulation/anchor.ts`): `SimulatedAnchor`, the anchoring authority. Once a period has ended and every entry it will have is on the board, it anchors the checkpoint after it, from `@abolishus/crypto`'s `copyCheckpoints` over the board's bytes (CIDs included), in the first simulated L1 block after the period's end: for every period that added entries, and for the seal even when it added none, which then repeats the board's state ([[board]], Segments and checkpoints, Anchoring, Timing). A period with no new entries is skipped, as the spec allows. Its clock is the run's (hourly from the Unix epoch, a half-hour deadline, `lastSegment` the seal); it starts at the election's first period and refuses to anchor past the seal.
- `runSimulation`: three reads of the board are crypto's `verifyBoard` over every anchor, with `now` the time of the first L1 block at or after the reader's moment, read once it is finalized, so only anchors in blocks up to it count; each must authenticate the whole board as it stands (T-23, T-24):
  - the trustees' read of the counted extent at `t_S`, once the checkpoint for `s_S` is due, which replaces the entry checks alone;
  - a node's read before it destroys, once the checkpoint of the last cell's first passing opening is due ([[tally]], Destruction, When): the openings must be anchored;
  - the verifier's 4.1 and 4.2 over the whole board at the first L1 block after the seal's window has closed, so a segment with entries and no timely checkpoint fails `unanchored`, a missing seal `unsealed`, and an entry past the seal is outside the anchored extent; the chain must end at the operator's head.
- The report adds `board.seal` and `board.anchors` (each anchor's segment, size, checkpoint hash and L1 time). `SIMULATION_STUBS` names what anchoring still leaves out.

Tested (part 2l): a property over random boards (entries over random periods, anchoring at random points once each point's periods are complete, a seal up to three periods after the last entry) that the closed board passes `verifyBoard` with nothing unauthenticated, with one anchor per period that added entries in order, then the seal's when it added none, each in a block after its period ends and within its deadline, the seal's holding the whole board; a reader counting only anchors in blocks up to its L1 time, an entry past them unauthenticated rather than failed; the seal's checkpoint equal to the one before it but for its segment, and nothing anchored twice; refusals past the seal and of a first period outside 0 to the seal, and nothing anchored while the board is empty; controls that the closed board fails `checkpoint-boundary` when a period is anchored before its last entry, `unanchored` when a period's entry comes after the authority passed it over, and `unsealed` without the seal's anchor; a run's anchors in order, each in the first block after its period, sizes growing but for the seal's, the seal holding the whole board and exactly one anchor holding the counted extent; the worker-thread equivalence run now comparing every anchor but its hash.

Known-weak (part 2l): the anchor contract, its clock and the authority's key are simulated, and the anchors are held in memory, never read from L1 state with storage proofs (P4-1, P1-19's reading of the anchor is the verifier's own); every period's entries are on the board before it ends, so no anchor races an append, and no anchor is late, missing or equivocal in a run (the controls show only that `verifyBoard` catches them); the CIDs name segment objects nothing publishes or pins (P4-3); readers check the operator's copy, and the tally (7.2) and destruction (8.5) checks read it before the final board check authenticates it; a node's check before destroying covers the whole board, run once for every seat, rather than each first passing opening's inclusion; the trustees' moments (`t_S`, the destruction segment) come from the clock, not from the blocks they saw.

Independent review (part 2l): an in-session subagent with `.github/review/crypto-review.md` found nothing blocking. Applied: readers no longer drop anchors as not yet finalized while judging windows by wall time (a timely anchor made late in its deadline could have read as `unanchored`): each passes every anchor with `now` the time of the block it read, as the spec defines it; the run's comments and README say which three reads go through the anchors, and the stubs name the copy-based reads, the node's whole-board check and the clock-based moments; dead length comparisons after `verifyBoard` removed; the authority starts at the election's first period rather than period 0; the report's anchor fields are built where the run's other hex fields are.
