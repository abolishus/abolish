## Scope

Moderation policy (written) and moderation tooling for poll content (never results).

## Plan

The item is built in parts, each its own PR on this branch's name:

1. **Policy and log** (done, #66): [[moderation-policy]], and the append-only moderation log with its state machine in `packages/core`.
2. **Reports and the public log over the API** (this part): reports that store the poll and a reason code only (never the reporter), counted per poll and ground; `apps/api` serving the public log and each poll's state; a per-address rate limit on reports at P2-11's proxy (T-52).
3. **Appeals and the moderators' tooling:** the creator's appeal from their account, which needs accounts (P2-5, #65) and a record of who created a poll (P2-9); the moderators' queue, reviews and decisions in `apps/admin-cli` or `apps/web`, whichever exists first.
4. **Front ends:** discovery and search leave out delisted and hidden polls, and a hidden poll's page shows the notice and its result by option number (P2-9); the ballot client's behaviour for hidden text (P2-8).
5. **Owner decision (2026-10-12): a `threat` hide must not stop voting.** The ballot client keeps showing an open poll hidden for `threat`, using its committed display text from IPFS (checked against the definition), while our web pages withhold it. Hides for `illegal` and `personal-data` keep stopping our client from showing the text. Update [[moderation-policy]] to match; required before Phase 2 goes public.

## What was built (part 1)

- [[moderation-policy]]: what moderation may touch (display text and listing, never the board, a definition, a tally or an election's timing); the actions `delist`, `hide`, `restore`; the closed list of reason codes and the grounds that never count (viewpoint, controversy, criticism, report volume, a poll's result or turnout); hiding an open poll only for illegal content, personal data or a threat; the public log; reports; appeals by a different moderator; moderators; and the legal questions, which are the owner's.
- `packages/core`:
  - migration `0002_moderation.sql`: `moderation_actions`, append-only for every role (grants and triggers), with checks that tie each reason code to its action, bound the moderator handle and the election ID, and leave no free-text column. P2-5's `0002_accounts.sql` (#65) is in review with the same number: whichever merges second renumbers to the next free number above everything merged, since `migrate` refuses a number below an applied one.
  - `src/moderation.ts`: `moderate`, `moderationStatus`, `readModerationLog`, `OPEN_POLL_HIDE_REASONS`. One advisory lock serialises decisions, so sequence numbers commit in order (a reader paging by sequence number never misses one) and every decision is judged against the state it changes. A decision's time is the database clock, read after the lock, never the caller's, so no caller can date a hide past a poll's close. A removal may be re-cited under a reason not yet cited since the state began; anything else that changes nothing is refused.
  - `TABLE_CLASSES.operational` gains `moderation_actions`.
- Threat files T-68 and T-17. The index row for T-68 stays `Planned` (AGENTS.md lets a PR edit the index only to add a threat), and so does the file's status line, to match it.

## Tested (part 1)

- `packages/core/tests/moderation.test.ts` against Postgres, as the app role: the Drizzle table matches the migration; the column list (no free text); grants; `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE` refused as the app and as the owner; the owner can't set `session_replication_role`, and the app can't disable the triggers or drop the table; the table's checks (unknown action, a reason of the other kind, an unknown reason, a name for a handle, a long or hyphen-led handle, a short election ID, an explicit `seq`).
- `moderate`: hide and restore; delist, escalate and de-escalate; no-change refused with nothing written; re-citing under another reason; an unknown election; ten malformed decisions; election IDs that aren't 32 real bytes; for every removal reason, hiding a poll still inside its close plus inclusion bound is allowed or refused (`open-poll`) as the policy says, and allowed after it; a forged far-future time is ignored (still `open-poll`, and the recorded time is the database's); six concurrent identical decisions record exactly one.
- A fast-check model of the state machine (random action sequences; the state is always the newest decision's, and only changes are recorded), at `FC_NUM_RUNS=1000` capped to 50 runs since each run stores a definition.
- Never results: moderating leaves an election's board and definition byte for byte, and its board still takes entries; a source check that the moderation module names no board table or board function, writes no definition, and that the board and definition modules never mention moderation.
- The public log pages in order across elections, and its entries carry no moderator.
- Mutation by hand: removing the advisory lock fails the concurrency test.

## Known-weak (part 1)

- The log lives in Postgres: a wipe loses every decision (polls go back to visible), and an insider can withhold rows from what we serve. Neither changes a result. Anchoring the log is not planned yet.
- The moderator handle is free to choose within its pattern; mapping handles to people is the operator's, and the policy's rule that a moderator doesn't decide on their own poll is not enforced.
- The `open-poll` check compares the database clock with `closes_at`, which the chain compares with L2 `block.timestamp`; the margin is the inclusion bound.
- Only `moderate` enforces the state machine, the open-poll rule and the in-order sequence. The app role can insert rows directly, and the owner can drop the triggers with DDL: the log binds the tooling, not an insider (T-68).
- Hiding an open poll for `illegal`, `personal-data` or `threat` stops voting through our client and so can lower turnout: listed under Decisions to review.
- Nothing serves the log or acts on the state yet (parts 2 and 3).

## What was built (part 2)

- `packages/core`:
  - migration `0003_moderation_reports.sql`: `moderation_reports`, one row per election and removal ground, with `total`, `pending` and `pending_since` (the hour the oldest unreviewed report arrived, which orders the queue and never moves when a poll is reported again); no column for the reporter or text. The app role holds `SELECT`, `INSERT` and `UPDATE`, never `DELETE` or `TRUNCATE`. P2-5's `0003_accounts.sql` (#65, waiting on the owner) has the same number: whichever merges second renumbers, as in part 1.
  - `src/reports.ts`: `report` (counts one report, refusing a ground outside the removal reasons or an election with no stored definition), `readReportQueue` (unreviewed reports, longest waiting first, paged by a cursor; never served by the API) and `reviewReports` (marks reviewed the reports a moderator saw, given the `total` the queue showed, so a report counted meanwhile stays pending; it records no decision).
  - `readModerationLog` answers an empty page past the largest Postgres `bigint` rather than failing on the comparison.
  - `TABLE_CLASSES.operational` gains `moderation_reports`.
- `packages/api-contract` and `apps/api`: `GET /moderation/log` (`after`, `limit` up to 256), `GET /moderation/status` (`ELECTION_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown election) and `POST /moderation/reports` (202, no account). Times are RFC 3339 UTC to the millisecond. None of them names a moderator or a reporter.
- [[moderation-policy]] (Reports, Transparency, Known-weak), [[railway]] (a rate-limit rule on reports), and history in T-11, T-52 and T-68.

## Known-weak (part 2)

- Anyone can report any poll as often as the proxy allows, so pending counts can be inflated; volume is never a ground and doesn't order the queue. Reports can't be withdrawn.
- An insider can time a report (counters read repeatedly, the write-ahead log, backups) and match it to an address in proxy or platform logs (T-11).
- The counters' property test runs at most 50 cases whatever `FC_NUM_RUNS` says, like the other database-backed property tests, since each case stores a definition.
- No moderator tooling yet (part 3): a moderator acts through `packages/core` directly.
- A Cloudflare rate-limit match on `POST /moderation/reports` is kept as a security event with the reporter's address (T-11).

## What was specified (part 5)

The owner's decision of 2026-10-12 (STATUS, Decisions to review): a `threat` hide must not stop voting. Docs only; the behaviour is built where the front ends and pinning are.

- [[moderation-policy]], a new Open polls section:
  - while a poll is open (until `closes_at` plus `l1_inclusion_bound`), a `threat` hide withholds the text from our web pages only; our ballot client fetches the committed display-text record from IPFS, checks it against the definition and shows it under a notice; we keep the text pinned until the poll closes;
  - `illegal` and `personal-data` hides still withhold the text from the ballot client too (the notice and the direct-submit path instead), and we unpin at once;
  - the newest decision decides, so a `threat` hide re-cited under `personal-data` is withheld from then on;
  - the ballot client reads the state from `GET /moderation/status` and treats a poll whose state it can't read as visible, so an outage or a blocked route doesn't stop voting;
  - after close a `threat` hide withholds the text everywhere.
- The `open-poll` rule in the tooling is unchanged: an open poll is still hidden only for those three grounds.
- [[STATUS]] Decisions to review and T-68 updated.
- To build: the ballot client's behaviour (P2-8), the pin kept until close (P4-3).

## Known-weak (part 5)

- A `threat`-hidden open poll's text stays readable in our ballot client by anyone who opens it.
- Failing open on the status route means that, during an outage, the ballot client shows the text of an `illegal` or `personal-data` hide if someone else still pins it. Failing closed would let anyone who can block the route stop voting through our client.
- `illegal` and `personal-data` hides of open polls still stop voting through our client on a code nobody outside can check (T-68).
- Follow-up from `claude-review` on #121 (non-blocking): T-68's residual and T-17's history now state the `threat`-text and fail-open residuals.
