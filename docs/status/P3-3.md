## What was decided (part 1)

[[0014-tier1-vouching]], `Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`. It picks option C:

- **Who vouches:** Tier 2 members, anonymously.
- **Each vouch** is two proofs under Semaphore v4's unchanged circuit and keys:
  - one with a per-vouchee scope, so the vouchers are distinct;
  - one with a per-slot scope, so each voucher has a lifetime budget.

  Each proof's message binds the vouchee and the other proof's nullifier.

- **Admission:** a commitment with vouches from `k = 3` distinct members, each member having `L = 12` vouches, ever.

It replaces the Safe's signature for Tier 1 additions, so the verifier checks the rule from the board.

The rejected options:

- an operator-held graph: unverifiable, and it stores the graph;
- a public signed web of trust: it publishes the graph;
- a transitive variant: fake identities could vouch for more, without bound.

Threat files T-02, T-06, T-22 and T-27 are updated.

## What was built (part 2b-2)

- `countTier1Additions` (`packages/crypto/src/vouch-count.ts`): every Tier 1 addition of a group registry decided in one replay over the registry's events in log order. Each Tier 2 group is replayed strictly first (`applyGroupRecords` under the pinned registrars); the earliest event breaking a Tier 2 history (a record the replay refuses, a members record naming a group with no earlier `GroupCreated`, or a repeated `GroupCreated`, each judged from earlier events alone) stops the count at its block, leaving every addition at or after it undecided. Then the checks in the spec's order, each over the vouches in record order: checks 1 to 6 of one addition, `group`, `root-grid`, `root-window`, `tier2-group`, `root-mismatch`, `slot-spent`, `member`, `proof`. Each Tier 2 group a vouch names is replayed once with an incremental tier tree (`TierTreeBuilder`, internal), its root taken at every block named. Counted additions append a leaf and consume their slot nullifiers; refused ones consume nothing. `tier1Leaves` gives a group's leaves at a block, or `group-history` at or past the break.
- `verifyDecodedMembershipProofs` (`membership-proof.ts`): the small-exponent batch test over strictly decoded proofs, coefficients uniform in `[1, 2^128)` from the CSPRNG and never supplied by a caller, `N + 3` pairings for `N` proofs, in batches of at most 64; a failed batch is split in halves, each batched again, and parts below 33 that fail are checked proof by proof, so each verdict is the individual one. Every proof of a replay is checked this way before the walk, for additions that pass the checks needing no counting state.
- `checkTier1Addition` now runs on `precheckTier1Addition` (checks 1 to 6 and the `2k` proof checks), which the counting shares.
- Measured costs in [[vouching]], Batched verification: batching cuts honest proof checks to about a sixth (about 8 ms a proof); one invalid proof in every 64 leaves about 85% of the one-at-a-time cost, and all junk about 1.35 times it.
- [[vouching]]: the two new break triggers, the costs, the vectors and `countTier1Additions`; Known-weak gains the held vouch that links two vouchees (#288's non-blocking finding, stated rather than closed). Threat files T-22, T-52 and T-71.

## What was specified (part 3c-1)

- [[tier1-relay]], a new spec page: the relay sends additions only to the published Tier 1 groups (one per region, listed beside the Tier 0 groups). Its route takes the record and a Turnstile token and answers in a fixed order. The record's own checks and the root grid come first (`RELAY_MALFORMED`), then the group, the index's freshness, each vouch's root at or below the safe head and remaining window (`VOUCH_WINDOW`, `TIER1_RELAY_MARGIN`), the solve, and the queue bound only after the solve (`RELAY_BUSY`), so a queue-occupancy probe costs a solve. No answer depends on the commitment's state.
- The queue is a set of records keyed by their bytes, never by `C_V`, so a junk record for someone's commitment can't keep their honest one out (T-25). In-flight records are kept with their signed transactions until final, rebroadcast only at their own nonce (with a fee bump at a second boundary), and returned to the queue and judged again if their nonce was consumed.
- At each ten-minute boundary the job rebroadcasts any pending transaction at its own nonce (re-judging a record whose nonce was consumed), brings its index and counting state up to the latest block, and shuffles the queue with a CSPRNG. Roots must be at or below the safe head, and a record needs 150 blocks of window left when sent. It then judges each record against one counting state, extended by the records already chosen, with proofs checked last. It never runs a full replay per record. At most 16 that count are sent, sorted by `C_V`, from the relay's account with a 2 million gas limit, after a simulation. What doesn't count is dropped. This is the replay [[contracts]] (Who pays) requires, now linked from there (T-52).
- The outcome is read from the chain by the client's own counting (added, refused, not yet), and the direct path is the same record sent to `addTier1Member` from an address of the vouchee's.
- [[registration]], Challenge page: the frame's fragment names the action (`tier0-register` or `tier1-relay`), and the page renders the widget only for those two.

## What was built (part 3c-2b-2a)

- **`Tier1Batches`** (`packages/core`, `tier1-batch.ts`), [[tier1-relay]]'s job behind `Tier1Chain` (heads with the latest block's hash, block hashes, the registry's logs as contract-event records, the account's nonce, inclusion by hash, simulation, signing and broadcast), under a Redis lock that every write checks, as Tier 0's:
  - **`refreshIndex()`**: brings the counting state up to the node's latest block and writes the route's `index` key (`at latest safe`, dated when the read began). The state is extended by the blocks after its own only; it is rebuilt by a full replay on a restart, once its block's hash changes (a reorganisation of a block it was extended past), or after an extension that failed partway; a node that is merely behind or inconsistent leaves it standing. Nothing read is used, and the index ages out, when the node is behind a block already read, its heads are out of order, the latest block's hash changes during the read, a log's `n` isn't the next, a log fails the event check (`groupRegistryEvents` under the pinned contract-event version), or a `RegistrarsPinned` names other pins (T-53, T-71).
  - **`runBoundary()`**, once a period: (1) each in-flight record in nonce order, deleted once its transaction (any it was signed in) is final, or once the last block of one of its vouch windows is final without it; returned to the queue if its nonce went to another transaction, the account's nonce being read with the block it was read at; otherwise rebroadcast unchanged at the first boundary, and replaced at its nonce with a higher fee, kept before it is sent, at the second and later; a rebroadcast the node refuses is checked again (kept if it landed meanwhile, returned if its nonce is now taken, and otherwise kept pending, to be replaced); a record that can never land frees its nonce, and the next send there outbids its last transaction; nothing new is sent while any is pending; (2) the index and the state, at or past the block the nonce was read at and with that block in their history, so a record given back is judged against whatever took its nonce; a broken Tier 2 history stops here, queue kept; (3) the queue shuffled by `selectUniformly`'s CSPRNG Fisher–Yates, after dropping entries that aren't a published group's well-formed record under their hash; (4) `Tier1CountingState.judge` with `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH` and `TIER1_RELAY_SEND_MARGIN`, dropped records leaving the queue and unreached ones staying; (5) the chosen ones by `C_V`, then bytes, each simulated, then signed at the next nonce, moved from the queue to `inflight` with its transaction in one fenced step, then broadcast; a reverting simulation drops the record and takes no nonce.
  - The report is counts only, by step (T-10, T-13). Redis gains `inflight` (each record with its nonce, its signed transactions and hashes, and how many boundaries it was found pending at: no time, address or arrival order), `boundary` and the lock.
- `tier1Keys` gains the job's keys. No new dependency.
- **Spec** ([[tier1-relay]], The batch, step 1): a refused rebroadcast no longer returns the record while its nonce is free (it could still land from another node's pool, and the record would be paid for twice); it stays in flight and is replaced. Step 2's replay must reach the block the nonce was read at, and a record that can never land frees its nonce for a replacement.

Known-weak (part 3c-2b-2a):

- **Nothing runs it yet** (closed in part 3c-2b-2b): the viem adapter, configuration and schedule are part 3c-2b-2b, so the route still answers `RELAY_UNAVAILABLE`.
- **The node is trusted for liveness, not for counts it can't show**: a node that lies consistently (a fabricated history with matching hashes) can make the relay pay for additions that don't count or skip ones that would; nothing it says changes what counts, which verifiers judge from the archive ([[tier1-relay]], Known-weak, The relay can refuse). The replay at the latest block can't be proven by the accumulator, which covers proven blocks only.
- **The cap isn't reached in a test**: no vector set has seventeen additions that count together, so `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH` is checked as the judgement's `limit` in `packages/crypto`'s tests, not end to end here.
- **A full replay inside the lock**: a restart or reorganisation replays the registry's whole history in one refresh; at scale it can outlast `TIER1_BATCH_LOCK_MS`, and the fenced writes then refuse, so the index isn't written until a replay fits (T-52; [[tier1-relay]], Known-weak, A full counting state on restart).

## What was built (part 3c-2b-2b)
