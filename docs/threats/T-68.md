# T-68: Moderation abuse

- Area: Development, supply chain and operations (G-14)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1
- Status: **Planned.** Residual: content anchored on-chain or on IPFS can't be erased, only hidden by our front ends; the moderation log lives in our database, so an insider can withhold rows from what we serve or lose them in a wipe (no result changes either way), and the log's rules bind the tooling, not an insider who inserts rows directly or drops its triggers with DDL; hiding an open poll for `illegal` or `personal-data` stops voting through our client and can lower turnout (listed for the owner under Decisions to review); while a `threat`-hidden poll is open our ballot client still shows its text; and because the client treats a poll whose state it can't read as visible, an outage or block of `GET /moderation/status` lets it show the text of an `illegal` or `personal-data` hide that someone else still pins ([[moderation-policy]], Open polls).

## Threat

Moderation is used to suppress polls or results, or anchored content can't be moderated at all.

## Mitigation

A written policy ([[moderation-policy]]); moderation acts on poll content, never results (P2-10). Built (P2-10 part 1): the policy, with its closed list of grounds and the grounds that never count (viewpoint, report volume, a poll's result or turnout); an append-only, public moderation log in `packages/core` whose only actions are delisting, hiding display text and restoring, each citing a reason code, with no free text and no moderator in the public view; the tooling never writes the board or a definition, and refuses to hide a poll's text before it closes except for illegal content, personal data or a threat, since hiding stops voting through our client. Specified (P2-10 part 5, the owner's decision): a `threat` hide of an open poll withholds the text only from our web pages, while our ballot client keeps showing the committed text, which we keep pinned until the poll closes; a ballot client that can't read a poll's state treats it as visible, so blocking the status route doesn't stop voting. Results stay verifiable from chain + IPFS whatever the log says (G-9). Built (P2-10 part 2): the public log and each poll's state over the API, without the moderator; anonymous reports, counted per poll and ground with nothing about the reporter, that never change a poll's state or the log. Built (P2-10 part 4a): the API's poll listing (`GET /elections`) leaves out delisted and hidden polls, and the website's list of polls (`/polls`, P2-10 part 4b) shows only that listing. Built (P2-10 part 3b-1): moderators' handles tied to their accounts, so the tooling refuses a decision on the moderator's own poll; creators' appeals, one per decision, reviewed by a moderator who neither made the decision nor created the poll, an upheld one being the only way `appeal-upheld` reaches the log. Planned: filing an appeal from the website (P2-10 part 3b-2); the ballot client's behaviour (P2-8); the legal questions in the policy are the owner's.

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P2-10 part 1): policy and log built (above). The log isn't anchored, so withholding a row from the public log isn't detectable yet.
- 2026-10-11 (P2-10 part 2): the API serves the log (`GET /moderation/log`) and each poll's state (`GET /moderation/status`), and takes reports (`POST /moderation/reports`). Reports are counted per poll and ground; a test checks that any number of them leaves a poll's state and the public log unchanged, since report volume is never a ground.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-9 part 1): `apps/web` shows the public log at `/moderation` and each poll's state on its page; a delisted or hidden poll is marked `noindex`.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-10 part 5): the owner narrowed open-poll hides. A `threat` hide no longer stops voting through our ballot client, which keeps showing the committed text from IPFS; only `illegal` and `personal-data` hides still do. The client treats a poll whose state it can't read as visible ([[moderation-policy]], Open polls). Built by P2-8 (the ballot client) and P4-3 (pinning).
- 2026-10-12 (P2-10 part 3a): the operator's moderation commands in `apps/admin-cli`: the report queue a page at a time, reviewing only the reports the queue showed, decisions through `moderate` (so its open-poll and no-change rules hold), and the public log. They connect as the app role and never print a moderator's handle.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-10 part 4a): the API lists polls for discovery at `GET /elections` (`listPolls` in core), leaving out every poll whose newest decision delists or hides it; such a poll stays served by ID. A property test over arbitrary logs, written directly as an insider could, checks that the newest decision alone decides. The listing is never a source of truth: the registry names every poll.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-10 part 4b): the website lists polls at `/polls`, a page of 20 at a time from `GET /elections`, so delisted and hidden polls are never listed there; each listed definition is decoded and checked against its ID as on the poll page (T-34), and the page says the registry on chain names every poll. A hidden poll's page already shows the notice, its reason and the definition's facts, with no text (P2-9 part 1).
- 2026-10-17 (P2-22 part 1, [[0032-poll-creation-operations]]): drafts are not pre-moderated. The worker refuses a submitted draft only for the creation limits or a definition the registry would refuse; moderation acts on a poll once registered, so every content decision stays in the public log. The account-side creator record that part 3b's appeals need is decided (rule 15) and built in [[P2-22]] part 2.
- 2026-10-17 (P2-8 part 2b-1): the API serves display text at `GET /elections/display-text` under the open-poll rules: a hidden poll's text is withheld (`DISPLAY_TEXT_WITHHELD`), except while a poll hidden as a `threat` can still take ballots, so the ballot client, which can reach only our API, keeps showing it as the policy requires (`readServedDisplayText` in core). Text is served only while every ground cited since the poll was hidden is `threat`, so a `threat` hide re-cited under `personal-data` is withheld at once, and an `illegal` or `personal-data` hide re-cited as `threat` stays withheld ([[moderation-policy]], Open polls, now says so). The rows stay until the unpinning job deletes them ([[P4-3]]); the app role may delete but never change one.
- 2026-10-17 (P2-10 part 3b-1): `account_moderators` gives each moderator's handle one account, never rebound (retiring it, or deleting its account, clears the account for good), and `moderate` refuses a handle that isn't active (`unknown-moderator`) and a decision on a poll the handle's account created (`own-poll`, from the creator record of [[0032-poll-creation-operations]], rule 15). `account_appeals` holds one appeal per removal decision, filed by the poll's creator against its newest decision; `decideAppeal` refuses the moderator who decided and the poll's creator, records an upheld appeal as a `restore` citing `appeal-upheld` and closes an appeal as `superseded` when a later decision changed the poll's state first. `moderate` itself refuses `appeal-upheld` (`appeal-only`), so from migration `0014` on every such entry in the public log answers a filed appeal. The reviewer may not be anyone who made a decision in the run the appeal contests (since the state last changed), an appeal stands while a removal is only re-cited under another ground, and an appeal is served only while its decision is about the appeal's own poll. Not mitigated: a moderator can supersede pending appeals by switching a poll between delisted and hidden, which the log shows; a stake other than authorship isn't recorded, and while the operator is the only moderator no appeal can be reviewed, as the policy says; an insider can still write either table directly.
- 2026-10-17 (P2-10 part 3b-2a): a poll's creator appeals its newest removal and sees the outcome from their account on the website (`POST /drafts/appeal` and `POST /drafts/moderation` on `apps/web`'s origin, signed in, our `Origin` required), under `fileAppeal`'s rules; the view shows only what the public log does (state, action, reason, time) and the creator's own appeals, never a moderator. The account page's screens for it come with [[P2-22]]'s creator pages.
- 2026-10-17 (P2-22 part 5d-2, #411): an author can retry or discard a poll that failed in setup, but never one whose election is registered on chain (it failed `registration-mismatch`, `open-mismatch` or `open-timeout`, or the registry sync recorded a registration under its identifier): that election is listed once the sync stores its definition and can be moderated, so its row stays as the creator record that `moderate`'s own-poll refusal and the author's appeals read. A discarded poll's identifier was never registered, so nothing lists it. Not mitigated: a stolen creation key could still register an old approved stub of a retried or discarded identifier before its `opens_at` ([[T-72]]), leaving a listed election with no creator record, as deleting an account does today.
