# T-14: Trustee collusion

- Area: Ballot secrecy, unlinkability and privacy (G-3, G-4, G-5, G-12)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1
- Status: **Needs decision** (P1-4, P1-7). Residual: k colluding trustees break secrecy for every ballot of that election. Automated trustee nodes add one more path: a malicious node release reaches every share at once (T-61, [[0006-trustees]] rule 6). That holds for as long as they, or anyone who seizes them, keep their keys and stored shares; destruction after the tally is required but can't be proven, and posted shares are permanent. k colluding trustees can also publish a ballot's shares, which anyone can check against the public VSS commitments, making that ballot permanently and verifiably public (as with a published decryption under ElGamal).

## Threat

k or more trustees combine shares and decrypt individual ballots.

## Mitigation

k-of-n threshold with independent named trustees (P1-7, owner decision; [[0006-trustees]] proposes 4-of-7 with at most k − 1 trustees per jurisdiction and per hosting provider, and at most one seat for us), Each voter splits the ballot's opening k-of-n among the trustees with Pedersen VSS, so no one ever holds a key that opens ballots, and no DKG is needed for plurality and approval (P1-12, P1-14, [[0002-everlasting-privacy]]). Honest trustees only ever send summed shares to each other, and publish openings, for cells of the partition registered in the election definition ([[0015-tally-publication]]) (tiers only until P1-15 specifies proven regional cells; verifier 2.8, which their nodes run before releasing a share), which open aggregates (P1-15, [[0003-tally-scheme]]); per-election keys and stored shares are destroyed after the tally.

Index: [[THREAT_MODEL]].

## History

- 2026-10-10 (P0-ops-5): summed shares limited to cells of the registered partition ([[0003-tally-scheme]], Decision; verifier 2.8, 7.4).
- 2026-10-11 (P1-15, part 1): trustees exchange summed shares ([[0015-tally-publication]], option C), so each trustee that receives `k` of them learns a cell's summed polynomials at every seat. Wherever posted shares fall into the pattern in 0015's Context (shaped by a relay or by trustees' downtime), one corrupt trustee with a later break of their classical encryption can complete a ballot with fewer than `k` posted shares; it can also disclose the summed shares, which can't be attributed to it, and a trustee seized before destruction yields the same ([[tally]], Residuals). Not mitigated; re-randomising the summed polynomials (0015 option B) would close it only against coalitions of fewer than `k − 1`, since `k − 1` trustees know the summed polynomials everywhere under every option.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-18 part 3): the trustee registry counts distinct seats, in strictly increasing order, never signatures, so one seat or one stolen key signing twice can't reach the quorum; the quorum is at least `k` ([[contracts]], Trustee registry).
- 2026-10-12 (P1-15 part 3, [[tally]], Destruction): once every cell has a passing opening anchored, or at `t_D` (the later of the tally window's end and the anchoring deadline of the segment before the one containing it, read from L1), each trustee destroys its per-election KEM secret, every stored share ciphertext and decrypted share, and its own and received summed shares, signs a destruction statement (record type `0x000f`) with its per-election signing key and then destroys that key. The verifier reports each seat `destroyed`, `destruction-early` or `destruction-missing` (verifier 8.5) and never fails an election on it. Residual unchanged: destruction can't be proven, so the statement is a signed public claim, and `k` seats that keep their material can still open the ballots they hold shares of.
- 2026-10-12 (P3-4 part 1, [[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]]): the panel holds a long-lived, `k`-of-`n` Tier 2 nullifier key (no public key; Pedersen VSS only), resharing it on any panel change; resharing relies on old shares' erasure, so any `k` members of one past epoch keep the key. `k` trustees together with a document's chip data can tell whether it registered and which commitment it registered; fewer can't.
- 2026-10-12 (P3-4 part 2b-2a, [[nullifier-key]]): each seat holds its share of the Tier 2 key, its registration KEM secret and signing secret for the key's life; fewer than `k` seats, with every reveal the transcript publishes, learn nothing about `sk`. The trustee role description must say so (P1-7).
- 2026-10-13 (P3-4 part 2b-2b, [[nullifier-reshare]]): the Tier 2 key can be reshared to a new panel or epoch, so at most `k − 1` old and `k' − 1` new shares learn nothing about `sk`, and each epoch's corruptions are bounded, but only if old seats erase their shares once the successor counts; `k` old shares kept anywhere still give `sk`, and no erasure can be proven. The trustee role description must say so (P1-7). New seats also erase everything an abandoned run gave them once its deadline passes, since an abandoned run's shares are a full sharing of `sk` that no later reshare refreshes; that too rests on erasure alone.
- 2026-10-13 (P2-16 part 1a, [[election-definition]] Layout (version 6), [[trustee-identity]] Author panel identifier): a version-6 definition can name an author panel, which holds one additive half of each ballot's opening, so a ballot opens only with both panels' thresholds and the author panel only adds protection; its keys must be distinct and none a default seat's (`author-panel`, 2.5 `default-seat`), so no trustee holds shares of both halves. A poll on the civic record needs a default panel the release pins as `independent` (2.5 `panel-kind`), so the transitional panel, under which ballot secrecy doesn't hold against us, runs only everyday polls. The sharing itself is part 2's.
- 2026-10-13 (P2-16 part 1b, `packages/crypto`): `electionDefinitionV6Rule` refuses an author panel with a key in two seats or a threshold outside `1 ≤ k_A ≤ n_A`, and `authorPanelRule` checks rule 4 (no author key a default seat's) given the pinned default panel, so the ballot client, trustee nodes and the verifier share one check before any share exists. Nothing calls rule 4 yet: the ballot version with both halves is part 2's, and the verifier's 2.5 part 4's.
