# T-49: Domain or hosting seizure

- Area: Availability and censorship resistance (G-10)
- Adversary: A-7, A-1
- Status: **Planned.** Residual: losing the GitHub organisation stops development and releases, not elections in progress. Seizing our hosting or the board's anchoring key for longer than the anchoring deadline while an election runs fails it permanently ([[board]], Timing). A mirror plan belongs in a runbook.

## Threat

abolish.us (`.us` is government-administered), Railway, Cloudflare or GitHub take us down.

## Mitigation

The ballot client lives on IPFS behind `abolishus.eth` and a second non-US-TLD domain (P4-3, P4-4); the verifier needs only chain + IPFS (P4-6); `apps/node` lets anyone serve and verify (P4-5).

- 2026-10-17 (P4-3 part 2b-2b-2, [[verifier]], Board by CID, Gateways): the verifier also reads the board copy by CID from IPFS gateways the user names, over the trustless gateway API's raw-block requests (such as `apps/node`'s), after `blocks.car` where the bundle holds one, so an election can be checked with nothing exported by hand and our servers and domain offline. A gateway's answer counts only if it hashes to the CID asked for.

Index: [[THREAT_MODEL]].

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P1-16): board anchors count only before their deadline ([[board]], Timing), so seizing our hosting or anchoring key for longer than the deadline while an election runs fails it permanently. P4-1 must set the deadline and run anchoring to survive one outage.
- 2026-10-12 (P4-1 part 1): the anchoring deadline is 12 hours, justified against outages and censorship in [[contracts]], Board anchor; each anchor call can carry the archive locator of [[0005-permanent-archive]], so the archive is found from L1 once our domain and hosting are gone.
- 2026-10-12 (P4-4 part 1, [[0020-entry-points]]): the second domain is a country-code TLD outside US jurisdiction (`.ch` recommended, `.is` the fallback), with registry lock, DNSSEC and nameservers from two providers; `abolishus.eth` can't be taken through a registry or registrar, only through the release Safe's keys or expiry, and anyone can renew it. Seizing the second domain takes one entry point and orphans every recovery backup (its name is the RP ID); voters at the ENS or `apps/node` entry points can still vote only with an identity held at that origin until a voter-initiated transfer between origins exists.
- 2026-10-13 (P4-3 part 2b-1): each anchored checkpoint names its segment's CIDs on L1, so with our domain and hosting gone the board's objects are found by CID from L1 and IPFS alone, and checked against the anchored roots; the verifier's fetch by CID is part 2b-2.
- 2026-10-17 (P1-12f part 3a, [[content-addressing]], Reading by CID): `packages/crypto` reads an object of known length from any sources' blocks by its CID, each block checked against its CID, so a leaf object is read with our servers gone from whoever holds its blocks; the network fetch and objects without a signed length (segment objects) come with the verifier's fetch ([[P4-3]] part 2b-2).
- 2026-10-17 (P4-1 part 2a): the anchoring run retries a failed call on every run while the checkpoint's window is open and reports it as overdue past half the deadline and missed after it, so an outage shorter than the deadline costs no anchor if the worker runs again within it. Alerting on those lines, the second runner and the Safe are part 2b; the seal's checkpoint isn't taken yet.
- 2026-10-17 (P4-1 part 2b-1): the anchoring run's chain adapter refuses to start against an anchor whose finalized clock or authority isn't the configured one, simulates each call before sending, and counts a call as made only once the anchor holds its checkpoints, so a call that reached the Safe but not the anchor is retried while its window is open rather than reported as anchored. The Safe's sender, alerting and the second runner are part 2b-2.
- 2026-10-17 (P4-3 part 2b-2a, [[content-addressing]], Reading within a bound; [[board]], A copy from L1's CIDs): `packages/crypto` reads a segment object by its CID within the bound its checkpoints give, and the whole board from the CIDs on L1, over sources that may wait on the network, so a node with our domain and hosting gone rebuilds the board from L1 and whoever holds its blocks. The verifier's sources over the network come next (part 2b-2b).
- 2026-10-17 (P4-3 part 2b-2b-1, [[verifier]], Board by CID): the verifier reads a board copy by the CIDs on L1 from a local block store (`blocks.car`, a CARv1 exported from any IPFS node) in place of `board.bin`, with the checkpoints from their `CheckpointAnchored` logs, so a user can verify from L1 and IPFS data alone with our domain and hosting gone. The store is untrusted: every block is hashed against the CID asked for and every entry against the anchored heads and roots, so a bad store only leaves the copy short (`checkpoint-missing`, unverifiable). Sources over the network (HTTP trustless gateways) are the next part.
- 2026-10-17 (P4-5 part 1): `apps/node` mirrors blocks: anyone can serve the CAR files they hold, each block hashed against its CID before it is kept, over the IPFS trustless gateway API, so the board's objects can be fetched by CID from community nodes with our domain and hosting gone. Fetching what to hold from L1's CIDs is part 2.
- 2026-10-17 (P4-3 part 2b-2c): the reference election rebuilds each board from L1 alone, the checkpoints from BoardAnchor's `CheckpointAnchored` logs and the copy by the CIDs they name, from a block store exported from a community node (`apps/node`) and from that node's gateway with no copy in the bundle, and a store that withholds the board must leave the election unverifiable, never failed. Every run of the end-to-end check now proves the fetch by CID works with our servers out of the path; the database wipe and the other results wait on the stages that write them.
- 2026-10-17 (P4-5 part 3a): `abolish-node verify` runs the verifier over every election the board anchor names with no input from our servers: the board read by CID from the node's own blocks, the anchors proven at the finalized L1 block, and the definition, its creation's L1 origin and its panel from the L2 registries' logs, each passing the contract-event check. It writes each bundle beside the verifier's report and `serve --reports` publishes the reports at the node's origin. The L2 logs, the L1 header and the anchor's clock are still unproven inputs, so the verdict stays unverifiable; the reference election requires the node's checks to equal the verifier's on its own bundles.
- 2026-10-18 (P4-1 part 2b-2a, `apps/worker` `src/anchor-safe.ts`): anchoring through the Safe exists: the sender signs the anchor call as a Safe v1.4.1 transaction with the threshold's owner keys and requires the Safe's `ExecutionSuccess` for exactly that transaction, and the anchor's own state is confirmed after it (part 2b-1), so a call the anchor refused is never counted as an anchor made. The run isn't scheduled until part 2b-2b adds the second runner and alerting, so nothing is anchored yet.
- 2026-10-18 (P4-1 part 2b-2b-1, `apps/worker`): the anchoring run is scheduled once configured, every five minutes by default (`ANCHOR_EVERY_MS`, at least a minute, and never longer than the anchor's period, so each checkpoint is sent soon after its period ends, or its deadline, so no checkpoint's window opens and closes between two runs), and the runner refuses to start on a node of another chain, an anchor fixing another clock or authority, or a Safe other than the record's, so a misconfiguration fails at startup rather than at the first deadline. Still open: a second runner on independent hosting and alerting a person well before a deadline (part 2b-2b-2), and the anchor's deployment.
- 2026-10-26 (P4-3 part 4b-2b-1, `packages/core` `anchorBoards`): anchoring runs hold a session lock on the worker's database, so two workers (a deploy's old and new) never sign at the same Safe and executor nonce at once, where one run's call could replace the other's pending checkpoint call and miss its window. A run that meets the lock does nothing (`anchor-busy`, logged), and the holding run sends what is due; another worker's runs meeting it without a break for more than a quarter of the deadline fail its job, since a worker that froze holding the lock keeps every run out. Not mitigated: that alarm needs a second worker; a lone worker whose run hangs holding the lock runs no later anchoring job at all (its queue runs one at a time), which was already so before the lock. Nothing ends a frozen holder's session on its own; an operator must, within the remaining three quarters of each window.
