# P3-2: Semaphore groups per tier

- State: in progress (part 1, the group registry with Tier 0 additions and removals, merged in #104; part 4a, [[0024-tier0-registration]], merged in #151; part 4b, the registration spec, next; Tier 1 and Tier 2 addition paths wait on P3-3 and P3-4)
- Branch: `claude/p3-2`
- Phase: Phase 3: Tier 2 ZK identity, Semaphore groups, indexer, per-tier results

## Scope

Semaphore groups per tier on L2, and a test that no on-chain data links a member to any poll.

## Plan

1. **The group registry and Tier 0** (this part): [[contracts]], Group registry. Groups whose tier is fixed at creation; Tier 0 additions and removals approved by a threshold of registrars pinned at deployment (EIP-712, [[0004-l2-choice]]), each signed batch bound to its group, tier and position; forbidden commitments refused ([[membership]], Commitments and removals); every record logged whole through the event accumulator so verifier 2.4 rebuilds tier trees from the chain. `packages/crypto` decodes the records and checks the approvals, for the verifier and the signing service.
2. **Tier 1 additions**, with [[0014-tier1-vouching]] (P3-3 parts 2 and 3), once Semaphore proofs verify (P1-17b).
3. **Tier 2 additions**, with document proofs (P3-4, [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]]).
4. **Tier 0 registration and sponsorship**, under [[0024-tier0-registration]]:
   - 4a (#151): the ADR. Registration is account-free, on the ballot path, each paid for by its own Turnstile solve on a separate challenge page, and batched on a fixed period with a shuffled order;
   - 4b: the registration spec and the challenge page's handoff to the ballot client;
   - 4c: our ERC-4337 registration paymaster ([[0004-l2-choice]]);
   - 4d: the signing service (route, `tier0-register` Turnstile action, queue and batch job, refusing forbidden and duplicate commitments, and whether it still signs a commitment whose self-paid copy appeared after it accepted the request, [[0024-tier0-registration]]) with its T-10 tests.
5. **A self-paid Tier 0 addition** (option D of [[0024-tier0-registration]]): the brief's direct-submit path at Tier 0, in the group registry and verifier 2.4, with [[membership]]'s first-occurrence rule changed so a self-paid copy never displaces an earlier registration, before any registry is deployed.

No group registry may be deployed before parts 2 and 3 exist: a deployed registry's rules can't change.

## Part 1: the group registry and Tier 0

Built:

- [[contracts]], Group registry: the spec, with two departures from [[0004-l2-choice]] listed under Decisions to review (registrars pinned at deployment rather than read from the Safe; dedicated keys, never the Safe's owners). [[verifier]] 2.4 names the registrars' approvals for Tier 0.
- `packages/contracts/src/GroupRegistry.sol`: `createGroup(uint8)` (Tier 0 only for now, `group_id` a counter up to 2⁶⁴ − 1), `addMembers(bytes)` and `removeMembers(bytes)` with canonical calldata and exact lengths, forbidden commitments refused, each batch bound to its group, tier and position (a removal to the member count it saw), and every approval checked (v ∈ {27, 28}, `r` and `s` in range, low-s, then OpenZeppelin's `ECDSA.tryRecover`). Every event goes through the event accumulator and carries the whole record. No owner, proxy, pause or tree. OpenZeppelin 5.6.1's `ECDSA.sol` is vendored unmodified.
- `packages/crypto/src/group-registry.ts`: strict decoders and encoders for both records, the EIP-712 domain separator and digests, `isForbiddenCommitment`, and `checkGroupApprovals` against a pinned registrar set (`@noble/curves` secp256k1 recovery, `@noble/hashes` Keccak), for verifier 2.4 and the signing service.
- `docs/spec/vectors/group-registry.json`, printed by `packages/crypto/scripts/group-registry-vectors.py`, an independent standard-library implementation that first reproduces EIP-712's own example and RFC 6979's secp256k1 signature.
- Threat files T-06, T-12, T-13, T-17 and T-62.

Tested:

- Both implementations against every vector: domain, type and struct hashes, digests, each approval's signer, and every rejected record with its error and seat. The TypeScript tests reproduce every signature byte for byte (RFC 6979).
- Contract: a test per refusal, stateful ones included (unknown group, wrong position, the 2³² leaf bound, the group-id bound, a removal's size and indices, replays), fuzzing of one-bit flips, arbitrary bytes and signatures by other keys, an invariant suite with a shadow model, the PII checks of the contracts skill, and a test that runs an election through the election registry alongside and finds no group-registry calldata or event carrying its identifier or definition hash.
- `packages/crypto`: fast-check round trips, refusal of arbitrary bytes and of every single-bit flip of a signed record, and a test per check showing that removing it lets a bad record through (where `@noble/curves` itself refuses the same input, the check is kept as defence in depth).

Known-weak (also in [[contracts]], Group registry, Known-weak):

- Tier 0 is the registrars' word (T-06): any `t` of them can add any commitments; the registry makes that attributable and unreplayable, not checked.
- The registrar quorum is held online by the signing service (T-69); dedicated keys keep the blast radius to Tier 0.
- Registrars can't rotate without a new registry and new groups.
- Duplicate commitments and the batch queue are the signing service's to manage (part 4).
- Tier 1 and Tier 2 have no addition path yet; no registry may be deployed until they do.
- `Poseidon2(0, 1)` comes from `poseidon-lite` 0.3.0 run outside the repository, not from our own Poseidon; P1-17b must check it.

## Independent review (part 1)

A fresh in-session subagent reviewed the diff against `.github/review/crypto-review.md`, reran the generator (identical vectors), fetched `poseidon-lite` 0.3.0 and OpenZeppelin 5.6.1 itself to confirm `Poseidon2(0, 1)` and the vendored `ECDSA.sol`, and ran both test suites. Nothing blocking. Findings and dispositions:

- **Non-blocking, fixed:** the registrars are in storage, outside verifier 1.2's bytecode check, so a registry deployed from release bytecode with other keys would accept additions verifiers don't count, and its removals would then zero other members' leaves in the verifier's numbering. The verifier now fails every group of a registry whose `RegistrarsPinned` differs from its pinned set (Verification; verifier 2.4).
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** registrar keys held online for automatic registration must not be the Safe's owners, or seizing the registration service would seize the anchoring authority and timelocks. Registrars are dedicated keys (Deployment), a second departure from [[0004-l2-choice]] under Decisions to review.
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** a removal signed for an index not yet reached stayed valid for good and could later remove whoever landed there. Removals now sign `size`, the member count when signed; the registry requires `size` at or below the count and every index below `size`, with tests that such a removal can't reach later members.
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** T-17's residual named registrars as choosing commitments; registrants do (about 253 bits each, on L2 for good), and registrars choose approval nonces.
- **Nits, fixed:** the verifier's counting rule now matches the code (every approval verifies); the immutables verifier 1.2 compares are listed; "will be specified"; the signing service signs one batch per position and submits its own records.
- **Re-review of the fixes:** all resolved, nothing blocking. Its three nits: a test that the removal checks run in the spec's order (added), verifier 2.4 stating the pinned-registrar rule itself (added), and the removal gas figure (re-measured: about 86,000 per call excluding base and calldata, as stated).

## `crypto-review` on #104

- **Blocking, fixed:** the verifier numbered leaves by log order and never rechecked a record's signed `first_index` or a removal's signed `size`, so the replay and reorder protection rested on L2 state, which T-71 treats as rewritable (governance, a false output root): a replayed, reordered or omitted batch would have inflated a group, dropped members or misapplied removals undetected. The verifier now replays each group with its own count and every registry check, and a record that breaks the signed sequence fails every definition naming the group (`group-history`). `applyGroupRecords` in `packages/crypto` does exactly that replay; `sequences` vectors (honest, replayed, reordered, omitted, stale, unsigned, forbidden) are checked by the Python generator, the TypeScript replay and the contract, which refuses exactly the histories the verifier rejects. The residual is a dropped tail, stated in T-71 and Known-weak.
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** the library refused records with forbidden commitments while the spec kept them with leaf 0; the replay rule now fails the group on any such record, so both agree, with a vector.
- **Non-blocking, fixed:** T-71's Mitigation still named the Safe's owners for Tier 0, and a NatSpec comment placed the registrar keys in hardware wallets; both corrected. ADR 0004 itself is unchanged under the touch-only rule; its departures are under Decisions to review.
- `claude-review`'s non-blocking finding (T-69 had no entry for the online registrar quorum): added.
- Untested: the `c + count ≤ 2³²` part of the replay's position check, which needs a history of 2³² leaves; the contract tests the same bound by setting its count directly.
- In-session re-review of the fix: one blocking finding, fixed (verifier 2.4 and [[membership]] still said forbidden commitments and stray removals never fail a definition; both now defer to the group registry's strict replay, and keep the lenient rules for group contracts without a signed sequence). Non-blocking, stated rather than closed: a rewrite can drop any removal, not only the tail, since removals carry no sequence number (`omitted-removal` vector; Verification, T-71, T-06, T-13). Nits fixed: the verifier's report order per record is specified, with a `two-faults` vector; records are selected by their events' topics, which must match the record; the Python replay checks the group too, with an `other-group` vector. The 2³² bound stays untested in the replay.
- Left for P1-19 (verifier part 4, which replays the registry's events): checking each record against its event's topics in the library, as `applyGroupRecords` takes records already selected by topic; the spec states the rule.

## Part 4a: [[0024-tier0-registration]]

Built: the ADR (option B with option D as the direct path, accepted by the agent, owner may veto), the Decisions to review entry in STATUS.md, and history lines in T-01, T-10, T-11, T-13, T-16, T-25 and T-52. Docs only.

Why B: an account costs one passkey ceremony and one Turnstile solve, so registering one commitment per account (option A) charges a Sybil exactly what a solve per registration does, while recording account → commitment for every registrant; account-gated blind tokens (option C, RFC 9497 and 9578) avoid the record at the price of a new protocol and issuer key, and buy nothing over B while accounts are free.

Known-weak:

- Cloudflare, terminating TLS, sees each registration request whole (address, token and commitment), and Railway may log the address; a join by address stays T-11's residual (T-11, T-67).
- Until part 5, Tier 0 registration can be refused by the registrars or Cloudflare, with no direct-submit path (T-25).
- Anyone can queue registrations at the price of a solve (T-52); 4b fixes the excess rule. A period's batch may hold a single registration at low volume (T-13).
- If the owner reads the brief's "Tier 0: passkey" or Phase 2's "at Tier 0 with accounts" as requiring an account to vote, option C replaces B before 4d builds the route.

Independent review (in-session subagent): four blocking findings, all fixed. Cloudflare sees the commitment, since it terminates TLS for the registration request (the ADR had said it didn't); the edge keeps rate-limit matches with addresses (the ADR had said it kept none); the brief's direct-submit rule went unmet at Tier 0 without saying so (now option D, part 5); and Phase 2's "with accounts" wasn't weighed (now stated as a reading the owner may veto). Non-blocking, fixed: the `turnstile.ts` rationale and host check (4d), [[contracts]]' stale Known-weak (4b), B's remaining join by address, the batch's anonymity set, no arrival time in the queue and BullMQ's events stream, T-11 and T-52 history lines, a citation, T-18 and T-46 in the threat list, the token handoff's form.

Re-review of the fixes: one blocking finding, fixed: as first written, option D refused a commitment already live in the tier on chain, which would let anyone kill a signed batch by self-adding one of its commitments first; D must never make a signed batch revert, and part 5 decides how duplicates across the two paths are handled. `claude-review` then found the second way, also fixed: a self-paid addition to a signed group would move the position every in-flight batch binds, so self-paid additions go only into self-paid groups, never signed against (and T-52 records both hazards). Its next pass found that D, with the contract leaving duplicates to the verifier, would let anyone move a voter between regional cells through the first-occurrence rule; fixed by requiring part 5 to make signed groups win over self-paid ones and, among self-paid groups, the earliest addition in chain order (T-16 records it). A further pass found that a signed copy displaces a self-paid registrant even when it lands later; kept as stated (ranking by chain order across both kinds would let cheap self-paid copies displace signed registrants), with the residual in the ADR and T-16, and 4b and 4d required to check self-paid groups against the latest chain state at request and before signing. Non-blocking, fixed: D's Sybil cost is below a solve, not equal to one; T-01's line names D; the literal reading of "Tier 0: passkey" (a passkey on the ballot RP ID at registration) is stated with why it's left out; the backup isn't called optional; a typo in T-13 and two overstatements.

CI's `claude-review`: one blocking finding, fixed: the check before signing lets a party that sees a request before its batch (Cloudflare, Railway, a compelled deploy) self-add the queued commitment into a self-paid group of its chosen region and so move the registrant; now a stated residual in the ADR and T-16, detected by the client, with part 4d to decide whether to sign such a commitment anyway. Non-blocking, fixed: T-16 added to the list of history lines.
