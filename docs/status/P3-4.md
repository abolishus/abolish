## What was decided (part 1)

[[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]], `Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`, option A: threshold 2HashDH (Jarecki–Kiayias–Krawczyk; TOPPSS) evaluated by the panel, with the key shared by Pedersen VSS (GJKR's first phase, no public key) and the nullifier `N = H2(x, k · H1(x))` kept with the attesters, never boarded. Rejected: RFC 9497's VOPRF with a public key and `N` on the board (a discrete-log break or the issuing state's own queries list the registrants), and an MPC-evaluated symmetric PRF (no audited implementation).

## Known-weak

- Anyone holding a document's chip data (the issuing state first) can query for it as its holder would, and learns its `N` and whether it registered (passive authentication only). The panel publishes query and duplicate counts so a mass probe is visible.
- A Diffie–Hellman OPRF leaves discrete-log relations on the key: a discrete-log break recovers the OPRF key `sk` from one recorded or self-made registration and, with chip data, maps a stolen attester list.
- `sk` is long-lived and held by people, unlike [[0006-trustees]] rule 7's per-election keys; resharing relies on erasure of old shares, and losing `n − k + 1` shares means re-registering Tier 2.
- Registration needs six of seven seats online; the attesters' nullifier lists aren't rebuilt from chain and IPFS.
- The query and output proofs carry `dg1` in their witness and are only computationally zero-knowledge towards the seats.
- Under the QR handoff the proof app must run the query; a malicious build learns `x` and `N` (T-19).

## Independent review (part 1)

An in-session subagent reviewed the ADR against the `crypto-review` prompt. Its two blocking findings were fixed: the attesters couldn't check the combined evaluation `Z` (now each recombines it from the seats' signed, proven answers, and `B` and `Z` are public inputs of the output proof), and one corrupt or lagging seat shared by two quorums could attest a duplicate (now `max(k, n − 1)` seats sign each group position, only once every earlier `N` reached them). Its non-blocking findings and nits were all taken: the evaluation proof as a proof of knowledge with `J` from hash-to-group and signed answers, the output proof's binding to the registration record, the online-probe residual for any chip-data holder, the active-authentication wording, harvested pairs and registration KEM keys, the key's lifecycle against rule 7, the owner attribution, SOD signature malleability, who runs the query, zero knowledge towards the seats, scoped hashes with Poseidon2 for `H2`, the threshold one-more Diffie–Hellman assumption, and a new nullifier type with a MOCK test key.
