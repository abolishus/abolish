# ADR 0023: Receipt evidence

- Status: Accepted (agent) — owner may veto
- Date: 2026-10-12
- Deciders: agent, owner may veto (rule A)
- Threats addressed: T-26, T-25, T-24, T-23, T-45, T-11, T-41, T-42, T-53, T-69, T-71 (see [[THREAT_MODEL]])

## Context

[[0013-ballot-receipts]] gave the voter a receipt: the board operator's P-256 signature on the board state it showed, with the voter's entry last, checked for inclusion at cast ([[receipts]]). What that receipt is evidence of later, to the voter and to anyone else, was split out to P1-13b after reviews of a first draft found five problems that have to be solved together ([[receipts]], Later checks):

- **Time.** A receipt carries no time. Whoever obtains a receipt key after an election (a leak, a seizure, a compelled hand-over, T-69) could otherwise forge evidence against that election for good, so evidence must be bounded in time, and a voter's evidence of a real drop must survive the bound.
- **Withheld boards.** The anchoring authority can anchor a fork that no checkpoint contradicts from L1 alone and withhold the board until any bound has passed ([[board]], Residuals).
- **Keys.** The receipt keys that judge an election must be fixed before voting starts, so a later release can't void receipts and a key not bound to the election can't forge against it.
- **Presence.** A dropped ballot is shown only by a valid ballot that no admitted entry of the anchored board carries by content, `(tier, ν, M)`; a rejected or re-randomised copy proves nothing ([[revoting]], Owned elsewhere).
- **Privacy.** Anything a voter posts or fetches to keep or check evidence must not name the entry to the operator or to the source (T-45, T-11).

Two more requirements come from elsewhere: the voter's later check must show the ballot's admission verdict and whether it is the selected ballot of its tier and nullifier, since Benaloh's challenge doesn't audit the L1 reference or `M` ([[revoting]], Owned elsewhere; T-41, T-42); and after any bound, the voter's client still shows its own result, marked as no longer evidence (G-7).

What is one-way: where the receipt keys are bound becomes a field of the election definition or a contract every verifier reads; the commitment format and the dispute window become constants every verifier applies to every past election; and the verdicts a third party reaches are what the dispute runbook and the public rely on.

## Options

Each option answers three questions: where the keys that judge an election are fixed, how evidence is bounded in time, and how a client checks its receipt without naming it.

### A. Keys registered on L1 by the anchoring authority and listed in the definition, a dispute window kept by salted L1 commitments, and whole-board hash lists

- **Keys.** The election definition gains `receipt_keys`, a short sorted list of P-256 keys (at most 4, so a key can rotate during an election), in the next definition version not yet frozen. Each key MUST also be registered on **L1**, in an append-only receipt-key registry with no removal that only the election's anchoring authority (our Safe on L1, [[board]], Anchoring) can write, the anchor contract itself or a contract beside it, in a finalized L1 block whose timestamp is before `opens_at`. The same registry keeps an append-only retirement record, written only by the same authority: a key retired in a finalized L1 block at or before `opens_at` can't judge that election, while a retirement after `opens_at` changes nothing for it, so retiring a key never voids receipts already given. A verifier proves each registration and retirement from L1 state with storage proofs, as it proves anchors ([[contracts]], Board anchor, Verification), and fails a definition listing a key that isn't registered in time or was retired by `opens_at`; the ballot client checks the same before casting. The authority retires a key as soon as it suspects a leak, and every key when it rotates it, so a seized key can't be listed in a later election by a hostile creator. Nothing about the keys rests on L2: the L2 can't read L1 state cheaply, and L2 governance can forge a transaction from any address there (T-71, [[0004-l2-choice]]), so an L2 registry trusting its sender would let it register a key of its own and frame the anchoring authority. On L1 the authority is already trusted, by its sender, for the checkpoints the evidence is judged against, so the keys add no new trust. So every key that judges an election is fixed before voting starts, by L1 records neither a release, the operator nor L2 governance can retract or forge, and is the anchoring authority's own: evidence under it is attributed to the anchoring authority, which also anchors the checkpoints the evidence is judged against, so no verdict can split the blame between two parties. A creator can't list a key of their own to frame the operator. The client accepts a receipt at cast only under a key the definition lists, checked against the same L1 registry; a release no longer pins receipt keys.
- **Time.** Each election has a dispute end, `t_E = end of the seal's anchoring window + DISPUTE_WINDOW`. `DISPUTE_WINDOW` (30 days) is a constant of the receipt version the definition's profile pins for record type `0x0004` (the profile already pins every type from `0x0001` to `0x0009`), and a receipt of any other version is no evidence for that election, so no later receipt version can lengthen an old election's window. A receipt is evidence for a judge whose latest finalized L1 block is before `t_E`; after that, only if its **commitment** was on L1 in a finalized block whose timestamp is at most `t_E`. The commitment is `C = H(DS("abolish/v1/receipt-commitment", salt ‖ encode(receipt)))` with a fresh 32-byte CSPRNG salt, carried as a 32-byte-aligned word of the calldata of any L1 transaction included in a block (to any address or none, reverted or not) whose calldata is a whole number of 32-byte words. The opening keeps the salt, the transaction, its proof in the block's transaction trie, the execution block header, and a proof that the header is canonical and finalized that a judge can check from a recent finalized beacon state: the header's hash in its beacon block's execution payload, and that beacon block's root in the state's `historical_summaries` (Capella), or in its `block_roots` while the block's 8192-slot period hasn't completed (about 27 hours), each a SHA-256 SSZ Merkle proof. So the evidence needs no old L1 block or log from anyone, as [[contracts]] (Board anchor, Verification) needs none for anchors, and survives history expiry ([EIP-4444](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4444)). A key obtained after `t_E` forges nothing, since no new commitment can predate `t_E`; a commitment made before `t_E` keeps the receipt evidence for good, however long the board is withheld.
- **When the client commits.** The client commits a receipt once it is **in doubt**: a later-check outcome of `unanchored`, `forked` or `unresolved` (below), or still `pending` within 7 days of `t_E`. `pending` alone isn't doubt, so a client checking during the election never commits near its cast time (T-11), and neither is `board-failed`, whose failure is attributed without the receipt. It may commit every receipt at cast if the voter chooses. It sends the commitment through a relayer that batches many voters' words into one transaction (community nodes, P4-5; any relayer, since the word reveals nothing), or from its own funded address, and retries through another if the word isn't on L1 within an hour. An honest receipt never needs one.
- **Privacy of the check.** The client fetches the entry-hash list of **every** segment of its election (32 bytes per entry, a published object derived from the segment objects, P4-3, fetched from any source by content address or from a community node it runs, P4-5), checks them against every counted checkpoint's root, and computes every proof itself, so what it fetches is the same for every voter and names no entry. For admission and selection it reads a whole verifier report ([[verifier]], 8) by the same route, never a query for its own nullifier. When it fetches mustn't depend on its segment either: it fetches once at a uniformly random time between the end of the seal's anchoring window and 14 days before `t_E`, and, while the election runs, may fetch on a fixed schedule that is the same for every voter.
- **Verdicts.** A third party holding the receipt, the payload it covers and, after `t_E`, the commitment's opening, judges it from the definition, L1 and, for some verdicts, the board. Let `s` be the receipt's segment and `K_s` the first counted checkpoint for a segment at or after `s`:
  - `not-evidence`: it doesn't decode under the pinned receipt version; the key isn't in the definition's `receipt_keys`; the signature fails; `election_id` isn't the election's; `entry_hash` isn't the entry built from `election_id`, `index`, `segment` and the payload; the inclusion path doesn't verify `entry_hash` at `index` under `root` (the checks at cast, steps 1 and 3 to 7, with step 2 against the definition); `s` is outside the election's segments (before its first, or after `lastSegment`), which no client accepts at cast and the operator MUST refuse to append; or the judge is past `t_E` with no admissible commitment.
  - `board-failed`: the board checks that need no copy already fail the election ([[board]], Board checks, steps 1 and 2, and `unsealed`); the receipt adds nothing to that verdict, which is attributed already.
  - `pending`: segment `s`'s anchoring window hasn't closed.
  - `unanchored`, attributable to the anchoring authority, from L1 alone: no counted checkpoint is for segment `s` though its window has closed. A valid receipt shows the operator boarded an entry in `s`, and every segment with entries must have a checkpoint ([[board]], Timing), so dropping the voter's entry and leaving `s` unanchored can't escape every verdict.
  - `forked`, attributable to the anchoring authority: the operator signed a board state that isn't a prefix of the anchored board. From L1 alone: a counted checkpoint for a segment before `s` has a size above `index`, or a counted checkpoint for `s` or later has a size at most `index`. With board data: a consistency proof from `(index + 1, r′)` to `K_s`, with `r′ ≠ root`, which anyone holding the entry-hash list can produce and which is binding under collision resistance of the tagged tree hashes.
  - `included`: a consistency proof from `(index + 1, root)` to `K_s` verifies, so entry `index` of the anchored board is the voter's entry.
  - `unresolved`: no copy of the anchored board up to `K_s` is available, so neither proof can be made; the election is then unverifiable ([[board]], Residuals: withheld tails), and the receipt stays evidence, committed, until a copy is found.
- **Drops.** An `unanchored` or `forked` receipt is also a **drop** if its payload is a ballot record that passes every admission check that depends only on the record, the definition and finalized L1 ([[verifier]] 5.1 to 5.5, and the L1-reference checks of [[revoting]]), and no admitted entry of the anchored board carries its `(tier, ν, M)`, whatever its selection outcome. A copy with the same `(tier, ν, M)` (a re-randomised proof, a direct-submitted copy, the client's resubmission) means the ballot wasn't lost, so the verdict stands and the drop doesn't; an entry carrying it that is rejected doesn't count as present. A ballot the close rule (5.5) rejects could never have counted, so its loss isn't a drop. Judging a drop needs the whole anchored board, which every verifier holds anyway.
- **What the voter sees.** After `t_E`, a client holding an uncommitted receipt still shows its own outcome, marked as no longer evidence. For an `included` receipt it shows the ballot's admission verdict and whether it is the selected ballot of its tier and nullifier, from the report it fetched, finding the ballot there by its tracking code ([[0021-challenged-ballots]]); a ballot that isn't admitted, or a tier and nullifier whose selected ballot isn't the latest one the voter cast, is an alarm, except a tie the voter made on purpose ([[revoting]], Client rule 5).

- **Pros:**
  - **Every requirement has a mechanism:** a time bound that leaked keys can't cross, committed evidence that withholding can't outlast, keys fixed before voting by records no release or operator can retract, presence by content, and fetches that name nothing.
  - **One accountable party.** Receipt keys and checkpoints both belong to the anchoring authority, so every verdict names it alone, and nobody else's key can frame it.
  - **The honest path costs nothing on chain:** only receipts in doubt are committed, and the commitment needs only L1 calldata.
  - `included`, `unanchored` and the L1-only `forked` cases need a proof of logarithmic size, so a dispute can be judged without the whole board; only a drop needs it.
- **Cons:**
  - **Before `t_E`, a leaked or compelled key forges evidence** against the anchoring authority, indistinguishably from its own signature (T-69, T-53), for every election that opened before the key was retired: a leak found only after `opens_at` still forges for elections already open, until their `t_E`. It answers for its keys' custody; the dispute window and retirement are what bound this. A key seized together with the anchoring authority, so it is never retired, forges for every later election that lists it, but that authority can fail elections anyway ([[board]], Residuals).
  - **A voter whose client doesn't check before `t_E` loses evidence** unless it committed at cast.
  - **Committing costs L1 gas,** paid by a relayer or the voter, and a relayer can refuse (the client then uses another, or its own address, whose funding can link it to having a receipt in doubt, though never to which).
  - **The full hash list is 32 bytes per entry** (32 MB for a million ballots), heavy for a phone on a weak connection; a community node the voter runs (P4-5) or a later private-retrieval scheme is the alternative.
  - **Admission and selection come from a report** that the client didn't compute itself; it trusts the verifier it chose, and can compare two.
  - **A new L1 registry and a beacon-state proof** in the verifier's trust base: an append-only L1 contract written by the anchoring authority (P4-1, P1-18), its registrations read with the storage proofs the verifier already uses for anchors, and SSZ Merkle proofs over SHA-256 (`@noble/hashes`), a published format composed, not a new primitive. Each receipt key costs an L1 transaction by the Safe, once per key, not per election.
  - A definition version, a parameter and a domain tag to add.

### B. Keys from the release current when the election opens; no time bound; proofs fetched per receipt

The ballot client's release pins the receipt keys, and an election is judged under the release current at its `opens_at`, read from the signed release history (P0-3). Receipts stay evidence for good. The client asks a mirror for the consistency proof from its own receipt.

- **Pros:** no definition change, no commitments, no gas, and small fetches.
- **Cons:** fails three requirements. Release history is an off-chain record that a later release can rewrite or contest, so the key set isn't fixed by anything a verifier reads from the election itself. A key leaked or compelled after the election forges evidence forever (T-69). A proof request for one size names the voter's entry to the mirror, and so its nullifier and every re-vote (T-45, T-11).

### C. As A, but every receipt committed at cast through an L2 contract

The client commits every receipt at cast through a contract on the L2, which stamps it with the block time. Evidence is bounded by the L2 timestamp.

- **Pros:** every receipt is evidence for good, whether or not the client ever checks again; L2 gas is cheap.
- **Cons:** one transaction per ballot, which someone pays for, and the commitment's timing reveals when each voter cast, to the second, beside the ballot's own L1 block (T-11). The L2's sequencer can delay or censor a commitment (T-25), and its timestamps are the sequencer's within drift. L1 calldata needs neither the sequencer nor a contract, and in-doubt commitments are rare.

## Recommendation

**A.** It is the only option that meets all five requirements together. B fixes no key set a verifier can read from the election or the chain, gives forgers unbounded time and names the entry on every check; C pays for every ballot and puts the commitment behind the sequencer.

What would change it: a private-retrieval scheme cheap enough for phones, which would replace the full hash-list fetch; a dispute contract that judges evidence on chain, which would move the commitment into that contract; or the owner choosing to commit every receipt at cast and fund it.

## Consequences

- **Spec (P1-13b part 2):**
  - [[receipts]], Later checks: the dispute end, the commitment and its opening, the client's check from whole-board hash lists, the verdicts and the drop rule, with codes.
  - [[election-definition]]: `receipt_keys` (`list<p256-key, 4>`, non-empty, strictly ascending, each a canonical key) in the next definition version not yet frozen. P1-15 part 4b is writing version 2 for regional cells; whichever of the two lands second adds its field to the other's draft, and version 2 is frozen only with both.
  - [[receipts]], Checks at cast: step 2 becomes "in the definition's `receipt_keys`, registered on L1 before `opens_at` and not retired by then"; Signing: the operator never appends or signs an entry whose segment is outside the election's segments.
  - [[board]], client rule 2: replaced by the later checks; its alarms become `unanchored`, `forked` and `unresolved`.
  - The entry-hash list as a published object derived from each segment object, and how it is found (with P4-3).
  - The commitment's opening, with its beacon-state proof of the block's canonicality through either `historical_summaries` or `block_roots`.
  - [[parameters]]: `DISPUTE_WINDOW` (2,592,000 seconds); [[domain-separation]]: `abolish/v1/receipt-commitment`.
- **Contracts (P4-1, P1-18):** the append-only L1 receipt-key registry with its retirement record, both written only by the anchoring authority, and the verifier's and client's check of `receipt_keys` against it before `opens_at`.
- **Code (part 3):** the commitment, the opening's check and the verdicts in `packages/crypto`, with vectors in `docs/spec/vectors/` and a negative test per verdict; `packages/verifier` judges evidence from the board it already holds; the reference election gains a dropped receipt and a forked one.
- **Ballot client (P2-8):** commits in doubt through relayers, fetches whole hash lists and reports, and shows the outcome before and after `t_E`.
- **Runbook:** the dispute procedure, for anyone holding evidence (T-26).
- **Known-weak:** before `t_E`, whoever holds a receipt key can forge evidence against the anchoring authority; a client that never checks before `t_E` and didn't commit at cast loses its evidence; a withheld board leaves committed evidence `unresolved` and the election unverifiable, until P4-3 binds content addresses into anchors so non-publication is itself attributable; the full hash list is heavy on weak connections; the admission verdict comes from a report the client trusts; a receipt shown as evidence names the voter's entry and nullifier, as before (T-45).

## Decision

Option A, `Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`, listed under Decisions to review in [[STATUS]]. Nothing is specified, signed or frozen under it yet, so a veto changes this ADR and P1-13b's later parts only.
