## Scope

The trustee node [[0006-trustees]] (Consequences) calls for, added after the owner decided the panel's structure on 2026-10-12: the deployable each trustee runs on infrastructure it controls (an app, or a mode of `apps/node`), since open polls close too often for trustees who act by hand. It runs only what the spec already defines, calling `packages/crypto` and `packages/verifier` and never reimplementing them:

- **Ceremony** ([[ceremony]]): every node duty the page sets, among them the stub checks; fresh keys and challenge secrets per run, refusing a `(stub_hash, run_id)` already seen; at most one round-4 message per run, recorded before it is sent; reveals only over round 3 as the coordinator closed it; and approval of at most one transcript per `stub_hash`, only one with at least `max(k, n − 1)` qualified seats (rule 7) that holds every message the seat sent and whose round chain it signed, with the ceremony registration signature ([[trustee-identity]]). The list here is a summary; [[ceremony]] decides.
- **Intake** ([[share-encryption]], Intake and receipts; [[delivery]]): [[verifier]] 2.5 on the election before receipting any share; receipts during voting, complaints within the complaint window, and the contract-path receipt obligation, bounded as [[P1-12c]] specifies.
- **Tally** ([[tally]]): [[verifier]] 2.8 before releasing any share; summed shares only for cells of the registered partition over the anchored counted set, checked against the L1 anchor first; the summed-share message; openings.
- **Destruction** (P1-15 part 3): the KEM secret after the tally, the signing key after its last statement.
- **Partitions** ([[0006-trustees]], Consequences): never a share for a partition the spec doesn't require; every tier is decrypted, and small tiers carry T-16's warning; below-floor regions are folded into their tier's remainder cell, never released on their own ([[0006-trustees]], Small cells).

## Plan

Each part is one PR on this branch's name. The node is its own app, `apps/trustee-node`, rather than a mode of `apps/node` ([[P4-5]], not started): the community node holds no secret, and keeping the node that holds a seat's keys apart from it keeps its code, and so what each trustee must rebuild and inspect under rule 6, small. Merging them later is a code move, not a protocol change.

1. **Ceremony duties** (done): `CeremonySeat` over a write-once `SeatStore`: the stub checks, run replay refusal, one message per round, at most one round-4 message per run, one approval per stub with rule 7's quorum and the ceremony registration signature.
2. **Transport** (done): the node's side of the coordinator protocol (it fetches rounds and posts its messages; nothing listens on the node), its configuration (the pinned panel, the seat, the identity key from a file or a hardware token), and a command to run a ceremony between nodes and a coordinator locally.
3. **Electorate check**: [[verifier]] 2.4 from finalized L2 state through `packages/verifier`, once [[P1-19]] builds it; definition versions 3 and 4 (regional cells).
4. **Intake** ([[P1-12c]] part 2 is done): 4a, 2.5 and the definition checks, intake and share receipts, refused until part 3's electorate check as the ceremony is; 4b-1, the intake endpoint and its limits; 4b-2, the obligation decided once at `c_I`; 4b-3, complaints.
5. **Tally and destruction**: 2.8, summed shares, openings, destruction statements.
6. **Contract-path duties** (waits on P1-18's direct-submit path).

## Part 1

`apps/trustee-node`: the seat's part in the key ceremony ([[ceremony]]), every message and check from `@abolishus/crypto`. Tested with whole ceremonies between nodes through a coordinator that withholds messages (a fast-check property: any approval is of a transcript that checks with at least `max(k, n − 1)` qualified seats including the approver's, and holding every message it sent), restarts between rounds, a crash injected at every write, replayed and concurrent requests (two round 3s raced for one reveal, two transcripts raced for one approval), equivocation, rule 7 at its boundary, one approval per stub, every stub refusal (each pinned record type at a version it doesn't read among them), and the store's write-once rule, modes and directory syncs on disk and in memory (T-14, T-17, T-37, T-54).

Known-weak: no production electorate check yet (part 3), so the node runs only in tests; the duties hold only while the store is never rolled back or shared between two hosts of one seat, which the node can't detect; definition version 1 only; the run's secrets sit unencrypted in owner-only files, with no destruction yet (part 5); the node refuses an equivocating transcript but doesn't yet publish the two messages; approval asks more than the spec's minimum (all four rounds sent and its own seat qualified).

## Part 2

The transport (`apps/trustee-node` README, Transport): `TrusteeNode` polls a `Coordinator` for what it asks of the seat and posts what `CeremonySeat` answers; `HttpCoordinator` speaks it over HTTPS (HTTP only to loopback), follows no redirect and bounds every body; nothing listens on the node. The bodies are envelopes in [[notation]]'s encoding around the spec's own records (the stub, and a ceremony transcript holding the rounds closed so far), and the node recomputes `stub_hash` rather than taking it. The configuration pins the panel against its `panelId` and reads the identity key from an owner-only file (`keygen` writes one); `run` runs one seat (refusing every stub until part 3's electorate check), and `local-ceremony` runs a whole ceremony between local nodes and `LocalCoordinator` over loopback HTTP. The trustee's runbook is [[trustee-node]] (`docs/runbooks/trustee-node.md`). Tested with whole ceremonies through lost polls, posts and responses (a fast-check property: every seat still qualifies and approves, and no seat is ever refused), a restart between rounds, a seat withheld from round 2, malformed and foreign requests, retried electorate checks, the HTTP client's URL rule, body bound and error bodies, the configuration's and key file's refusals, and every command, `run` stopping at once when aborted mid-poll (T-14, T-37, T-54).

Known-weak: the deployed node refuses every stub until part 3; the identity key comes from a file only (a hardware token needs crypto's ceremony functions to take a signer); the coordinator side is `LocalCoordinator` only, for local runs and tests; the envelopes aren't in `docs/spec/`, which a second node implementation needs; the node doesn't tell the coordinator why it refused.

## Part 4a

`IntakeSeat` (`apps/trustee-node` README, Intake): before taking any ballot of an election, the seat checks that it approved the stub and that the transcript is the one it approved, whose check still returns the key set its approval signed (3.2); the stub checks again (2.5's pinned panel, 2.3, 1.3 and 2.4 through the `ElectorateCheck`); and finalized L2 showing the definition registered under that key set, through a `RegistrationCheck` the deployment supplies (part 3) and that refuses until it can decide. Only a pass is kept. Each delivery then goes through crypto's `intakeBallot` (verifier 5.1 to 5.4 with the membership proof, then decryption); on a pass the ciphertext, shares, tier and nullifier are written once under the ballot hash, and each copy's L1 reference beside it, before the share receipt record (`0x000b`) is returned; the cutoff is checked again once the proofs are done. From `c_I` (`intakeCutoff` at the release's windows) the seat refuses intake. `CeremonySeat.intakeSecrets` gives intake the approval's transcript and key-set hashes and the approved run's KEM and signing secrets. Tested with ballots carrying real Groth16 membership proofs from `@abolishus/membership-prover`: every seat's receipt verifies under its registered signing key, the same receipt after a restart over the same store, `share-hash`, `delivery-malformed` and `membership-proof` refusals storing nothing, each election refusal (an unapproved stub, another transcript, the electorate and registration checks until they decide, other pins), the same core cast again under a newer L1 reference (one receipt, both references kept), and the cutoff at its boundary, including a delivery that finishes at it (T-52, T-54, T-37, T-14, T-41).

Known-weak: no endpoint and no `RegistrationCheck` in a deployment, so a deployed node takes no ballot; the obligation isn't decided yet (part 4b), though what it reads is stored; storage per member isn't bounded (every distinct ballot is kept); the node's own clock closes intake; definition version 1 only; shares sit unencrypted in owner-only files until the destruction duty (part 5).

## Part 4b-1

The intake endpoint (`apps/trustee-node` README, Intake endpoint): `serveIntake` serves an `IntakeSeat` at `POST /v1/intake`, the route a client reaches through an anonymiser when the operator's fails ([[delivery]], Client rules 4) and the operator's relay delivers through. The body is a transport envelope (`{stub, transcript, record, ciphertext}`, each `bytes<2^20>`); 200 returns the share receipt record, 422 a refusal's code, 429 `intake-rate`, 503 a full queue with `Retry-After`. It allows any origin, since a delivery carries no credential and the receipt is public. Its limits: a body bound checked on the declared length before reading and while streaming; a queue bounding the deliveries held at once, checked one at a time (`intakeBallot` is synchronous); header and request timeouts; and, in `IntakeSeat`, a per-member allowance (`MemberLimit`, 8 distinct ballots and one more an hour since `opens_at`), counted only after the membership proof verifies and one decision at a time per member, while a ballot the seat holds is never limited. It serves HTTPS with TLS 1.3 and `X25519MLKEM768` only ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Consequences), or plain HTTP on loopback behind a proxy. `run` serves it with an `intake` section, refusing every delivery until part 3. Tested with the envelope's round trip and truncations (fast-check), a real seat's receipt over HTTP equal to its in-process one, every status, the body bound declared and streamed, the queue and one-at-a-time checking, the member limit with real membership proofs (burst, a held ballot under a new L1 reference, another member, the period's boundary, a race for the last slot), TLS refusing classical-only and TLS 1.2 clients, the configuration and TLS key file, and `run` serving and closing the endpoint (T-39, T-52, T-11, T-54).

Known-weak: no deployment takes a ballot until part 3 supplies the electorate and registration checks; definition version 1 only, while the website creates version 5; storage is bounded by the allowance's pace (176 ballots per member in a week's poll), not by the two-receipt obligation, and a coercer who spends a member's allowance delays their re-vote by up to an hour; no per-address limit (deliveries come through anonymisers and the relay), so non-members can fill the queue and delay members (liveness only); clients without `X25519MLKEM768` can't reach the endpoint directly; nothing pins the endpoint's certificate beyond the Web PKI.

## Notes

- These rules bind honest nodes only: a trustee can change its own node, and `k` trustees together can decrypt anything (T-14).
- Rule 6 of [[0006-trustees]] applies in full, since whoever controls the node software reaches every share at once (T-61): among its points, no auto-update; each trustee rebuilds the signed, reproducible release (P0-3) and publishes which one it runs; releases roll out one trustee at a time with a delay between them, so no release reaches `k` nodes before trustees and the public have had time to inspect it; a share sits in an HSM only where decryption runs inside it (a TPM-sealed key protects against disk theft, not the node software); and a second implementation should exist before Phase 5.
- Phase 1's reference election (P1-20) and simulations (P1-21) simulate seven trustees, 4-of-7, with two offline; this node replaces the simulation in Phase 2's reference election against staging (P2-12).
