## Scope

Moderation policy (written) and moderation tooling for poll content (never results).

## Plan

The item is built in parts, each its own PR on this branch's name:

1. **Policy and log** (this part): [[moderation-policy]], and the append-only moderation log with its state machine in `packages/core`.
2. **Reports, appeals and the public log over the API:** reports that store the poll and a reason code only (never the reporter), the creator's appeal, `apps/api` serving the public log and each poll's state, and the moderator's tooling (`apps/admin-cli` or `apps/web`, whichever exists first). Rate limits on reports with P2-11's proxy (T-52).
3. **Front ends:** discovery and search leave out delisted and hidden polls, and a hidden poll's page shows the notice and its result by option number (P2-9); the ballot client's behaviour for hidden text (P2-8).

## What was built (part 1)

- [[moderation-policy]]: what moderation may touch (display text and listing, never the board, a definition, a tally or an election's timing); the actions `delist`, `hide`, `restore`; the closed list of reason codes and the grounds that never count (viewpoint, controversy, criticism, report volume, a poll's result or turnout); hiding an open poll only for illegal content, personal data or a threat; the public log; reports; appeals by a different moderator; moderators; and the legal questions, which are the owner's.
- `packages/core`:
  - migration `0002_moderation.sql`: `moderation_actions`, append-only for every role (grants and triggers), with checks that tie each reason code to its action, bound the moderator handle and the election ID, and leave no free-text column. P2-5's `0002_accounts.sql` (#65) is in review with the same number: whichever merges second renumbers to the next free number above everything merged, since `migrate` refuses a number below an applied one.
  - `src/moderation.ts`: `moderate`, `moderationStatus`, `readModerationLog`, `OPEN_POLL_HIDE_REASONS`. One advisory lock serialises decisions, so sequence numbers commit in order (a reader paging by sequence number never misses one) and every decision is judged against the state it changes. A decision's time is the database clock, read after the lock, never the caller's, so no caller can date a hide past a poll's close. A removal may be re-cited under a reason not yet cited since the state began; anything else that changes nothing is refused.
  - `TABLE_CLASSES.operational` gains `moderation_actions`.
- Threat files T-68 and T-17. The index row for T-68 stays `Planned` (AGENTS.md lets a PR edit the index only to add a threat), and so does the file's status line, to match it.

## Tested (part 1)

- `packages/core/tests/moderation.test.ts` against Postgres, as the app role: the Drizzle table matches the migration; the column list (no free text); grants; `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE` refused as the app and as the owner; the owner can't set `session_replication_role`, and the app can't disable the triggers or drop the table; the table's checks (unknown action, a reason of the other kind, an unknown reason, a name for a handle, a long or hyphen-led handle, a short election ID, an explicit `seq`).
- `moderate`: hide and restore; delist, escalate and de-escalate; no-change refused with nothing written; re-citing under another reason; an unknown election; ten malformed decisions; election IDs that aren't 32 real bytes; for every removal reason, hiding a poll still inside its close plus inclusion bound is allowed or refused (`open-poll`) as the policy says, and allowed after it; a forged far-future time is ignored (still `open-poll`, and the recorded time is the database's); six concurrent identical decisions record exactly one.
- A fast-check model of the state machine (random action sequences; the state is always the newest decision's, and only changes are recorded), at `FC_NUM_RUNS=1000` capped to 50 runs since each run stores a definition.
- Never results: moderating leaves an election's board and definition byte for byte, and its board still takes entries; a source check that the moderation module names no board table or board function, writes no definition, and that the board and definition modules never mention moderation.
- The public log pages in order across elections, and its entries carry no moderator.
- Mutation by hand: removing the advisory lock fails the concurrency test.

## Known-weak (part 1)

- The log lives in Postgres: a wipe loses every decision (polls go back to visible), and an insider can withhold rows from what we serve. Neither changes a result. Anchoring the log is not planned yet.
- The moderator handle is free to choose within its pattern; mapping handles to people is the operator's, and the policy's rule that a moderator doesn't decide on their own poll is not enforced.
- The `open-poll` check compares the database clock with `closes_at`, which the chain compares with L2 `block.timestamp`; the margin is the inclusion bound.
- Only `moderate` enforces the state machine, the open-poll rule and the in-order sequence. The app role can insert rows directly, and the owner can drop the triggers with DDL: the log binds the tooling, not an insider (T-68).
- Hiding an open poll for `illegal`, `personal-data` or `threat` stops voting through our client and so can lower turnout: listed under Decisions to review.
- Nothing serves the log or acts on the state yet (parts 2 and 3).
