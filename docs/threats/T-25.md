# T-25: Ballot or registration censorship

- Area: Integrity and verifiability (G-7, G-8, G-9)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1, A-7
- Status: **Planned.** Residual: direct submit needs gas; funding an address can itself link a voter (T-11-like), and voters are told so. Under P1-3 (option B), a ballot also needs a receipt from, or a public posting to, each trustee's intake by the delivery deadline; trustees can't block it (posting needs no cooperation), but a posted share has only classical privacy.

## Threat

Our servers refuse, delay or silently drop a valid submission.

## Mitigation

Receipts let the voter detect a drop: at cast only against the operator's own signed claim ([[receipts]]); a drop after the receipt, or a receipt over a made-up tree, only by the later checks ([[P1-13b]]). The direct-submit path lets anyone post a registration or ballot straight to the contracts (P1-18, P4-2); the indexer and verifier include direct-submitted ballots (P3-5), and each contract's event accumulator ([[contracts]]) lets the verifier prove that no direct-submitted ballot was left off the board, or boarded only after the counted extent the tally combines over ([[verifier]] 4.4, 5.6); a late entry that would change a counted set fails the election against the operator (`tally-late`, 7.1). The re-vote rule must not let the operator void a direct-submitted re-vote by withholding or reordering the voter's earlier ballots (requirement R3 in [[verifier]] check 6.1, P1-13).

## History

- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 2a): [[board]]'s client rule 2 becomes [[receipts]], Later checks: a receipt over a made-up tree or a dropped entry is `unanchored` or `forked` once its segment's window closes, and a drop when no admitted entry carries the ballot by `(tier, ν, M)`.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 1, [[0023-receipt-evidence]]): a drop after the receipt becomes evidence a third party can check: an `unanchored` or `forked` receipt for an admissible ballot that no admitted entry of the anchored board carries by `(tier, ν, M)`. A board withheld past the dispute end doesn't defeat it, since the client commits a receipt in doubt on L1 first; the receipt then stays `unresolved`, and the election unverifiable, until a copy is found.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-15): the tally combines over the counted extent (entries with `segment ≤ s_S`), and a late entry is never ignored: a direct-submitted ballot first boarded after that extent fails 5.6, and any late entry that would change a cell's counted set fails the election as `tally-late`, both attributed to the operator ([[tally]], Tally window; [[verifier]] 5.6, 7.1). So holding back a re-vote past the counted extent fails loudly instead of keeping the earlier ballot counted. `s_S` is at least the period after close's, so client rule 1's tolerance can't place a receipted ballot outside it.
- 2026-10-11 (P1-18): the event accumulator that 4.4 and 5.6 rely on is specified and built ([[contracts]]); the direct-submit path itself is still to come.
- 2026-10-11 (P1-17): a registrant can't fail later elections by registering a forbidden commitment: it gets leaf 0 and never fails the definition, and group contracts must revert it ([[membership]], Commitments and removals). Direct-submit contracts must verify membership proofs against the registered tier root, so non-members can't post at all.
- 2026-10-11 (P1-13, receipts): [[board]]'s client rule 1 refuses an envelope with the wrong segment at cast without alarming: nothing in the refusal is checkable by anyone else, since the operator hasn't signed anything the client accepted. The client keeps the ballot and resubmits it or submits it directly ([[receipts]], Checks at cast).
- 2026-10-12 (P1-12 part 3b, [[delivery]]): every ballot needs each qualified seat's receipt, or a posting sent through the contract path, on the board by the delivery deadline, and the board operator can withhold board-path receipts and complaints, silently leaving a ballot undelivered or an invalid one counted. A contract path for receipts, postings and complaints, for every ballot (a posting call can carry the ballot's public part, so ballots cast through our API are covered), MUST exist before any election registry is deployed (P1-18); until then the residual is open, and nothing can post at all. Postings deliver only through it, so the operator can't withhold one through the complaint window, and trustee nodes send their receipts and complaints through it unless one is covered by an anchored checkpoint they read from L1 themselves, so an operator that serves a receipt and then drops it from the checkpoint can't drop a receipted ballot either; clients likewise stand down only on an anchored receipt. The contract accepts any number of postings per seat whose ciphertext hashes to the ballot's share hash, so no one can block a voter's posting by sending junk first. Residual: a trustee that receipts and never sends, with an operator and every contract-path route of the client's refusing it, leaves the ballot undelivered (the client may not post a receipted seat), with only the voter's copy of the signed receipt as evidence (the owner's decision in [[0002-everlasting-privacy]], 2026-10-12).
- 2026-10-12 (P1-12 part 3b, [[delivery]], Residuals): a trustee node's contract-path receipt obligation is unbounded while intake checks no membership: an ineligible-ballot flood plus an operator withholding board data until the node's cutoff can exhaust it and drop receipted ballots whose clients have gone away. The owner accepted this as a residual until [[P1-12c]] (membership-checked intake, obligations bounded by the electorate, batched contract receipts) closes it, before any election registry is deployed.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-7 follow-up, [[contracts]], Trustee registry): under [[0006-trustees]] rule 7 an election opens only with `max(k, n − 1)` qualified seats and as many registration signatures, so any two seats of a 4-of-7 panel can stop every election from opening by refusing to take part or to sign. That is below the decryption threshold, and rule 1 lets one state hold up to `k − 1` seats, so a single state compelling two trustees (T-69) can do it. Re-runs don't help against a deliberate refusal, and a refusal shows only as absence, never attributed. The refusal can also be selective (a node sees the stub and can fetch its committed display text, so it can block chosen polls while looking like an outage), and rule 2 lets one hosting provider run up to `k − 1` nodes, so a compelled or seized provider can block too. Before rule 7, blocking needed `n − k + 1` seats, which is more than rule 1 lets one state hold when `n ≥ 2k − 1` (4 seats for 4-of-7). This follows from rule 7; the owner's decision doesn't state it, so it is listed in [[0006-trustees]] (Known-weak) and STATUS (Known-weak) for the owner to see.
- 2026-10-12 (P3-2 part 4a, [[0024-tier0-registration]]): part 1's registry has no direct-submit path at Tier 0, since every addition needs the registrars' signatures, and registration through our service needs a Turnstile solve, so the registrars or Cloudflare can refuse a Tier 0 registrant. The ADR adds a self-paid Tier 0 addition (option D, P3-2 part 5) to the registry before any is deployed; its gas-paying address can link the registrant, as for direct-submitted ballots.
- 2026-10-13 (P1-12c part 1, [[delivery]], Direct submit and Residuals): membership-checked intake bounds a trustee node's contract-path receipt obligation by the electorate (at most two receipts per tier and nullifier: the ballot the re-vote rule selects from the node's view, and the newest receipted ballot whose L1 reference fails, whose core may have been cast again after a reorganisation), so an ineligible-ballot flood can no longer exhaust it. Payability (the windows' capacity with P1-18's batched receipts) and a bound on complaints are P1-12c part 2's, and the residual stays until then. A new, narrower residual: when a voter's newest ballot reached the trustees but never the board and the operator withholds board data, nodes owe only that newest ballot's receipt, so an operator that also refuses the older, selected ballot's board-path receipts can drop it unattributed; P4-3's data availability closes it. The node decides what it owes once, at the intake cutoff `c_I = t_D − F − R` that every node shares, keeps intake open until then (so retries after an outage at the close still get receipts) and refuses it afterwards, since the union of decisions over a changing view wouldn't be bounded; the delivery-window bound now reads `max(2 · period + deadline, W_C) + R + F`.
- 2026-10-13 (P1-18 part 4b-3a): [[0028-board-contract-checks]] fixes where the direct-submit contract's inputs come from: ballot parameters recorded by the registry at registration (so a registered election takes direct ballots at once, with nothing extra to send) and a key set the ceremony quorum signs. Its membership check is built; the ballot check, the contract and the close rule follow.

- 2026-10-13 (P1-18 part 4b-3b-3, [[contracts]], Board, Ballots): the board's ballot check is built: it reads the election's parameters from the registry by the record's definition hash, and refuses only records the verifier rejects (framing, `ballot-stray`, `ballot-tier` or `ballot-cell`, `validity-shape`, `share-hash-unqualified`, the membership proof), never an honest ballot; the validity proofs, the rest of each element's decoding and the L1 reference are left to the verifier, which sets such a record aside as a rejected entry. - 2026-10-13 (P1-18 part 4b-3d-1, [[contracts]], Board, Delivery records, Calls, Events): the board contract is built: a voter's ballot, and every share receipt, posting and complaint, has its contract path, each emitted whole under the event accumulator. - 2026-10-15 (P1-12c part 2, [[delivery]], What a node owes, Capacity; [[ceremony]], Stub checks): a trustee node's complaints are bounded as its receipts are, at most two per tier and nullifier (its complaint scope, fixed at `c_I` from the ballots it holds, never from postings), and a node lends its keys only to elections whose receipts and complaints, with every overlapping election it serves, it can force through L1 by the deadlines at the portal's sustainable deposit target (stub check 4, `forcedPathFits`). That closes the unbounded obligation the owner accepted until P1-12c, as a cap on electorates: about 1,150 members with 3 seats and `R` of one hour, about 23,600 with `R` of 21 hours, so honest seats refuse larger elections until [[P1-12d]] carries receipts many to a signature; the trustee node runs the check. - 2026-10-15 (P1-19 part 4o, [[verifier]], Counted set and tally): the verifier runs the delivery check (5.9) over the selected ballots: a ballot without every qualified seat's receipt or passing posting in time counts nothing, and is listed with its missing seats. - 2026-10-16 (P1-12f part 1a, [[delivery]], Obligation root, Leaf availability, Capacity): specified, not built. An election whose batched obligation alone doesn't fit the forced path commits roots: each seat sends one signed root of its receipts and one of its complaints through the contract path, so the forced path carries `s · ROOT_CALL_GAS` per interval at any electorate. A root's leaves count only if `q − k + 1` of the default panel's seats name it in their counted-extent attestations ([[0029-board-data-availability]], P4-3 part 2c), so availability never depends on a verifier's network; a seat that withholds its leaves drops only the ballots it alone would deliver, named `root-withheld`, and a client never stands down on a leaf. Until the board call, the code and the attestation are built, nodes refuse every election that would commit roots, so the cap stands.

- 2026-10-16 (P1-12f part 1b-1, residual): a root takes its seat's slot, unlike a batch, so whoever holds a seat's per-election signing key (the seat itself, or a thief of the key) can fill both of its slots with junk roots for an election, and the honest root can then never be sent. In an election that commits roots the batches can't carry the whole obligation, so the ballots that seat alone would deliver fall back on their voters' postings or re-votes. It costs only that seat's share of delivery, as refusing intake already does, and the delivery check reports a wrong root against its seat; not mitigated further.
