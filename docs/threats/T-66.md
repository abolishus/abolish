# T-66: Database loss or corruption

- Area: Development, supply chain and operations (G-14)
- Adversary: A-2, A-7
- Status: **Planned.**

## Threat

Database loss or corruption.

## Mitigation

Postgres isn't authoritative: every result is rebuilt from chain + IPFS, and the reference election wipes and rebuilds (P1-20, P2-1).

Index: [[THREAT_MODEL]].

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P2-6 part 1): [[0017-identity-recovery-backup]] stores voters' recovery envelopes in Postgres. They affect no result, so nothing here changes for results, but they can't be rebuilt: a wipe or loss of the table loses every backup, and a voter who then loses their device loses their voting identity.
- 2026-10-11 (P2-4 part 1): the worker's scheduled board audit detects stored columns that disagree with the stored payloads (corruption, rows written around `appendEntry`). It can't detect a restore of an older but consistent snapshot, a truncated tail, or a consistent rewrite with the triggers off: it remembers nothing between runs, and only anchored checkpoints (P4-1) catch those. Detection only: the board is rebuilt from chain and IPFS (P4-6). An audit reads a whole board into memory at once.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-6 part 3): the `identity_backups` table is built. A wipe loses every backup, as part 1 said; expiry also deletes a backup not rewritten for 24 whole months. A rewrite needs a backup-passkey ceremony, so P2-8 must ask for one well before then; a voter who skips it loses the backup, though the identity stays on their device.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-6 part 3b): expiry now runs: the worker deletes identity backups past their 24 whole months daily. A worker whose clock runs months fast would delete live backups early; it trusts the host's system clock.
- 2026-10-13 (P3-5 part 1): `apps/indexer` keeps the group registry's events in Postgres and nothing else: every row is a log's own bytes, and its test wipes the index and Ponder's RPC cache and rebuilds the same rows from the chain alone. Readers replay the records under their own pinned registrars, so an altered or reordered row fails the replay; a deleted removal or a deleted tail of additions does not, and only the event accumulator's proof catches it (P3-5 part 2).
- 2026-10-14 (P3-5 part 2a): the index also keeps every log of the group registry as emitted (`contract_log`: block, timestamp, topics, data), the constructor's `RegistrarsPinned` included, and `readContractEvents` returns them as contract-event records numbered by their order in the index. Checked against the registry's accumulator (`checkEventAccumulatorProof`), a deleted row, the removal that replay misses among them, fails as `accumulator`, so a reader holding an honest storage proof catches what replay alone doesn't. The rebuilt index passes the same check. No reader runs it in production yet: the API and worker read nothing from the index (P3-5 part 3).
