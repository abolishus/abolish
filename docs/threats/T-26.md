# T-26: Unprovable drops and false complaints

- Area: Integrity and verifiability (G-7, G-8, G-9)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1
- Status: **Planned.**

## Threat

A voter whose ballot is missing can't prove to anyone else that they submitted it, and a false complaint can't be refuted, so a drop becomes a he-said-she-said.

## Mitigation

Receipts are meant to be attributable: either the operator's signature on the board it showed the voter, with the voter's entry last, checked for inclusion at cast ([[receipts]], [[0013-ballot-receipts]]), or the voter's own L2 inclusion via direct submit (P4-2). What a receipt is evidence of later, to third parties, is [[P1-13b]]; until then it is not attributable evidence. The dispute procedure goes in a runbook.

## History

- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 1, [[0023-receipt-evidence]]): what a receipt is evidence of later is decided: keys fixed by the election definition and registered beforehand by the anchoring authority in an append-only L1 registry, so evidence is attributed to it alone; evidence bounded by a dispute end, and kept past it by a salted L1 commitment the client makes when a receipt is in doubt; verdicts a third party can check (`forked` and `included` with a logarithmic proof, a drop by content against the whole anchored board). Residual: before the dispute end, whoever holds a receipt key can forge evidence against the anchoring authority. The spec and code are P1-13b's next parts.
- 2026-10-11 (P1-13, receipts): the receipt record, its signed statement and the check at cast specified and built ([[receipts]], [[0013-ballot-receipts]]). What a receipt is evidence of later, to third parties, is [[P1-13b]], with the requirements #48's reviews found (time bound, withheld boards, keys fixed per election, presence by admitted content, privacy).
