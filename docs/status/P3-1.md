## Scope

ADR: zkPassport vs Self vs longfellow-zk. Must confirm proofs verify off-chain or on the chosen L2, with no third-chain dependency. Rule A applies (an identity-scheme decision is the agent's, with an owner veto). Only the owner-only questions, the nullifier's PII reading and bb.js in the verifier's trust base, get `needs-decision`.

## Outcome

[[0010-tier2-identity-provider]] chooses zkPassport:

- **No third chain:** it verifies off-chain in TypeScript with bb.js, or on Base.
- **Proving:** client-side, on the device.
- **Uniqueness:** a per-document nullifier scoped to Abolish.
- **Binding:** arbitrary bound data, so the proof commits to our identity commitment.
- **Coverage:** US passports.
- **Licence:** Apache-2.0 for the circuits and the app.

The other two were rejected:

- **Self** depends on Celo state, and proves registration in a TEE that receives the document data.
- **longfellow-zk** has no uniqueness nullifier.

## Hand-offs

To P3-4, with P3-2 and [[membership]] (details in the ADR's Consequences):

- **Verification.**
  - Verify locally with the pinned bb.js against vendored, provenance-checked verification keys. Never use the hosted verifier, the cloud prover (on the verifier's or the voter's side) or zkPassport's upgradeable contracts. Allowing that publisher needs a `ci:` PR first.
  - Confirm that zkPassport's bb version matches our toolchain pins, or move all four pins together.
- **Roots and certificates.**
  - The verifier pins the inner-circuit set and rejects any other circuit root.
  - For US-scope tiers it accepts only documents whose issuing state is the USA (a predicate), with signers chained to US CSCA certificates pinned in the release. It checks each addition against the certificates and archived, signed revocation lists valid at its own L2 block. zkPassport's on-chain roots are never required. Confirm where the US CSCA certificates and revocation lists are published and how they are signed.
  - Confirm that the proof is structured with a circuit registry root as the ADR assumes, and amend the ADR if not.
- **The proof itself.**
  - Its date is within a stated bound of the addition's L2 time, and the document is unexpired.
  - It proves predicates only, never disclosed values. List every public input and show that none is a low-entropy function of document data.
- **Binding.** A bound-data record in `docs/spec/` under its own tag: the identity commitment, the tier group, the chain ID and the registry, decoded strictly.
- **Nullifier.** One Tier 2 scope per deployment. The nullifier type is fixed per deployment. One counted addition per nullifier, and a removal never frees it (P3-2 defines who authorises removals).
- **Mock documents.** MOCK is accepted only under the definition's test flag on a pinned list of local and test chains, never on mainnet. Confirm that the circuit fixes MOCK rather than the prover.
- **Spec edits:** [[verifier]] 2.4 and [[membership]].
- **Owner gate on the verifier side:** confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-12; P3-4 may add `@aztec/bb.js` to `packages/verifier`.
- **Nullifier burning:** design a dispute path for a holder whose document was pre-registered from cloned chip data, without reopening double registration (with P3-2).
- **Before any real document on any public chain:**
  - A reviewed argument that the published proof is statistically zero-knowledge. If there isn't one, use trustee-attested additions instead (the agent's fallback), after the trustee role description says the trustees see each nullifier with its commitment.
  - The owner's answer on the nullifier question: given on 2026-10-12, it is PII. Real additions are trustee-attested, with nullifiers off the board, until a trustee-run VOPRF nullifier exists ([[0010-tier2-identity-provider]], Owner decision).

## Known-weak

The full list is in the ADR's Residuals. The main points:

- passive authentication only, and renewed documents whose old chip still reads;
- anyone holding a passport's chip data can pre-register it and burn its holder's nullifier;
- zkPassport's circuits have no public audit yet;
- zkPassport's oracle chooses which certificates its roots admit;
- an issuing state, or anyone holding chip data, can recompute a document's nullifier (owner question);
- Tier 2 covers US passport holders only at launch (no mDLs or state ID cards);
- voters run zkPassport's app until P5-3 and P5-4 ship ours.

The facts were read from each project's docs and repositories on 2026-10-11. P3-4 re-checks them before building.
