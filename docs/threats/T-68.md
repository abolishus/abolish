# T-68: Moderation abuse

- Area: Development, supply chain and operations (G-14)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1
- Status: **Planned.** Residual: content anchored on-chain or on IPFS can't be erased, only hidden by our front ends; the moderation log lives in our database, so an insider can withhold rows from what we serve or lose them in a wipe (no result changes either way), and the log's rules bind the tooling, not an insider who inserts rows directly or drops its triggers with DDL; hiding an open poll for `illegal` or `personal-data` stops voting through our client and can lower turnout (listed for the owner under Decisions to review); while a `threat`-hidden poll is open our ballot client still shows its text; and because the client treats a poll whose state it can't read as visible, an outage or block of `GET /moderation/status` lets it show the text of an `illegal` or `personal-data` hide that someone else still pins ([[moderation-policy]], Open polls).

## Threat

Moderation is used to suppress polls or results, or anchored content can't be moderated at all.

## Mitigation

A written policy ([[moderation-policy]]); moderation acts on poll content, never results (P2-10). Built (P2-10 part 1): the policy, with its closed list of grounds and the grounds that never count (viewpoint, report volume, a poll's result or turnout); an append-only, public moderation log in `packages/core` whose only actions are delisting, hiding display text and restoring, each citing a reason code, with no free text and no moderator in the public view; the tooling never writes the board or a definition, and refuses to hide a poll's text before it closes except for illegal content, personal data or a threat, since hiding stops voting through our client. Specified (P2-10 part 5, the owner's decision): a `threat` hide of an open poll withholds the text only from our web pages, while our ballot client keeps showing the committed text, which we keep pinned until the poll closes; a ballot client that can't read a poll's state treats it as visible, so blocking the status route doesn't stop voting. Results stay verifiable from chain + IPFS whatever the log says (G-9). Built (P2-10 part 2): the public log and each poll's state over the API, without the moderator; anonymous reports, counted per poll and ground with nothing about the reporter, that never change a poll's state or the log. Built (P2-10 part 4a): the API's poll listing (`GET /elections`) leaves out delisted and hidden polls. Planned: appeals (P2-10 part 3b); front-end behaviour (P2-8, P2-9); the legal questions in the policy are the owner's.

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P2-10 part 1): policy and log built (above). The log isn't anchored, so withholding a row from the public log isn't detectable yet.
- 2026-10-11 (P2-10 part 2): the API serves the log (`GET /moderation/log`) and each poll's state (`GET /moderation/status`), and takes reports (`POST /moderation/reports`). Reports are counted per poll and ground; a test checks that any number of them leaves a poll's state and the public log unchanged, since report volume is never a ground.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-9 part 1): `apps/web` shows the public log at `/moderation` and each poll's state on its page; a delisted or hidden poll is marked `noindex`.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-10 part 5): the owner narrowed open-poll hides. A `threat` hide no longer stops voting through our ballot client, which keeps showing the committed text from IPFS; only `illegal` and `personal-data` hides still do. The client treats a poll whose state it can't read as visible ([[moderation-policy]], Open polls). Built by P2-8 (the ballot client) and P4-3 (pinning).
- 2026-10-12 (P2-10 part 3a): the operator's moderation commands in `apps/admin-cli`: the report queue a page at a time, reviewing only the reports the queue showed, decisions through `moderate` (so its open-poll and no-change rules hold), and the public log. They connect as the app role and never print a moderator's handle.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-10 part 4a): the API lists polls for discovery at `GET /elections` (`listPolls` in core), leaving out every poll whose newest decision delists or hides it; such a poll stays served by ID. A property test over arbitrary logs, written directly as an insider could, checks that the newest decision alone decides. The listing is never a source of truth: the registry names every poll.
