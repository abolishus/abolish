# T-17: PII on-chain or on IPFS

- Area: Ballot secrecy, unlinkability and privacy (G-3, G-4, G-5, G-12)
- Adversary: A-2, A-9, A-10
- Status: **Planned.** Residual: a poll creator can put personal data in a poll's text, which copies on IPFS may keep; a malicious client can hide chosen bytes, up to the full width of each field that must be random (salts, CSPRNG outputs), since random and chosen bytes look alike ([[notation]], Result-critical fields); a poll creator can likewise hide somewhat over 100 chosen bytes per election definition, in its `election_id`, its display-text commitment and the free bits of its bounded integers ([[election-definition]]); and anyone can archive arbitrary bytes for the price of gas inside a direct submission's inclusion evidence (its access list, say) while the archive keeps raw transactions and receipts (P1-16, P1-18 decide; [[notation]]); data in result-critical fields is archived for good; T-68.

## Threat

A poll title, election definition, registration or ballot carries personal data into permanent storage.

## Mitigation

Contract inputs are fixed-size commitments, hashes and proofs, never free text tied to a person; Foundry tests assert this (P1-18). Display text is committed by salted hash and kept off the permanent archive, so it can be taken down while the result stays verifiable ([[0005-permanent-archive]], P1-8); otherwise moderation can hide poll content in our front ends, never erase it (P2-10, T-68).
