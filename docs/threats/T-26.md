# T-26: Unprovable drops and false complaints

- Area: Integrity and verifiability (G-7, G-8, G-9)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1
- Status: **Planned.**

## Threat

A voter whose ballot is missing can't prove to anyone else that they submitted it, and a false complaint can't be refuted, so a drop becomes a he-said-she-said.

## Mitigation

Receipts are meant to be attributable: either the operator's signature on the board it showed the voter, with the voter's entry last, checked for inclusion at cast ([[receipts]], [[0013-ballot-receipts]]), or the voter's own L2 inclusion via direct submit (P4-2). What a receipt is evidence of later is specified in [[receipts]], Later checks ([[0023-receipt-evidence]]): verdicts a third party reaches from the definition, L1 and the board, bounded by a dispute end that salted L1 commitments outlast. Until the receipt keys are bound in the definition and registered on L1 (P1-13b part 2b) the keys that judge a receipt are the set the client's release pinned when it accepted it at cast, so it is evidence to its voter but not yet attributable to anyone else. The dispute procedure goes in a runbook.

## History

- 2026-10-13 (P1-13b part 3b-2): the opening's checks are code in `packages/crypto` (`checkReceiptOpening`, with `transactionTrieValue`, `transactionCalldata` and a strict RLP decoder): the transaction-trie proof, the commitment in calldata, the time bound, the payload branch and the beacon block's place in `historical_summaries` or `block_roots`, in order, under fork schedules pinned for mainnet and Sepolia ([[receipts]], Fork schedule). Its vectors' execution side is two real blocks; the beacon side is synthetic. Sepolia's Gloas fork leaves every Sepolia opening `opening-fork-unknown` (unjudged, never failed) until a release specifies it.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 3b-1): the later checks are code in `packages/crypto` (`disputeEnd`, `receiptCommitment`, `calldataCarries`, `judgeReceipt`): every verdict in order from L1 anchors and entry-hash lists, `forked` and `included` with their consistency proofs, checked against vectors from an independent Python implementation. The opening's checks against L1 (transaction trie, beacon branches) are part 3b-2's, so until then `judgeReceipt` takes their results from the caller; the drop rule, [[verifier]] 2.9 from storage proofs and the client's schedule are later parts.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 3a): the board anchor's receipt-key record is built (`registerReceiptKey`, `retireReceiptKey` in `packages/contracts`): only the authority writes it, a key is registered once and retired at most once, and the word a verifier proves is checked against every writable record of the version-4 judging vectors. Nothing reads it yet: [[verifier]] 2.9 from storage proofs and the ballot client's check before casting are later parts.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 2b): election-definition version 4 lists the receipt keys (`receipt_keys`, at most 4, strictly ascending, in the stub), and the board anchor keeps an append-only L1 record of each key's registration and retirement, written only by its `authority` ([[election-definition]], Receipt keys; [[contracts]], Board anchor, Receipt keys). A listed key judges the election only if registered before `opens_at` and not retired by then, which [[verifier]] 2.9 checks from L1 state and the ballot client checks before casting, so under version 4 every verdict is attributable to the anchoring authority alone. Versions 1 and 3 bind no keys and are to be retired before any election registry is deployed. `packages/crypto` decodes and checks version 4 and the judging rule, with vectors from an independent Python implementation; the anchor's code and the verifier's storage proofs of the record are part 3's.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 2a): the later checks specified ([[receipts]], Later checks): the dispute end `t_E` with `DISPUTE_WINDOW` (30 days) in [[parameters]], the commitment `abolish/v1/receipt-commitment` in L1 calldata and its opening through a beacon block's execution payload and `historical_summaries` or `block_roots`, the client's check from whole-board entry-hash lists ([[board]]), the verdicts in order with their codes, the drop rule and the client's rules for following the board, committing and alarming. Receipt keys in the definition and the L1 registry are part 2b's.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 1, [[0023-receipt-evidence]]): what a receipt is evidence of later is decided: keys fixed by the election definition and registered beforehand by the anchoring authority in an append-only L1 registry, so evidence is attributed to it alone; evidence bounded by a dispute end, and kept past it by a salted L1 commitment the client makes when a receipt is in doubt; verdicts a third party can check (`forked` and `included` with a logarithmic proof, a drop by content against the whole anchored board). Residual: before the dispute end, whoever holds a receipt key can forge evidence against the anchoring authority. The spec and code are P1-13b's next parts.
- 2026-10-11 (P1-13, receipts): the receipt record, its signed statement and the check at cast specified and built ([[receipts]], [[0013-ballot-receipts]]). What a receipt is evidence of later, to third parties, is [[P1-13b]], with the requirements #48's reviews found (time bound, withheld boards, keys fixed per election, presence by admitted content, privacy).
