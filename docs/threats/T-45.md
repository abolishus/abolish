# T-45: Observed coercion

- Area: Coercion and vote buying (G-11)
- Adversary: A-4
- Status: **Partial.** Defeated by a coercer who watches until the poll closes; who votes last with the voter's secret; who watches a cast in the last blocks before close, leaving no time for a newer L1 block (the voter can then only void the watched ballot with a tie); who proposes, or bribes the proposers of, a run of upcoming L1 slots, and so can have ballots made ahead for those blocks; who works with the sequencer to land a ballot made after close that names the last block before it, unless P1-18's close rule keeps such ballots out (all in [[revoting]], Residuals); or who demands the voter abstain (forced abstention is visible through the nullifier to anyone holding the secret). Re-voting also gives no deniability against anyone who has seen one of the voter's ballots or receipts, since later ballots under that nullifier are public ([[verifier]] check 6.1). Where ballots are decrypted individually (a ranked-choice mixnet, P1-4), re-voting doesn't stop a pattern attack (T-70).

## Threat

A coercer watches the voter vote, or demands the receipt.

## Mitigation

Re-voting where the last ballot counts, protected against replay by T-38; receipts prove inclusion, not content. "Last" is the ballot naming the newest L1 block ([[revoting]], [[0011-revote-rule]]): a block hash can't be known in advance, so ballots made while watched once and posted later all lose to any re-vote made after them, and a watched voter can void the watched ballot at once by casting on the same block, then outrank it about 36 seconds later.

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P1-13): pre-made ballots defeated by binding a fresh L1 block into each ballot; the last-moment window and tie escape stated ([[revoting]]).
- 2026-10-11 (P1-13, receipts): a receipt names the voter's entry and nullifier, so showing it to a coercer reveals every later re-vote ([[receipts]], Residuals).
- 2026-10-12 (P1-12 part 3b, [[delivery]], Client rules): for a seat whose trustee didn't receipt, the client keeps `ct_i` and `m_i` after cast, until the voter decides whether to post it or the delivery deadline passes, never `e_i` or `w_i`, so nothing kept proves the vote; a device thief in that window could post the seat against the voter's wishes, dropping that share to classical privacy (stated in [[delivery]], Residuals).
- 2026-10-14 (P1-19 part 4m): the verifier judges each ballot's L1 reference (5.8) against a run of finalized L1 headers, before repeats, so a ballot naming a block before the window, after close or reorganised away is rejected and never ranks against the voter's re-vote. The run is a local input until P1-19 part 3b trusts a finalized head ([[verifier]], L1 references, Residuals).
- 2026-10-14 (P2-15 part 1a, [[tally]], Live results): in a live poll a voter's re-vote never enters a release: a nullifier with two ballots whose membership proofs pass, whatever else is later decided about them, never enters running sets, and one that re-votes after entering keeps its first ballot there until the close, so the re-vote appears only in the final tally. Not mitigated: the final tally against the releases shows a released voter's old ballot on one side, so a lone re-voter's released choice can be read at a `−1`, and a coercer who demanded option `j` sees a zero at `j` with probability about `(1 − p_j)^B`; re-voting is weaker against observed coercion in live polls than in sealed ones, and the creation flow and notice must say so. Until [[P2-13]] specifies the nodes' withholding check, an operator can withhold a first ballot beside its boarded re-vote, which then enters a release. So live polls run only where we hold at least `k` default seats and there is no author panel, enforced by [[verifier]] 2.3; the operator can open ballots there anyway.
- 2026-10-16 (P2-15 part 2b, `packages/crypto` `live-release.ts`): running sets as specified: a nullifier enters only with exactly one distinct ballot whose membership proof passes, pending and later-rejected ones counting, admitted and fully receipted, and stays with it whatever follows; checked against `live-release.json`'s one-nullifier-per-rule cases and by fast-check (sets only grow, each nullifier in one cell with one ballot, the same whatever the order nullifiers and ballots are given in).
- 2026-10-18 (P2-15 part 3b-2, `packages/verifier` `live.ts`, `packages/crypto` `stepMembershipV4`): the verifier's running sets read every copy admission judged: one that 5.4 rejects for its shape, share hashes or validity proofs has its membership proof checked alone over its own `M`, so a member's ballot rejected for anything else still holds a re-vote out, as the spec requires. Full receipting is crypto's delivery check over the receipts boarded by each snapshot. Not yet end to end: no fixture reaches a sealed live board (P2-15 part 7), and 2.3 still fails every live definition until part 3b-3.
- 2026-10-18 (P2-15 part 3b-3, verifier 2.3): live polls run only under the live restriction (no author panel, at least `k` default seats pinned as ours), so a re-vote entering alone beside a withheld first ballot gains an operator nothing it lacks until the nodes' withholding check exists ([[tally]], Live results, What a node does). Status unchanged.
- 2026-10-22 (P2-23 part 2b, `apps/ballot` `link.ts`, `main.tsx`): a stream card's answers link to the ballot client with the tapped option in the fragment (`#election=<id>&pick=<option>`, [[first-answer]]). The client takes the pick out of the address as soon as it reads it (`history.replaceState`), so the tab's history entry, and a reload, keep only the election, and the pick only ever chooses on screen: nothing is sealed or sent until the voter taps. Residual: the browser's history list, address-bar suggestions and session restore may still hold the URL as it was opened, so someone holding the unlocked device can learn which answer was tapped on a card (not what was sent, nor whether anything was); re-voting is unaffected.
