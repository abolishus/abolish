# Protocol parameters

Limits that apply across record types in protocol version 1, and the post-close windows that enter the seal ([[election-definition]], The seal). A window is a constant of its record version, changed only by a new version, never lowered or raised in place. A record version may set a lower maximum for its own field, never a higher one. Raising a protocol-wide limit is a new record version of every record type it affects, with this page recording which versions use the new limit ([[versioning]]); lowering one before it is frozen is an ordinary spec change. Threats: T-52 (bounded input sizes, so flooding and hostile lengths stay cheap to reject), T-28 (ballot shape), T-50 (direct-submitted ballots must fit the L2's forced-deposit limits).

## Why these values

- **`MAX_OPTIONS`.** A plurality or approval ballot carries one ciphertext or commitment and one validity proof per option, plus a proof for the sum ([[0003-tally-scheme]]), so its size grows linearly with the option count. Under perfectly hiding commitments ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], accepted), that ADR estimates about 320 bytes of public data per option at k = 4, so 64 options is about 20 KB, plus per-trustee data. Most real contests have far fewer options; a civic contest with more candidates than this (the 2003 California recall had 135) would need a new record version with a higher limit, or a ranked-choice or multi-question design. P1-18 checks that a ballot at this limit fits a direct submission through the L2's forced-deposit path and its calldata cap ([[0004-l2-choice]]), and lowers the limit here if it doesn't, before anything is frozen.
- **`MAX_TRUSTEES`.** Bounds per-ballot and per-ceremony data that grows with `n` (per-trustee shares and receipts under P1-3's option B, per-trustee transcript entries under either option). Sixteen leaves room above the proposed panel of seven for a second panel or replacements without a new version.
- **`MAX_BOARD_PAYLOAD`.** Every record on the board travels in a board entry, so this bounds what one entry can make a verifier read, hash and archive (T-52). The largest records expected are ballots (about 20 KB of commitments and proofs at `MAX_OPTIONS`, plus a membership proof of tens of kilobytes, P1-12, P1-17) and the ceremony and tally transcripts, which grow with `MAX_TRUSTEES` and the option count; 1 MiB leaves an order of magnitude of room above them and matches the chunk size of [[content-addressing]]. Each record's own layout sets a tighter bound; P1-12, P1-14 and P1-15 lower this one if their records come out much smaller, before anything is frozen.
- **`DELIVERY_WINDOW` and `COMPLAINT_WINDOW`.** Two days for receipts and postings after the close, enough for a ballot boarded at the close to be anchored or forced through L1 and its receipts still sent through the contract path, and three more for trustees to complain about invalid postings: the reasons are in [[delivery]], Windows.
- **`TALLY_WINDOW`.** Seven days after the counted set's anchoring checkpoint is due (`t_S`) for the tally to reach the board: the reasons are in [[tally]], Tally window.
- **`DESTRUCTION_WINDOW`.** One day after `t_D` for trustees' destruction statements to reach the board; nothing the result rests on waits for them, so it is short ([[tally]], Destruction).
- **`VOUCH_K` and `VOUCH_SLOTS`.** Three document-verified secrets per Tier 1 identity, and twelve vouches each, bound Tier 1 at four times Tier 2 and collusion at four identities per colluding document: the reasons are in [[0014-tier1-vouching]], Recommendation. Both are constants of the group registry's deployment, so changing either is a new registry with full re-admission to Tier 1, never a new record version alongside the old.
- Questions per election, ballots per election, board entries per segment and the lengths of every variable-length field are set by the record tables that use them (P1-10 to P1-16), each within these limits.
