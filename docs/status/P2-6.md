# P2-6: Voting identity

- State: in progress (part 1 merged in #61, #62 and #63; part 2 merged in #91; part 3, storage, in progress)
- Branch: `claude/p2-6`
- PR: part 3 to follow
- Phase: Phase 2: Open polls end to end at Tier 0

## Scope

Voting identity: a device-held Semaphore secret, encrypted with a WebAuthn PRF-derived key for recovery.

## Plan

The item is built in parts, each its own PR on this branch's name:

1. **ADR** (done): [[0017-identity-recovery-backup]]. Where the encrypted copy lives, which credential the PRF comes from, and how a new device finds the copy (T-20, T-10, T-18).
2. **Spec and `packages/crypto`** (done, #91): `docs/spec/identity-backup.md` with the PRF salt, the HKDF tags, the fixed-length plaintext (the bare 32-byte secret, versioned through the envelope's header and associated data), the envelope and its strict decoder, and the locator id; the `packages/crypto` API the ballot client calls; cross-language vectors from an independent implementation. Uses RFC 8439's ChaCha20-Poly1305 from `@noble/ciphers`, which P1-12 part 2 (#58) adds, so it starts once #58 merges.
3. **Storage** (this part): the operational table in `packages/core` (no account column, classed in the T-10 no-join test), the ballot-path procedures in `packages/api-contract` and `apps/api` (put, get and delete by locator, fixed body size, no session), a test that a request carrying an account cookie or token is refused, and the expiry rule.
4. The ballot client's flows (create the backup passkey with user verification, back up, recover) come with [[P2-8]]. Before it ships backups, the ballot RP ID host must be chosen under the ADR's rules (dedicated, stable, on a registrable domain other than `apps/web`'s, no other subdomains or wildcard DNS); P2-8 puts the RP ID's `/.well-known/webauthn` in the signed release and tests that it lists no `apps/web` origin; P1-19's served-client check covers it; P2-11 keeps the DNS clean.

## Open

- Revoking a stolen identity and re-registering (T-18) has no design yet. At Tier 2 the uniqueness nullifier lets one document register once, so a voter who loses the device and every backup loses Tier 2 until it exists. It needs its own ADR, after P3-2.

## Part 2

- [[identity-backup]], a new spec page: the identity secret (`1 ≤ s < l`, 32 bytes big-endian, drawn by rejection from 251-bit values); the fixed PRF salt `H(DS("abolish/v1/identity-backup/prf-salt", ""))`; `wrap_key` and `locator` from the PRF output by HKDF-SHA-256 under two tags; the server's row id `H(DS("abolish/v1/identity-backup/id", locator))`; the envelope, record type `0x000a` version 1 (a 12-byte random nonce and the 48-byte ChaCha20-Poly1305 output, 63 bytes in all), with associated data binding the type, version and locator; strict decoding and the opening codes `backup-open` and `backup-secret`.
- Five tags in [[domain-separation]], `0x000a` in [[versioning]], rows in the spec and vectors indexes, `circuit.json` regenerated for the new tags; T-18 and T-20 history.
- `packages/crypto/src/identity-backup.ts`: `newIdentitySecret`, `isIdentitySecret`, `identityBackupPrfSalt`, `identityBackupLocator`, `identityBackupId`, `sealIdentityBackup`, `openIdentityBackup`, the codec and `IdentityBackupError`. The seams that take their randomness (`newIdentitySecretWith`, `sealIdentityBackupWith`) aren't exported from the entry point. Keys, the locator and copies of the PRF output, secret and random draws are overwritten after use; the secret's range check compares bytes against `l` and never makes a BigInt of it. The sampler gives up after 128 rejected draws.
- Vendored RFC 5869 (`test-vectors/rfc/rfc5869.txt`) with a test of `@noble/hashes`' HKDF against its three SHA-256 cases. No new dependency: `@noble/hashes` and `@noble/ciphers` are already the package's.

Tested:

- `docs/spec/vectors/identity-backup.json` from an independent Python implementation (`scripts/identity-backup-vectors.py`, standard library only: HKDF over `hmac`, checked against RFC 5869 A.1–A.3 first; ChaCha20-Poly1305 from `share-encryption-vectors.py`, checked against RFC 8439): the salt; 10 secret encodings at every boundary (0, 1, `l − 1`, `l`, `l + 1`, 2²⁵¹, 2²⁵⁶ − 1, short, long); 5 sampling sequences (a kept first draw, top bits cleared, rejected 0 and `l` with and without the top bits set); 4 backups with every intermediate value, each also opened with the published key and associated data alone; 20 envelopes that don't open (another PRF output, each field altered, another locator or version in the associated data, non-canonical plaintexts, and eleven decode rejections).
- fast-check (`FC_NUM_RUNS`): seal then open round-trips and another PRF output fails; any one-bit change of the body fails to open; `decode(encode(x)) = x` and every accepted encoding re-encodes to itself. Fresh secrets are in range and distinct, and two seals of one secret use different nonces.
- Inputs: anything but an identity secret is refused for sealing; wrong-length or non-`Uint8Array` PRF outputs, locators and nonces are refused; the caller's buffers aren't changed.

Known-weak (part 2):

- **The recovered identity's commitment isn't checked here.** That needs Baby Jubjub scalar multiplication and circomlib's Poseidon, which `@noble/curves` doesn't provide; P1-17b brings Poseidon, and P2-8's recovery flow needs an audited Baby Jubjub before it can check a recovered secret against the tier groups ([[0017-identity-recovery-backup]], Option A, step 6). Until then a recovered secret is only checked to be in range.
- **Random 96-bit nonces under one key per passkey.** Safe for any realistic number of re-backups (about 2³² seals before a repeat has probability 2⁻³³), but nothing counts seals.
- **No freshness** ([[identity-backup]], Security notes): storage can return an older envelope sealed under the same passkey, and it opens. Only the client's commitment check (P2-8) catches a wrong identity; the T-18 revocation design must revisit it.
- **Zeroising is best effort.** JavaScript copies buffers freely, and `@noble/*` keeps internal state; overwriting our own copies of `P`, the key and the secret narrows, but doesn't close, what a memory dump finds (T-41).
- Everything of part 1's Known-weak still holds: nothing stores or uses backups until parts 3 and P2-8.

Independent review (part 2): a fresh in-session subagent reviewed the diff against `.github/review/crypto-review.md`. It regenerated both vector files byte for byte, checked the vendored RFC's hash against the RFC Editor's, and ran one-line mutations of `src/identity-backup.ts`: every one was caught except three it judged harmless (an `isView` check that `new Uint8Array` duplicates, the version in the associated data while version 1 is the only one, and a wipe). It found nothing blocking and no injection attempt. Its five other findings are all addressed:

1. No freshness: a Security note and Known-weak entry above.
2. The ADR's "versioned plaintext record" against the bare secret: the spec now says the plaintext is unframed and why, and the scope line above matches.
3. Uncleared copies: the draw buffer, the range check's copy and the opened locator are wiped, and the range check compares bytes against `l` instead of making a BigInt of the secret.
4. An unbounded sampler: it gives up after 128 draws, tested.
5. Missing vectors: a header-and-version-only input and a test-range record type.
   A second pass over the fixes checked the range check against a BigInt reference on every boundary and several hundred thousand random inputs, and found nothing blocking; its two wording nits (the range check's comment claims no more than no early exit, and the sampler's error names its constant) are applied.

## Part 1

- [[0017-identity-recovery-backup]]: option A (a separate, UV-gated backup passkey on a dedicated ballot RP ID, an envelope stored by us under a PRF-derived locator through the ballot path). Option B (a plaintext recovery code) is rejected because the brief keeps the secret on the device; the owner may still ask for it. Option C (keyed by account, with the account passkey's PRF) is rejected for T-20. `Accepted (agent) — owner may veto`.
- T-20 moves from Needs design to Planned (file and index row); history entries in T-10, T-18, T-20, T-41, T-52 and T-66; a Known-weak entry in STATUS.
- Tested: `vp check`; in-session subagent review with the `crypto-review` checklist (the ADR fixes the privacy model of a protocol secret). Its first pass found four blocking points, all fixed: the PRF output depends on user verification (both ceremonies now require it); the boundary is the RP ID's whole domain, related origins and synced devices, not one origin (stated, with a dedicated-host rule); the live well-known file could let `apps/web` in (now signed with the release, checked by P1-19, and stated as a residual); and a plaintext recovery code contradicts the brief (dropped, left to the owner). Its second pass found nothing blocking; its five notes (the BS flag, the threat list, passkey-provider takeover, the owner question's wording, an unchecked CTAP anchor) are applied, except the anchor, which the session's proxy couldn't reach.
- Follow-up to #61, from `claude-review`'s two non-blocking notes: the ADR now states what a recovered client must do before it casts (show earlier ballots under its nullifiers for the voter to confirm, name only newer blocks, warn against casting from two devices) since cast records aren't backed up, and T-41's residual no longer says the native app closes it for voters with a backup.
- Second follow-up, from `claude-review` on #62: the recovered client's board reads come from segments downloaded in full, never a per-nullifier query (T-11, T-13), and the voter can judge earlier ballots only by their approximate cast time (T-18).
- Known-weak: nothing is built yet. Whoever serves the ballot RP ID reaches the secret on every device the passkey syncs to (T-41); anyone can write backup rows (flooding bounded only at the proxy, T-52); a database wipe loses every backup (T-66); voters without PRF support, or whose passkey doesn't sync, have no lasting backup; recovery works only on origins the RP ID's well-known file lists.
