# Tally: summed shares and cell openings

How the result of a plurality or approval election is computed, and how anyone checks it from the board: the aggregate commitments of each cell of the tally partition; each trustee's summed shares, which go only to the other trustees, and their check against the aggregates; the summed-share message (record type `0x000b`, version 1) that carries them from one trustee to another; the combination of any `k` valid summed shares into the opening of each aggregate; the opening record (record type `0x0006`, version 1), its signature and the window in which it must reach the board; the checks on the published openings; and each trustee's destruction of its key material after the tally, with the signed statement (record type `0x000f`, version 1) that says so. A live poll's running totals, and which snapshot each release opens, are Live results. What is published follows [[0015-tally-publication]] (option C): the opening, never a trustee's summed shares. This is the tally of [[0002-everlasting-privacy]] (option B, accepted by the owner) for the homomorphic scheme of [[0003-tally-scheme]] (`tally_scheme` 1, [[election-definition]]). Notation is from [[notation]], the group from [[group]], the committed vote and shares from [[ballot-validity]]. Threats: T-29, T-14, T-15, T-16, T-54, T-36, T-31, T-34, T-39, T-40, T-69.

Regional cells come from election-definition version 3 ([[election-definition]], Regional cells; [[0019-regional-cells]]): everything here holds per cell of its partition. Version 4's partition is version 3's. A ballot in a version-3 or version-4 election names its cell (ballot version 2, [[ballot-validity]], Ballot record (version 2)), and a cell's ballots are the admitted ballots naming it.

Under ballot versions 4 and 5 (definition version 6, and version 7 under `electorate_mode` 1 or, with ballot version 5, 2; [[ballot-validity]], Ballot record (version 4) and (version 5)), a ballot's `E_{j,t}` here are its default half's, `vss`, and the trustees are the default panel's seats. Without an author panel that half is the whole sharing (`E^D_{j,0} = C_j`), so everything on this page applies unchanged, under tally-opening version 1. With an author panel each panel sums and opens its own half and the count is the sum of the two partial openings ([[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]], Part 1, Tally); that tally-opening version is [[P2-16]] part 3b's, and until it exists no definition with an author panel passes [[verifier]] 2.3.

## Sources

- **Summing Pedersen VSS sharings** and checking each sum against the product of the VSS commitments: R. Cramer, M. Franklin, B. Schoenmakers and M. Yung, "Multi-Authority Secret-Ballot Elections with Linear Work", EUROCRYPT 1996, Section 3 (each authority publishes the sum of its shares over the counted ballots; the sum is checked against the product of the ballots' commitments, and any `k` of them interpolate the tally). The commitments and the check are Pedersen's (T. P. Pedersen, CRYPTO 1991, Section 4), as in [[ballot-validity]], Shares.
- **The summed-share message's encryption** is the hybrid KEM of [[share-encryption]] (X-Wing's combiner over ML-KEM-768 and ristretto255, then ChaCha20-Poly1305, RFC 8439) under its own tags, so no ciphertext or key of one is valid for the other.
- **Lagrange interpolation at 0** over the scalar field: A. Shamir, "How to Share a Secret", CACM 1979.

No new primitive: the group operations are `@noble/curves`' ristretto255, and the scalar arithmetic is modulo `ℓ` ([[group]]).

## Aggregates

For cell `c`, option `j` and `t = 0, …, k − 1`, the aggregate VSS commitment is the product of the selected ballots' VSS commitments:

```
Ê_{c,j,t} = Π_{b ∈ S_c} E^{(b)}_{j,t}
```

`Ê_{c,j,0} = Ĉ_{c,j}` is the aggregate commitment of option `j` in cell `c`: it commits to `Σ_b v^{(b)}_j`, the number of the cell's counted ballots that select option `j`, with randomness `Σ_b r^{(b)}_j`. For an empty cell every aggregate is the identity. An aggregate is computed, never decoded, so it may be the identity; it is encoded, where an encoding is needed, as `element` with the identity allowed ([[group]]).

## Summed shares

Trustee `i` (its seat, `1 ≤ i ≤ n`) holds, for every ballot `b` in `S_c`, its shares `(f^{(b)}_j(i), g^{(b)}_j(i))` for every option, each checked when it received them ([[ballot-validity]], Shares). Its summed share for cell `c` and option `j` is

```
V_{c,i,j} = Σ_{b ∈ S_c} f^{(b)}_j(i)   (mod ℓ)
R_{c,i,j} = Σ_{b ∈ S_c} g^{(b)}_j(i)   (mod ℓ)
```

the evaluation at `i` of the summed polynomials `F_{c,j} = Σ_b f^{(b)}_j` and `G_{c,j} = Σ_b g^{(b)}_j`, whose coefficients `Ê_{c,j,t}` commits to. A trustee's summed shares for a cell are a `list<share, MAX_OPTIONS>` of `m` entries, one per option in order, with `share` as in [[ballot-validity]] (`v`, `r`, both `scalar`). For an empty cell every summed share is `(0, 0)`.

A trustee sums over exactly the anchored `S_c`, never over a set it chooses, and only for the cells of the registered partition, once per cell (T-14, T-16; [[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Tally). It sends its summed shares only to the other trustees, encrypted to each under the hybrid KEM, never through the board, IPFS or the archive, and with no posting fallback: a copy there, even encrypted, is harvestable by every future adversary ([[0015-tally-publication]]). Summed shares from `k` seats fix `F_{c,j}` and `G_{c,j}`, and so their value at every seat; with shares posted under the fallback of [[0002-everlasting-privacy]], those values let anyone who later breaks the posted shares' classical encryption complete ballots that have fewer than `k` posted shares (the attack in [[0015-tally-publication]], Context). A summed share found on the board is a disclosure that can't be attributed: its sender, every recipient, anyone who seized a trustee before destruction and anyone who later decrypts a harvested copy all hold it, and summed shares check themselves against the aggregates, so a discloser needs no signature at all ([[verifier]] 7.4).

## Summed-share check

A trustee checks every summed share it receives, from seat `i`, against the aggregates, as it checks a ballot's shares ([[ballot-validity]], Shares):

```
g^{V_{c,i,j}} · h^{R_{c,i,j}} = Ê_{c,j,0} · Ê_{c,j,1}^{i} · … · Ê_{c,j,k−1}^{i^{k−1}}   for every j
```

The check passes when the list has exactly `m` entries, each a canonical `scalar`, and every option's equation holds. Summed shares that pass are the evaluations at `i` of `F_{c,j}` and `G_{c,j}` (binding, under discrete log in ristretto255). Summed shares that fail are excluded from the combination; the trustees know which seat sent them, but nothing on the board attributes them (T-29, [[0015-tally-publication]]). No Fiat–Shamir proof accompanies a summed share: the check is a deterministic equation over public values, so `abolish/v1/fs/tally-share` ([[domain-separation]]) isn't needed and is retired, never used.

## Summed-share message

Trustee `i` sends its summed shares for cell `c` to each other trustee `j` in a **summed-share message**, record type `0x000b`, version 1 ([[versioning]]). It is private: it travels only between trustee nodes, over a channel that negotiates hybrid post-quantum TLS (`X25519MLKEM768`), as [[0002-everlasting-privacy]] requires of share intake, so a recorded session falls only to an adversary who breaks both ML-KEM and X25519; it is never put on the board, IPFS, the archive or any store that keeps it after delivery (Summed shares; [[0015-tally-publication]]). The board's envelope refuses it (`unexpected-record-type`, [[board]], Allowed payloads). No election profile pins it: the tally-opening version the profile pins fixes it ([[versioning]], Three levels), and version 1 of the tally opening uses version 1 of this message, so neither side chooses a version for one election (T-31, T-34).

| Field             | Type          | Meaning                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `definition_hash` | `bytes[32]`   | The election's definition hash                                                                                                                            |
| `cell`            | `u8`          | The cell, by its position in the registered partition, as in the tally opening                                                                            |
| `aggregate_hash`  | `bytes[32]`   | The cell's aggregate over the anchored counted set, as the tally opening's `aggregate_hash` (Opening record): the sender's view of the set it summed over |
| `sender`          | `u8`          | `i`, the seat whose summed shares these are                                                                                                               |
| `recipient`       | `u8`          | `j`, the seat they are encrypted to; never `i`                                                                                                            |
| `ct_mlkem`        | `bytes[1088]` | `ct_M`                                                                                                                                                    |
| `ct_dh`           | `element`     | `ct_DH`. Never the identity, as in [[share-encryption]]                                                                                                   |
| `sealed`          | `bytes<4116>` | The AEAD output: `encode(summed shares)`, a `list<share, MAX_OPTIONS>` of `m` entries (a 4-byte count and 64 bytes per option), and the 16-byte tag       |
| `signature`       | `p256-sig`    | The sender's signature (below)                                                                                                                            |

**Encryption.** With recipient `j`'s registered KEM key `(ek_j, dh_j)`, taken by seat from the registered key set the transcript check returned for the transcript the definition names (`ceremony_transcript_hash`; [[ceremony]], Transcript check) and never from a set served by anyone else, or from another run's or election's ([[share-encryption]], Trustee KEM key; [[ceremony]], Transcript check; a substituted key would hand the sums to whoever holds it, T-37), the sender draws 32 bytes `μ` and a scalar `e`, uniform on 1 to `ℓ − 1`, from the CSPRNG, fresh for every message, and computes:

```
(ss_M, ct_M) = ML-KEM-768.Encaps_internal(ek_j, μ)
ct_DH        = g^e
ss_DH        = dh_j^e
K            = H(DS("abolish/v1/kem/summed-share", ss_M ‖ encode(ss_DH) ‖ encode(ct_DH) ‖ encode(dh_j)))
aad          = DS("abolish/v1/kem/summed-share-aad",
                 u16(0x000b) ‖ u8(1) ‖ definition_hash ‖ u8(cell) ‖ aggregate_hash ‖ u8(i) ‖ u8(j))
sealed       = ChaCha20-Poly1305.Seal(K, nonce = 12 zero bytes, aad, encode(summed shares))
```

`K` is [[share-encryption]]'s combiner under its own tag, so it stays secret while either ML-KEM-768 or discrete logs in ristretto255 hold, and each `K` seals exactly one message (a fresh `μ` and `e`), so the fixed nonce never repeats under a key. The tags keep the two uses of one KEM key apart: a ballot's share ciphertext never opens as a summed-share message, or the reverse. `aad` binds the record type and version, the election, the cell, the counted set and both seats, so a message opens only for the recipient, cell and counted set it was sealed for, and a sender field changed in transit makes it fail to open.

**Signature.** `signature` is an ECDSA P-256 signature by seat `i`'s per-election signing key ([[trustee-identity]], Which key signs what), under `abolish/v1/sig/summed-share`, over the record's encoding without its signature: the header and every field from `definition_hash` to `sealed`. It authenticates the sender to the recipient, binds the version through the header, and covers both KEM ciphertexts, so nothing in the message can be swapped without the sender's key.

**Receiving.** Trustee `j`, holding its `kem_secret` and the anchored counted set, checks each summed-share message in this order; the first failure is the result, and a message that fails is ignored:

| Code               | Check                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `summed-malformed` | It decodes strictly as version 1 ([[notation]])                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `summed-context`   | `definition_hash` is the election's, `cell` a cell of its registered partition, `recipient` is `j`, `sender` a seat other than `j` in the key set the transcript check returned for the transcript the definition names, and `aggregate_hash` the cell's over the counted set `j` computed |
| `summed-signature` | `signature` verifies under the registered signing key of seat `sender`                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `summed-decrypt`   | `ss_M = ML-KEM-768.Decaps_internal(dk_j, ct_M)`, `ss_DH = ct_DH^{x_j}`, `K` and `aad` as above; the AEAD opens and its plaintext decodes as a `list<share, MAX_OPTIONS>` with exactly `m` entries                                                                                          |
| `summed-check`     | The summed shares pass the summed-share check (above) for seat `sender`                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

Summed shares that pass are seat `sender`'s, and `j` may combine them (Combination). Notes:

- **Why check before decapsulating.** Checking the context and signature first means only a registered seat's key can make a trustee decapsulate a chosen `ct_M`, so this path isn't a decapsulation endpoint open to anyone holding a member's secret, as share intake is ([[share-encryption]], Residuals; T-39). An `aggregate_hash` other than the recipient's means the two disagree about the counted set; the recipient doesn't decrypt summed shares over a set it can't check.
- **No proof of knowledge of `e`.** A share ciphertext carries one because a trustee's complaint reveals its DH value ([[share-encryption]], Complaints). Here nothing a recipient derives from a message is ever revealed: there is no complaint, a failure is only ignored, and no result code goes back to the sender. Anything that would reveal `ss_DH` or `K`, or tell `summed-decrypt` from `summed-check` to the sender, needs a proof of knowledge of `e` first. A registered sender that can observe whether a recipient accepted a message it crafted learns at most that accept or reject bit, a decisional Diffie–Hellman query on `x_j`, which is the setting every CCA-secure hashed-ElGamal KEM is proved in (DHIES, M. Abdalla, M. Bellare and P. Rogaway, CT-RSA 2001, under gap Diffie–Hellman in the random-oracle model; X-Wing's IND-CCA argument for its DH half). No computational DH value is ever answered, so the static-DH concern of [[group]] doesn't arise.
- **Signed, but not publicly attributable.** The signature tells the recipient which seat sent a failing message; it gives no formal proof to anyone else: checking a decryption needs the recipient's ML-KEM secret (the DH half alone could be proved with a Chaum–Pedersen proof, the ML-KEM half can't). It isn't deniable either: a recipient that publishes a message's `K` lets anyone open the signed `sealed` and check the values against the aggregate, and it can't forge that, since only the ciphertext's author can find a second key that opens it. That is convincing evidence of what seat `sender` sent, though not a proof that `K` is the key the KEM gives. This page defines no such complaint, because it would publish per-seat values, which [[0015-tally-publication]] keeps off the board; so a wrong summed share stays attributed only among the trustees (Residuals), and a disclosure by a recipient that also publishes `K` points at the sender as well as at itself (T-29, T-15).
- **Resending.** A sender may resend a message, or re-encrypt the same summed shares in a new one, until the recipient has it; every passing copy carries the same values. It sends a message only for a cell it summed over the anchored counted set, once its sums pass its own summed-share check.
- **Size.** `3 + 32 + 1 + 32 + 1 + 1 + 1088 + 32 + 4 + (4 + 64·m + 16) + 64` bytes: 1918 bytes at 10 options.

## Combination

A trustee holding passing summed shares from `k` or more seats for a cell, its own (which it checks too) and those it received in summed-share messages, combines them. Let `Q` be the `k` lowest seats among them. With fewer than `k`, it can't (`tally-quorum`), and waits for more. Any trustee whose summed shares pass may be combined, including one that missed the complaint window and so isn't tally-eligible in [[0002-everlasting-privacy]] (Tally liveness): summed shares that pass are correct points of the cell's summed polynomials whoever computed them, and a trustee that couldn't sum every counted ballot correctly fails the check, so eligibility needs no separate filter, which would only make a missing opening likelier. With the Lagrange coefficients at 0

```
λ_i = Π_{l ∈ Q, l ≠ i} l · (l − i)^{−1}   (mod ℓ)     for i ∈ Q
```

the cell's opening of option `j` is

```
V_{c,j} = Σ_{i ∈ Q} λ_i · V_{c,i,j}   (mod ℓ)
R_{c,j} = Σ_{i ∈ Q} λ_i · R_{c,i,j}   (mod ℓ)
```

and the trustee publishes, per cell, the list of `(V_{c,j}, R_{c,j})` for `j = 0, …, m − 1`, each a canonical `scalar`, in a tally opening (Opening record, below). Any `k` passing summed shares give the same opening: they lie on the same polynomials (binding), and `k` points fix a polynomial of degree `k − 1`, so `V_{c,j} = F_{c,j}(0)` and `R_{c,j} = G_{c,j}(0)` whichever are combined. Before publishing, the trustee runs the checks below on its own opening.

## Opening checks

The verifier recomputes `Ĉ_{c,j}` from the counted ballots ([[verifier]] 7.1) and checks each cell's published openings (Opening record, Which openings are a cell's), in cell order and then option order:

| Code                   | Check                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `tally-late`           | Election-level, before any cell's checks: no entry boarded after the counted extent changes a cell's counted set (Tally window), attributed as that section says                      |
| `tally-missing`        | The cell has a published opening with exactly `m` entries                                                                                                                             |
| `tally-aggregate`      | Reported per opening, never a cell's verdict on its own: the opening's `aggregate_hash` is the cell's over the counted set the verifier recomputed                                    |
| `tally-opening`        | `g^{V_{c,j}} · h^{R_{c,j}} = Ĉ_{c,j}`: the opening opens the product of the cell's ballot commitments                                                                                 |
| `tally-conflict`       | Every opening of the cell on the board that passes `tally-opening` is the same: two different passing openings show that binding is broken                                            |
| `tally-range`          | `V_{c,j}`, read as an integer from 0 to `ℓ − 1`, is at most `\|S_c\|`, the number of the cell's counted ballots                                                                       |
| `tally-question-range` | Under definition version 5, for each question `q` in order, `V_{c,o_q} + … + V_{c,o_q+m_q−1}`, each read as an integer as above, lies between `min_q · \|S_c\|` and `max_q · \|S_c\|` |

The openings of a cell are every opening of it within the board's sealed extent ([[board]]; [[election-definition]], The seal), signed or not: one found after the seal or outside the anchored extent is never part of the board. `tally-late` runs first, once for the election, and its failure is reported together with the per-cell results below, which still run over the counted extent (so the report shows whether the trustees' openings were right); a late direct-submitted ballot also fails [[verifier]] 5.6, and both are reported. Per cell, the checks run in this order, and the first failure, in cell order, fails the election ([[verifier]] 7.2; T-29, T-54):

1. `tally-missing` fails if no opening of the cell has exactly `m` entries (each is a canonical `scalar`, or the record wouldn't decode). An opening of the cell with another number of entries is reported, attributed as its signature allows.
2. `tally-opening` fails if none of those passes: an opening passes if its `aggregate_hash` is the cell's (`tally-aggregate` otherwise) and it opens every option. The cell reports the first of those openings in board order, with its first failing option, or with no option if it is over another counted set; each failing opening is reported, attributed to a trustee only if its signature verifies over a message binding this election's definition hash, this cell and `m` (Opening record, Signature), and otherwise reported as unattributed, so an honest trustee's opening replayed into another cell or election never frames it.
3. `tally-conflict` fails if two passing openings differ.
4. `tally-range` runs on the passing opening.
5. `tally-question-range` runs on the passing opening after `tally-range`, under definition version 5, and reports the first failing question.

A passing opening is correct whoever computed it, so an unattributed one counts; if no passing opening of the cell is attributed, [[verifier]] 8.4 reports the cell's opening as unsigned. Under binding every passing opening is the same, so a relay can't crowd out an honest one by posting others, and two different ones reveal `log_g h`; no vector exercises `tally-conflict`, since building two passing openings needs that discrete log.

Notes:

- **The opening is what makes the result verifiable.** Under binding, no one can publish `(V, R)` opening `Ĉ_{c,j}` with `V` other than the cell's real count. `tally-range` can't fail while binding holds, since option proofs bound every counted `v^{(b)}_j` to 0 or 1; it is checked anyway, as it is what the result rests on (T-29, T-36), and a vector exercises it with a ballot no validity check would admit.
- **`tally-question-range` can't fail while binding holds either.** Every counted ballot passed its question range proofs ([[ballot-validity]], Question range proof), so each question's count per ballot is within its limits and their sum over `S_c` within `|S_c|` times them. It is checked because each question's result, the slice of the opening at its options, rests on it, and a vector exercises it with ballots no validity check would admit. Results per question are those slices, and coarser totals are summed per question and option (Coarser totals); nothing relates two questions, so no joint figure across questions exists to report ([[0026-multi-question-ballots]], No cross-tabulation; T-29, T-16).
- **An honest combination always passes `tally-opening`,** even if binding is broken: `Σ_{i∈Q} λ_i · i^t` is 1 for `t = 0` and 0 for `0 < t < k`, so `Π_{i∈Q} (Π_t Ê_{c,j,t}^{i^t})^{λ_i} = Ê_{c,j,0}` holds in the exponent.
- **The opening reveals the count and nothing else about any ballot.** `V_{c,j}` is the count. `R_{c,j}` is the sum of the counted ballots' randomness, which each perfectly hiding commitment leaves uniform; even with discrete logs in ristretto255, `V + x·R` already follows from `Ĉ_{c,j}`. A ballot with fewer than `k` posted shares stays hidden: no per-seat value of the summed polynomials is published ([[0015-tally-publication]], Context). A cell with one counted ballot reveals that ballot: that is the cell's size, flagged by [[verifier]] 8.2, and why openings are only ever per cell of one partition (T-16, [[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Tally).

## Opening record

A trustee publishes a cell's opening as a **tally opening**, record type `0x0006`, version 1 ([[versioning]]), on the election's board ([[board]], Allowed payloads). One record carries one cell's opening; an election's tally transcript is every tally opening on its board. Version 1 of the tally opening also fixes the summed-share message its trustees exchange at version 1 (Summed-share message).

| Field             | Type                       | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `definition_hash` | `bytes[32]`                | The election's definition hash ([[election-definition]], Definition hash)                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `cell`            | `u8`                       | The cell, by its position in the registered partition, from 0: under version 1 of the definition, the position of its tier in `electorate`; under version 3, its number in the definition's cells, at most 255 ([[verifier]] 2.8)               |
| `aggregate_hash`  | `bytes[32]`                | The aggregate the opening is over, which names its counted set: `H(DS("abolish/v1/tally-aggregate", encode(Ĉ_{c,0}, …, Ĉ_{c,m−1})))`, the cell's aggregate commitments as a `list<element, MAX_OPTIONS>` with the identity allowed (Aggregates) |
| `signer`          | `u8`                       | The seat that combined the opening and signs it                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `opening`         | `list<share, MAX_OPTIONS>` | `(V_{c,j}, R_{c,j})` for `j = 0, …, m − 1`, in option order, with `share` as in [[ballot-validity]] (`v`, then `r`, both `scalar`)                                                                                                              |
| `signature`       | `p256-sig`                 | The signer's signature (below)                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

The record decodes strictly ([[notation]]), so every `V` and `R` is a canonical `scalar`: a count encoded as `V + ℓ` is no record. Decoding doesn't compare `definition_hash`, `cell`, `aggregate_hash`, `signer` or the number of entries with the election; the checks below do, so a record that names another election, cell, counted set, seat or option count is still read, and reported.

**Which counted set.** `aggregate_hash` lets a verifier tell an opening over another aggregate (reported `tally-aggregate`) from a wrong opening of the verifier's own aggregate (`tally-opening`). It hashes the aggregate, not the ballots, so two counted sets with the same aggregate (one adding a ballot that commits to `(0, 0)`, say) share it, and their openings are the same anyway. The counted set comes from the counted extent alone, anchored by `t_S` before anyone combines (Tally window), and nothing boarded later changes it, so the verifier's aggregate is the one every honest trustee computed: a signed `tally-aggregate` opening means its signer opened a set other than the counted extent's, and the report attributes it to the signer as an opening over another set (T-29).

**Signature.** `signature` is an ECDSA P-256 signature by a per-election signing key, under `abolish/v1/sig/tally-opening` ([[trustee-identity]], Signatures), over

```
m = definition_hash ‖ u8(cell) ‖ aggregate_hash ‖ u8(signer) ‖ encode(opening)
```

the record's fields without its header and signature: `70 + 64·m'` bytes for an opening of `m'` entries. The signature **attributes** the opening to seat `signer` if and only if `signer` is a seat of the election's registered key set ([[ceremony]], Transcript check) and the signature verifies under that seat's registered signing key, never the identity key ([[trustee-identity]], Which key signs what). Any other opening is **unattributed**: it still counts if it passes, since a passing opening is correct whoever made it, but a failure in it is no one's (T-29). `m` binds the definition hash, the cell, the counted set and, through the list's count, the number of options, so an honest trustee's opening replayed into another election or cell, or with entries added or removed, is unattributed and never frames its signer. A relay that strips or replaces a signature only makes the opening unattributed: a passing one still counts, and a failing one is blamed on no one. A trustee signs at most one opening per cell, each after its own checks pass (Combination), so a second, different opening signed by the same seat is evidence against it.

**Which openings are a cell's.** For [[verifier]] 7.2 and 7.4, every tally opening within the sealed extent of the board ([[board]]; [[election-definition]], The seal) is, in board order, either:

- an **opening of cell `c`**, if its `definition_hash` is the election's and its `cell` is `c`, a cell of the registered partition; or
- a **stray opening**, if it names another definition or a cell the partition doesn't have. A stray opening is reported, attributed as its signature allows, and is never an opening of any cell ([[verifier]] 7.4).

Anyone can post a tally opening (an unattributed one counts if it passes), so one that fails to decode, including one whose version the profile doesn't pin (`profile-mismatch`), is a rejected entry ([[board]], Allowed payloads; [[verifier]] 2.3), listed in the report, never an election failure and never an opening of any cell, so it can't stand in for a missing one either.

**Boarding.** The board operator boards a tally opening only if it names this election's definition and a cell of the registered partition, its `aggregate_hash` is that of the cell over the anchored counted set, it passes `tally-opening` against that aggregate, its signature attributes it to a registered seat, and no opening of that cell by that seat is on the board yet; it refuses every other. A passing opening's `V` and `R` are fixed by the aggregate (binding), so a boarded opening carries no chosen bytes beyond a registered trustee's choice of ECDSA nonce, at most 64 bytes per seat and cell ([[notation]], Result-critical fields; T-17). Without this rule anyone could archive about 4 KB of chosen bytes per failing or stray record, without limit, and a trustee or any recipient of summed shares could disguise summed shares as a failing opening, which [[0015-tally-publication]] keeps off the board (T-15). The verifier still reads whatever the board holds, as above: a record the operator should have refused (a failing, unattributed or stray opening, or a second one by the same seat for the same cell) is reported, never trusted, and also reported against the operator as a breach of this rule ([[verifier]] 7.4). The breaches decided without the counted set have their own codes: `tally-stray`, `tally-missing` (another number of entries than options), `tally-unattributed`, `tally-second` (a second opening of a cell attributed to the same seat, over another signed message: evidence against that seat) and `tally-repeat` (the same signed message again, which anyone can replay, so it is the operator's breach alone); the rest are the opening's own `tally-aggregate` or `tally-opening` ([[verifier]], Tally openings).

## Tally window

Version 1 of the tally opening fixes the **tally window**: `TALLY_WINDOW = 604,800` seconds (7 days). It starts at `t_S`, the time by which the checkpoint anchoring the counted set is due, and each cell's opening must be on the board by `t_S + TALLY_WINDOW`, boarding latency included. The destruction window follows `t_D`, at or after the tally window's end (Destruction), and the end of that window is P1-15's term in the seal's `W` ([[election-definition]], The seal). `TALLY_WINDOW` is a constant of this record version, so no creator, operator or verifier release chooses it for one election, and changing it is a new version (T-34, T-54).

`s_S` is the later of `period(closes_at) + 1` and the anchoring period containing `closes_at + l1_inclusion_bound + W_S`, and `t_S` is the end of period `s_S` plus the anchor's `deadline` ([[board]], Timing), when its checkpoint is due; where `W_S` is the latest end, counted from `closes_at + l1_inclusion_bound`, of every window that must close before the counted set is fixed: the share-delivery deadline and the complaint window of [[0002-everlasting-privacy]] (`DELIVERY_WINDOW` and `COMPLAINT_WINDOW`, constants of the delivery records' version 1, which ballot version 1 fixes, each followed by the contract-event window, since a delivery record sent through the contract path at its window's end still has to reach the board: [[delivery]], Windows), (spoiled-ballot openings add none, since they never change a counted set: [[challenge]], Publication), the close statements' `CLOSE_WINDOW`, followed by the contract-event window ([[close]], Close statement; statements never change a counted set, but the close check is decided over them once the counted set is), and the **contract-event window** (P1-18), in which a contract event, a direct-submitted ballot among them, goes from submission to the board. That window is one summed constant in seconds, of the immutable contracts the definition names, never set per election: the longest forced-inclusion delay (a ballot forced through L1 at close can reach L2 that much later, [[0004-l2-choice]]) with a margin for missed L1 slots, then L1 finality, then the boarding window, one after another. [[verifier]] 2.6 then requires the definition's `sequencing_window`, in L1 blocks, to fit inside that delay; the definition's own field never moves `s_S` or the seal. The floor `period(closes_at) + 1` covers [[board]]'s client rule 1, whose one-period tolerance lets a ballot cast in close's period, with a valid receipt (`receipt-segment`), carry the next segment, so no receipted ballot falls outside the counted extent even when `l1_inclusion_bound` and `W_S` are 0. Each window is a constant of the record version the profile pins or of the immutable contracts the definition names, so `t_S`, and with it the seal, follows from the definition alone. Counting the window from `t_S` rather than from close means a longer earlier window never eats into the trustees' time, whatever the ballot version or contracts the profile pins beside this one; the openings are combined only over the counted extent, the entries with `segment ≤ s_S`, already anchored, so an honest trustee's aggregate is the verifier's.

An entry first boarded in a segment after `s_S` never enters the counted set: not a ballot, a posting, a receipt-bearing record or a complaint, and not the contract event of a ballot direct-submitted before close. It isn't ignored either. The verifier also computes each cell's counted set over the whole sealed extent, `S_c` exactly as defined above (selection, then delivery and complaints) but reading every entry in it, with each record's own window applied by its submission time (its L2 block's timestamp, or for a ballot forced through L1, the L1 timestamp its relay call recorded, [[close]], Window), never by its segment, so a record sent after its window never enters either set. If that set differs from `S_c` for any cell, a late entry would have changed what the cell counts, and the election fails `tally-late` ([[verifier]] 7.1). A late entry that changes no cell's counted set (a lower-ranked ballot, a duplicate) fails nothing.

`tally-late` is attributed to the board operator for each late entry that changes a counted set and that it could have boarded by the end of `s_S`: a record that reached it through the board path, whose submission time is its segment's anchoring period unless P1-18 defines another (the operator MUST NOT board a board-path record that could change a counted set in a segment after `s_S`, and [[board]]'s client rule 1 never asks it to, since `s_S ≥ period(closes_at) + 1`; contract events are always boarded, late or not, as [[verifier]] 4.4 and 5.6 require), or a contract event whose L2 block is derivable from finalized L1 data at least the P1-18 boarding window before the end of `s_S`. A contract event that became derivable later is a settlement overrun (an L1 finality stall, or a sequencer delay beyond the bounds the definition assumes; T-50, [[verifier]] 2.6): the election still fails, unattributed, and is never blamed on the operator or a trustee. So no voter, trustee or relay can make an honest operator fail: anything they send in time is boardable in time, and anything sent late is outside its window. Without this check an operator could hold back a voter's direct-submitted re-vote past the counted extent, keep the earlier, perhaps coerced, ballot counted, and the election would still verify (T-25, T-45); with it the drop fails loudly and names the operator, as 5.6 does for a direct-submitted ballot left off the board. Nor can the operator make honest openings look wrong this way: the trustees combined over the counted extent, as the verifier does, so their openings still pass, and the failure is the operator's (T-29, T-54, T-53). Each item that specifies a record defines its window by submission time, fixes it inside `W_S`, and says which records can change a counted set: [[delivery]] does so for share receipts, postings and complaints (all three can), [[challenge]] for spoiled-ballot openings (which can't, so they add nothing to `W_S`), and P1-18 for contract events.

Why 7 days: a cell with no opening by the seal fails the election permanently (`tally-missing`, T-54), so the window has to cover the exchange of summed shares (Summed-share message), the combination and `k` trustees' nodes being briefly unreachable, a weekend included. A longer window postpones the seal, and with it the moment the result is final and checkable from L1 alone. An opening boarded after the tally window but before the seal (another post-close window may end later) still counts: the window fixes what the seal must cover, not which openings are read.

## Destruction

After the tally, each trustee destroys what would let it, or anyone who later seizes or compels it, open ballots, and says so on the board in a signed **destruction statement**, record type `0x000f`, version 1 ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Consequences; T-14, T-40, T-69). Destruction can't be proven: the statement is a public, attributable claim, never evidence that the material is gone (Residuals).

**When.** A trustee node destroys, at the earlier of these two moments:

- every cell of the registered partition has a **passing opening**, one of `m` entries over the cell's counted set that opens every option (it passes `tally-missing`, `tally-aggregate` and `tally-opening`, Opening checks), in a segment whose counted checkpoint (timely and sent by the anchoring authority) the node has seen anchored on L1, read from its own finalized-L1 source and never the operator's ([[board]], Timing), each opening checked by the node itself against its own aggregate and its inclusion against that anchored checkpoint ([[board]], Inclusion proofs), never taken from a server's word; or
- `t_D`, the later of the end of the tally window, `t_S + TALLY_WINDOW` (Tally window), and the anchoring deadline of segment `s_T − 1` (the end of that segment plus the anchor's `deadline`, [[board]], Timing), where `s_T` is the anchoring period containing `t_S + TALLY_WINDOW`; whether or not the tally is complete.

Both moments are read from L1, as segments are: a node acts on a checkpoint it has checked against a finalized L1 block, and on `t_D` only once it has seen a finalized L1 block with a timestamp at or after it, never by its own clock. After `t_D` no entry can still join segment `s_T − 1` (its checkpoint, if any, is anchored, and a later one isn't timely), so an operator whose L1 view lags, by accident or on purpose, can't board an honest statement into an earlier segment than the report expects (Destruction checks).

The second bound caps how long a trustee keeps the material when the tally stalls. Openings boarded after the tally window still count until the seal (Tally window), and a trustee keeps every tally opening it has already signed and resends it until the seal (it is public once boarded and reveals only the cell's counts), so an operator that withholds openings until the window ends can't make the trustees destroy them; but a trustee that has destroyed its material can no longer combine new ones: the tally window is what [[parameters]] gives the tally, and a longer hold would extend every trustee's exposure for an election that is failing anyway (T-54).

**What.** Every copy, backups included, of:

- its per-election KEM secret `kem_secret`, both its ML-KEM and its ristretto255 half ([[share-encryption]], Trustee KEM key);
- every share ciphertext it stored for the election, and every share it decrypted from one, whether received at intake, read from a posting or opened for a complaint ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Private part);
- its own summed shares, every summed-share message it received and every summed share decrypted from one (Summed-share message; [[0015-tally-publication]]);
- any other value derived from them that isn't public (a partial sum, an interpolation intermediate), and the summed-share messages it sent, wherever its node kept them. Its signed tally openings are not such values: it keeps them, as above.

Then it signs the statement below and, as soon as it holds the signed bytes, destroys its per-election signing key: the statement is that key's last use ([[ceremony]], Residuals: Per-election keys). Resending the same signed statement needs no key. A node that can't reach the operator keeps resending until the statement is boarded or the destruction window (below) ends. Public data (the board, the aggregates, the openings, the ceremony transcript) is kept; so is the identity key, which is long-term ([[trustee-identity]]).

**Record.** Version 1 has no field beyond who makes the statement and for which election; what it claims is the list above, fixed by the version.

| Field             | Type        | Meaning                        |
| ----------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------ |
| `definition_hash` | `bytes[32]` | The election's definition hash |
| `signer`          | `u8`        | The seat making the statement  |
| `signature`       | `p256-sig`  | The seat's signature (below)   |

`signature` is an ECDSA P-256 signature by seat `signer`'s per-election signing key ([[trustee-identity]], Which key signs what), under `abolish/v1/sig/destruction`, over the record's encoding without its signature: `u16(0x000f) ‖ u8(1) ‖ definition_hash ‖ u8(signer)`. The header is signed so a later version that gives the same fields other meaning can't reuse a version 1 signature (T-31). The definition hash fixes the chain, registry, election and ceremony transcript, and with it which key set the seat's key is in. A statement is 100 bytes.

No election profile pins the destruction statement: the tally-opening version the profile pins fixes it ([[versioning]], Three levels), and version 1 of the tally opening uses version 1 of this statement, as it does version 1 of the summed-share message (T-31, T-34).

**Destruction window.** Version 1 of the tally opening fixes the **destruction window**, `DESTRUCTION_WINDOW = 86,400` seconds (1 day), which follows `t_D`: a statement must be on the board by `t_D + DESTRUCTION_WINDOW`, boarding latency included, where `t_D` (When) follows from the definition and the anchor's pinned clock as `t_S` does. That end, not the tally window's, is P1-15's term in the seal's `W` ([[election-definition]], The seal), so the seal always leaves a trustee that destroyed at `t_D` a day, less L1 finality, to board its statement, whatever the anchor's `deadline`. A day is enough because nothing waits on it: a missing statement fails nothing (Destruction checks), and it postpones the moment the election is final by as little as it can while covering a node that is briefly unreachable. Like `TALLY_WINDOW`, it is a constant of the record version, never chosen per election (T-34). The window applies only to an election whose pinned board-entry version allows the destruction statement ([[board]], Allowed payloads); under any other, no statement can reach the board, P1-15's term in `W` stays the end of the tally window, and [[verifier]] 8.5 is reported not applicable.

Adding this window and the statement to tally-opening version 1, rather than making a version 2, changes no byte or check of the opening record and no published vector: none fixes a seal, and the seal can't be computed for any election yet, since `W_S`'s other windows are still being specified (P1-12 part 3, P1-13, P1-18). The summed-share message was added to version 1 the same way. Once a seal can be computed and an election registered, the set of records and windows version 1 fixes is frozen like everything else ([[versioning]], Change rules).

**Boarding.** Statements reach the board only in board-entry versions 4, 5 and 7, which allow `0x000f` ([[board]], Allowed payloads). A profile that pins tally-opening version 1 MUST pin one of them ([[verifier]] 2.3, `profile-combination`), so no election tallied under version 1 leaves its trustees without a way to state destruction; P1-18's election registry MUST refuse such a definition, as [[delivery]] requires for its own rule. The board operator boards a destruction statement only if it decodes strictly as version 1, names this election's definition, its signature attributes it to a registered seat (`signer` is a seat of the key set the transcript check returned for the transcript the definition names, and the signature verifies under that seat's signing key; [[ceremony]], Transcript check), and no statement by that seat is on the board yet; it refuses every other. A boarded statement then carries no chosen bytes beyond the seat's ECDSA nonce, 64 bytes per seat and election ([[notation]], Result-critical fields; T-17). The operator doesn't judge timing: a statement boarded early is the seat's to explain, and the report shows it.

### Destruction checks

The verifier reads every destruction-statement payload in the board's sealed extent ([[verifier]] 8.5). They never fail the election or make it unverifiable: destruction can't be proven, so a statement adds nothing the result rests on, and a rule that failed an election without one would hand every trustee a veto over the result (T-54). The checks only report.

A payload that doesn't decode strictly under version 1 is a rejected entry ([[verifier]] 8.3). A decoded statement naming another definition is stray, and one naming this definition whose `signer` isn't a registered seat or whose signature doesn't verify under that seat's key is unattributed; both are listed, with the seat a stray statement's signature attributes it to, if any, and reported against the operator as a breach of the boarding rule. Each registered seat's first attributed statement in board order is its statement; any later one is listed as a duplicate, also a breach.

With `s_T` as above (When), if every cell of the partition has a passing opening (When, above) in the sealed extent, let `s_O` be the latest, over cells, of the segment of the cell's first passing opening in board order; the **destruction segment** is then `min(s_O + 1, s_T)`, and otherwise `s_T`. A trustee following the rule above destroys no earlier than the checkpoint for `s_O` is anchored, which is after the end of segment `s_O`, or at `t_D`, after which nothing joins a segment before `s_T`; either way its statement lands in the destruction segment or later. Each registered seat is reported as:

| Code                  | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `destroyed`           | Its statement is in the destruction segment or later                                                                                                                              |
| `destruction-early`   | Its statement is in an earlier segment: the seat claimed destruction before the tally it should have waited for was anchored, or the operator boarded a statement it signed early |
| `destruction-missing` | No attributed statement of the seat is in the sealed extent: the seat may still hold its material, or the operator withheld its statement (Residuals)                             |

`destruction-early` is the seat's: an honest seat signs only after the moment above, and the operator can't board a statement before it exists. `s_O` uses the first passing opening of each cell because that is the earliest a trustee watching the board could have seen the tally complete; the operator can delay openings, which only moves `s_O` later and the destruction segment with it, never an honest statement before it, since `s_T` caps it.

## Rolling tiers

Under definition version 7 with `electorate_mode` 2 the partition is one cell per rolling tier, in `rolling_electorate`'s order ([[verifier]] 2.8), and everything above holds per tier. What changes is what a result says about its electorate, since the tier grew during the poll ([[0028-rolling-electorates]], Anonymity set and reporting; T-16, T-13, T-01):

- **The final result** of each tier carries its counts at opening, at its last boundary and at the smallest step used, its eligible count and its growth, each as [[membership]] (Reported counts) defines it, and states its ballots as counted ballots among the eligible count, those who could answer during the poll, never as a share of the opening count ([[verifier]] 8.1).
- **Each live release** ([[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]], Part 2; [[P2-15]]) carries, per rolling tier beside its ballot count, the eligible count at that release, with its step ([[membership]], Reported counts, At a live release), which its ballot count never exceeds. The trustee nodes compute it from the listed groups' events, as every reader does, and the verifier recomputes it rather than reading it from the release; the record that carries it, and the code for a release that states another, are P2-15's.

## Coarser totals

A total over several cells (per tier where cells are regions, or overall) is the sum, per option, of those cells' `V_{c,j}`. It is derived, never opened from a separate aggregate: a second opening over a set overlapping a cell would let anyone subtract the two and isolate the ballots in their difference ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Tally; T-16). A live poll's running openings (Live results) are the one exception, and only under that section's rules, which bound every combination of the releases (not the final tally against them). In version 1 each cell is one tier, so the per-tier result is the cell's opening, and the overall result is the sum over every cell.

## Live results

A live poll (definition version 6 or 7 with `results_mode` 2, [[election-definition]], Poll class and results) also publishes **running totals** while voting is open: each cell's aggregate over its running set at a scheduled snapshot, opened and released per tier under the rules below ([[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]], Part 2; [[P2-15]]). Its final tally is everything above, unchanged, and a sealed poll has no running totals. This section fixes what a release counts, when a tier releases and which snapshot it releases, and what a node checks before taking part. The running opening that carries a release (its record, signature tag and window) is [[P2-15]] part 1b, and the verifier's checks and reports over releases, `live-final-batch` among them, are part 1c; until the tally-opening version that fixes the running opening exists, a live definition fails [[verifier]] 2.3 (`profile-combination`). Threats: T-16, T-45, T-01, T-29, T-54, T-14.

### Schedule

With `s_open = period(opens_at)` and `s_close = period(closes_at)` ([[board]], Timing), the **scheduled snapshots** are `σ_r = s_open + r · live_every − 1` for `r = 1, 2, …` while `σ_r < s_close`. Snapshot `r`, `S_r`, is the board's entries with `segment ≤ σ_r`, read with `h_r`, the last L1 block whose timestamp is before the end of period `σ_r`, as its L1 head. Every snapshot ends at or before `closes_at`, so running totals stop before the close; a poll shorter than `live_every` periods has none, and one with a single snapshot never releases (Release choice).

Snapshot `r` is **fixed** once a timely checkpoint for `σ_r` sent by the anchoring authority is in a finalized L1 block, or once a finalized L1 block has a timestamp at or after the end of period `σ_r` plus the anchor's `deadline` (a period that added no entries may go unanchored). After that no entry can join a segment at or before `σ_r` without failing the election ([[board]], Timing), and `h_r` is finalized. A node acts on a snapshot only once it is fixed, read from its own finalized-L1 source and never the operator's, and only over board data it has checked against the latest timely checkpoint at or before `σ_r` ([[board]], Inclusion proofs and Consistency proofs). The schedule is fixed by the definition before opening, so no one, the author and the operator included, can trigger or move a release: choosing when to release is choosing which ballots fall into a batch (T-16, T-34).

### Running set

For a tier, a cell `c` of it and a snapshot `r`, the **running set** `R_{r,c}` holds nullifiers of the tier, each with one ballot. A nullifier `ν` **enters** it at `r`, with ballot `b`, when it is in no running set of the tier at an earlier snapshot and:

1. among `S_r`'s ballots of `ν` whose membership proof passes ([[verifier]] 5.1 to 5.3, under the step a version-5 ballot names once that step is final from L1 blocks at or before `h_r`; one whose step isn't final yet is **pending** and counts too), whatever any other check later decides about them, there is exactly one distinct ballot, `b` (one `M`: repeats are one ballot, [[revoting]], Selection, step 1); `b` is admitted with `h_r` as the L1 head (5.1 to 5.5, 5.7 and 5.8, as at the final tally; a ballot whose L1 reference is above `h_r` is pending, not admitted); and `b` names cell `c`. Only the holder of a nullifier's identity secret can make a ballot whose membership proof passes, so counting every such ballot, even one later rejected for its L1 reference, its validity proofs or its window, lets no third party hold a voter out. This also holds for a pending rolling ballot whose step root can't be computed yet. If it came by direct submission, the contract has already verified its proof against the root the submitter supplied ([[membership]], Checks). That proof binds `ν` to the identity secret under the rolling scope whatever the root, so a stranger's tree gives only the stranger's own `ν`. If it came by the board path, only the operator boarded it, and an honest operator, like every seat's intake, refuses a step it hasn't seen settle ([[share-encryption]], Decryption and intake (ballot version 4)). So only the operator, or whoever controls its board path, can board a junk pending ballot carrying a voter's `ν` (Residuals of live results, Operator withholding). Counting pending ballots can't be narrowed to the ones whose proofs can be checked by `h_r`. A coercer's first ballot cast on the voter's device would then not count while its step settles, and the voter's real re-vote could enter alone at that snapshot. Counting every such ballot also stops a coercer from making the voter cast a first ballot that it knows will fail later (naming a future block with a made-up hash, say), so that the voter's real re-vote enters alone at a time the coercer picks. An honest voter whose first ballot named a block later reorganised away is held out of running totals, and counted at the close; and
2. `b` is **fully receipted** in `S_r`: for every qualified seat of every panel the definition has, `S_r` holds a share receipt for `b` that passes [[delivery]]'s receipt checks (Delivery check (version 2)). A posting never lets a ballot enter.

**Staying.** Once in, a nullifier stays in every later running set of its cell with the ballot it entered with, whatever follows: a re-vote, a tie or a posting never removes it. A fully receipted ballot can't later be excluded while its receipting seats are honest: each honest seat's receipt says its share passed intake, so no complaint against that seat can be proven ([[delivery]], Delivery check), and summing it never makes an honest seat fail the summed-share check. A seat that receipts an invalid share lies, and a proven complaint then contradicts its receipt (`receipt-contradicted`); with a cheating voter it can thus put a ballot in running sets that the final tally excludes, which affects only that voter's own ballot. So running sets only grow: `R_{i,c} ⊆ R_{j,c}` for `i ≤ j`.

Every other nullifier is **held out**: a running total never counts it, and the final tally counts it as in a sealed poll. Running totals therefore count each voter's first fully receipted choice, and "votes changed since they were first cast are counted at the close", as [[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]] (Influence) has the results page say. Condition 1 keeps a nullifier with two distinct ballots out of running sets it hasn't entered, and staying keeps one that re-votes after entering at its first ballot, so a re-vote's content appears only in the final tally, never in a release an adversary can time or pad (T-45). Every node and verifier computes the same sets: `S_r` and `h_r` only grow, admission is stable once pending is read as above ([[revoting]], Admission; R4), and receipts and postings, once boarded, stay.

This departs from [[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]]'s running set, under which a re-voter leaves at its second ballot and a ballot leaves once a share of it is posted. Any departure from a running set puts the departed ballot on one side of some combination of releases, or of the final tally against them (Change and the release graph); and departures could be forced by anyone who can post: a seat's own trustee can make a passing posting for any ballot's share to it ([[share-encryption]], Public posting), so under the ADR's rule one trustee, below threshold, could remove released ballots at will and, with the final tally, isolate a voter who never re-voted. Entry still needs receipts, as in the ADR, never a posting: a posting's public check proves only that `m_i` opens `ct_i`, never that the share inside is valid, so a voter who posted invalid shares for `n − k + 1` seats of a panel would put a ballot in every later running set over which those seats' summed shares fail, and stop the tier's releases for good. The cost: a seat's trustee can keep a ballot out of the running sets by not receipting it (Residuals of live results), and a ballot posted after it entered stays, with its posted share (T-15).

### Change and the release graph

The **change** between two running sets of a cell is the number of nullifiers whose entry differs between them; since running sets only grow, it is the **entries** from the earlier to the later, the nullifiers in the later and not the earlier. `R_0`, the empty set, stands for a release made at opening.

A **combination** of totals `T_1, …, T_m` over sets of ballots is `Σ_i a_i · T_i` for integers `a_i`, per option. It equals `Σ_b w_b · v_b` over the ballots in any of the sets, so whoever sees the totals learns `Σ_{w_b > 0} w_b v_b − Σ_{w_b < 0} |w_b| v_b`: an aggregate of the combination's **positive side** less one of its **negative side**. Each side must be empty or hold at least `B` ballots. A side of fewer is disclosed almost as if alone: if one ballot `z` leaves between two totals while 19 others enter, `T_2 − T_1` at an option is the 19 entrants' count less `z`'s vote, and a `−1` there is `z`'s choice, which happens for an option of share `p` with probability `p(1 − p)^19` (13.5% summed over ten options of 10% each) although 20 ballots changed. And sides can't be checked pairwise only: with `X` and `Y` 20 ballots each and `z` one more, `R_1 = X ∪ {z}`, `R_2 = X ∪ Y` and `R_3 = Y` have every pairwise change, the empty set's included, at least 20, yet `T_1 − T_2 + T_3 = v_z`. Both need ballots to leave a set, which running sets never do, though the final tally can (The final tally against releases). [[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]] (The minimum batch) bounded only pairwise changes and left combinations open; this section settles them.

The **graph** of a sequence of sets `1, …, m` in which every ballot's sets form an unbroken run is a directed multigraph with vertices `0, …, m` (vertex 0 is the empty set) and, for each ballot in any of the sets, one edge from `a − 1` to `e`, where `a` and `e` are the first and the last set holding it. Every edge goes forward; ballots with the same run are parallel edges. For a vertex set `U`, the edges **entering** it are those from outside `U` to `U`, and those **leaving** it those from `U` to outside. The **least positive directed cut** `d` is the least positive number of edges entering any vertex set (the edges leaving `U` are those entering its complement, so this covers both); none if the graph has no edge.

**Theorem.** The least number of ballots on a non-empty side of a combination is `d`.

_Proof._ Write `P(0) = 0` and `P(i) = a_1 + … + a_i`, so that `w_b = P(e) − P(a − 1)` for a ballot with run `a` to `e`. (At least `d`.) Take a combination with a non-empty side; negating every `a_i` swaps the sides, so let it be the positive one, with a ballot of run `a` to `e`, and let `U = {i : P(i) ≥ P(e)}`. That ballot's edge enters `U`, and every edge entering `U`, from `u ∉ U` to `v ∈ U`, has `P(v) ≥ P(e) > P(u)`, so its ballot is on the positive side: the side holds at least the edges entering `U`, a positive number, so at least `d`. (At most `d`.) Take `U` with `d` edges entering it. If `0 ∉ U`, let `a_i = 1_U(i) − 1_U(i − 1)`, so that `P = 1_U` and `w_b` is `+1` for the edges entering `U`, `−1` for those leaving it and `0` otherwise: the positive side has `d` ballots. If `0 ∈ U`, the same with the complement of `U`, whose leaving edges are `U`'s entering ones, gives a negative side of `d`. ∎

So "every non-empty side holds at least `B`" is exactly `d ≥ B`, and the least side is reached with coefficients `0` and `±1` (the theorem bounds how many ballots a side holds, not what an observer can infer about them from it). Mixing cells or tiers gains nothing, counted in ballots: each cell's sets are disjoint from every other's, so a combination's sides are the unions of its sides in each cell, and the least is reached in one cell. One person in two tiers has two unrelated nullifiers but usually the same vote, and tiers release on their own schedules, so a combination across tiers can cancel such people (Residuals of live results).

**Releases.** A cell's chosen releases form such a sequence, and since running sets only grow every edge ends at vertex `m`: an edge from `u` is a ballot that entered after release `u` and by release `u + 1`. No edge enters a vertex set without `m`, and the edges entering one with `m` are those from the vertices outside it; so `d` is the least positive number of entries between two consecutive releases (counting the empty set as release 0). Rule 1 of the release choice (below) makes each of those at least `B`, so it makes every non-empty side of every combination of releases at least `B` (T-16). `d` is computed exactly, in integers, by maximum flow: for an edge from `u` to `v`, the fewest edges entering a set that holds `v` and not `u` is the maximum flow from `u` to `v` with each edge of capacity 1 (L. R. Ford and D. R. Fulkerson, "Maximal flow through a network", Canadian J. Math. 8, 1956), and every set with an entering edge holds such a pair, so `d` is the least maximum flow along an edge. The algorithm of J. Edmonds and R. M. Karp ("Theoretical improvements in algorithmic efficiency for network flow problems", J. ACM 19(2), 1972) computes each flow; the value never depends on how ties are broken. The verifier uses it for the final tally (below); trying every vertex set is a second implementation for small graphs, as the vectors do.

### Release choice

Each tier releases on its own, every cell of it at once, so a tier total derived from its cells (Coarser totals) always refers to one snapshot; tiers are never blended ([[PROJECT_BRIEF]], Design direction). Every ballot carries every question, one flat option vector with blanks as encrypted zeros ([[0026-multi-question-ballots]]), so a cell's entries are the same for every option; a ballot version that lets a ballot omit a question must apply this section per question.

For each tier, with `B` the definition's `live_batch` and `C` the tier's releases chosen so far (initially none), the **decision** at each scheduled snapshot `r ≥ 2` is made once `S_r` is fixed. Let `l` be the latest snapshot in `C` (0 if none). The candidates are `q = r − 1, r − 2, …, l + 1`, in that order, and the decision releases the first `q` such that, in **every cell** `c` of the tier, in this order:

1. **batch:** the change between `R_{q,c}` and `R_{i,c}` is at least `B` for every `i` in `C` and for `i = 0`;
2. **lag:** the entries from `R_{q,c}` to `R_{r,c}` are at least `B`.

`q` is then added to `C`. If no candidate qualifies, the tier releases nothing at `r`. Rule 1 is [[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]]'s rule 1; with running sets that only grow it bounds every combination of releases, not only pairs (Releases, above). Rule 2 is the ADR's lag, counted in entries, which each count a ballot of their own in the final tally unless they later tie (The final tally against releases, below).

The choice is a function of the fixed snapshots, their L1 heads and the definition alone, so every node computes the same one and a verifier recomputes it. A chosen release counts in every later decision whether or not its running openings ever reach the board: the combinations of fewer releases are among those of more, so publishing fewer only raises `d`. A chosen release is never moved to another snapshot or made later over another set, and one whose openings are missing is simply skipped.

### What a node does

A seat's node, for each panel it sits on, at each decision:

1. computes the decision itself from the fixed snapshots (Schedule), never from a coordinator's or the operator's word;
2. takes part only once the **withholding check** passes, which [[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]] (The nodes' completeness check) requires and [[P2-13]] specifies, with a tally-opening version that fixes it. An operator that withholds honest ballots, or a seat's receipts, from a snapshot can leave a target alone in an interval with ballots it knows (A-2 with T-01, T-16), or let a voter's re-vote enter alone beside a first ballot it withheld, which then stays in every later release, where it can be padded and read (T-45); an honest node must refuse such a release. A sound check needs three things the protocol doesn't have yet, so it is specified together with them rather than here: clients forwarding the operator's signed board receipts to the seats, so a node knows which board-path ballots the operator owed; a boarding deadline within each snapshot's period, checked at the snapshot where each nullifier of `R_{q,c}` entered as well as at `q`, since a running set grows at every snapshot, chosen or not; and receipts boarded through the contract path or exchanged among the seats, so a withheld receipt is seen. It must also bound when the node receipted a ballot, not only when the ballot was boarded (intake stays open until `c_I`, so a voter who delivers its shares after a snapshot's period, or an honest client retrying intake, must not stall releases; the ADR's rule counts only ballots receipted at least one anchoring period before the snapshot's cutoff), and never count a ballot delivered only to intake, which a voter can deliver to every seat and never board ([[delivery]], Residuals). **Until that check exists, a live poll runs only with no author panel and under a default panel of which the release pins at least `k` seats as ours** ([[verifier]], Pinned trust roots): the transitional panel before any seat is handed over, or a handover step at which we still hold a threshold. There the operator can already open any ballot ([[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]], Part 3), so withholding gains the operator as a whole nothing it lacks. A handover step that leaves us fewer than `k` seats can't run live polls, even though its kind is still `transitional`. [[verifier]] 2.3 rejects any other live definition (`profile-combination`) unless its profile pins a tally-opening version that fixes the check. Nodes run 2.3 on the stub before the ceremony ([[ceremony]], Stub checks), so the restriction rests on a check every node and verifier runs, not on a registration rule;
3. if it may take part, sums and exchanges its shares of the release's aggregates over `R_{q,c}`, for each cell of the tier, exactly as for the final tally's counted sets (Summed shares, Summed-share check, Combination), and signs the running openings ([[P2-15]] part 1b), only until a finalized L1 block has a timestamp at or after the end of period `σ_r + live_every` plus the anchor's `deadline`, which is when snapshot `r + 1` is fixed at the latest (for the last decision, the snapshot that would follow it); after that it treats the release as skipped;
4. destroys each release's summed shares, the summed-share messages that carried them and anything derived from them that isn't public, once that release's running openings are boarded in a segment whose timely checkpoint it has seen anchored on L1, or at the next decision if the release was skipped. Its per-election keys and stored shares are kept, in use, until Destruction after the final tally, as in a sealed poll.

In a live poll the per-election keys are also exercised online during voting, at every release, which widens the window in which a compromised node can be made to act (T-14).

### The final tally against releases

The final tally is the sealed poll's and is never withheld: the brief requires the result. Its counted set comes from the re-vote rule ([[revoting]], Selection), not from running sets, so it can leave out a released ballot: a released voter's re-vote that counts, or a tie (a released ballot is fully receipted, so never excluded, Running set). But each ballot's sets are still an unbroken run when the final tally is put after the releases as vertex `m + 1`: a released ballot is in every release from its entry, then in the final tally or not; any other counted ballot (an entrant after the last release, a re-vote's new ballot, or a ballot of a nullifier that never entered) is in the final tally alone. So the theorem applies to the releases with the final tally, and its `d` is exactly the least non-empty side of any combination of them. A lone released voter who re-votes or ties gives `d = 1`: the final tally less the last release has the entrants and the new ballot on one side and that voter's released ballot alone on the other. The lag puts at least `B` entrants in the running sets after each release, and those the final tally still counts (all but those who later tie) bound the positive sides; nothing bounds how few released ballots go uncounted, and the result can't be withheld or delayed for it. The verifier computes `d` per cell over the chosen releases and the final tally and flags a cell where it is below `B`, with the ballots on the least side (`live-final-batch`, [[P2-15]] part 1c), and the result stands (Residuals of live results).

### Residuals of live results

- **The final tally re-opens released ballots (T-16, T-45).** Each released voter who re-votes or ties puts their released ballot on one side of the final tally against the releases (The final tally against releases). If they are few in a cell, a released ballot is disclosed almost as if alone: with one such voter, and the lag's `B` entrants plus the new ballot on the other side, a count of `−1` at an option of share `p` shows their released choice with probability about `p(1 − p)^{B+1}`, about 11% summed over ten options of 10% at `B = 20`, to any observer, with no adversary needed. Only the voter can cause it (a seat's trustee can post a share but can't make a valid ballot uncounted; a lying seat can, with a cheating voter, only for that voter's ballot), the verifier flags it, and nothing prevents it, since the result is never withheld. This is a cost of live results with re-voting that a sealed poll doesn't have; the creation flow and the notice before answering a live poll must say that a vote changed after a release may become readable from the results.
- **A side of `B` still discloses (T-16, T-45).** `MIN_LIVE_BATCH` was set by the chance that a batch of `B` is unanimous, `p^B` ([[parameters]]), but a count of zero discloses too: that no one in the batch chose that option, with probability `(1 − p)^B`, which for small `p` is large: about 12% for an option of share 0.1 at `B = 20`, 0.5% at the default of 50. A coercer who knows a voter's watched released ballot and sees them re-vote reads the final tally less the last release, and a zero there for the option it demanded, after its own count, shows that the voter didn't choose it again. Re-voting in a live poll is therefore weaker against observed coercion than in a sealed one. Not mitigated beyond `B`; a larger floor in a later definition version would narrow it.
- **Padding (T-16, T-01, T-45).** Sides count ballots, not people, and not what an adversary already knows: one who can cast `B − 1` ballots whose votes it knows into a release interval in which a target is the only other new voter learns the target's vote. At Tier 0 that costs `B − 1` cheap identities per target and interval, and works best in quiet polls and hours. Tiers 1 and 2 raise the cost and condition 1 keeps re-votes out of reach. One person in two tiers has two nullifiers, so combinations across tiers count people twice. Not mitigated.
- **Operator withholding (T-23, T-16, T-01, T-45).** Until [[P2-13]]'s withholding check exists (What a node does), the operator can withhold honest ballots, or one seat's receipts, from any snapshot: to leave a target alone in an interval with `B − 1` ballots it knows, however busy the poll, or to let a voter's re-vote enter alone beside a withheld first ballot and then pad and read it. A withheld ballot still fails the election if it is missing from the counted extent ([[verifier]] 5.6 for direct submissions, the voter's receipt otherwise), but running totals have already been released. Live polls are therefore limited to default panels on which we hold at least `k` seats, with no author panel (What a node does). Against the operator as a whole this adds nothing, since it can open any ballot there anyway ([[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]], Part 3). It does add against a single compromised component, though. The panel's seats are spread so that no one machine, provider or keyholder holds `k` (Part 3, rule 3), but withholding needs only the board or API path. So one compromised board host, holding no seat, can make an interval quiet and pad it to read a target, which opening the ballot would have needed `k` keyholders for. It can also hold voters out of every running set without withholding anything, by boarding a junk version-5 ballot for each of them that names a step that can't be final at any snapshot. No proof can be checked against such a step before the close, so the junk ballot counts as pending until the final tally rejects it. [[P2-13]]'s check closes this too. Until then an author who adds trustees to protect a poll from us can't also choose live results, and no organisational vote can be live, since those need an independent panel.
- **Withheld receipts by a trustee.** Once author panels and independent panels can run live polls, one seat's trustee (A-3, below threshold) that never receipts a ballot holds it out of the running sets, which only that seat can see. A ballot whose delivery is honestly incomplete looks the same, so stalling on any incomplete delivery would let one voter stall every release. A trustee that refuses intake is visible to the voter, whose client then posts, but its holding a ballot out of running totals is not mitigated (T-16, T-23, T-14).
- **Posted shares in running sets (T-15).** A ballot whose share is posted after it entered stays in the running sets, so each later release is one more opening over a set holding that posted share: a trustee that knows a release's summed polynomials and later breaks the posted shares' classical encryption gains what [[tally]] (Residuals: a trustee learns the summed polynomials) describes for the final tally, once per release. Honest clients never post for a seat that receipted, so only a deviating voter or the seat's own trustee makes such a posting. Removing the ballot instead, as [[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]] did, would let one trustee remove ballots by posting (Running set), which is worse.
- **A quiet cell stalls its tier.** A release needs `B` entries in every cell of its tier, so a regional cell with few voters, or none, keeps the whole tier from ever showing running totals; the creation flow must say so for a regional live poll, and a creator who wants live results draws coarser regions ([[0019-regional-cells]]).
- **Relay delay.** An operator relay can hold a ballot's intake back past a release so no seat receipted it, which the voter sees as a slow cast; delivery straight to the seats' intake removes it ([[P2-13]]; T-11).
- **Influence** is a property of live results, not a flaw to fix ([[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]], Influence).

## Residuals

- **`k` colluding trustees can still open any single ballot** from the shares they hold (T-14). The tally doesn't change that; destruction after the tally (Destruction) limits how long they can, if they destroy.
- **Destruction can't be proven** (T-14, T-40, T-69). A seat that keeps its material and boards a statement looks exactly like one that destroyed it, so `k` such seats, or anyone who seizes or compels them, can still open every ballot whose shares they hold, and summed shares they kept still give the corrupt-trustee leak below. The statement turns the duty into a signed public claim: a seat later found with material it said it destroyed has made a false attributable statement, which is all a protocol can add. Shares posted on the board stay there, and recorded share ciphertexts stay harvestable, whatever the trustees destroy ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Consequences).
- **The operator can withhold a destruction statement.** Statements reach the board only through its operator, so a seat it refuses is reported `destruction-missing`, and nothing tells the seat's silence from the operator's refusal. No contract path is required, since nothing the result rests on depends on a statement; the seat can publish its signed statement elsewhere, where anyone can check it against its registered key.
- **A trustee that has destroyed its material can't help a late tally.** After `t_D`, openings still count until the seal, and trustees resend the openings they had already signed, but a cell no trustee had combined by then stays unopened: only trustees that kept their material could still combine it, and by the destruction rule none should have (T-54).
- **A trustee learns the summed polynomials at every seat.** Any trustee that receives `k` passing summed shares can evaluate `F_{c,j}` and `G_{c,j}` anywhere. Wherever the posted shares fall into the pattern of [[0015-tally-publication]] (Context), whether a relay shaped it (A-2) or trustees were simply offline at different times, one corrupt trustee with a later break of the posted shares' classical encryption (A-11) can complete a ballot with fewer than `k` posted shares. It can also hand that power to every future adversary by disclosing the summed shares, which can't be attributed to it. And a trustee seized before destruction (A-1) yields the same. Not mitigated. Re-randomising the summed polynomials ([[0015-tally-publication]], option B) would close it only against coalitions of fewer than `k − 1` trustees: `k − 1` trustees know `k − 1` points of `F` plus the published `F(0)`, so under every option they know `F` at every seat (with `k = 2`, one trustee does) (T-14, T-15).
- **Ballots readable after a break shrink a cell's anonymity set.** A future adversary reads every counted ballot with `k` or more valid posted shares ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]]), and the opening then gives the sum over the rest. A cell of 1,000 where 999 ballots had `k` posted shares reveals the last. [[verifier]] 8.2 reports and flags, per cell, the number of counted ballots with postings that pass the public check at fewer than `k` distinct seats; a passing posting is counted as readable, since its share's content can't be checked publicly (T-15, T-16).
- **Wrong summed shares aren't publicly attributable.** The trustees see which seat sent a failing sum, but nothing on the board records it; the published opening is still checked by everyone, so the result can't be wrong undetected (T-29). A trustee that sends nothing, or nothing valid, only delays the tally (T-54).
- **Summed shares in transit are harvestable** like ballot shares: a recorded summed-share message falls to an adversary who later breaks both ML-KEM-768 and discrete logs in ristretto255 ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], T-15). A recipient's ML-KEM arithmetic isn't constant time either ([[share-encryption]], Residuals). Only registered seats can make it decapsulate a summed-share message, but the same `dk_j` serves share intake, which anyone can reach with a chosen `ct_M`: a `dk_j` recovered by timing there also strips the ML-KEM half from every recorded summed-share message to `j`, leaving it to a later discrete-log break alone. Intake's rate limits and the mainnet ML-KEM timing audit protect both uses. This message is a second composition of the self-audited `@noble/post-quantum` hybrid (the same key under its own tags, decapsulation gated by a signature, no proof of knowledge of the ephemeral), so the mainnet audit [[0016-share-kem-dh-group]] requires covers it and its argument above (Summed-share message, Notes) as well as share encryption.
- **The result rests on binding, which is computational.** Someone who can compute discrete logs in ristretto255 could publish an opening of another count, for an election verified after the break; with an honest opening also on the board, `tally-conflict` shows it, and without one the forged count stands. The record anchored on L1 before the break is what historical verification rests on ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Context; [[ballot-validity]], Residuals).
- **The operator can withhold every opening.** Openings reach the board only through its operator: no contract path lets a trustee board one directly yet. An operator that refuses every opening until the seal makes each cell fail `tally-missing` permanently, and nothing on the board attributes it; it can also board junk so the verdict reads `tally-opening`, though junk can't be attributed to a trustee, and the report lists every failing opening with its attribution, so a cell whose only full-length openings are unattributed failures shows that. A contract path for tally openings, emitted as events under the event accumulator ([[contracts]]), MUST exist before any election registry is deployed (P1-18; T-54, T-53). It accepts only openings signed by a registered seat's per-election key (P-256 verification on L2) naming this definition and a partition cell, one per seat and cell; a contract can't check an opening against the aggregate. An opening it accepted that the boarding rule above also admits and that is missing from the board then fails attributably to the operator, as [[verifier]] 5.6 does for direct-submitted ballots. What remains is a registered seat's own failing opening through the contract, up to about 4 KB per seat and cell, attributed to that seat.
- **Liveness needs `k` trustees.** A cell with no opening on the board fails the election (`tally-missing`, T-54); a trustee that withholds its sums is visible to the other trustees but not compellable.
- **Small cells reveal votes.** An opening over one ballot, or over a cell where every ballot chose the same option, reveals those votes; [[verifier]] 8.2 flags small counts, and nothing here prevents them (T-16).

`docs/spec/vectors/live-release.json`: release schedules (an opening inside a period, an interval of three periods, a close on a period boundary and a poll shorter than its interval); running sets over four snapshots with one nullifier per rule (receipted at once, receipted late, never delivered, a first ballot pending while its re-vote is admitted, which holds the nullifier out, a first ballot rejected later for its L1 reference, which still holds it out for good, and a first ballot whose membership proof fails, beside which the re-vote enters, delivered by a valid posting and by an invalid one, neither of which enters, a re-vote after entering, which stays, a re-vote pending on its L1 reference, a tie before entering and one after, another cell, a re-vote into another cell, an unreceipted first ballot followed by a re-vote, and a ballot admitted after its receipts), with each cell's final counted ballots; graphs of sets with arbitrary runs, as the ADR's departing running sets would give, with every pairwise change, the least positive directed cut, a vertex set and side reaching it and the combination whose side is exactly those ballots (three sets isolating one ballot behind pairwise changes of 20, one ballot leaving as 19 enter, entries only, a combination that is identically zero, a single set); and tiers' release choices at `B = MIN_LIVE_BATCH` with every decision, the rule refusing each candidate, the final tally's counted ballots and its graph's least positive directed cut against the releases with the ballots on the least side (steady entries, released voters re-voting and tying, a lone released voter re-voting after the last release, a small cell delaying its tier, the latest qualifying candidate, and an empty cell). They are printed by `packages/crypto/scripts/live-release-vectors.py`, written from this page with Python's standard library, which computes each directed cut both as the least maximum flow along an edge (Edmonds–Karp) and by trying every vertex set, stops if they differ, checks the theorem itself on every graph of up to five vertices by trying every combination with coefficients from −2 to 2, and checks that every set of chosen releases has a cut of at least `B`.

`docs/spec/vectors/destruction.json`: destruction statements re-made byte for byte with their signed messages; decode rejections (truncation, a trailing byte, another record type, version 2, a high `s`, `r = 0` and `r = n`); the verifier's report over a board's statements (every seat on time, early, missing, an unopened cell, a late tally, unattributed, stray, duplicate and rejected statements, an empty board); and board entries under versions 1, 3 and 4, of which only version 4 carries a statement and none a posting, a summed-share message, a voter's receipt or a definition. They are printed by `packages/crypto/scripts/destruction-vectors.py`, which uses `trustee-vectors.py`'s P-256 ECDSA and shares nothing with `packages/crypto`.
