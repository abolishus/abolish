# P2-17: Rolling electorates

- State: in progress (part 1, definition version 7 with rolling tiers, the rolling tier tree, the root clock and the rolling scope in the spec with vectors, merged in #207; part 2, the ballot version with its step, admission and reporting, next)
- Branch: `claude/p2-17`
- Phase: Phase 2: Open polls end to end at Tier 0

## Scope

Rolling electorates for everyday polls, as the owner decided on 2026-10-12 and [[0028-rolling-electorates]] designs (option B): people who register after a poll opens can answer it. A rolling tier's tree holds its listed groups' additions in chain order, and its roots are taken at the boundaries of a protocol-wide root clock (`ROOT_PERIOD`, proposed 600 s). Each ballot names its step: the client uses the newest settled step, and the verifier accepts only steps within `ROOT_WINDOW` (proposed 2 h) before the ballot's L1 reference. The nullifier scope is `H(DS("abolish/v1/rolling-nullifier-scope", definition_hash ‖ u8(t)))`, so one secret has one nullifier per tier and poll whatever root it proves against. A rolling tier is one cell. Results report the member count at opening, at close, at the smallest step any counted ballot used and at each live release. Direct submission supplies the root it proved against, and the verifier checks it. Civic-record polls are never rolling (`rolling-class`), which the verifier, the election registry and the ballot client all check. Fixed polls are unchanged.

## Known-weak (part 1)

- No production codec, rule or tree reads version 7 yet (part 3); until then its labels are checked only by the generator's rules and the TypeScript test's reading of the spec.
- No version-7 election can be registered until part 2 specifies the ballot version with its step (2.3).
- The board carrying a rolling poll's group events through close, the admission codes and the reporting are part 2's; the verifier's replay through close is part 4's.
- From the ADR: Tier 0 Sybils made during a poll; gas-only spam control on rolling direct submission; a non-conforming client's step leaks join time within `ROOT_WINDOW`; a first ballot close in time to its registration batch; no regional everyday results; the first answer waits for a settled step.
- A batcher that posts nothing for over about 90 minutes, or a sequencer stall, leaves no settled step inside the window: a liveness residual of the sequencer's power over posting (T-50).
- A removal doesn't stop a removed member's ballot in a poll already open: nothing ties a ballot's L1 reference to when it is boarded, so a back-dated reference can name a step before the removal, as in a fixed poll. Removal and revocation protect later polls only; T-18 records the constraint any replacement design must keep.

## Independent review (part 1)

In-session subagent with `.github/review/crypto-review.md`, on the complete diff; it reran the generator (identical output), confirmed the generator's independence and rule order, and found no prompt injection and no way to cast two counted ballots per secret or to steer a step root.

- **Blocking, fixed:** the spec (and ADR 0028) claimed removals take effect after `ROOT_WINDOW`; a removed member can still back-date its L1 reference until close. The claim is replaced by the true property ([[membership]], Root clock and steps), the window's rationale no longer cites it, and the ADR is corrected.
- **Fixed:** the lower bound on accepted steps used `root_l2_block`'s timestamp, which no archived event carries; it is now the definition's registration, whose `DefinitionRegistered` event the accumulator binds (vectors updated). "Settled" now means in the chain derived from L1, not merely posted. T-18's constraint gains removal per occurrence and whole-tier re-registration. Nits: the `electorate` rule's scope, versioning's status column, the `ROOT_PERIOD` rationale, sequencer stalls in the `ROOT_WINDOW` rationale, the ADR's text aligned with the spec, and the 2³² bound in the TypeScript test's acceptance check.
