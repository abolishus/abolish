# T-49: Domain or hosting seizure

- Area: Availability and censorship resistance (G-10)
- Adversary: A-7, A-1
- Status: **Planned.** Residual: losing the GitHub organisation stops development and releases, not elections in progress. Seizing our hosting or the board's anchoring key for longer than the anchoring deadline while an election runs fails it permanently ([[board]], Timing). A mirror plan belongs in a runbook.

## Threat

abolish.us (`.us` is government-administered), Railway, Cloudflare or GitHub take us down.

## Mitigation

The ballot client lives on IPFS behind `abolishus.eth` and a second non-US-TLD domain (P4-3, P4-4); the verifier needs only chain + IPFS (P4-6); `apps/node` lets anyone serve and verify (P4-5).

Index: [[THREAT_MODEL]].

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P1-16): board anchors count only before their deadline ([[board]], Timing), so seizing our hosting or anchoring key for longer than the deadline while an election runs fails it permanently. P4-1 must set the deadline and run anchoring to survive one outage.
- 2026-10-12 (P4-1 part 1): the anchoring deadline is 12 hours, justified against outages and censorship in [[contracts]], Board anchor; each anchor call can carry the archive locator of [[0005-permanent-archive]], so the archive is found from L1 once our domain and hosting are gone.
- 2026-10-12 (P4-4 part 1, [[0020-entry-points]]): the second domain is a country-code TLD outside US jurisdiction (`.ch` recommended, `.is` the fallback), with registry lock, DNSSEC and nameservers from two providers; `abolishus.eth` can't be taken through a registry or registrar, only through the release Safe's keys or expiry, and anyone can renew it. Seizing the second domain takes one entry point and orphans every recovery backup (its name is the RP ID); voters at the ENS or `apps/node` entry points can still vote only with an identity held at that origin until a voter-initiated transfer between origins exists.
- 2026-10-13 (P4-3 part 2b-1): each anchored checkpoint names its segment's CIDs on L1, so with our domain and hosting gone the board's objects are found by CID from L1 and IPFS alone, and checked against the anchored roots; the verifier's fetch by CID is part 2b-2.
- 2026-10-17 (P1-12f part 3a, [[content-addressing]], Reading by CID): `packages/crypto` reads an object of known length from any sources' blocks by its CID, each block checked against its CID, so a leaf object is read with our servers gone from whoever holds its blocks; the network fetch and objects without a signed length (segment objects) come with the verifier's fetch ([[P4-3]] part 2b-2).
- 2026-10-17 (P4-1 part 2a): the anchoring run retries a failed call on every run while the checkpoint's window is open and reports it as overdue past half the deadline and missed after it, so an outage shorter than the deadline costs no anchor if the worker runs again within it. Alerting on those lines, the second runner and the Safe are part 2b; the seal's checkpoint isn't taken yet.
- 2026-10-17 (P4-1 part 2b-1): the anchoring run's chain adapter refuses to start against an anchor whose finalized clock or authority isn't the configured one, simulates each call before sending, and counts a call as made only once the anchor holds its checkpoints, so a call that reached the Safe but not the anchor is retried while its window is open rather than reported as anchored. The Safe's sender, alerting and the second runner are part 2b-2.
- 2026-10-17 (P4-3 part 2b-2a, [[content-addressing]], Reading within a bound; [[board]], A copy from L1's CIDs): `packages/crypto` reads a segment object by its CID within the bound its checkpoints give, and the whole board from the CIDs on L1, over sources that may wait on the network, so a node with our domain and hosting gone rebuilds the board from L1 and whoever holds its blocks. The verifier's sources over the network come next (part 2b-2b).
