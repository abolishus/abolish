# T-22: Tier 1 social graph exposure

- Area: Ballot secrecy, unlinkability and privacy (G-3, G-4, G-5, G-12)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1, A-10
- Status: **Planned** ([[0014-tier1-vouching]], P3-3). Residuals: anyone holding a voucher's secret (a seized, compelled or compromised device or recovery backup: T-69, T-18, T-20) can recompute every vouch that voucher made; a vouchee knows their vouchers; the timing of vouches around an addition; and the vouchee's IP address seen by whoever relays the addition (T-11).

## Threat

The vouching graph is sensitive personal data, and publishing or storing it links people to each other and to their Tier 1 membership.

## Mitigation

Store only what Tier 1 strictly needs; the vouching design states what is public and what the server learns (P3-3).

## History

- 2026-10-11 (P3-3): [[0014-tier1-vouching]]: vouches are Semaphore proofs, so nothing on the board or on any server records who vouched for whom. A voucher's vouches have nullifiers unrelated to each other, to their votes and to their Tier 2 commitment, except for someone holding the voucher's secret, who can recompute them all. Vouching runs device to device with no account, and the vouchee submits the addition. A public signed web of trust (it publishes the graph) and an operator-held graph were rejected. The graph's privacy is computational (Poseidon preimages, T-15). Residuals: the voucher's secret (T-69, T-18, T-20), the vouchee's own knowledge, timing, and the submitter's IP address (T-11).
- 2026-10-12 (P3-3 part 2a, #144): the vouch record ([[vouching]]) carries a Tier 2 group, a root block, a slot number and two nullifiers per vouch, and nothing naming a voucher. The slot number is public; the client picks it at random so it doesn't count a voucher's vouches.
- 2026-10-16 (P3-3 part 2b-1, [[vouching]], The root a vouch names): a vouch's public root block is a multiple of 1,800 L2 blocks (an hour, as coarse as the anchoring periods the ADR first chose), so it shows when its voucher synced Tier 2 only to within an hour; a voucher's client checks whether a held slot was consumed by fetching every consumed slot nullifier, never by asking about its own, and never reuses a slot whose nullifier appeared in any logged addition, since a reused slot would show one `ν_S` beside two vouchees.
