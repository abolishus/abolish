# T-74: Tier 0 token issuer misuse

- Area: Eligibility and Sybil resistance (G-1, G-2)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1, A-6
- Status: **Planned.** Decided in [[0039-tier0-token-issuance]] and specified in [[tier0-token]]; built by [[P2-30]] parts 2 to 5. Residual: a stolen key mints Tier 0 identities until its key stops redeeming, detected only in aggregate.

## Threat

Each account takes one blind-issued token, for the Tier 0 group of its region ([[0024-tier0-registration]], Owner decision; [[0039-tier0-token-issuance]]), so:

- whoever holds an issuer key (a thief, a malicious deploy, A-6, A-2) computes tokens for any commitment and any group with no account, and stuffs Tier 0 groups (T-01, T-06);
- an issuer that answers one account under a key of its own, or under another group's tweak, marks that account's token, and through its redemption joins the account to its commitment (T-10);
- the issuer that sees the account and the signing service that sees the commitment are the same operator, so the times of issuance and redemption can join them (T-11, T-13).

## Mitigation

- **Blind and verifiable.** RFC 9497's POPRF mode: the issuer sees a blinded element, uniformly distributed whatever the input, and proves its evaluation against the period's key tweaked by the group's public `info`. The client checks the proof and refuses any other key.
- **One key for everyone.** The client takes each period's public key only from the signed release's deployment record, so a per-account key fails the proof check, and a period's key never changes across releases, nor is replaced if retired early, which the verifier's report checks against the L1 release registry, so a release can't split its clients.
- **Bound tokens.** A token's input holds its key's identifier and the commitment it registers, so a token seen in transit registers nothing else, and the issuer's `info` stops a token for one group admitting to another (T-16).
- **Bounded keys.** One key per 30-day period, issuing in that period and redeeming in it and the next, then destroyed, held apart from every other secret (T-65).
- **A compromise shows in counts.** The service can count, per key and group, the tokens issued and the commitments newly queued by redemption without linking either; more redemptions than issuances is a compromise, and the key is retired early. The operator keeps these counts and holds `skS` itself, so they catch a thief, not the operator: an operator minting identities is T-06's group stuffing, which it could already do with the registrars' keys.
- **One token per account.** An account takes one token, for the group of its region, recorded by an atomic insert-if-absent, so neither several regions nor concurrent requests give it a second identity (T-01).
- **Timing.** The client redeems at a uniformly random instant of the day after it got its token; neither the issuer's record nor the queue keeps a time. The join time is public on L2, so a new commitment's anonymity set is the region's accounts that took a token in the day before.

Residuals:

- With one token per account, a self-paid copy of a waiting commitment, which [[registration]]'s batch drops, would shut the account out for one self-paid fee. [[tier0-token]] (Redemption) seals the redemption to the signing service, so only the operator, which can refuse anyway (T-69), sees the commitment before its batch; part 5 builds the sealing before tokens replace Turnstile.
- A stolen key mints identities until it stops redeeming, and the counts catch only a thief who outspends honest redemptions' shortfall; the registrations already queued or signed stay.
- A token can be sold: an account holder can blind a buyer's commitment. A token costs only what a verified account costs ([[P2-31]]).
- POPRF's security rests on the 3HashSDHI assumption, stronger than VOPRF mode's.
- The issuer chooses when each account gets its answer, so it can delay every other account of a quiet region for a day and answer only a target, whose commitment is then the region's next new one (an active form of the timing residual, T-13). A client-side guard against it is a precondition before tokens replace Turnstile ([[0039-tier0-token-issuance]], Consequences), which part 5 designs.
- A key retired early, or past its redemption window while a voter was away, would strand the tokens issued under it and not yet redeemed; [[tier0-token]] (Keys) lets such an account show its dead token and unregistered commitment, which it then abandons, to take one token under the current key.
- An account that re-issues after its key's natural expiry shows the operator that it registered nothing under that key, which slightly narrows the timing anonymity set of the region's other commitments of that window.
- The issuer's record goes with its account, so account deletion ([[P2-27]]) must keep a deleted account's phone from taking a second token through a new account, or one phone buys an identity per deletion.
- Network-level timing between an account's issuance and its redemption, seen by our proxy and platform, remains T-11's, and in a quiet region the day's anonymity set is small.

## History

- 2026-10-22 (P2-30 part 1): entry created with [[0039-tier0-token-issuance]] and [[tier0-token]].
- 2026-10-22 (P2-30 part 2): `packages/crypto` implements the token steps (`src/tier0-token.ts`): the client's proof check against the published key and the group's `info`, the service's constant-time comparison at redemption, and re-issuance's check that a shown `blind` and `C` give the record's `B`. No change to the residuals above.
- 2026-10-22 (P2-30 part 3a): `packages/core` holds the issuer (`src/tier0-tokens.ts`, migration 0026): the period keys, parsed from the issuer's environment; the account's record, one per account and deployment by an atomic insert-if-absent, holding the region, the key and `B` and no time; retries of the recorded request only; the dead mark at the end of `q + 1`, keyed on the stored period and the clock; and the tokens issued per key and group `info`, with no account, for the compromise count. Issuance answers only an account verified by e-mail until [[P2-31]] part 3 adds the phone, and no route calls it yet (part 3b). New residual: the record is deleted with its account, so once accounts can be deleted ([[P2-27]]), deleting one and verifying a new account with the same phone would take a second token, unless the phone's claim outlives the account.
- 2026-10-22 (P2-30 part 3b): `apps/web` serves issuance at `POST /token` behind the account session (`src/node/token.ts`), with the issuer keys in its own environment (`TIER0_ISSUER_KEYS`) and none of them, nor `B` or the answer, in any log or error. Issuance now answers only an account verified by e-mail and phone, checked under row locks in the transaction that writes the record, so a verification can't leave mid-request; a key whose redemption window has ended is refused at start; the account prune's timer runs the dead mark. New residual: a number moves to another account 90 days after it was verified ([[0038-verified-accounts]]), and the account it left keeps its token, so one phone yields a token per 90 days (as account deletion does once its 90-day hold ends).
- 2026-10-22 (P2-30 part 3c-1): [[deployment]]'s version 2 lists the issuer's period keys (`tier0_token_keys`, each with its period and a `retired` mark), well formed only with one distinct valid key per consecutive period, and Across releases states the rules a verifier's report applies between releases (a period's key never changes, a key never moves to another period, a retirement never lifts), with vectors; a release lists the period before, the current one and at least six ahead. The client's use of the keys, `apps/web`'s check of its keys against the record and the report itself are later parts, so the "one key for everyone" mitigation isn't enforced yet.
- 2026-10-22 (P2-30 part 3c-2b): `apps/web` doesn't start with a held issuer key that the deployment record its release names (`TIER0_DEPLOYMENT_RECORD`) doesn't list for that key's period, lists under another `pkS` or lists retired, nor with a record that isn't a well-formed version 2 of its own L2, group registry and region 0's signed Tier 0 group (`checkTier0IssuerDeployment` in `packages/core`). So an operator running with its release's record can't issue under a key no client finalises with by mistake, and a key retired early in a release stops issuing at the next restart. Residual: the operator supplies the record, so an insider can pass any well-formed one; an issuer already running keeps a key retired by a newer release until it restarts; and nothing here checks that it is the one a published release names (its hash in the release record, [[P4-4]] part 2c), nor that every key ahead is listed before its period (the verifier's report, next).
- 2026-10-22 (P2-30 part 3c-2c-1): [[verifier]] (Token keys across releases) specifies the keys run: from the pinned release registry's L1 state and records authenticated by hash, it judges every pair of published releases' deployment records, revoked or not, by [[deployment]]'s Across releases, and fails a period whose key was first published at or after the period began (`token-key-late`). It reads one registry, and isn't built yet (part 3c-2c-2, after the release record's codec).
