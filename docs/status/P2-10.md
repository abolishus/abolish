## Scope

Moderation policy (written) and moderation tooling for poll content (never results).

## Plan

The item is built in parts, each its own PR on this branch's name:

1. **Policy and log** (done, #66): [[moderation-policy]], and the append-only moderation log with its state machine in `packages/core`.
2. **Reports and the public log over the API** (this part): reports that store the poll and a reason code only (never the reporter), counted per poll and ground; `apps/api` serving the public log and each poll's state; a per-address rate limit on reports at P2-11's proxy (T-52).
3. **Appeals and the moderators' tooling**, split in two:
   - **3a, the moderators' tooling** (done, #123): the queue, reviews and decisions as the operator's commands in a new `apps/admin-cli`, since the operator is the only moderator until the owner names others ([[moderation-policy]], Moderators).
   - **3b, appeals:** the creator's appeal from their account, on the creator record of [[0032-poll-creation-operations]] (rule 15, built in [[P2-22]] part 2). With that record, the tooling also refuses a decision by a moderator on their own poll. In two PRs:
     - **3b-1** (this part): moderators' handles tied to accounts, the own-poll refusal, and appeals in `packages/core` with the operator's commands.
     - **3b-2:** filing an appeal and seeing its outcome from the website: a route beside `/drafts` on `apps/web`'s origin (the API refuses every account credential, [[T-10]]) and the account page's poll view.
4. **Front ends**, split in two:
   - **4a, the API's listing** (done, #125): `GET /elections` lists polls for discovery, leaving out delisted and hidden ones, so P2-9's discovery page has something to read.
   - **4b, the website's list** (this part): `/polls` lists polls from 4a's listing, so delisted and hidden polls are never listed; a hidden poll's page already shows the notice (P2-9 part 1). Its result by option number comes with P2-9's results (part 4); search, with P2-9's discovery.
   - **4c:** the ballot client's behaviour for hidden text (P2-8, once it fetches an election).
5. **Owner decision (2026-10-12): a `threat` hide must not stop voting.** The ballot client keeps showing an open poll hidden for `threat`, using its committed display text from IPFS (checked against the definition), while our web pages withhold it. Hides for `illegal` and `personal-data` keep stopping our client from showing the text. Update [[moderation-policy]] to match; required before Phase 2 goes public.

## What was built (part 1)

- [[moderation-policy]]: what moderation may touch (display text and listing, never the board, a definition, a tally or an election's timing); the actions `delist`, `hide`, `restore`; the closed list of reason codes and the grounds that never count (viewpoint, controversy, criticism, report volume, a poll's result or turnout); hiding an open poll only for illegal content, personal data or a threat; the public log; reports; appeals by a different moderator; moderators; and the legal questions, which are the owner's.
- `packages/core`:
  - migration `0002_moderation.sql`: `moderation_actions`, append-only for every role (grants and triggers), with checks that tie each reason code to its action, bound the moderator handle and the election ID, and leave no free-text column. P2-5's `0002_accounts.sql` (#65) is in review with the same number: whichever merges second renumbers to the next free number above everything merged, since `migrate` refuses a number below an applied one.
  - `src/moderation.ts`: `moderate`, `moderationStatus`, `readModerationLog`, `OPEN_POLL_HIDE_REASONS`. One advisory lock serialises decisions, so sequence numbers commit in order (a reader paging by sequence number never misses one) and every decision is judged against the state it changes. A decision's time is the database clock, read after the lock, never the caller's, so no caller can date a hide past a poll's close. A removal may be re-cited under a reason not yet cited since the state began; anything else that changes nothing is refused.
  - `TABLE_CLASSES.operational` gains `moderation_actions`.
- Threat files T-68 and T-17. The index row for T-68 stays `Planned` (AGENTS.md lets a PR edit the index only to add a threat), and so does the file's status line, to match it.

## Known-weak (part 1)

- The log lives in Postgres: a wipe loses every decision (polls go back to visible), and an insider can withhold rows from what we serve. Neither changes a result. Anchoring the log is not planned yet.
- The moderator handle is free to choose within its pattern; mapping handles to people is the operator's, and the policy's rule that a moderator doesn't decide on their own poll is not enforced.
- The `open-poll` check compares the database clock with `closes_at`, which the chain compares with L2 `block.timestamp`; the margin is the inclusion bound.
- Only `moderate` enforces the state machine, the open-poll rule and the in-order sequence. The app role can insert rows directly, and the owner can drop the triggers with DDL: the log binds the tooling, not an insider (T-68).
- Hiding an open poll for `illegal`, `personal-data` or `threat` stops voting through our client and so can lower turnout: listed under Decisions to review.
- Nothing serves the log or acts on the state yet (parts 2 and 3).

## Known-weak (part 4a)

- The listing is only by election ID: no search, no ordering by opening or closing time, no filter for open polls. A client reads each definition to show or sort polls. Search and sorting come with P2-9's discovery page (4b), which may need an index of definition fields.
- A poll stored or restored below a client's cursor while it pages isn't seen until its next pass from the start.
- Like the log, the listing is served from our database: an insider can leave a poll out, and a wipe loses the log, so delisted and hidden polls are listed again once definitions are rebuilt (T-68, part 1). The registry stays the source of truth for which polls exist.
- A page's cost grows with the run of delisted or hidden polls it skips: Postgres walks definitions in order, probing each one's newest decision, until it has `limit` listed polls. Each probe is one index lookup and the scan is bounded by the table, so it's cheap at today's scale; a stored current state per poll is the fix if removals grow large (T-52).
