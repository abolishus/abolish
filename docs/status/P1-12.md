# P1-12: Ballot commitments and validity proofs

- State: in progress (part 1 done in #53; part 2: share encryption, in review; part 3 to follow)
- Branch: `claude/p1-12`
- Phase: Phase 1: Threat model, spec, crypto core, circuits, verifier, reference election (CLI only)

## Scope

Ballot commitments and validity proofs per [[0002-everlasting-privacy]] and the tally ADR: Pedersen and VSS commitments, disjunctive Chaum–Pedersen (CDS) proofs, hybrid-KEM share encryption, Fiat–Shamir with full statement binding.

## Plan

The item is built in parts, each its own PR on this branch's name:

1. **Commitments, VSS and validity proofs** (done, #53): [[ballot-validity]]. The committed vote, the trustees' share check, the statement hash and the option and sum proofs.
2. **Share encryption** (this part): the hybrid KEM and AEAD that carry each trustee's shares (`abolish/v1/kem/share`), `H(ct_i)`, per-election trustee KEM keys with P1-14 part 2, and the public check of a posted ciphertext. The owner's answer in [[0002-everlasting-privacy]] (Decision) names X25519 with ML-KEM-768, while the ADR's option B puts the Diffie–Hellman half in the commitment group (ristretto255) so that a trustee's complaint can prove a DH value with a Chaum–Pedersen proof. Part 2 must reconcile the two: either ristretto255 DH (which the complaint proofs need, and which is the same curve as X25519 in another encoding) with an ADR recording the reading, or X25519 with a complaint proof that works over it.
3. **The ballot record** (`0x0002`): the committed vote with the tier, nullifier, L1 reference and membership proof, and the ballot hash (`abolish/v1/ballot`), with P1-13 and P1-17b. Also the board records for receipts, postings and complaints, their signatures, and a posting limit that keeps complaint queries below 2³² per trustee key ([[share-encryption]], Complaints).

## What was built (part 1)

- [[ballot-validity]], a new spec page:
  - **Committed vote.** For each option, `k` Pedersen VSS commitments to the sharing polynomials of `(v_j, r_j)`, the first being `C_j`. It also carries the `n` share-ciphertext hashes, one option proof per option, and the sum proof with one branch per allowed value.
  - **Shares.** Each trustee checks its shares against the VSS commitments (Pedersen 1991).
  - **Statement hash** (`abolish/v1/ballot-statement`, new plain-hash tag). It covers the definition hash, the ballot record type and version, the tier, the nullifier, every VSS commitment and every share-ciphertext hash, hashed once.
  - **Proofs.** The option proof (each `C_j` opens to 0 or 1) and the sum proof (`C_Σ` opens to a value from `min` to `max`) are CDS disjunctions of Schnorr proofs base `h`, in compact form. A proof carries only challenges and responses, and the verifier recomputes the prover commitments.
  - **Challenges.** Each challenge's input is `statement_hash`, the position (option index, or `min` and `max`), the commitment, and every recomputed prover commitment.
  - **Check codes:** `validity-shape`, `option-proof`, `sum-proof`.
  - **Residuals:** trustees can open any ballot; binding is computational; cast-as-intended is P1-13's; the prover isn't constant time.
- [[domain-separation]]:
  - the two Fiat–Shamir tags are now specified, and `abolish/v1/ballot-statement` is added;
  - the Fiat–Shamir rule allows a statement digest under its own tag, provided the challenge still carries the position, the allowed-value set and the prover commitments.
- [[verifier]] 5.4 names the codes. [[versioning]]'s `0x0002` row points at the committed vote. The spec README and the vectors README are updated, and `circuit.json` gains the new tag's field element.
- `packages/crypto/src/ballot-validity.ts`:
  - `shareVote`, `proveValidity`, `checkValidity`, `checkShares`, `statementHash`, and the `COMMITTED_VOTE` and `SHARES` codecs.
  - Internal `shareVoteWith` and `proveValidityWith` take explicit randomness. The package entry point doesn't export them, and a test checks that.
  - Every sharing is frozen and registered when it is made, and the prover refuses any other, so no caller can choose a commitment's randomness (a hand-built `r_j = 0` would publish an openable commitment, T-15). A test checks that a copy and a hand-built sharing are refused.
- Threat files T-15, T-28, T-30 and T-32.

## Tested (part 1)

- **Spec vectors.** `docs/spec/vectors/ballot-validity.json` is printed by `packages/crypto/scripts/ballot-validity-vectors.py`, an independent Python implementation of the page. Its share check evaluates power by power where TypeScript uses Horner. Its only import is `group-vectors.py`'s self-checked ristretto255.
- **Nine valid ballots,** each with the randomness that made it, and each reproduced byte for byte by the TypeScript prover, with its statement hash and every trustee's shares:
  - plurality (first, last and blank);
  - approval (two selected, at its limit, a minimum of 1, all selected);
  - `MAX_TRUSTEES` (`k = n = 16`);
  - `MAX_OPTIONS` (64 options, 65 sum branches).
- **22 invalid ballots,** each with its expected code:
  - shape: options, degree, share hashes, option proofs, sum branches, another range;
  - option proofs: copied under another nullifier, election or tier; another share hash; swapped proofs; swapped branches; challenges that don't sum; an altered response; a vote of 2; a vote of −1 that would cancel another option while the sum proof stays valid;
  - sum proofs: a plurality over-vote, over an approval limit, under a minimum, another range of the same size, challenges that don't sum, another ballot's sum proof.
- **29 share checks:** every trustee of three ballots (24), plus `v + 1`, `r + 1`, another trustee's shares, a missing option and swapped options.
- **14 decode rejections:**
  - an identity or non-canonical commitment;
  - every list over its maximum;
  - a scalar equal to ℓ, and one of 2²⁵⁶ − 1;
  - truncated and trailing input;
  - the shares' own rejections.
- **fast-check properties** (passing at `FC_NUM_RUNS=1000`):
  - honest ballots verify and every trustee's shares pass;
  - any `k` shares interpolate to the vote and open `C_j`;
  - summed shares of several ballots pass against the summed VSS commitments;
  - a ballot fails under another nullifier, election or tier;
  - any changed byte of a committed vote fails decoding or validity;
  - random bytes are rejected or re-encode to themselves.
- **Input checks:** the prover refuses votes the rules don't allow, a polynomial whose constant term isn't the vote, rules outside the protocol limits, and seats outside 1 to 16.

## Known-weak (part 1)

- **Share hashes are opaque.** They are bound into every proof, but nothing yet defines or checks the ciphertexts behind them (part 2).
- **Nothing on the board carries a committed vote yet** (part 3).
- **The prover's scalar arithmetic isn't constant time.** It uses JavaScript `bigint`. The scalar multiplications run the same whatever the vote, but their order within a proof depends on the real branch (T-18).
- **`VoteSharing` holds secrets as `bigint`s,** which JavaScript can't zeroise; the caller can only drop its references.
- **No published vectors exist for this composition.** The independent implementation and the review carry the weight (T-36, [[0002-everlasting-privacy]]).

## What was built (part 2)

- [[0016-share-kem-dh-group]] (Accepted (agent), owner may veto): the DH half is ristretto255, the commitment group, reading the owner's "X25519" as DH on Curve25519; X25519 proper would need a new complaint proof.
- [[share-encryption]], a new spec page:
  - **Trustee KEM key** `kem_key`: the ML-KEM-768 encapsulation key (FIPS 203 §7.2 modulus check in the decoder) and `dh = g^x`; `kem_secret`: the 64-byte ML-KEM seed and `x ≠ 0`.
  - **Ciphertext** `share_ciphertext`: `ct_mlkem`, `ct_dh` and the ChaCha20-Poly1305 output, under `K = H(DS("abolish/v1/kem/share", ss_M ‖ ss_DH ‖ ct_DH ‖ dh))` (the X-Wing combiner) and associated data `DS("abolish/v1/kem/share-aad", definition ‖ 0x0002 ‖ 1 ‖ seat ‖ vss)`. The associated data leaves out the tier and nullifier, so a challenged ballot's ciphertexts can be made before the nullifier (P1-13); the validity proofs bind both with every ciphertext hash. `H(ct_i)` is `H(DS("abolish/v1/share-ciphertext", ct_i))`.
  - **Decryption** codes `share-malformed`, `share-decrypt`, `share-check`; **public posting** codes `posting-malformed`, `posting-hash`, `posting-encapsulation`; **complaints**: a Chaum–Pedersen proof of `D = ct_DH^x` under `abolish/v1/fs/share-complaint`, checked with `complaint-proof` and `complaint-unfounded`.
- [[domain-separation]]: `abolish/v1/kem/share` specified; `abolish/v1/kem/share-aad`, `abolish/v1/share-ciphertext` and `abolish/v1/fs/share-complaint` added. [[verifier]] 5.9 names the posting and complaint checks. [[ballot-validity]] points at the new page and records the nullifier-independent ciphertexts. `circuit.json` and `hash.json` gain the new tags.
- `packages/crypto/src/share-encryption.ts`: `kemKeygen`, `encryptShares`, `openShares`, `checkPosting`, `proveComplaint`, `checkComplaint`, `shareCiphertextHash` and the four codecs. Internal `kemKeyPairWith`, `encryptSharesWith` and `proveComplaintWith` take explicit randomness; the entry point doesn't export them (tested). Encryption takes shares only from a sharing `shareVote` made.
- New dependencies `@noble/post-quantum` 0.7.1 (ML-KEM-768 only; self-audited, allowed on testnets by the owner) and `@noble/ciphers` 2.4.0 (ChaCha20-Poly1305 only), both `@noble/*`, both also already in `@noble/post-quantum`'s own closure.
- Threat files T-15, T-36, T-39 and T-41.

## Tested (part 2)

- **Published vectors:** `@noble/post-quantum`'s ML-KEM-768 against Go's accumulated FIPS 203 vectors (`crypto/mlkem` `TestAccumulated`, go1.25.0: 100 and 10 000 iterations, with implicit rejection); `@noble/ciphers`' ChaCha20-Poly1305 against RFC 8439 §2.8.2 and Appendix A.5, parsed from the vendored RFC text (`rfc/rfc8439.txt`, added to the manifest).
- **Spec vectors:** `docs/spec/vectors/share-encryption.json` from `packages/crypto/scripts/share-encryption-vectors.py`, an independent Python implementation with its own ML-KEM-768 (from FIPS 203; it matched Go's 100- and 10 000-iteration hashes) and ChaCha20-Poly1305 (from RFC 8439; it checks the vendored RFC first). Three key panels (2, 3 and 16 seats); three honest ballots (plurality, `MAX_OPTIONS` with the 4116-byte sealed maximum, `MAX_TRUSTEES` with seat 16) reproduced byte for byte; three dishonest ones (a bad share sealed correctly, a seal under the wrong DH value, a short share list); nine decryption failures (another seat, another seat's associated data, another definition, another `vss`, flipped tag, ML-KEM and DH bytes, identity and truncated ciphertexts); eight postings; ten complaints (proven for each dishonest ballot, unfounded for an honest one, altered `z`, `c` and `D`, another key, another definition, and a complaint on a failing posting), the honest ones reproduced from their nonce; 21 codec cases (the modulus check at both 12-bit positions and the largest allowed coefficient, identity keys and ephemerals, `x = 0` and `x = ℓ`, the sealed maximum and one over, truncation and trailing bytes).
- **fast-check properties** (at `FC_NUM_RUNS/20`, at least 10 runs, because ML-KEM is slow in JavaScript): every trustee opens its own shares; an honest posting passes and a complaint against it is unfounded; any changed byte of a ciphertext stops it opening and fails its posting; any altered complaint field fails the proof. At `FC_NUM_RUNS`: random bytes decode as a ciphertext or a KEM key only if they re-encode to themselves.
- **Input checks:** an unregistered sharing, a key or encapsulation count that isn't `n`, `e = 0`, a 31-byte `m`, a key failing the modulus check or with an identity DH key, and `x = 0` are all refused.

## Known-weak (part 2)

- **`@noble/post-quantum` has no independent audit** (self-audited at 0.6.1). Testnets only until one exists; funding it is the owner's ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Decision).
- **ML-KEM's published vectors aren't vendored.** Go's accumulated hashes are copied into the test with their URL; neither `golang/go` nor C2SP's CCTV is an allowlisted publisher, so CI's provenance step doesn't re-fetch them. NIST ACVP vectors need a publisher added in a `ci:` PR.
- **No published vectors for the combination** (it isn't X-Wing). The independent implementation and the review carry the weight (T-36).
- **Not constant time.** ML-KEM in `@noble/post-quantum` makes no constant-time claim; `x`, `e_i` and `w` are `bigint`s that can't be zeroised. The derived byte arrays (`dk`, `ss_M`, `K`) are overwritten after use.
- **Nothing on the board carries a posting or complaint yet,** and nothing bounds the complaints per trustee key below 2³² ([[group]], Static Diffie–Hellman) until part 3's posting records do.
- **KEM keys aren't registered yet** (P1-14 part 2), so nothing checks a key's possession before shares are encrypted to it.
