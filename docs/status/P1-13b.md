## Scope

The receipt's later checks ([[receipts]], Later checks): the checks after the segment's deadline against the checkpoints anchored on L1, the check with a copy of the anchored board, and what makes a receipt evidence a third party can check, with [[board]]'s client rule 2. Split out of P1-13 part 2 (#48) at the owner's request in the session of 2026-10-12.

## Requirements

From #48's reviews (its draft of these checks and the review threads are in #48's history):

- **Time.** A receipt carries no time, so a key obtained after an election could forge evidence against it for good. The evidence must be bounded in time (the draft used 30 days of L1 time after the seal), and a voter's evidence of a real drop must survive the bound (the draft used salted L1 commitments carried by any transaction whose input is a list of 32-byte commitments, judged at `min(latest, dispute_end)`, never at the commitment's block).
- **Withheld boards.** An operator can anchor a fork that no checkpoint contradicts from L1 alone, and withhold the board until any bound has passed; clients must keep their evidence (the draft committed every receipt in doubt).
- **Keys.** The receipt key set that judges an election is fixed before voting starts (in the election definition, or recorded per election from the release current when it opens), so a later release can't void receipts, and a key not bound to an election can't forge against it.
- **Presence.** A dropped ballot is shown only by a valid ballot that no admitted entry of the anchored board carries by `(tier, ν, M)`, whatever its selection outcome; a rejected or re-randomised copy proves nothing. Needs P1-12's ballot record and admission.
- **Privacy.** Commitments are salted and posted through relayers; proofs are fetched for every segment, never for the voter's own alone (T-45, T-11).
- **Admission and selection.** The voter's later check shows the ballot's admission verdict and whether it is the selected ballot of its tier and nullifier ([[revoting]], Residuals; T-41, T-42).
- **The voter's own view.** After any bound, the voter's client still shows its own result, marked as no longer evidence (G-7).

## Part 1: the ADR

- [[0023-receipt-evidence]], option A: receipt keys listed in the election definition (`receipt_keys`, in the next unfrozen definition version, coordinated with P1-15 part 4b's version 2) and registered beforehand on L1 in an append-only registry only the anchoring authority writes (never on L2, where governance can forge any sender, T-71), so evidence is attributed to it alone; a dispute end `t_E` (the seal's anchoring window plus the pinned receipt version's 30 days), after which a receipt is evidence only with a salted commitment in L1 calldata from before `t_E` (opened with a beacon-state proof of its block), made by the client when the receipt is in doubt; the client's later check from every segment's entry-hash list and a whole verifier report, fetched at a time independent of its segment; verdicts `not-evidence`, `board-failed`, `pending`, `unanchored`, `forked`, `included`, `unresolved`, and a drop as an `unanchored` or `forked` receipt for an admissible ballot that no admitted entry carries by `(tier, ν, M)`. Rejected: keys from the release history with no time bound (B), and every receipt committed at cast on L2 (C).
- Independent review (in-session): four blocking findings, all fixed: a receipt for a segment outside the election could frame an honest operator (now `not-evidence`, and the operator must refuse such entries); attribution rested on a release-pinned key list the operator controls (now an L1 registry the anchoring authority writes); the dispute window followed the presented receipt's version (now the definition's pinned version); a dropped entry in an unanchored segment escaped every verdict (now `unanchored`). Also taken: a canonicality proof so evidence survives L1 history expiry, the inclusion path in `not-evidence`, fetch timing independent of the segment, the tie escape exempted from the selection alarm, the close rule in the drop rule, and a T-69 history line
- Threat files T-26, T-25, T-69 and T-71 record the change.
- CI review (`claude-review`): the first version put the registry on L2, trusting its sender, which L2 governance can forge (T-71); moved to L1. A seized key could never be retired, so a hostile creator could list it in any later election; the registry gains an append-only retirement record, and a definition listing a key retired by its `opens_at` fails. Its non-blocking note is taken too: `pending` and `board-failed` aren't doubt, so no commitment follows a cast closely. Its non-blocking note (recent blocks aren't yet in `historical_summaries`) is taken: the opening may use `block_roots`.
- Tested: documentation only; `vp check` formats it.

Known-weak:

- Before `t_E`, whoever holds a receipt key (a leak, a seizure, a compelled hand-over) can forge evidence against the anchoring authority for elections that opened before the key was retired, each until its `t_E` (T-69, T-53).
- A client that never checks before `t_E` and didn't commit at cast loses its evidence.
- A withheld board leaves committed evidence `unresolved` and the election unverifiable until P4-3 makes non-publication attributable.
- The whole-board hash list is 32 bytes per entry, heavy on weak connections; admission and selection come from a verifier report the client trusts.

## Part 2b: receipt keys

- [[election-definition]], Layout (version 4): version 3's fields, then `receipt_keys` (`list<p256-key, 4>`), in the stub; well-formedness `receipt-keys` (non-empty, strictly ascending encodings), checked last. Receipt keys: a listed key **judges** the election if the anchor's record, read at a finalized L1 block at or after `opens_at`, shows it registered before `opens_at` and not retired by then; the ballot client checks every listed key before casting; the operator signs only under listed keys. Versions 1 and 3 bind no keys and MUST be retired before any election registry is deployed ([[versioning]] registry row).
- [[contracts]], Board anchor, Receipt keys: `registerReceiptKey` and `retireReceiptKey` in the anchor itself (so the writer is its immutable `authority`, with no second contract or pin), exact calldata, prefix-only framing, write-once registration and retirement, events through the accumulator, and the record's slot (`A + 4`, a packed `registered_at`/`retired_at` word). - [[verifier]] 2.9: every listed key judges the election (`receipt-key-retired`, `receipt-key-unregistered`; unverifiable while the verifier's block is before `opens_at`); 2.3's `profile-combination` covers version 4 as version 3.

- Vectors: `docs/spec/vectors/election-definition-v4.json` from `scripts/definition-v4-vectors.py` (independent Python, reusing the version-3 and P-256 generators): 21 definitions (valid at the boundaries, decode rejections including four non-canonical keys, the self-pin cases, and `receipt-keys` with its order against version 3's rules) and 18 judging cases (each judgement at its boundaries, and impossible records).

## Part 3a: the receipt-key record

## Part 3b-1: the verdicts in code

- `packages/crypto`, `receipt-evidence.ts`: `disputeEnd` (`t_E`), `receiptCommitment` (`C`, with a 32-byte salt), `calldataCarries` (an aligned word of calldata whose length is a multiple of 32), and `judgeReceipt`, every verdict of [[receipts]], Verdicts, in order: `not-evidence` (the checks at cast against the keys that judge the election and the segments `f` to `lastSegment`, or `expired`), `board-failed` (board checks 1 and 2, `unsealed`, or a listed key retired or unregistered), `pending`, `unanchored`, `forked` from L1 alone, `forked` from a copy with `r′` and its consistency proof, `included` with a proof to every counted checkpoint from `s` on, and `unresolved`; `isReceiptAlarm` (client rule 3). - `packages/crypto`, `board.ts`: board checks 1 and 2 extracted from `verifyBoard` (unchanged behaviour) and exported with `unsealed` as `anchoredCheckpoints`, for judges that hold no copy.
- [[receipts]]: a judge whose finalized L1 block is before `opens_at` leaves the receipt unjudged (`receipt-key-unsettled`); and from `t_E` an opening from a fork the judge's release doesn't know leaves it unjudged whatever the other openings' failures, since that opening may pass under a release that knows the fork.
- Vectors: `docs/spec/vectors/receipt-evidence.json` from `scripts/receipt-evidence-vectors.py` (independent Python, reusing the receipt and board generators): three dispute ends, four commitments, nine calldata cases (aligned, batched, empty, unaligned, a selector prefix, a split word), and 39 verdict cases, each verdict and reason at least once, with the consistency proofs.
- Independent review (in-session): no blocking findings; seven non-blocking, six taken: proofs are built only where a verdict needs them (one for `forked`, the set once for `included`); a far-future `beacon_slot` could make a forged opening `opening-fork-unknown` and keep a receipt forged after `t_E` unjudged, so [[receipts]], Opening, step 5 now fails a `beacon_slot` not before the trusted state's slot and leaves `opening-fork-unknown` to the judge's own state; anchors' segments and the openings' results are validated; `calldataCarries` documents which check a false result belongs to; the spec says why an unsettled key leaves the receipt unjudged even beside a retired one; vectors for `receipt-election`, `receipt-inclusion`, `receipt-key-unregistered`, an anchor before `f`, anchors after `now` and the seal's window at its last second. Not taken: sharing `unsealed` with `verifyBoard` (the boundary vector pins it instead).
- Threat file T-26 records the change.
- Tested: the vectors through the production code; fast-check properties: any honest board's receipt is `included` and a receipt for any other entry at its index is `forked`, junk sources in any order don't change an inclusion, an unknown fork anywhere among failing openings leaves the receipt unjudged, salts change the commitment, a commitment is found at every aligned position of a batch; the `included` proofs verify; anchors before `f` are ignored; malformed inputs refused; `board.ts`'s existing suites for the extraction.

Known-weak:

- `judgeReceipt` takes the opening checks' results from its caller until part 3b-2 builds them, so from `t_E` on it is only as good as that caller's checks.
- An `included` verdict builds a consistency proof to every counted checkpoint from `s` on, each a pass over that checkpoint's leaves: for a board of a million entries and hundreds of segments, heavy on a phone; building them from cached subtrees (`consistencyProofSubtrees`) is for the client's part.
- The drop rule (admission by content against the whole anchored board) is the verifier's, a later part, and so are [[verifier]] 2.9 from storage proofs, the client's fetch schedule and the reference election's dropped and forked receipts.
