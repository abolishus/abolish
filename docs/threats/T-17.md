# T-17: PII on-chain or on IPFS

- Area: Ballot secrecy, unlinkability and privacy (G-3, G-4, G-5, G-12)
- Adversary: A-2, A-9, A-10
- Status: **Planned.** Residual: a poll creator can put personal data in a poll's text, which copies on IPFS may keep; a malicious client can hide chosen bytes, up to the full width of each field that must be random (salts, CSPRNG outputs), since random and chosen bytes look alike ([[notation]], Result-critical fields); a poll creator can likewise hide chosen bytes in an election definition: somewhat over 70 bytes in its display-text commitment and the free bits of its bounded integers (the registry assigns `election_id` as a hash output, P1-18), plus about 180 more in fields that neither the registry nor, so far, the trustees check before the definition reaches the board (the tier roots, group IDs and sizes, `membership_vk_hash`, and the pinned versions of types 2 to 9; verifier 1.3, 2.3 and 2.4 fail such a definition only afterwards), about 250 in all, until trustee nodes run the stub checks [[ceremony]] specifies (P1-14 part 2) before taking part in a ceremony and before approving it; no trustee node implements them yet, and the root and key-hash parts wait on P1-17b ([[election-definition]]); data in result-critical fields is archived for good; anyone who registers at Tier 0 chooses their identity commitment, about 253 bits the signing service can't tell from a real Poseidon output, logged on L2 for good with each group addition (bounded by registration rate limits; inherent to Semaphore commitments), and registrars choose 32 bytes of nonce per approval ([[contracts]], Group registry); the L1 board anchor's authority can put chosen bytes on L1 for good, 108 per checkpoint (one for any election identifier) and up to 126 per archive locator, unbounded in number beyond gas ([[contracts]], Board anchor, Known-weak); a member can direct-submit a ballot whose fields the contract doesn't check are chosen, which the verifier rejects while the archive keeps it: the 40-byte L1 reference and the validity proofs (64 bytes per option and sum branch) even once P1-18's contract runs the record's cheap checks, and the whole record after its membership proof until then ([[ballot-validity]], Ballot record, Residuals); a voter can archive about `n · (64·m + 36)` chosen bytes per ballot and re-vote in share postings sent through the contract path, about 66 KB at `MAX_OPTIONS` and `n = 16`, since neither the contract nor the public check of a posting opens its sealed shares ([[delivery]], Direct submit); through that contract path anyone can also archive, for gas, postings naming a made-up ballot (a committed vote of their choosing), and a registered seat about 130 to 190 chosen bytes per made-up ballot hash in receipts and complaints, which the delivery check sets aside but the archive keeps ([[delivery]], Residuals); a registered trustee seat can put its own failing tally opening, about 4 KB per seat and cell, on the board through the contract path P1-18 must provide, while a boarded passing opening carries only its 64-byte ECDSA nonce choice ([[tally]], Opening record, Residuals); T-68.

## Threat

A poll title, election definition, registration or ballot carries personal data into permanent storage.

## Mitigation

Contract inputs are fixed-size commitments, hashes and proofs, never free text tied to a person; Foundry tests assert this (P1-18). Display text is committed by salted hash and kept off the permanent archive, so it can be taken down while the result stays verifiable ([[0005-permanent-archive]], P1-8); otherwise moderation can hide poll content in our front ends, never erase it (P2-10, T-68).

Index: [[THREAT_MODEL]].

## History

- 2026-10-10 (P1-10b): the residual counts a definition's creator-chosen bytes (`election_id`, the display-text commitment and the free bits of its bounded integers, [[election-definition]]).
- 2026-10-11 (P1-16): the board allows only five payload record types (never display text). The operator MUST board only payloads that decode strictly; an undecodable one that slips through is reported and stays archived ([[board]], Allowed payloads).
- 2026-10-11 (P3-1): a Tier 2 document proof's private inputs include the document's data groups. [[0010-tier2-identity-provider]] publishes those proofs only if P3-4 establishes that they are statistically zero-knowledge (otherwise additions are attested, and only the owner could choose to publish computationally hiding proofs), and only predicates are proven, never disclosed values. Whether a chip-holder-recomputable nullifier counts as PII is an owner question. No real document is registered on any public chain until both are settled.
- 2026-10-11 (P1-18, part 2): the election registry admits a definition only with canonical ABI calldata, strict decoding, well-formedness and this deployment's chain IDs and addresses, and its events carry only the identifier, the creator's address, the counter, the hash and the definition's own encoding ([[contracts]], Election registry). `election_id` is now a hash output the creator doesn't choose, so the residual drops it.
- 2026-10-11 (P1-18, part 2, review): the definition residual counts the fields only the verifier checks (about 250 bytes until trustee nodes check the stub, P1-14 part 2), and the inclusion-evidence residual covers any transaction that produces one of our events, `createElection` included.
- 2026-10-11 (P2-10 part 1): [[moderation-policy]] names `personal-data` as a ground for hiding a poll's display text, at any time, and the moderation log carries reason codes only, never free text, so the log itself can't hold personal data. Hidden text stays in copies others pinned.
- 2026-10-12 (P4-1 part 1): the L1 board anchor takes fixed-width checkpoints and an archive locator ([[0005-permanent-archive]]), framed as `u16(version) ‖ body` of at most 128 bytes but not interpreted, all with canonical calldata, from the anchoring authority only. The authority chooses 108 bytes per checkpoint and up to 126 per locator, kept on L1 for good, with no limit on how many beyond gas (locator-only calls at any time); this is in the Status residual ([[contracts]], Board anchor, Known-weak).
- 2026-10-12 (P1-14 part 2, [[ceremony]], Stub checks): trustee nodes must decode the stub under their pinned profile, check its panel, chain and registry, and run verifier 1.3, 2.3 and 2.4 on it before taking part in a ceremony and again before signing its registration, so a creator can't archive chosen bytes in the fields only the verifier checks with the trustees' approval. Specified, not built: no trustee node exists yet, and until P1-17b specifies the membership key hash and the tier tree's hash, nodes refuse every stub.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-15 part 2a, [[tally]], Opening record, Boarding): anyone can post a tally opening, and a failing or stray one could carry about 4 KB of chosen scalars and signature bytes; the board operator boards only openings that pass against the anchored aggregate and are attributed to a registered seat, one per seat and cell, so a boarded opening carries at most a trustee's 64-byte ECDSA nonce choice. A record the operator should have refused is still read and reported by the verifier.
- 2026-10-12 (P3-2 part 1): the group registry takes fixed-layout records only (canonical calldata, exact lengths, canonical field elements, low-s signatures), so its events carry no undefined bytes. Its residual is in the Status line: each registrant chooses its commitment, about 253 bits on L2 for good, and registrars choose each approval's nonce; anyone can create an empty group, an event with no data ([[contracts]], Group registry).
- 2026-10-12 (P1-12 part 3b, [[delivery]]): the board operator boards a share receipt or complaint only for a ballot already on the board, one per type, ballot and seat, and only if it passes (a proven, attributed complaint; a receipt attributed to its seat), so a boarded receipt carries no chosen bytes beyond a trustee's 64-byte ECDSA nonce choice, and a boarded complaint none beyond that and its choice of the Chaum–Pedersen nonce `w`. It never boards a posting: postings reach the board only as contract events (the owner's decision in [[0002-everlasting-privacy]]). A posting can carry chosen bytes: its sealed plaintext and `m_i` are never opened by the contract or the public check, so a voter archives about `n · (64·m + 36)` bytes per ballot and re-vote (Status). Through the contract path P1-18 must add, anyone can post, for gas, postings naming a made-up ballot, which the archive keeps like any contract event. Unfounded complaints are refused too, which keeps a readable share out of the archive (T-15).
- 2026-10-12 (P1-15 part 3, [[tally]], Destruction): the operator boards only destruction statements that name the election and are attributed to a registered seat, one per seat, so each carries no chosen bytes beyond the seat's ECDSA nonce (64 bytes per seat and election); anything else on the board is reported against the operator.
- 2026-10-12 (P2-10 part 5, [[moderation-policy]], Open polls): hiding poll text in our front ends has one exception. Our ballot client treats a poll whose moderation state it can't read as visible, so during an outage or block of `GET /moderation/status` it shows a `personal-data`-hidden poll's text if it can still fetch it from IPFS and check it. We unpin that text at once, so this exposes only copies someone else pins.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-12 part 4, [[ballot-validity]], Ballot record): a ballot's share hash for a seat outside the registered key set must be 32 zero bytes (`share-hash-unqualified`), closing the `32 · (n − q)` chosen bytes per ballot [[delivery]] had left open until the ballot record existed, on the board path. On the contract path the archive keeps rejected records too, so P1-18's contract must run the record's cheap checks (definition, tier, shape, unqualified seats) with the membership proof; the L1 reference and the validity proofs stay free to members there (Status).
- 2026-10-12 (P1-13b part 2b): election-definition version 4's `receipt_keys` let a creator place about 32 chosen bytes per key, up to 4, in a registered definition; a key the anchoring authority never registered fails the election ([[verifier]] 2.9) but stays archived. The anchor's receipt-key record checks only a key's framing, so the authority can put 33 chosen bytes on L1 per registration.
- 2026-10-12 (P1-18 part 4a, [[0025-contract-event-evidence]]): the archive keeps no L2 transaction, receipt or header for our contracts' events, only the events themselves (each as the event accumulator hashes it) and the accumulator's storage proofs, so the inclusion-evidence residual of [[notation]] (an access list, an outer call's calldata or another contract's logs, archived for gas) closes once P1-18 part 4b builds it. Chosen bytes left on the contract path are those inside our own events' fields.
- 2026-10-13 (P1-18 part 4b-2b, [[contracts]], Contract-event record): the record `0x0008` is specified: each of our contracts' events is archived exactly as its accumulator hashes it, with the accumulator's storage proofs as the only evidence, and counts only if its topics and data are the canonical ABI encoding of an event its contract declares, each embedded record passing that contract's stateless checks. The inclusion-evidence residual (an access list, an outer call's calldata or another contract's logs, archived for gas) is closed and removed from Status; chosen bytes on the contract path are now only those inside our own events' fields.
- 2026-10-13 (P2-16 part 1a, [[election-definition]]): version 6 adds up to 16 author keys, about 32 chosen bytes each, that nothing on chain checks, so a definition naming keys no one holds is still registered and archived (its poll never opens). P2-16 part 3 closes it by having default seats sign a ceremony registration only once the author panel's approved transcript is on the board. A sealed definition's live fields and a panel-less definition's author transcript hash must be zero (`results`, `author-panel`).
- 2026-10-13 (P1-19 part 4h, [[verifier]], Tally openings): a tally opening the operator should have refused, and so a carrier of chosen bytes, is reported against it by the verifier when it is stray, has another number of entries than options, is unattributed or is a seat's second opening of a cell; a failing opening of its cell's own shape is reported once the counted set is known.
