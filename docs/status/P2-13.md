## Scope

The trustee node [[0006-trustees]] (Consequences) calls for, added after the owner decided the panel's structure on 2026-10-12: the deployable each trustee runs on infrastructure it controls (an app, or a mode of `apps/node`), since open polls close too often for trustees who act by hand. It runs only what the spec already defines, calling `packages/crypto` and `packages/verifier` and never reimplementing them:

- **Ceremony** ([[ceremony]]): every node duty the page sets, among them the stub checks; fresh keys and challenge secrets per run, refusing a `(stub_hash, run_id)` already seen; at most one round-4 message per run, recorded before it is sent; reveals only over round 3 as the coordinator closed it; and approval of at most one transcript per `stub_hash`, only one with at least `max(k, n − 1)` qualified seats (rule 7) that holds every message the seat sent and whose round chain it signed, with the ceremony registration signature ([[trustee-identity]]). The list here is a summary; [[ceremony]] decides.
- **Intake** ([[share-encryption]], Intake and receipts; [[delivery]]): [[verifier]] 2.5 on the election before receipting any share; receipts during voting, complaints within the complaint window, and the contract-path receipt obligation, bounded as [[P1-12c]] specifies.
- **Tally** ([[tally]]): [[verifier]] 2.8 before releasing any share; summed shares only for cells of the registered partition over the anchored counted set, checked against the L1 anchor first; the summed-share message; openings.
- **Destruction** (P1-15 part 3): the KEM secret after the tally, the signing key after its last statement.
- **Partitions** ([[0006-trustees]], Consequences): never a share for a partition the spec doesn't require; every tier is decrypted, and small tiers carry T-16's warning; below-floor regions are folded into their tier's remainder cell, never released on their own ([[0006-trustees]], Small cells).

## Plan

Each part is one PR on this branch's name. The node is its own app, `apps/trustee-node`, rather than a mode of `apps/node` ([[P4-5]], not started): the community node holds no secret, and keeping the node that holds a seat's keys apart from it keeps its code, and so what each trustee must rebuild and inspect under rule 6, small. Merging them later is a code move, not a protocol change.

1. **Ceremony duties** (done): `CeremonySeat` over a write-once `SeatStore`: the stub checks, run replay refusal, one message per round, at most one round-4 message per run, one approval per stub with rule 7's quorum and the ceremony registration signature.
2. **Transport** (done): the node's side of the coordinator protocol (it fetches rounds and posts its messages; nothing listens on the node), its configuration (the pinned panel, the seat, the identity key from a file or a hardware token), and a command to run a ceremony between nodes and a coordinator locally.
3. **Electorate check**: [[verifier]] 2.4 from finalized L2 state through `packages/verifier`, once [[P1-19]] builds it; definition versions 3 and 4 (regional cells).
4. **Intake** ([[P1-12c]] part 2 is done): 4a, 2.5 and the definition checks, intake and share receipts, refused until part 3's electorate check as the ceremony is; 4b-1, the intake endpoint and its limits; 4b-2a, the obligation decided once at `c_I`; 4b-2b, sending the owed receipts as batches by `c_D`, in two: 4b-2b-1, the receipt duty, the receipt-batch call and its recorded read; 4b-2b-2, in two: 4b-2b-2a, the deployment's view (finalized L1, the copies from the attested extent and the contract's records, what the seat shows absent); 4b-2b-2b (done, #505), the view built and the duty stepped in `run`; 4b-3, complaints, in two: 4b-3a (done, #507), the complaint duty over a deployment-supplied path; 4b-3b (done, #509), the L2 reads of postings and complaint batches, the complaint-batch call, the ballot source from the attested extent and the contract, and `ComplaintDuties` in `run`.
5. **Tally and destruction**: 2.8, summed shares, openings, destruction statements, in three: 5a (done, #511), the tally duty over a deployment-supplied counted set and channel; 5b, the counted set from the anchored counted extent and the availability attestations through `packages/verifier`, the node-to-node channel over hybrid post-quantum TLS, the openings sent to the board operator and `TallyDuties` in `run`; 5c, destruction at the earlier of every cell's anchored passing opening and `t_D`, the statement and the store's removal of the material.
6. **Contract-path duties** (waits on P1-18's direct-submit path).

## Part 4a

`IntakeSeat` (`apps/trustee-node` README, Intake): before taking any ballot of an election, the seat checks that it approved the stub and that the transcript is the one it approved, whose check still returns the key set its approval signed (3.2); the stub checks again (2.5's pinned panel, 2.3, 1.3 and 2.4 through the `ElectorateCheck`); and finalized L2 showing the definition registered under that key set, through a `RegistrationCheck` the deployment supplies (part 3) and that refuses until it can decide. Only a pass is kept. Each delivery then goes through crypto's `intakeBallot` (verifier 5.1 to 5.4 with the membership proof, then decryption); on a pass the ciphertext, shares, tier and nullifier are written once under the ballot hash, and each copy's L1 reference beside it, before the share receipt record (`0x000b`) is returned; the cutoff is checked again once the proofs are done. From `c_I` (`intakeCutoff` at the release's windows) the seat refuses intake. `CeremonySeat.intakeSecrets` gives intake the approval's transcript and key-set hashes and the approved run's KEM and signing secrets. Tested with ballots carrying real Groth16 membership proofs from `@abolishus/membership-prover`: every seat's receipt verifies under its registered signing key, the same receipt after a restart over the same store, `share-hash`, `delivery-malformed` and `membership-proof` refusals storing nothing, each election refusal (an unapproved stub, another transcript, the electorate and registration checks until they decide, other pins), the same core cast again under a newer L1 reference (one receipt, both references kept), and the cutoff at its boundary, including a delivery that finishes at it (T-52, T-54, T-37, T-14, T-41).

Known-weak: no endpoint and no `RegistrationCheck` in a deployment, so a deployed node takes no ballot; the obligation isn't decided yet (part 4b), though what it reads is stored; storage per member isn't bounded (every distinct ballot is kept); the node's own clock closes intake; definition version 1 only; shares sit unencrypted in owner-only files until the destruction duty (part 5).

## Part 4b-1

The intake endpoint (`apps/trustee-node` README, Intake endpoint): `serveIntake` serves an `IntakeSeat` at `POST /v1/intake`, the route a client reaches through an anonymiser when the operator's fails ([[delivery]], Client rules 4) and the operator's relay delivers through. The body is a transport envelope (`{stub, transcript, record, ciphertext}`, each `bytes<2^20>`); 200 returns the share receipt record, 422 a refusal's code, 503 a full queue with `Retry-After`. It allows any origin, since a delivery carries no credential and the receipt is public. Its limits: a body bound checked on the declared length before reading and while streaming; a queue bounding the deliveries held at once, checked one at a time (`intakeBallot` is synchronous); header and request timeouts checked each second; and a bound on open connections per queue place. A per-member cap on stored ballots was built and dropped in review: intake doesn't check the L1 reference, so a coercer holding a member's device once could make ballots ahead and spend the allowance as it opens, leaving the member's later re-vote with no receipt (T-45); it waits on part 4b-2's L1 reads. It serves HTTPS with TLS 1.3 and `X25519MLKEM768` only ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Consequences), or plain HTTP on loopback behind a proxy. `run` serves it with an `intake` section, refusing every delivery until part 3. Tested with the envelope's round trip and truncations (fast-check), a real seat's receipt over HTTP equal to its in-process one, every status, the body bound declared and streamed, the queue and one-at-a-time checking, an abandoned upload, TLS refusing classical-only and TLS 1.2 clients, the configuration and TLS key file, and `run` serving and closing the endpoint (T-39, T-52, T-11, T-54).

Known-weak: no deployment takes a ballot until part 3 supplies the electorate and registration checks; definition version 1 only, while the website creates version 5; storage per member isn't bounded (every distinct ballot is kept) and each repeated delivery runs every check, decapsulation included; a slow sender holds a queue place for up to about 31 seconds; no per-address limit (deliveries come through anonymisers and the relay), so non-members can fill the queue and delay members (liveness only); clients without `X25519MLKEM768` can't reach the endpoint directly; nothing pins the endpoint's certificate beyond the Web PKI.

## Part 4b-2a

The decision at the intake cutoff (`apps/trustee-node` README, Obligation): `decideObligation` decides once, from `c_I` by the node's clock, what the seat owes ([[delivery]], What a node owes). Intake reads its clock again after its records are durable and gives no receipt past `c_I`, so the decision covers every receipt the seat gave. Tested with ballots carrying real membership proofs: nothing decided before `c_I`, waiting on L1, the receipted ballot owed, a newer board ballot superseding it (owed nothing, the newer one in the complaint scope), a ballot shown absent dropped, a copy boarded after `s_A` displacing nothing, a reorganised reference owed once over two casts of one core, the first decision returned over a changed view, both refusals writing nothing, and a delivery whose records become durable at `c_I` getting no receipt (T-52, T-54, T-25, T-38).

Known-weak: nothing sends the decision yet (part 4b-2b), and no deployment supplies the view or calls it; the view's source is trusted to have checked each copy and gathered it before `c_I`, which the decision can't tell, and `2M` catches only non-members' copies; a node down at `c_I` decides over what it holds when it comes back; the clock is the node's own, and one stepped backwards across `c_I` mid-decision could let a receipt leave uncovered; the per-member storage bound from part 4b-1's Known-weak still waits (it needs the node's view of the L1 head at intake, not only at `c_I`); definition version 1 only.

## Part 4b-2b-1

Known-weak: no deployment supplies the decision's view or steps the duty yet (part 4b-2b-2); every owed receipt is sent, including one whose passing receipt is anchored, which the spec lets a seat skip (gas only); the seat's own L2 node is trusted for which batches are recorded (hiding one makes the seat resend, a no-op; showing one the board never recorded stops the resend); the signed transactions are held in memory, so a restart signs again at new nonces; elections that commit roots are still refused at the stub; definition version 1 only.

## Part 5a

The tally duty (`apps/trustee-node` README, Tally duty): `TallyDuty` takes the counted set from the deployment (`waiting`, `unavailable`, or each cell's counted ballot records once fixed) and sends nothing until it is fixed, never if the election is unavailable. It checks the definition and transcript the deployment gives against the seat's approval (the approved definition hash, transcript and key set, with its own keys in it; T-37), and the counted set before summing: one list per cell of the registered partition, each ballot of the election and the cell's tier, decoding as version 1 and of the election's shape, no voter twice; the aggregate of each cell is written once, so a later, different set is refused for good (T-14, T-16, T-29). The seat sums the shares intake stored under each ballot hash recomputed from the record (T-38), checks its own sums, and each step seals them to every other registered seat through `SummedSharePeers` until it has signed the cell's opening; `receive` keeps the sums of messages that pass crypto's checks (context and signature before decapsulation, T-39), ignoring the rest without answer, and only once `step` has fixed the set; the transport must acknowledge on arrival so timing tells the sender nothing (T-39). With `k` passing sums it combines the lowest (`openCell`), signs the opening as itself and writes it once (T-29). Sums and received messages stay in memory, never in the store (T-15). An independent in-session review found nothing blocking; its findings are addressed above (the source and transport requirements, `receive` no longer reading the set, the pins checked, more negative tests). Tested over real ballots with Groth16 membership proofs taken in by every seat's intake: openings judged by `checkOpening` and the signature under the signer's registered key, with the expected counts; nothing sent while waiting or unavailable, nor a message kept that arrived before the set was fixed; the opening unchanged and no second signed after a restart; a seat lacking a ballot's shares sending nothing but combining the others'; an unreachable seat not holding up the rest; tampered, badly signed, misaddressed, other-set and undecodable messages ignored; nothing but aggregate hashes and signed openings written to the store; refusals of another number of cells, a ballot of another election or tier, one that doesn't decode, a voter twice, a set other than the one fixed before, another definition, transcript or key set, another seat's keys under this seat's index, a re-vote beside the voter's other ballot, a ballot of another shape, and sums from a store whose shares fail; and a fast-check property over which messages arrive that a seat opens exactly when it holds `k` passing sums, every opening the same.

Known-weak: no deployment reads the counted set, carries messages or boards the openings, and `run` doesn't step the duty (part 5b); a seat sums only over ballots it took in at intake, not shares it could open from a contract-path posting; received messages are lost on a restart until resent, and the seat resends each step with no acknowledgement; a well-formed but wrong counted set given first is fixed for the seat, since the duty checks the set's form, not that it is the anchored extent's, and sums over a set differing from the anchored one by a ballot would let `k − 1` other trustees open that ballot beside the published opening, so part 5b must read it only from the seat's own finalized nodes through `packages/verifier` (T-14, T-16, T-29); a seat stops resending once it has signed its opening, so if every seat that opened a cell fails before boarding it the rest wait for them; nothing is destroyed yet (part 5c); definition version 1 only.

## Part 5b-1

The summed-share channel (`apps/trustee-node` README, Summed-share channel): `serveSummedShares` takes one summed-share message per `POST /v1/summed-shares` and answers 202 as soon as it holds the whole body, before the seat's `receive` runs, so its answer never tells a sender which check failed (T-39); it bounds the body at `MAX_SUMMED_SHARE` (version 2 at `MAX_OPTIONS`), the messages held at once, checked one at a time, and the time and connections a peer may take (T-52), and keeps nothing (T-15). `httpPeers` is `SummedSharePeers` over the other seats' endpoints: TLS 1.3 offering only `X25519MLKEM768`, the peers' certificates checked against a configured CA or the system's roots, plain HTTP only on loopback for a TLS proxy, resolving on the acknowledgement and throwing `PeerError` (the seat and a status or code, never an address) otherwise. Both sides hold to the hybrid post-quantum group the spec requires of this channel. An independent in-session review found that the check's duration is not hidden (below); its other findings are addressed (one loopback set per side, a throw of anything logged safely, the seat named in a timeout, a fresh connection per send, a test that polled less robustly). Tested: the acknowledgement before the seat's check and the same when it throws, logged by name only; the other paths, methods and types; a body past the bound declared or streamed and one at it taken; one message at a time and 503 past the queue; an abandoned upload; each `PeerError`; plain HTTP only on loopback on both sides; over TLS a send over the hybrid group to a peer chaining to the CA, refused without it and against a classical-only peer, and the endpoint refusing a classical-only sender; and three real tally duties over real ballots opening the cell with nothing between them but their endpoints.

Known-weak: `run` neither serves the channel nor steps the duty, and the configuration names no peers (part 5b-2); the endpoint takes a message from anyone, so an unregistered sender can fill its queue and delay the seats' messages, which they resend each step (liveness only, T-52); the check of a message is synchronous and costs more the further it gets, so a registered seat sending two messages back to back can time the first from the second's acknowledgement and tell `summed-decrypt` from `summed-check`, which the spec allows only behind a proof of knowledge of `e` (T-39): the fix is `openSummedShares` doing the same work on every failure, or the check off the answering thread, before `run` serves the channel (closed in part 5b-2a). Part 5b-2's counted set must come from the counted extent once a quorum attests it, and the availability duty attests only the ballot extent so far: point 2 needs `t_S`, which needs P1-18's contract-event windows.

## Part 5b-2a

The summed-share check (`packages/crypto`, `openSummedShares`): every message that reaches decapsulation, which only a registered seat's signed one does, now runs the whole summed-share check over every option without stopping at the first that fails, with each share's scalars blinded so a zero costs what any scalar does (`checkEveryShare`), and over placeholder shares when it doesn't decrypt to `m` canonical ones. Its cost no longer tells `summed-decrypt` from `summed-check` to a sender timing it from its next message's acknowledgement, which closes the opening 5b-1 left before `run` may serve the channel (T-39). Result codes and accept or reject are unchanged. Tested: the published ballot-validity vectors' share checks through both checks; a property that `checkEveryShare` agrees with `checkShares` on honest shares and with any one option's `v` or `r` changed, and refuses a short list; its seat bounds; every receiving vector that reaches decapsulation (`ok`, `summed-decrypt`, `summed-check`) running it once over every option, and no other running it; and the summed-share tests of every result code, unchanged.

Known-weak: a pass still costs more (the seat keeps the sums), which tells a sender the accept or reject bit the spec allows. The costs are equal in curve work, up to the AEAD body and the decode of a message that opens, which only a sender knowing its key can make; they aren't measured: JavaScript gives no hard constant-time guarantee, and no test times the check or proves `checkEveryShare` never stops early (the code and the property do). Everything 5b-1 left for `run` is part 5b-2b's.

## Notes

- These rules bind honest nodes only: a trustee can change its own node, and `k` trustees together can decrypt anything (T-14).
- Rule 6 of [[0006-trustees]] applies in full, since whoever controls the node software reaches every share at once (T-61): among its points, no auto-update; each trustee rebuilds the signed, reproducible release (P0-3) and publishes which one it runs; releases roll out one trustee at a time with a delay between them, so no release reaches `k` nodes before trustees and the public have had time to inspect it; a share sits in an HSM only where decryption runs inside it (a TPM-sealed key protects against disk theft, not the node software); and a second implementation should exist before Phase 5.
- Phase 1's reference election (P1-20) and simulations (P1-21) simulate seven trustees, 4-of-7, with two offline; this node replaces the simulation in Phase 2's reference election against staging (P2-12).
