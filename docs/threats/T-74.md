# T-74: Tier 0 token issuer misuse

- Area: Eligibility and Sybil resistance (G-1, G-2)
- Adversary: A-2, A-1, A-6
- Status: **Planned.** Decided in [[0039-tier0-token-issuance]] and specified in [[tier0-token]]; built by [[P2-30]] parts 2 to 5. Residual: a stolen key mints Tier 0 identities until its key stops redeeming, detected only in aggregate.

## Threat

Each account takes one blind-issued token per Tier 0 group ([[0024-tier0-registration]], Owner decision), so:

- whoever holds an issuer key (a thief, a malicious deploy, A-6, A-2) computes tokens for any commitment and any group with no account, and stuffs Tier 0 groups (T-01, T-06);
- an issuer that answers one account under a key of its own, or under another group's tweak, marks that account's token, and through its redemption joins the account to its commitment (T-10);
- the issuer that sees the account and the signing service that sees the commitment are the same operator, so the times of issuance and redemption can join them (T-11, T-13).

## Mitigation

- **Blind and verifiable.** RFC 9497's POPRF mode: the issuer sees a blinded element, uniformly distributed whatever the input, and proves its evaluation against the period's key tweaked by the group's public `info`. The client checks the proof and refuses any other key.
- **One key for everyone.** The client takes each period's public key only from the signed release's deployment record, so a per-account key fails the proof check.
- **Bound tokens.** A token's input holds its key's identifier and the commitment it registers, so a token seen in transit registers nothing else, and the issuer's `info` stops a token for one group admitting to another (T-16).
- **Bounded keys.** One key per 30-day period, issuing in that period and redeeming in it and the next, then destroyed, held apart from every other secret (T-65).
- **A compromise shows in counts.** The service can count, per key and group, the tokens issued and the commitments newly queued by redemption without linking either; more redemptions than issuances is a compromise, and the key is retired early.
- **Timing.** The client redeems at a random instant of the registration period after the one it got its token in; neither the issuer's record nor the queue keeps a time.

Residuals:

- A stolen key mints identities until it stops redeeming, and the counts catch only a thief who outspends honest redemptions' shortfall; the registrations already queued or signed stay.
- A token can be sold: an account holder can blind a buyer's commitment. A token costs only what a verified account costs ([[P2-31]]).
- POPRF's security rests on the 3HashSDHI assumption, stronger than VOPRF mode's.
- Network-level timing between an account's issuance and its redemption, seen by our proxy and platform, remains T-11's.
