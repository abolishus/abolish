# Protocol parameters

Limits that apply across record types in protocol version 1, and the post-close windows that enter the seal ([[election-definition]], The seal). A window is a constant of its record version, changed only by a new version, never lowered or raised in place. A record version may set a lower maximum for its own field, never a higher one. Raising a protocol-wide limit is a new record version of every record type it affects, with this page recording which versions use the new limit ([[versioning]]); lowering one before it is frozen is an ordinary spec change. Threats: T-52 (bounded input sizes, so flooding and hostile lengths stay cheap to reject), T-28 (ballot shape), T-50 (direct-submitted ballots must fit the L2's forced-deposit limits).

## Why these values

- **`MAX_OPTIONS`.** A plurality or approval ballot carries one ciphertext or commitment and one validity proof per option, plus a proof for the sum ([[0003-tally-scheme]]), so its size grows linearly with the option count. Under perfectly hiding commitments ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], accepted), that ADR estimates about 320 bytes of public data per option at k = 4, so 64 options is about 20 KB, plus per-trustee data. Most real contests have far fewer options; a civic contest with more candidates than this (the 2003 California recall had 135) would need a new record version with a higher limit, or a ranked-choice or multi-question design. P1-18 checks that a ballot at this limit fits a direct submission through the L2's forced-deposit path and its calldata cap ([[0004-l2-choice]]), and lowers the limit here if it doesn't, before anything is frozen.
- **`MAX_QUESTIONS`.** Every question has at least 2 options and a poll at most `MAX_OPTIONS` in all, so 32 questions is the most the option budget allows: the list bound never binds before it ([[0026-multi-question-ballots]], Limits). Keeping one budget for the whole poll keeps every per-option bound above (share ciphertexts, summed-share messages, openings, `MAX_BOARD_PAYLOAD`) unchanged.
- **`MAX_DISPLAY_TEXT`.** Four MiB: above version 1's worst case (about 3.5 MB) and far above any real poll's text, but well under the hundred-odd MB version 2's list maxima would otherwise allow, so a hostile record is rejected on its length alone (T-52).
- **`MAX_TRUSTEES`.** Bounds per-ballot and per-ceremony data that grows with `n` (per-trustee shares and receipts under P1-3's option B, per-trustee transcript entries under either option). Sixteen leaves room above the proposed panel of seven for a second panel or replacements without a new version.
- **`MAX_BOARD_PAYLOAD`.** Every record on the board travels in a board entry, so this bounds what one entry can make a verifier read, hash and archive (T-52). The largest records expected are ballots (about 20 KB of commitments and proofs at `MAX_OPTIONS`, plus a membership proof of tens of kilobytes, P1-12, P1-17) and the ceremony and tally transcripts, which grow with `MAX_TRUSTEES` and the option count; 1 MiB leaves an order of magnitude of room above them and matches the chunk size of [[content-addressing]]. Each record's own layout sets a tighter bound; P1-12, P1-14 and P1-15 lower this one if their records come out much smaller, before anything is frozen.
- **`DELIVERY_WINDOW` and `COMPLAINT_WINDOW`.** Two days for receipts and postings after the close, enough for a ballot boarded at the close to be anchored or forced through L1 and its receipts still sent through the contract path, and three more for trustees to complain about invalid postings: the reasons are in [[delivery]], Windows.
- **`BALLOT_FETCH_ALLOWANCE` and `COUNTED_FETCH_ALLOWANCE`.** Twelve hours each for the trustees to finish fetching an extent they have followed through the poll, so a withheld one fails the election before any result instead of leaving it unverifiable: the reasons are in [[availability]], Windows.
- **`TALLY_WINDOW`.** Seven days after the counted set's anchoring checkpoint is due (`t_S`) for the tally to reach the board: the reasons are in [[tally]], Tally window.
- **`DESTRUCTION_WINDOW`.** One day after `t_D` for trustees' destruction statements to reach the board; nothing the result rests on waits for them, so it is short ([[tally]], Destruction).
- **`AUTHOR_OPENING_WINDOW`.** Four days after `t_S` for an author panel's partial openings, so the default seats, which publish only after them, keep the other three of `TALLY_WINDOW`'s seven days, a weekend included, to see them anchored, combine and board theirs ([[tally]], Author panel).
- **`DISPUTE_WINDOW`.** Thirty days after the seal's window for a voter's client to check its receipt and, if it is in doubt, commit it on L1, so a receipt key obtained later forges nothing; long enough for a client that only opens once a fortnight after the close, short enough that a key's exposure after an election is bounded. The reasons are in [[0023-receipt-evidence]].
- **`OPENING_WINDOW`.** Seven days after the counted extent is fixed for a voter to decide to publish an opening as dispute evidence and send it; nothing counted waits for openings, and the window ends with the tally's, so it never moves the seal ([[challenge]], Publication).
- **`MIN_INCLUSION_BOUND` and `MAX_INCLUSION_BOUND`.** δ is how long after `closes_at` an ordinary ballot's L2 block may take to become derivable from L1 before the close check stops vouching for it ([[close]]). The floor must cover the batcher's normal latency and L1 inclusion, so that an honest ballot cast at close is derivable through `L_δ` unless the batcher is down; otherwise an honest batcher's ordinary rhythm could fail elections `close-contested` (T-50, T-53). The ceiling bounds the window in which a sequencer withholding batches can rebuild unposted blocks before close with ballots made after it and still land below the close head, which nothing can detect (T-35, T-45). Measured on Base Sepolia (L1 Sepolia blocks 11,906,309 to 11,908,108, six hours, October 2026): its batcher posted 462 batch transactions to its inbox `0xff00000000000000000000000000000000084532` in 246 L1 blocks, with gaps between them of 96 seconds at the median, 168 at the 99th percentile and 180 at most, and, sampled every 15 seconds for 18 minutes, its safe head trailed its unsafe head by 90 seconds at the median and 202 at most (Base mainnet's by 44 and 80). Ten minutes is over three times both the longest gap and the worst lag, with room for a channel that spans two posts and for L1 inclusion under congestion; an hour keeps the sequencer's silent window short beside a poll's days, and the deadlines counted from `closes_at + δ` ([[delivery]], Windows) move by at most an hour. Mainnet's latency and Base's batcher policy can differ from the testnet's and can change; the bounds are constants of the immutable registry, so changing them is a new registry, never a change to a deployed one, and [[verifier]] 2.6 checks δ against the bounds its release pins.
- **`MAX_SEQUENCING_WINDOW` and `MAX_SEQUENCER_DRIFT`.** A definition records the L2 sequencing bounds its close rule assumes, and [[verifier]] 2.6 requires the chain's configuration to have stayed within them for the whole poll ([[0004-l2-choice]]), so a creator's larger value only makes 2.6 accept a worse chain. The ceilings are Base's values today, 3,600 L1 blocks (12 hours) and 1,800 seconds. The window must fit the forced-inclusion delay `F` that every contract-path deadline is sized with: at 12 seconds per L1 block plus an hour of missed slots, 3,600 blocks is the 13 hours of `F` and of the contract-event window `W_C` that [[delivery]], Windows, assumes, so no registered definition can outrun the counted extent (T-50, T-35). The drift ceiling makes 2.6 fail closed when L2 governance lets the sequencer lead L1 by more than Base's 30 minutes, the figure 0004's analysis and `ROOT_WINDOW`'s margin above assume, instead of a creator's headroom absorbing the raise (T-35, T-71); the L1-origin checks of [[contracts]] use the release's pinned drift, never the definition's. There is no floor: a value below the chain's fails 2.6 for the creator's own election, which the registry can't see. If Base raises either value, every poll of this deployment open while the raise holds fails 2.6, unattributed, until a new registry with new ceilings is deployed, which 0004 anticipates for any raise; Base lowering them breaks nothing.
- **`VOUCH_K` and `VOUCH_SLOTS`.** Three document-verified secrets per Tier 1 identity, and twelve vouches each, bound Tier 1 at four times Tier 2 and collusion at four identities per colluding document: the reasons are in [[0014-tier1-vouching]], Recommendation. Both are constants of the group registry's deployment, so changing either is a new registry with full re-admission to Tier 1, never a new record version alongside the old.
- **`VOUCH_ROOT_INTERVAL` and `VOUCH_ROOT_WINDOW`.** The grid and the window are counted in L2 block numbers, which every archived event carries, so a verifier judges both with no block header and no anchoring clock ([[0014-tier1-vouching]], Amendment). An hour of blocks, the anchoring period on Base that [[0014-tier1-vouching]] chose, coarsens what a vouch's root block says about when its voucher synced as much as that choice did (T-22), and makes every voucher who syncs within the same hour name the same root (T-13), at a cost of at most an hour of staleness, small beside the window. A finer grid would reveal sync time more finely and split vouchers across more roots. The window has to cover the interval, the root's settling on L1 and the time a vouchee needs to meet `k` vouchers in person and submit, which can be days; a week leaves room for that. It also bounds how long a removed Tier 2 member can still vouch and how long a vouch held back by its vouchee keeps its voucher's slot spent ([[vouching]], Prover rules), which argue against more; [[nullifier-reshare]]'s `MAX_RESHARE_WINDOW` uses the same seven days of blocks. Both assume Base's 2-second blocks ([[0004-l2-choice]]); a shorter block time shrinks them in seconds, and both are constants of the registry's deployment, so changing them is a new registry with full re-admission to Tier 1, like `VOUCH_K` and `VOUCH_SLOTS`.
- **`MIN_CELL_ELECTORATE`.** A thousand registered members, about a US precinct, whose results are already published, so no regional cell is finer than results voters already see; with typical turnout a cell holds hundreds of ballots. 100 would allow cells where a few dozen voters, or unanimity, reveal votes; 10,000 would leave most districts unsplittable in a young deployment ([[0019-regional-cells]], Recommendation). It counts registrations, not people, so at Tier 0 an attacker can pad a region past it (T-16). It may be raised by a new definition version, never lowered in place.
- **`MIN_LIVE_BATCH`.** A batch in which every voter chose the same option reveals each of their votes, and a batch of `B` is unanimous for an option of share `p` with probability `p^B`: below 1% for `p ≤ 0.79` at 20 and for `p ≤ 0.91` at the creation flow's default of 50. Ten, the usual cell-suppression floor in official statistics, leaves over 10% at `p = 0.8`; a hundred would keep most everyday polls from ever showing a running total ([[0027-trustee-panels-and-live-results]], The minimum batch). It counts nullifiers, not people, so at Tier 0 padding defeats it (T-16). Unanimity isn't the only disclosure: a zero count in a batch says no one in it chose that option, with probability `(1 − p)^B`, about 12% for `p = 0.1` at 20 and 0.5% at 50 ([[tally]], Residuals of live results), which argues for a higher floor in a later version. It may be raised by a new definition version, never lowered in place.
- **`ROOT_PERIOD` and `ROOT_OFFSET`.** Ten minutes is [[registration]]'s `REGISTRATION_PERIOD` ([[0028-rolling-electorates]], Accepted roots); boundaries need not line up with anchoring periods, which nothing about a step reads. The clock is UNIX time, like the registration periods, never a deployment's anchoring epoch, so a step's boundary follows from these constants alone and a verifier needs no pinned clock to judge it. The offset puts each root boundary half a period after a registration boundary: a batch formed at a registration boundary lands on L2 within seconds to a few minutes and is in the next step, so a new member waits about half a period for a step, not a whole one. A shorter period would time each step's additions more finely and give a verifier more roots to compute; a longer one lengthens the first-answer wait ([[first-answer]]).
- **`ROOT_WINDOW`.** Two hours. The step a conforming client names is the newest settled one at or before its L1 reference, so the window must cover the age that step can reach on an honest chain, which [[0004-l2-choice]]'s figures for Base bound: up to `ROOT_PERIOD` (10 minutes) until the next boundary; then an L2 block with a later timestamp reaching the chain derived from L1, minutes in normal operation; an L2 timestamp may lead its L1 origin by up to `max_sequencer_drift` (30 minutes today), which can make a step settle earlier in L1 time, but a reference block chosen from L1 time can then be up to that much behind `τ_n`, so the client waits for a later reference rather than name a future step; and the client's own slack between choosing its reference and proving, which the sealed-answer flow keeps to seconds. That sums to well under an hour, and two hours leaves the rest for a slow batcher or a client on a poor connection. The window is also what bounds what a non-conforming client's choice of step reveals about its voter's join time (T-13), so it is no longer than that margin needs. A batcher that posts nothing for over about an hour and a half, or a sequencer stall after which L2 time catches up from behind real time (so blocks at or before `τ_n` are still being made long after L1 time passed it), leaves no settled step inside the window: clients then wait, or prove against an unsettled step and re-cast if it changes; that is a liveness residual of the sequencer's power over posting (T-50), not a way to admit a stale root.
- Ballots per election, board entries per segment and the lengths of every variable-length field are set by the record tables that use them (P1-10 to P1-16), each within these limits.
