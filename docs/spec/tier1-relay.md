# Tier 1 relay

How a vouchee's Tier 1 addition reaches the group registry through our API, which pays its gas, as [[0014-tier1-vouching]] (Who pays; Rules for P3-2, P2-6, P2-8 and P2-3) and [[contracts]] (Group registry, Tier 1 additions, Who pays) require: the request and its answers, the queue, the relay job's replay and its batch, and how the client learns the outcome. Notation is from [[notation]]; the record, its checks and the counting rule are [[vouching]]'s; groups and the registry's call are [[contracts]]'. Threats: T-06, T-10, T-11, T-13, T-22, T-25, T-52, T-53, T-69, T-71.

The relay is operational, never part of an election: no verifier reads the request, the queue or the relay's state. What a verifier checks is the registry's logged additions, counted by [[vouching]] (Counting across the deployment) whoever sent them. So this page binds the relay, its route and the ballot client, not the verifier. It adds no record type, hash or encoding: the request carries [[vouching]]'s Tier 1 addition record unchanged. `packages/crypto` has every check the relay runs (`checkTier1Addition`'s cheap checks, `groupRegistryEvents`, `countTier1Additions`), but `countTier1Additions` replays the whole history at each call; the relay needs a counting state it extends and judges candidates against (The batch, The counting state), which part 3c-2 adds to `packages/crypto` beside it, held to the same vectors.

What this page doesn't cover:

- **Making the vouches** ([[vouching]], Prover rules; [[0014-tier1-vouching]], device to device): the voucher's and the vouchee's clients, which hand vouches over in person, never through us (T-22, T-10). That is the ballot client's (P2-8).
- **The build:** the route, the queue and the relay job (P3-3 part 3c-2), and the client's side (P2-8).

## Roles

- **The vouchee's ballot client** holds the Tier 1 identity secret and its commitment `C_V`, collects exactly `k` vouches, assembles the record and sends it. It learns the outcome from the chain (Outcome) and can always submit directly instead (The direct path).
- **The relay** (a route of `apps/api`, a queue and a scheduled job in `apps/worker`) checks the request, queues the record, and once a period replays the registry, sends the additions that count from its own L2 account and keeps them until they are final.

The relay has no keys of the registry's and signs nothing the registry reads: an addition's authorisation is its vouches ([[contracts]], Group registry, Tier 1 additions). Its account only pays.

## Values

| Name                      | Value         | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `TIER1_RELAY_PERIOD`      | 600           | Seconds in a relay period, aligned to UNIX time as `REGISTRATION_PERIOD` is ([[registration]], Values); records are sent only at period boundaries.                                                 |
| `TIER1_RELAY_QUEUE_MAX`   | 64            | The most records queued at once, across every group.                                                                                                                                                |
| `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH`   | 16            | The most additions the relay sends at one boundary.                                                                                                                                                 |
| `TIER1_RELAY_MARGIN`      | 1,800         | L2 blocks a record must still have in every vouch's root window, past the relay's latest block, to be queued: an hour of 2-second blocks, room for the wait to the next boundary and for resending. |
| `TIER1_RELAY_SEND_MARGIN` | 150           | L2 blocks a chosen record must still have in every vouch's root window past the block it is judged at, for the time between the judgement and the block that includes it.                           |
| `TIER1_RELAY_GAS_LIMIT`   | 2,000,000     | The gas limit of every relayed call, above the about 1.79 million a counted addition needs on cold state ([[contracts]], Group registry, Tier 1 additions, Who pays).                               |
| `TURNSTILE_ACTION_TIER1`  | `tier1-relay` | The Turnstile action a relay request's token must be solved for.                                                                                                                                    |

They are values of this page, not of any record: changing one changes the relay and its clients, never an election. Why these values is below.

## Groups

The relay sends additions only to **published Tier 1 groups**: for each region code the deployment serves, exactly one Tier 1 group of the group registry, created by the registrars' operator with `createGroup(1)` and listed in the deployment record and the signed release beside the region's Tier 0 groups ([[registration]], Groups), its `GroupCreated` in finalized L2 history. Our definitions list them, so an addition we pay for adds a member to the polls we create; an addition to any other Tier 1 group, which anyone can create, is the direct path's. Until the group registry records a region per group ([[0019-regional-cells]]), there is one published Tier 1 group, for region 0.

## Request

`POST /registrations/tier1` on the API, on the ballot path: like every route it carries no account cookie or `Authorization` header and is refused with one (T-10), and the handler sees the body alone, never a header, cookie or address. The body is a strict JSON object of exactly:

| Field       | Value                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `record`    | The Tier 1 addition record ([[vouching]], Tier 1 addition record): exactly `41 + 626 · VOUCH_K` bytes (1,919), as lowercase hex with no `0x`. |
| `turnstile` | The challenge page's token for `tier1-relay`, 1 to 2,048 characters from `0x21` to `0x7e` ([[registration]], Challenge page).                 |

The token comes from the same challenge page and handoff as a Tier 0 registration's, with the action named in the frame's fragment ([[registration]], Challenge page). There is no region field: the record's `group_id` names the group.

## Answers

The relay checks, in this order, and answers the first failure:

| Step | Check                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Answer                                                                        |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | The transport: no account credential, a body within the route's bound, strict JSON with no repeated key (as every route).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | 400 (as every route)                                                          |
| 2    | The body has exactly the fields above in their forms, `record` exactly 3,838 hex digits (the contract's input schema).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | 400                                                                           |
| 3    | The record passes checks 1 to 6 of [[vouching]], Checks of one addition (`decode` to `duplicate-slot`), and every vouch's `root_block` is a multiple of `VOUCH_ROOT_INTERVAL` (`root-grid`). Each needs the record alone.                                                                                                                                                     | 422 `RELAY_MALFORMED`, with the check's code and the failing vouch's position |
| 4    | The record's `group_id` is a published Tier 1 group (Groups).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | 422 `GROUP_UNKNOWN`                                                           |
| 5    | The relay's index is current: the registry's events kept up to the L2 node's latest block, unfinalized included, never read per request; if it lags by more than a minute, it stops here. Let `b` be its latest block and `s` the L2 safe head (the newest block derived from L1 data) it last read.                                                                          | 503 `RELAY_UNAVAILABLE`                                                       |
| 6    | Every vouch's `root_block ≤ s`, as the prover rules require ([[vouching]], Prover rules, The root), so a reorganisation of unsafe blocks can't change a root after the relay pays, and `b + TIER1_RELAY_MARGIN ≤ root_block + VOUCH_ROOT_WINDOW`: the record can still land within each vouch's window by the time the relay sends it ([[vouching]], The root a vouch names). | 422 `VOUCH_WINDOW`                                                            |
| 7    | Turnstile: the token passes siteverify once, for `tier1-relay` and the challenge origin's host, without `remoteip` (T-11).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | 403 `CHALLENGE_REFUSED`, or 503 `CHALLENGE_UNAVAILABLE`                       |
| 8    | In one atomic step (one Redis script, say): if the queue now holds `TIER1_RELAY_QUEUE_MAX` records, answer busy, whatever the record; otherwise queue the record, unless the same bytes are already queued.                                                                                                                                                                   | 503 `RELAY_BUSY` (the token is spent), else 200, an empty object              |

**Busy only after the solve.** The queue's size is read in step 8 alone, after Turnstile, never before it: a busy answer before the solve would let anyone watch the queue fill for free (Known-weak, Queue occupancy). A request refused as busy has spent its token, and the client solves again later.

**What the route doesn't check.** Whether the record counts needs the Tier 2 roots, the slots already spent, the group's members and six Groth16 verifications ([[vouching]], Checks), which is the job's, at the boundary (The batch). The route spends no pairing and reads no counting state, so it stays a parse and two Redis round trips; a record naming a made-up root takes a queue place until the next boundary (Known-weak, A junk flood). So a flood of well-formed junk costs a parse, a set size and an index lookup per request, and one Turnstile solve per queued record (T-52). An addition the job later finds doesn't count is dropped, not answered: the client learns it from the chain (Outcome).

**No answer depends on `C_V`'s state.** Steps 2 to 4 and 6 depend only on the record, the published list and the index's blocks; step 8's busy answer on the queue's size, checked before anything about the record; steps 5 and 7 on the index's and Cloudflare's availability. A record whose `C_V` is already a member, whose slots are spent, whose root is made up, or that is already queued gets the same 200 as a new honest one, so the route says nothing about any commitment or slot (T-13, T-22), and a repeat costs its sender a solve and changes nothing.

**Records, not commitments, are the queue's keys.** Two different records for the same `C_V` both stay queued, and the job keeps the first in its order that counts (The batch). Keying by `C_V` would let anyone who saw a commitment (T-11, T-69) queue a junk record for it first and keep the vouchee's honest one out (T-25).

## Queue

The queue is one set of records (a Redis hash from each record's SHA-256 to its bytes, say), with no arrival time, order, request identifier, address, token or count per record, and no job, key or log line derived from a record (T-10, T-11, T-13). BullMQ's events stream keeps job IDs and times, so the relay is one scheduled job per boundary, never a job per request. The route's log is the API's: method, route, status and duration (T-10). The queue MUST survive a restart, its store MUST NOT evict keys, and its persistence MUST NOT sync each write before answering, as for Tier 0's queue and for the same reasons ([[registration]], Queue).

The relay also keeps its **in-flight** additions durably: each record it has sent and that isn't yet final, with the signed transaction that carried it and its nonce, so a restart rebroadcasts that transaction rather than judging the record again (The batch, step 1). An in-flight record is **pending** while its transaction isn't in the latest L2 block's history. A record leaves the queue when the job sends or drops it; an in-flight record is deleted once it is final on L2, or once the L2 block `root_block + VOUCH_ROOT_WINDOW` of one of its vouches is final without it, after which it can never land. Nothing about a request remains then that the chain doesn't show.

## The batch

At each period boundary, once per boundary, the job:

1. **Rebroadcasts.** Every pending in-flight record's signed transaction is broadcast again, **identical**, with the same nonce and hash; its fee may be raised only by a replacement at the same nonce, carrying the same record. If that nonce has been consumed by another transaction of the account, or the transaction can't be rebroadcast, the record is returned to the queue, even past `TIER1_RELAY_QUEUE_MAX` (it was accepted, and is never dropped silently), and judged at step 4 with the queued ones, never sent as a new transaction without that, so a copy that landed first in the meantime is never paid for twice. A record that can never land (Queue) is deleted. A transaction still pending at a second boundary is replaced at its nonce with a higher fee, so a fee that was too low never holds the relay for the rest of a window. Nothing else is sent at this boundary while any in-flight record is pending, so the relay's spend is at most `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH` additions per period, rebroadcasts included.
2. **Brings the index up.** The registry's events up to the L2 node's latest block `b`, unfinalized included, and the safe head `s`, through `groupRegistryEvents` under the pinned contract-event version, with every `RegistrarsPinned` checked against the release's pins. The counting state follows (The counting state). If the index can't be brought up, or the registry's Tier 2 history is broken at or before `b` ([[vouching]], The root a vouch names, A broken Tier 2 history), nothing else happens at this boundary, and the queue keeps its records.
3. **Shuffles.** The queued records in an order drawn uniformly at random with a CSPRNG (a Fisher–Yates shuffle drawing its indices from `crypto.getRandomValues` by rejection sampling), so a flood's records don't come first by arriving first.
4. **Judges.** Walks the shuffled records, each judged against the counting state at `b` extended by the records already chosen at this boundary, as if logged in block `b + 1`, with the published group in force: the record is **chosen** if it counts, with every check of [[vouching]] (Counting across the deployment, Checks) and its proofs, and if every vouch's window still has `TIER1_RELAY_SEND_MARGIN` blocks past `b` (`b + TIER1_RELAY_SEND_MARGIN ≤ root_block + VOUCH_ROOT_WINDOW`), so the block that includes it is still in every window; it is **dropped** otherwise. The walk stops once `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH` are chosen; the records it didn't reach stay queued for the next boundary. Judging each record after the ones chosen before it keeps two records that spend one slot, or add one `C_V`, from both being sent, since only the first could count; a record that doesn't count consumes nothing, so the walk chooses exactly what one replay of the events followed by the shuffled records would count, up to the cap.
5. **Orders and sends.** Sorts the chosen records by `C_V`, ascending, then by record bytes, keeps them as in flight, removes them from the queue, and sends each as the calldata of `addTier1Member(bytes)` to the registry the release pins, from the relay's account with gas limit `TIER1_RELAY_GAS_LIMIT`, each simulated against the latest block first. A record whose simulation reverts isn't sent; it is dropped, so the relay never pays for a revert that publishes a record anyway. Nonces are assigned after simulation, consecutively over the records actually sent, so a dropped record leaves no gap that would hold the rest back. The order is a public function of the batch, independent of arrival, so the chain shows no request order (T-13).

### The counting state

The relay never calls `countTier1Additions` per candidate: that replays the whole history each time, every Tier 2 record's signatures and every counted addition's proofs included, which at scale can't finish within a period, and a junk flood would force one replay per queued record (T-52). It keeps instead one **counting state**, the replay of [[vouching]]'s counting rule up to a block: every group's creation block and tier, each Tier 2 group's tier tree, with its roots at every grid block still inside the window, the spent slot nullifiers, each Tier 1 group's members, and the break, if any. At each boundary the state is extended by the events after the block it was last at, each judged as the rule judges it (the proofs of newly logged additions last, batched where [[vouching]] allows), and a candidate is judged against it. The state is a function of the events alone, so it MUST give every verdict a full replay gives (part 3c-2 tests it against `vouch-count.json` and against `countTier1Additions` over the same events), and it is rebuilt by a full replay on a restart, or once a reorganisation removes a block it was extended past.

So a candidate costs its own decoding, its lookups and at most its own `2k` proofs, the proofs run last: a junk record naming a made-up root costs a lookup, a copy of a counted addition a nullifier compare, and a boundary at most `TIER1_RELAY_QUEUE_MAX · 2k` = 384 Groth16 verifications for its candidates, plus the extension by the period's new events, which the chain's own throughput bounds (T-52).

**Why this is the replay [[contracts]] asks for.** A record is sent only if it counts under every check a verifier makes, `root-mismatch`, `slot-spent`, `member` and `proof` included, against the relay's own copy of the registry, never on a simulation, which junk and copies both pass ([[contracts]], Group registry, Tier 1 additions, Who pays). What the relay can't foresee is what lands before it in `b + 1` or later: an addition spending one of the record's slots or adding its `C_V` first (a front-run copy, a voucher's double spend, or a reorganisation). The record then lands and doesn't count, its gas spent; the relay doesn't resend it, and the next boundary's replay sees it.

**Excess.** The relay sends at most `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH` additions per period, about 30 million gas at the measured 1.49 million each; the queue holds at most `TIER1_RELAY_QUEUE_MAX`. With a full queue, a queued honest record is reached at a boundary unless the walk chose `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH` others first, so a flood of junk, which never counts, delays it by nothing, and a flood of honest-looking additions needs real vouches, which the Tier 2 members' slots bound ([[vouching]]; T-02). New requests get `RELAY_BUSY` while the queue is full: a flooder who keeps it full with junk (about 64 solves per period, since the job drops all of it at each boundary) turns new vouchees away for as long as it pays, and they can go direct (T-25, Known-weak).

What a batch shows: on L2, each relayed addition landed at about one boundary, among the others the relay sent then, in an order of their contents. The block says the request reached the relay in the period before it, or earlier while the queue was full or a send was pending (T-13). The record itself already says more about its vouchers: their `root_block`s ([[vouching]], Known-weak, Sync time).

## Outcome

The client never asks the relay about its record (T-11). It learns the outcome from the chain, reading the registry's events in full from an L2 node or an indexer copy it downloads whole, and running [[vouching]]'s counting itself (`countTier1Additions`):

- **Added** once an addition of its `C_V` to the group counts and is final, through its own record or any other, a copy included: the member is the commitment, whoever logged it.
- **Refused** once a final, counted addition consumes one of its vouches' slot nullifiers without adding its `C_V` to the group: a voucher spent the slot elsewhere first, and the record can never count. It asks other vouchers for new vouches. An addition that carries one of its vouches and doesn't count consumes nothing ([[vouching]], Checks of one addition), so a junk copy changes nothing.
- **Not yet** otherwise. After about two periods plus L2 finality with nothing logged, the client may solve a new challenge and send the same record again, as long as every vouch's window still has `TIER1_RELAY_MARGIN` blocks left; a repeat is harmless (Answers). Past that, it asks its vouchers for new vouches.

## The direct path

When the relay or Cloudflare refuses a vouchee, or can't be reached, or the retry has passed with nothing logged, the ballot client offers the direct path: `addTier1Member(bytes)` with the same record, from an address of the vouchee's ([[contracts]], Group registry, Tier 1 additions; [[0014-tier1-vouching]], Rules, no account on submission). It needs nothing of ours but the signed release (T-25, T-49). As for Tier 0's ([[registration]], The direct path), the client offers it only once the vouchee confirms, having been told that the transaction is paid (about 1.5 million gas, with a limit of `TIER1_RELAY_GAS_LIMIT`) from an address whose funding is public on L2 next to `C_V` (T-11, T-13); that the address MUST be one used for this addition alone; and that the addition's block time is when they sent it, to the second. It simulates the call first and sends nothing that would revert. It may send to any Tier 1 group, published or not.

## Why these values

- **`TIER1_RELAY_PERIOD`.** The same ten minutes as Tier 0's period, and on the same UNIX-time boundaries, so a relayed addition's block says no more about when its request arrived than a Tier 0 batch's does. There is no minimum batch: a Tier 1 addition's own fields already date its vouchers' sync to the root grid (`VOUCH_ROOT_INTERVAL`, an hour), and holding the queue would eat into a window the vouchee needs.
- **`TIER1_RELAY_QUEUE_MAX` and `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH`.** Sixty-four records are about 120 KiB and at most 384 Groth16 verifications per boundary in the worker; sixteen additions a period are 96 an hour, far beyond what in-person vouching produces, while bounding the account's spend to about 30 million gas a period.
- **`TIER1_RELAY_MARGIN`.** An hour of blocks covers the wait for the next boundary and several resends; a vouchee who has less left goes direct or asks again.
- **`TIER1_RELAY_SEND_MARGIN`.** Five minutes of blocks for the judgement's proofs, the simulation and the sequencer's inclusion; a record that would need more is dropped rather than sent into a revert.
- **`TIER1_RELAY_GAS_LIMIT`.** The least limit a counted addition succeeds with was measured at about 1.79 million ([[contracts]], Group registry, Tier 1 additions, Who pays); two million leaves room without letting a call burn much more.

## Known-weak

- **Our proxy and hosting see each request whole** (T-11, T-69): Cloudflare and our hosting see the vouchee's address next to the record, and so next to `C_V` and the vouchers' slot nullifiers, before the chain does. Nothing of ours keeps them by design; Tor or the direct path through an endpoint of the vouchee's choice is the vouchee's mitigation ([[0014-tier1-vouching]], The network).
- **A relayed addition can be front-run.** Between the judgement and the block, anyone who sees the pending call, the sequencer among them, can land a copy first: the vouchee is still added through the copy, and the relay's addition lands, doesn't count, and costs the relay its gas. Bounded by `TIER1_RELAY_MAX_BATCH` per period; each copy costs its sender the same gas (T-52).
- **A junk flood turns vouchees away.** About 64 solves a period keep the queue full of junk the job drops at each boundary (the route doesn't check roots, so the junk needs no real root), so new requests are refused as busy for as long as it is paid for; the direct path remains, at the price of gas from an address (T-25, T-52). Edge rate limits per address (P2-11) raise the price.
- **The relay can refuse.** A relay that drops or delays a vouchee's record is indistinguishable from one that judged it not to count; the direct path is the answer (T-25), and nothing the relay does changes what counts.
- **Unfinalized replay.** The roots a record names are at or below the safe head, but its slots and `C_V` are judged against the latest block, unfinalized included, so a reorganisation can make a sent addition not count; the relay pays for it and the client sees **refused** or **not yet** and resends (Outcome).
- **Queue occupancy times other requests.** A flooder who fills the queue to one below `TIER1_RELAY_QUEUE_MAX` with junk (63 solves a period) and then probes it with a further well-formed record learns from a `RELAY_BUSY` that another request was queued since its last probe. Each probe spends a solve, since the busy answer comes only after Turnstile (Answers, step 8), so timing a request to a second costs about 600 solves a period, and edge rate limits per address (P2-11) bound the rate. Since the job drops the flooder's junk at the boundary, the one addition the relay then sends is that request's, so the flooder learns its `C_V` and slot nullifiers tied to the probe interval rather than the period, which a network observer can match against connection logs (T-13, T-11). Not closed: a minimum batch would hold every vouchee's record, and a flooder who pays for the solves can still isolate one; Tor or the direct path is the vouchee's mitigation.
- **A full counting state on restart.** Rebuilding the counting state replays the registry's whole history, as a verifier does ([[vouching]], Known-weak, Replay cost); boundaries are skipped until it is whole, and the queue keeps its records meanwhile.
- **Timing.** The answer to a queued record and to one already queued is the same, but the store does different work for them (a hash write or none); as for Tier 0, microseconds against Turnstile's round trip ([[registration]], Known-weak, Timing).
