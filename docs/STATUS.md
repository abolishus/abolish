- [[0011-revote-rule]] (P1-13): for each tier and nullifier, the ballot naming the newest L1 block counts (its membership proof binds the block's number and hash); two distinct ballots naming that block tie and count nothing. Board order, segments and arrival times never decide. Costs 40 bytes per ballot and a wait for L1 finality before a closed election verifies.
- [[0013-ballot-receipts]] (P1-13): a receipt is the board operator's ECDSA P-256 signature (the trustee suite of 0012) on the board it showed the voter, with the voter's entry last (index, segment, entry hash, chain head, root), plus the entry's audit path, so inclusion is checked at cast. Rejected: a CT-style promise, an Ethereum-account signature, direct submit only. What a receipt is evidence of later is [[P1-13b]], split out at the owner's request (2026-10-12) after reviews kept finding problems in that evidence model.
- [[0012-trustee-identity-signatures]] (P1-14): trustee identity keys and every trustee statement use ECDSA over P-256 with SHA-256 and the low-s rule, chosen over secp256k1 for hardware custody (FIPS HSMs, PIV cards, cloud KMS); both are checkable on chain with audited OpenZeppelin code, and secp256k1's rigid curve parameters are the trade-off the owner may prefer (P-256's come from an unexplained seed). Ed25519 has no audited on-chain verifier; a post-quantum suite waits on the `@noble/post-quantum` question.
- [[0014-tier1-vouching]] (P3-3): Tier 1 admits a commitment vouched for by 3 distinct Tier 2 (document-verified) members, anonymously. Each vouch is two Semaphore proofs: one per vouchee (distinct vouchers) and one per budget slot (12 per member across the deployment, ever). The verifier checks it from the board, which replaces the Safe's signature for Tier 1. Nothing on the board or any server records who vouched for whom, though a voucher's secret recomputes their own vouches. The costs: Tier 1 can't admit real people before Tier 2 can, vouchers aren't accountable, and collusion is bounded at 4 fake identities per colluding document rather than prevented. A transitive web of trust, a public signed graph and an operator-held graph were rejected.
- [[0015-tally-publication]] (P1-15): the tally publishes only each cell's opening; trustees exchange summed shares privately under the hybrid KEM. Publishing them, as [[0002-everlasting-privacy]] had it, fixes the summed polynomials at every seat, so a later break of posted shares' classical encryption would complete ballots with fewer than `k` posted shares. Costs public attribution of a trustee's wrong summed share (a wrong opening is still attributed), and a corrupt trustee keeps that power. Re-randomising the sums (option B) would keep attribution at the cost of a new round; publishing them anyway (option A) narrows ADR 0002 and is the owner's.
- [[0017-identity-recovery-backup]] (P2-6): the voting identity's recovery backup opens only with a user-verified WebAuthn PRF evaluation of a separate passkey on a dedicated ballot RP ID (never the account passkey; never same-site with `apps/web`; its well-known file signed with the client release), and we store the envelope under a PRF-derived locator through the ballot path, with no account session or account column. Joining a backup to an account gives nothing usable without that evaluation. Costs: whoever serves the ballot RP ID reaches the secret on every device the passkey syncs to (T-41), anyone can write rows (flooding bounded at the proxy), a database wipe loses backups, and voters without PRF support get none. Keyed by account with the account passkey's PRF (T-20) was rejected; a plaintext recovery code was rejected because the brief keeps the secret on the device, and the owner may still ask for one.
- [[0018-ceremony-possession]] (P1-14 part 2, #80): trustees prove possession of their per-election keys in four signed, hash-chained rounds: a Schnorr proof for the DH key, a signature for the signing key, and an ML-KEM challenge from every other seat, answered before the challenges are revealed, so possession rests on one non-colluding qualified seat, never on the creator. Costs four rounds with every seat online and about 261 KB of challenges at 16 seats. Rejected: one challenger (the creator) and no ML-KEM possession check. The protocol's floor is `k` qualified seats; [[0006-trustees]] rule 7, adopted on 2026-10-12, requires `max(k, n − 1)`.
- [[contracts]], Archive locator (P4-1 part 1, #73): two readings of [[0005-permanent-archive]] in the L1 board anchor. The "final anchor" that waits for the archive to confirm is a locator-only call after the seal's checkpoint, which never waits (so a slow archive can't miss the 12-hour deadline). And a call may omit the locator, though the anchoring job must include the latest confirmed one in every checkpoint call once one exists. The anchor also keeps the latest locator in state, so the archive is found after L1 history expiry. A veto means amending the ADR or redeploying a stricter anchor before mainnet.
- [[contracts]], Trustee registry quorum (P1-18 part 3, #88; P1-7 follow-up): each pinned panel's registration quorum `q` is fixed at deployment, and the contract accepts any `k ≤ q ≤ n` rather than hard-coding the owner's [[0006-trustees]] rule 7. Every deployment MUST use `q = max(k, n − 1)` under that rule (adopted 2026-10-12), and a release never pins one that doesn't, so the contract holds only the protocol's floor and a change to the owner's panel rules needs no new contract. The cost: rule 7 in the registry rests on the deployment and the release, not on the contract. Transcripts are recorded per stub and panel, so another pinned panel can't block a stub's own. A veto means checking `q` in the constructor, before anything is deployed.
- [[contracts]], Group registry (P3-2 part 1, #104): two departures from [[0004-l2-choice]]'s "threshold signature of the Safe's owners" on Tier 0 additions. The registrars (at least 2 of a set) are pinned in the registry at deployment and in the verifier's release, never read from the Safe, whose owners, threshold and modules can change; rotating them means a new registry and new groups. And they are dedicated keys, never the Safe's owners, because automatic Tier 0 registration holds them online and seizing them must not seize the anchoring authority or a timelock (T-69). Each signed batch binds its group, tier and position (a removal, the member count it saw), so it lands once, in order. The registry keeps no Merkle tree (verifiers rebuild tier trees from its events). A veto means reading the Safe's state at each addition, with its proofs in the verifier, and accepting the Safe's quorum online.
- [[tally]], Destruction (P1-15 part 3, #103): a reading of [[0002-everlasting-privacy]]'s deletion rule. Trustees destroy once every cell has a passing opening anchored, as the ADR says, or at the latest at `t_D`, once the tally window and the anchoring deadline of the segment before it have passed by L1 time, even if the tally is incomplete. They keep the openings they already signed. So a stalled tally doesn't extend everyone's exposure, at the cost that a cell no trustee had combined by then stays unopened. A missing statement is only reported, never an election failure. The seal gains a 1-day destruction window after `t_D`. A veto means a longer hold, or no fallback, in the spec before any election is registered.
- [[0019-regional-cells]] (P1-15 part 4a): regional results come from registered region groups (the group registry fixes a group's region at creation, as its tier). A definition lists each tier's regions and one root block; regions of at least 1,000 registered members (`MIN_CELL_ELECTORATE`) are cells, smaller ones fold into one remainder cell, and a remainder below the floor leaves the tier unsplit, the owner's rule applied mechanically and recomputed by the verifier. Each cell is proven against one cell tree, so no voter picks a region when voting and the remainder doesn't reveal which small region a ballot came from. Ballots name their cell. Costs: regions are the registrant's declaration at Tiers 0 and 1, each commitment's declared region is public, the floor counts registrations (Sybil padding at Tier 0 defeats it), and two elections with different remainders can difference a region. Rejected: one root per region inside a remainder cell (leaks small regions' turnout), a region proof on each ballot, and no regional cells.
- [[0021-challenged-ballots]] (P1-13 part 3a): a challenged ballot is never boarded on its own. The casting device commits to the nullifier-independent core by its tracking code (the full core hash, shown as a QR code before the cast-or-challenge choice); a second device the voter chooses records it, checks a challenge's opening against it, and finds the cast ballot by it later. The opening record carries its own core and is published only at the voter's request, after close, canonically ordered. Costs: a published opening is evidence only of what the core encrypts, not that the device showed that code before the choice (the voter's word, as under the alternative); a voter without a second device checks only the software; the client can guess which voters check; an opening's randomness lets a voter cast the opened core as a receipt for a buyer. Rejected: boarding the challenged core at challenge time (a timing link to the cast ballot and an unauthenticated flood during voting) and a nullifier-bound challenged ballot.
- [[0022-tier2-keyed-nullifier]] (P3-4 part 1): the Tier 2 nullifier is keyed by a threshold 2HashDH OPRF that the panel runs (key shared by Pedersen VSS, no public key), so neither chip data alone nor fewer than `k` trustees recompute it offline, and the attesters never see zkPassport's own nullifier. Each attester recombines the evaluation itself, and an addition counts only with `max(k, n − 1)` attesters signing its group position after every earlier nullifier reached them, so a duplicate needs 5 corrupt seats of 7. Two points for the owner: the keyed nullifier stays off the board, though the owner's 2026-10-12 decision would allow proofs there (a Diffie–Hellman OPRF leaves discrete-log relations on the key, and any chip-data holder, the issuing state first, can query for a document), so Tier 2's uniqueness stays a trust assumption in the attesters; and the key is long-lived, unlike [[0006-trustees]] rule 7's per-election keys, and is reshared on panel changes, which 0006's Rotation consequence leaves out of scope, and old shares kept only by erasure. Other costs: six of seven seats online per registration, proofs only computationally zero-knowledge towards the seats, the proof app must speak the protocol (or real-document Tier 2 waits for P5-3 and P5-4), attester lists outside the wipe-and-rebuild invariant. Rejected: RFC 9497's VOPRF with a public key and the nullifier on the board; an MPC-evaluated symmetric PRF (no audited implementation). Its part 2a amendment found that zkPassport SDK's request can't name our panel, and salted mode needs strict FaceMatch. So real-document Tier 2 through the panel waits for our own app (P5-3, P5-4), and P3-4's QR handoff runs against MOCK documents. The query and output circuits are ours, built from TACEO's gadgets, with `H1` as `hash_to_curve`'s two-map structure.
- [[0009-membership-proof]], Amendment (P2-21 part 1): the ballot client proves membership with snarkjs 0.7.5 single-threaded (3.7 s on a desktop-class host, about 16 s under 4× throttling; no real phone measured yet), in a prover worker of its own, and the ballot worker verifies the proof under the pinned key and re-randomises it with CSPRNG scalars in `packages/crypto`, so zero knowledge doesn't rest on snarkjs. A prover of our own over `@noble/curves` was rejected (a second implementation of Semaphore's witness, and an estimated 5.5× slower). Owner asks, all approved on 2026-10-17 and landed: a gated `packages/membership-prover`, a second worker, snarkjs and ffjavascript in the prover worker's bundle allow-list with the worker `fetch`/`Worker` ban kept, `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` and `connect-src 'self'` in its CSP, the key vendored and re-fetched by CI, and GPL-3.0 code in the AGPL client.
- [[0024-tier0-registration]] (P3-2 part 4a, #151): Tier 0 registration needs no account. The ballot client sends a commitment, its group and a Turnstile token (solved on a separate challenge page) on the ballot path, and the signing service adds commitments in shuffled batches on a fixed period. One commitment per account was rejected: an account costs the same one solve, and it would record account → commitment for every registrant. A self-paid Tier 0 addition (part 5) meets the brief's direct-submit rule, which part 1's registry leaves unmet at Tier 0. For the owner: this reads "Tier 0: passkey" and Phase 2's "at Tier 0 with accounts" as not requiring an account to vote; if either does, account-gated blind tokens (option C) replace B. Costs: Cloudflare sees each registration request whole, the gas payer of a self-paid addition can link the registrant, and, under the self-paid path, a signed copy, or a self-paid copy made by anyone who sees a queued request, can move a registrant to another regional cell (T-16). Amendment 1 (P3-2 part 5a) settles the self-paid path: one commitment per call with no position and a fee of 0.0002 ETH locked in the registry against floods, no duplicate check (signed occurrences rank first, then self-paid ones by chain order), and no removals in self-paid groups, so no Tier 0 removal revokes a commitment where a self-paid group is listed.
- [[moderation-policy]] (P2-10): moderation acts only on display text and listing, never on the board, a definition, a tally or an election's timing, with a closed list of grounds and an append-only public log. One trade-off reaches results indirectly: hiding an open poll for `illegal` or `personal-data` stops voting through our ballot client, which won't show text it can't check, so it can lower turnout on a reason code nobody outside can check; voters keep the direct-submit path. Since the owner's narrowing (P2-10 part 5) a `threat` hide of an open poll withholds the text only from our web pages: the ballot client keeps showing the committed text from IPFS, which we keep pinned until the poll closes, and it treats a poll whose state it can't read as visible. Delisting only, or extending the `threat` treatment to the other two grounds, were the alternatives.
- [[0020-entry-points]] (P4-4 part 1): the ballot client runs only on the second domain, which is also the recovery backup's RP ID, and at `abolishus.eth` through a gateway that gives the name its own origin (`abolishus.eth.limo`), plus `apps/node`'s local origin; abolish.us serves `apps/web` and links to the client, since a ballot host under it would be same-site with `apps/web` ([[0017-identity-recovery-backup]]). `.ch` is recommended for the second domain, `.is` as the fallback. Costs: seizing the second domain orphans backups and puts its seizer inside the RP boundary; a voter's identity doesn't follow them to another entry point until a voter-initiated transfer exists; the ENS gateway is trusted for the bytes it serves. Rejected: a ballot host on another US name, and listing the gateway as a related origin of the RP ID.
- [[0025-contract-event-evidence]] (P1-18 part 4a): a contract-event record (`0x0008`) carries exactly what its contract's event accumulator hashes, and the accumulator now also binds each event's L2 block number and timestamp (a new tag, before anything is deployed); the only evidence archived is the accumulator's storage proofs at each anchored period and at close, never a transaction, receipt or header, amending [[0005-permanent-archive]]. ## Known-weak

- **An obligation root holds an election's counted set open** ([[P1-12f]] part 1b-2, [[verifier]], Counted set and tally, step 2). The verifier reads contract-event version 5 but not a root's leaves (parts 2 and 3) or the attestations that make them available ([[P4-3]] part 2c), so a root naming an election in its counted extent leaves 5.9 and the tally unverifiable. One registered seat, or anyone holding its per-election key, can send one for any election it serves and so hold that election unverifiable (never failed) until part 3 (T-25): any deploy of the board with the root call waits on P1-12f part 3.
- **The election registry may not fit a large definition in one transaction** (P1-18 part 4b-3b-2, [[contracts]], Known-weak). A version-6 definition with all three tiers at their list limits costs about 37 million gas to register; under a per-transaction cap such as EIP-7825's `2^24` it could never be registered. The decoder must be made cheaper for the target L2's cap, or the limits lowered in a later definition version, before any registry is deployed.
- **Election-definition versions 1 and 3 are not yet retired.** They bind no receipt keys, so their receipts are never evidence a third party can attribute; [[election-definition]] requires both retired, with a cutoff, before the first election registry is deployed ([[P1-18]]), and nothing enforces it until a verifier release pins that cutoff (P1-13b part 2b).
- **`@noble/post-quantum` (ML-KEM-768) is unaudited** (P1-12 part 2, #58). It is self-audited at 0.6.1, and the pinned 0.7.1 has changes since. The brief allows it on testnets only, in a hybrid KEM with X25519, which the owner reads as covering #58's ristretto255 hybrid ([[0016-share-kem-dh-group]]); an independent audit of the code in use is a mainnet prerequisite, and funding it is the owner's ([[0002-everlasting-privacy]], Decision). Its scope covers both compositions of the hybrid: share encryption and the summed-share message of [[tally]] (P1-15 part 2b, #93), which gates decapsulation by a signature and has no proof of knowledge of the ephemeral.
- **Any two trustees can stop elections from opening** (T-25, T-69; P1-7), and, since P3-4 part 2b-2b ([[nullifier-reshare]]), any two of the Tier 2 key's holders can stop every change of its panel, including one that would replace them (T-54). Under [[0006-trustees]] rule 7 an election opens only with `max(k, n − 1)` seats taking part and signing, so two refusals block it, selectively if they choose; one state (rule 1) or one hosting provider (rule 2) may hold enough seats to do it. This follows from rule 7 and isn't stated in the owner's decision; for the owner to confirm or revisit.
- **The API has no cache or metrics no-join test yet** (T-10). P2-1 covers tables, P2-3 part 1 the API's request log and P2-4 part 1 queue job data, Redis and the worker's log. The API part that adds a cache or metrics extends its test, and so does each new queue (by its side and fields). The proxy in front of the API logs on its own; [[P2-11]] must configure it to keep no addresses or query strings (T-11).
- **A trustee node's bounded receipt obligation can leave a replaced ballot undelivered** (P1-12c part 1, [[delivery]], Residuals). A node owes a contract-path receipt only for the ballot the re-vote rule selects from its view. When a voter's newest ballot reached the trustees but never the board, and the operator withholds board data so no node can show it absent, nodes owe only that newest ballot's receipt, so an operator that also refuses the older, counted ballot's board-path receipts can drop it unattributed. A newest ballot boarded only after `s_A` no longer does this: since P1-12c part 2 the node's view applies the close rule (5.5). Before P1-12c nodes owed both, at the price of an unbounded obligation. It MUST be closed (P4-3's data availability) or accepted by the owner before any election registry is deployed (T-25, T-54).
- **The forced path caps an election's electorate** (P1-12c part 2, [[delivery]], Capacity). A trustee node lends its keys only to elections whose contract-path receipts and complaints, with every overlapping election it serves, it can force through L1 by the deadlines at the portal's sustainable deposit target (2,000,000 gas per L1 block, shared with every depositor). With receipts and complaints forced as batches ([[P1-12d]], [[P1-12e]]: about 4,100 gas a receipt and 8,900 a complaint, measured cold) that is at most 20,992 members with 3 seats and `R` of one hour, or 434,688 with `R` of 21 hours (the most the windows allow), so honest seats refuse larger elections, which then can't be registered. A national electorate needs more than any record per receipt or complaint on L1: [[0031-national-delivery-obligation]] commits each seat's obligation as one root, which [[P1-12f]] builds; its record, leaves, availability rule and capacity are specified ([[delivery]], Obligation root, Leaf availability), and the cap stands until its board call, its code and [[P4-3]] part 2c's counted-extent attestation are built. The trustee node runs the check for the definition version it reads (1); a release that reads later versions MUST count their seats and members the same way.
- **Approved stubs nobody registers hold a seat's forced-path capacity until they open** (P1-12c part 2, [[delivery]], Residuals). Whoever can get ceremonies run (the coordinator, and creators it accepts) can take every seat's capacity for chosen closing times without registering anything, until each stub's `opens_at`, so honest seats refuse real elections closing then (T-25). Bounding it needs an admission rule for stubs (a quota per creator, or moderation), [[P2-13]]'s; until a node reads registrations from finalized L2 it releases nothing. It MUST be closed, or accepted by the owner, before any election registry is deployed.
- **Withheld board data can keep a failing posting's ballot out of a node's complaint scope** (P1-12c part 2, [[delivery]], Residuals). A node owes complaints only for the ballots it holds at `c_I`, at most two per tier and nullifier, so an operator withholding a counted ballot that a cheating client posted invalid keeps it counted, and that seat's summed shares fail (posted to every seat, it stalls the tally). It replaces an obligation to complain about every failing posting, which anyone with a member's proof could grow without bound. It MUST be closed by P4-3's data availability or accepted by the owner before any election registry is deployed.
- **Live results run only where we hold a threshold of default seats until the nodes' withholding check exists** (P2-15 part 1a, [[tally]], What a node does and Residuals of live results). Authors who add trustees can't choose live results until then. A trustee withholding its own receipt to hold a ballot out stays unmitigated.
- **Live results: the final tally can disclose a released ballot, and a batch's zero count discloses** (P2-15 part 1a, [[tally]], Residuals of live results). - **CODEOWNERS doesn't cover every instruction source.** The `main` ruleset requires code-owner review (verified 2026-10-12), and CODEOWNERS covers `/.github/`, `/.claude/`, `/AGENTS.md`, `/CLAUDE.md`, `/docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`, `/pnpm-workspace.yaml`, `/vite.config.ts` and `/tsconfig.base.json`. Not covered, by the owner's choice so agents can keep writing ADRs and adding packages unattended: `/docs/THREAT_MODEL.md`, `/docs/adr/`, `/pnpm-lock.yaml` and the root `/package.json`. A PR that edits those is judged by the two model reviews (CI's lockfile policy still enforces package age and integrity).
- **`crypto-review` covers only the protocol packages, `docs/spec/` and agent instruction files** (the brief's scope, kept narrow so each run finishes). Changes to `packages/core`, `packages/sdk`, both Capacitor plugins, `apps/ballot`, ADRs, the threat model, STATUS, the brief and the build config get `claude-review` plus the in-session subagent review only. CI pins the brief verbatim; until the owner adds ADRs and the threat model to CODEOWNERS, nothing but `claude-review` stands between a weakening edit there and `main`.
- **Review workflow bodies run from the PR head.** The review prompts and verdict script are read from the base commit, but GitHub runs the workflow file itself from the PR, so a PR that edits `claude-review.yml` or `crypto-review.yml` could weaken its own gate. Only owner review of `/.github/` (CODEOWNERS plus code-owner review) closes this.
- **The review model's file access is limited by a deny-list.** It has no shell and can't read `/proc`, `/etc`, the runner's temp and tool directories or its home configuration, but anything else readable on the runner outside those paths isn't explicitly blocked. The job holds the review token and a `pull-requests: write` GitHub token; the verdict is decided in a separate job the model never touches.
- **Toolchain hashes are trust-on-first-use.** The sha256 pins in `.github/scripts/install-toolchain.sh` were recorded from the upstream GitHub releases on 2026-10-09; nothing cross-checks them against upstream attestations, and `repro-build` can't detect a deterministic malicious binary because both builds use the same one.
- **Merge queue:** the review checks skip `merge_group`. If the owner enables a merge queue, it must use batch size 1, or the combined tree of a batch is never model-reviewed.
- **Lockfile policy covers `packages:` only.** It checks which bytes can be installed; rewiring a `snapshots:` edge to another version already in `packages:` isn't checked. `crypto-review` doesn't run on lockfile or catalog changes (the brief scopes it to the four protocol packages). CI rejects overrides, patches and package extensions, but a catalog bump of `@noble/*` or `@aztec/bb.js`, or a rewired edge, is seen only by `claude-review` and owner review. P1-9's closure check (#18) keeps the gated packages' closure `@noble/*`-only but doesn't judge a version bump; P1-16b (#20) ties the `@aztec/bb.js` and `@noir-lang/*` pins to the toolchain.
- **Required checks and auto-merge depend on repo rulesets** that agents may not change. Verified 2026-10-12: `main` requires `ci`, `reference-election`, `repro-build`, `claude-review` and `crypto-review`, up-to-date branches, resolved conversations and code-owner review, so two PRs can't merge into a combination no review saw.
- **Publishing is only as safe as the `npm` environment's branch policy.** npm trusted publishing binds to the repository, workflow file name and environment, not to a branch. Verified 2026-10-12: the `npm` environment deploys from `main` only, with a 30-minute wait timer, so a modified `release.yml` on another branch can't publish.
- **`release.yml` is unexercised** until the first changeset merges after #4. The three packages exist on npm (placeholder `0.0.0` as `latest`). Unverified until then: `vp node` running the npm CLI in a sparse checkout, and npm's OIDC exchange. Releases publish under `next`; the owner promotes `latest`.
- **Jobs in one release run share its runner token.** Code run by Changesets or by a package build can upload and delete artifacts in the run and write Actions cache entries on `main`. So nothing trusts an artifact on its own: `verify` requires each tarball to match the hash its own build job output (job outputs can't be written by other jobs) and fails on any other `tgz-*` artifact, and `sign` and `publish` check the release against `verify`'s manifest output. Tampering with artifacts can only fail the run (denial of service). A re-run of a release fails at `verify` (its artifacts already exist), so a stuck release needs a new commit on `main`. Cache entries are a residual (T-56): a poisoned dependency cache could be restored by later `ci` runs. The `checks` job trusts check-run names from GitHub Actions; a second workflow defining a job called `ci` could mask a failing one (needs a `.github/` change, so owner-reviewed).
- **Release signatures are kept 90 days.** The cosign bundles live in the run's `release-signed` artifact; the SLSA attestations (GitHub attestations API) and npm's own provenance are permanent. A permanent home for the bundles (GitHub releases, IPFS) comes with the ballot-client release (P2-8).
- **`next` snapshot versions don't sort by time** (`<x.y.z>-next-<commit>`); the `next` dist-tag always points at the last publish, but semver ranges over prereleases are meaningless. The ballot client, OpenAPI spec and container images aren't released yet (P2-8, P2-2, P2-11).
- **Voting-identity recovery is specified and stored, not yet used** ([[0017-identity-recovery-backup]], P2-6: the spec and `packages/crypto` in #91, storage and the API routes in #94, expiry run daily by the worker in part 3b; the ballot client's flows are P2-8). Once the client uses it: whoever serves the ballot RP ID can read a voter's secret on any device the backup passkey syncs to (T-41, T-20); the backup routes are an anonymous write surface bounded only at the proxy (T-52); a Postgres wipe loses every backup (T-66); and voters without PRF support have no backup.
- **The ballot client's bundle and CSP check judges the client's own build** (P2-8, T-59). Since #82 the policy (runtime allowlist, no analytics SDK, the pinned CSP) is an owner-reviewed `.github/tools/ci` check, but the build it judges runs code the PR controls, including any `apps/ballot/vite.config.*` (a `.js` or `.mjs` file takes precedence over `.ts`), any `postcss.config.*` from `apps/ballot` up to the root, and Tailwind `@plugin`/`@config` code in any CSS the build loads. Any of it could detect the check and emit clean output only there. Requested from the owner: CODEOWNERS for `apps/ballot/vite.config.*`, `postcss.config.*` and the CSS of `apps/ballot` and `packages/ui`, or a decision to accept the residual.
- **Share receipts, postings and complaints have no direct-submit path yet** (P1-12 part 3b, [[delivery]], Direct submit; T-25, T-54). Until P1-18 adds one, nothing can post (postings deliver only as contract events, the owner's decision in [[0002-everlasting-privacy]]), and the board operator can withhold board-path receipts and complaints: a ballot whose receipts it refuses is silently undelivered, and a refused complaint keeps an invalid ballot counted. [[delivery]] requires the contract path before any election registry is deployed. The verifier doesn't run the delivery check yet either (P1-19, with the ballot record).
- **Tally openings have no direct-submit path yet** (P1-15 part 2a, [[tally]], Residuals; T-54). An operator that boards no opening before the seal fails the election, unattributed. [[tally]] requires a contract path for tally openings, emitted as events under the event accumulator, before any election registry is deployed; P1-18 owns it.
- **`tally-late` is specified, not built** (P1-15 part 2a; [[verifier]] 5.6, 7.1; T-25). The tally combines over the counted extent (`segment ≤ s_S`), and a late entry that would change a cell's counted set must fail the election, against the operator where it could have boarded the entry in time and otherwise as an unattributed settlement overrun, or an operator could silently drop a direct-submitted re-vote. P1-18 must also fix the contract-event window as one summed constant in seconds (forced-inclusion delay with a missed-slot margin, L1 finality, boarding). Since P1-19 part 4o the verifier decides 7.1 only where every entry that could change a counted set lies at or below the floors of `s_S` (`max(s_F, s_K)`), where `tally-late` can't fail; a board with a contract-path delivery record or an ordinary ballot leaves it unverifiable until 4.4 authenticates their timestamps and `s_S` is known. P1-12 part 3, P1-13 and P1-18 must say which of their records (postings, receipts, complaints, contract events) can change the counted set and keep their windows inside `W_S`. No election registry is deployed before it.
- **No deployment verifies until verifier 1.2 binds creation code** (P1-18 part 5 follow-ups, T-64; [[contracts]], Board anchor, Known-weak). 1.2 compares runtime code only, so a deployer's own initcode can write storage and emit logs (pre-written anchor words, a future `DefinitionRegistered`, mismatched panel or registrar logs) and still return the release runtime code, forging election failures blamed on others. 1.2 stays `not-implemented` until binding to the release creation code and pinned constructor arguments is specified and checked, and it MUST be specified before anything is deployed: P4-1 part 2 (the anchor's Sepolia deployment) waits for that design.
- **The threat model's mitigations are almost all planned, not built** (see [[THREAT_MODEL]], status column). Its "Not mitigated" section lists what no planned work addresses. P1-1 merged in #3, so feature work may start.
