# T-38: Ballot substitution and re-vote replay

- Area: Integrity and verifiability (G-7, G-8, G-9)
- Adversary: A-4, A-2, A-9
- Status: **Planned** for the signal binding (part a); **needs design** (P1-13) for the re-vote rule (part b, [[verifier]] check 6.1). Residual: a coercer holding the voter's secret can always re-vote (T-45, T-46).

## Threat

Re-voting means a nullifier legitimately appears more than once, so (a) anyone who sees a voter's membership proof (on the board, or in the L2 mempool on direct submit) attaches a different ciphertext to it, and that ballot becomes "last"; or (b) after the voter re-votes, someone (a coercer, say) resubmits the voter's earlier ballot-and-proof pair so the coerced ballot is "last" again.

## Mitigation

The membership proof's signal binds the hash of the ballot (ciphertext and validity proofs) and the election definition, so a proof can't be moved to another ballot. Board and contracts reject a byte-identical repeat of an earlier ballot entry, and the re-vote rule rejects a replayed earlier pair whatever its bytes (requirement R1 in [[verifier]] check 6.1), so an old pair can't become last again. The re-vote rule must reject replays whatever their bytes (Groth16 proofs are malleable), must not let any value chosen at cast time freeze a ballot as last (T-45), and must not let the board operator choose the counted ballot by withholding or reordering unanchored entries (T-23, T-25); [[verifier]] check 6.1 states these requirements and the candidate constructions, and P1-13 specifies a rule that meets them (P1-8, P1-13, P1-17).
